# Pre-Publication Checklist (בדיקה לפני פרסום)

A worksheet for checking a post, leaflet, report, video or speech that names people or organisations before it goes out: a defamation scan, the evidence and defences behind each claim, a privacy check, a right-of-reply record, and an online-safety plan. Taught in lessons [08.1](../lessons/module-08/lesson-01.md) to [08.5](../lessons/module-08/lesson-05.md) and used in the [Module 8 project](../projects/m08-publication-check.md).

> [!IMPORTANT]
> For use in your own name (or in the name of the group that is publishing) only. This is not legal advice — check current rules before publishing. A checklist lowers risk; it does not remove it. For a serious accusation against a named person, consider asking a lawyer before you publish.

## How to use it

1. Fill section 0 once for the whole publication.
2. Copy every sentence that names or points to a person, company or organisation into section 1. Do not paraphrase: the exact words are what a court reads.
3. Work through sections 2–5 only for the rows that section 1 marks as defamation or as a privacy risk.
4. Decide in section 6, and keep the completed sheet with your evidence file. If a demand letter arrives later (lesson 08.3), this sheet is your first piece of evidence of good faith.

Risk guide: **high** = a factual accusation (especially of a crime or dishonesty) about an identifiable private person; **medium** = factual claims about a company or public figure, or a harsh opinion that mixes in facts; **low** = opinion on known public conduct, or criticism of an institution or decision.

| # | The factual core in one sentence | Evidence I hold (document, data, photo, witness, official record) and its date | Public interest: why the public needs to know | Defence I rely on (s. 13 / s. 14 / s. 15(__)) | What is still unproven |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| | | | | | |
| | | | | | |

Questions before relying on a defence:

- [ ] **Truth (s. 14):** can I prove the core of the claim with admissible evidence, not only "everyone knows"?
- [ ] **Public interest:** does the claim concern public conduct, public money, public safety, or a public debate — not only private life?
- [ ] **Good faith (s. 15, s. 16):** did I take reasonable steps to check the facts; do I believe them; is the harm no greater than needed?
- [ ] **Opinion (s. 15(4), (6)):** are the facts the opinion is based on stated or well known, and are they true?
- [ ] **Complaint route (s. 15(8)):** would a complaint to the competent authority do the job better than a public post?

| Item in the publication | Personal information? (name, face, address, phone, health, sex life, political views, criminal past, location) | Taken in a public or private place? | Consent? | Public interest that justifies it | Action (keep / blur / remove / ask consent) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| | | | | | |
| | | | | | |

- [ ] No home address, phone number, ID number, licence plate or workplace of a private person.
- [ ] No faces of minors, victims of an incident, or bystanders who appear incidentally, unless blurred.
- [ ] No private letter, chat or email published without permission (s. 2(5)), unless a strong public interest justifies it and I have taken advice.
- [ ] No captions that make a photographed person look ridiculous or humiliated (s. 2(4)).

A fair chance to respond, given in time, and the response reported fairly, is strong evidence of good faith.

## 5. Online safety and harassment risk (lesson 08.5)

- [ ] The post targets a decision, institution or public conduct, not a private person's home or family.
- [ ] Comment moderation is on; group rules forbid threats and personal details.
- [ ] I have archived the final version (screenshot with date and URL, or a PDF).
- [ ] If the publication may draw harassment toward me or others, I have the evidence log from lesson 08.5 ready.
