# IHL Incident Analysis (ניתוח אירוע לפי המשפט ההומניטרי הבינלאומי)

A worksheet in English for analysing an incident in an armed conflict under international humanitarian law (IHL), neutrally: facts, applicable framework, rules, evidence gaps, competing interpretations and a limited conclusion. Taught in lessons [15.1](../lessons/module-15/lesson-01.md) to [15.5](../lessons/module-15/lesson-05.md) and used in the [Module 15 project](../projects/m15-incident-analysis.md).

> [!IMPORTANT]
> For your own study and your own name only. This is not legal advice and not an investigation. The course takes no position on the conflict. Use invented or public material only: do not travel to conflict areas, contact armed groups or collect evidence on the ground. If you write about a real incident, describe the debate and the evidence gaps; do not publish a verdict about named people.

## How to use it

1. Work through the sections in order. Do not write section 7 until sections 1–6 are complete.
2. In every section, attribute every contested statement: "X says …", "the Court found …". Nothing contested goes in your own voice.
3. Apply each rule to every party, the attacker and the defender.
4. Keep the date you checked each source. Live proceedings and official positions change.
5. When you finish, reread the whole sheet as each party's representative and as an independent researcher, and fix anything that is unfair rather than merely unwelcome.

## 7. Limited conclusion (no more than 150 words)

```text
On the public evidence as of [date], [what can be said].
[What cannot be determined, and why.]
If [fact], the conduct may have complied with [rule]; if [other fact], it may not have.
The evidence that would decide it: [list from section 5].
The duties of [party 1] and [party 2] are both engaged: [one sentence each].
Bodies that have examined the incident: [name, finding, each attributed], or "none known".
```

## 8. Final check

- [ ] Every fact has a source, a date and a status.
- [ ] Every contested statement is attributed.
- [ ] Every rule is quoted exactly with its source.
- [ ] The rules are applied to every party.
- [ ] The evidence gaps say what would decide each rule.
- [ ] At least two competing interpretations are stated fairly, each with its source.
- [ ] The conclusion does not go beyond the evidence and says what would change it.
- [ ] Nothing in this sheet names a private person or invites action against anyone.
