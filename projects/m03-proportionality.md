# Module 3 project: Proportionality analysis of a real measure

Choose one real law, regulation, by-law or published decision that limits a basic right, and analyse it the way a constitutional court would: the right and the infringement, the limitation clause, the four proportionality tests, the likely remedy, and the strongest argument on the other side. Finish with a one-page position letter, in Hebrew and in your own name, asking the body that controls the measure for one concrete change.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> This project is a dry run. If you decide to send the letter, act in your own name only (or in the name of the group that is acting). This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing.

## What you produce

1. **A completed proportionality analysis** using the [proportionality analysis template](../templates/proportionality-analysis.md), sections 0–8 (about 3–4 pages).
2. **A constitutional fact sheet** for the right involved (lesson 03.1), attached as an appendix.
3. **A Hebrew position letter** (one page) to the Knesset committee, the minister, the authority or the council that controls the measure, built from section 9 of the template and the [formal letter template](../templates/formal-letter.md). It asks for one concrete change.
4. **A neutrality note** (one paragraph): how the same analysis would apply if the measure targeted a group on the other side of your issue.

Time: about 5 hours.

## Steps

1. **Choose the measure.** It must be real, in force or before the Knesset, and in writing. Good sources: the Knesset's legislation database, Wikisource, a ministry's regulations page, your municipality's by-laws. It may come from your Module 1 issue map or Module 2 research memo. Avoid measures already decided by the Supreme Court unless you analyse a part the Court did not decide.
2. **Fact sheet (03.1).** Basic Law, section, exact text, entrenchment, leading ruling.
3. **Stage one (03.2, 03.4).** Name the right, written or derived. If derived, cite the ruling that recognises it and its test. Describe the infringement with at least three concrete facts.
4. **Threshold conditions (03.2).** By law or under express authorisation; the state's values; the date of the measure (s. 10); special rules (s. 9, s. 12, Freedom of Occupation s. 8).
5. **Proportionality (03.3).** Proper purpose with its source; rational connection with evidence; at least two concrete less harmful alternatives; marginal benefit against marginal harm.
6. **Remedy and forum.** What exactly should change, and who can change it. If a court, what remedy is realistic (void, suspended invalidity, narrow reading)?
7. **The other side.** Write the strongest argument for the measure, as its supporters would make it, and your answer.
8. **Context check (03.5).** In one or two sentences, note whether the current constitutional dispute affects your measure (for example, a pending bill on judicial review) and the date you checked.
9. **Write the letter.** One concrete request, the section, a reasoned alternative, a reply date, and a delivery method that proves receipt (lesson 02.5).
10. **Write the neutrality note.**
11. **Self-assess** with the rubric, then compare with the model answer.

## Self-assessment rubric

Score each row 0, 1 or 2. 16 or more out of 20 means the analysis is ready to use; below 12, redo the weakest rows.

| Criterion | 0 — not yet | 1 — partly | 2 — done well |
|---|---|---|---|
| The measure | Vague or not in writing | Named, but section, date or link missing | Exact name, section, date, maker and link |
| Right and source | No Basic Law or ruling named | Right named, source vague | Basic Law and section quoted, or derived right with its ruling and test |
| Infringement | Adjectives only | One or two facts | Three or more concrete facts, with who is affected |
| Threshold conditions | Skipped | Some checked | Law/express authorisation, date (s. 10) and special rules all checked |
| Proper purpose | Missing, or attacked without basis | Stated without source | Stated precisely, with its source, and conceded where legitimate |
| Rational connection | Asserted | Reasoned without evidence | Evidence given; over- or under-inclusiveness noted |
| Least harmful means | No alternative | One vague alternative | Two or more concrete alternatives, each tested against the purpose |
| Narrow sense and remedy | Missing | Benefit and harm compared with doing nothing | Marginal comparison with the best alternative; realistic remedy and forum |
| Other side and neutrality | Missing or a straw man | Present but weak or unattributed | Strongest counter-argument, fairly stated and answered; neutrality note |
| Letter | Missing, accusatory, or for someone else | Own name, but vague request | Own name, one concrete change, section, alternative, date, proof of receipt |

<details><summary>Model answer</summary>

This condensed model uses an invented law, so that it does not take a side in a real dispute. Your analysis will be about a real measure and longer.

**0. The measure.** The (invented) "Public Order at Demonstrations Law" s. 3: a police officer may require any person within 100 metres of a demonstration to show identification, and the details are kept in a police database for five years. Passed 2026, in force; text on the Knesset database.

**1. Right and infringement.** Privacy (Basic Law: Human Dignity and Liberty, s. 7(a), "כל אדם זכאי לפרטיות ולצנעת חייו"), and freedom of demonstration, derived from dignity (*Levi v. Southern District Police Commander*, HCJ 153/83: protect first, ban last). Facts: (1) everyone near any demonstration is covered, including passers-by and residents; (2) records are kept for five years regardless of any offence; (3) people told reporters they now avoid protests for fear of being listed — the chilling effect falls on protests of every political colour.

**2. Threshold.** In a law: yes. Values: not in dispute. Date: after 1992, so s. 10 does not protect it. Section 9 and s. 12: not relevant.

**3. Purpose.** Explanatory notes: identifying people who commit violence at demonstrations. Proper; conceded.

**4. Rational connection.** Partly: identification helps trace violent offenders, but checking everyone within 100 metres is over-inclusive, and five-year retention of non-offenders does not advance the stated purpose.

**5. Less harmful alternatives.** (a) ID checks only on reasonable suspicion of an offence (achieves the purpose almost as well; police already film demonstrations); (b) deletion within 30 days unless an investigation is opened; (c) a log of every check, reviewed by a senior officer.

**6. Narrow sense.** The marginal benefit of checking everyone, and keeping everything for five years, over (a)+(b) is small; the marginal harm to privacy and to the willingness to protest is large. Conclusion: s. 3 is likely disproportionate in its scope and retention period, though its purpose is proper.

**7. Remedy.** Ask the Knesset Constitution Committee to amend s. 3 to require reasonable suspicion and a 30-day retention limit. If challenged in court, a realistic remedy is a suspended declaration of invalidity or a narrow reading of s. 3, not cancellation of the whole law.

**8. The other side.** Supporters: violence at demonstrations is hard to trace afterwards; a general power deters it and protects lawful protesters. Answer: reasonable-suspicion checks plus existing filming achieve most of that, without listing thousands of lawful participants.

**Context check.** As of 12 October 2026, no bill changing judicial review of laws had passed; the analysis applies the existing limitation clause.

**Letter (extract).**

```text
לכבוד יו״ר ועדת החוקה, חוק ומשפט

הנדון: סעיף 3 לחוק (הסדר הזיהוי בהפגנות) – בקשה לתיקון

1. סעיף 3 לחוק מאפשר לדרוש הזדהות מכל אדם בטווח 100 מטרים מהפגנה ולשמור את הפרטים חמש שנים. ההסדר פוגע בזכות לפרטיות המעוגנת בסעיף 7(א) לחוק־יסוד: כבוד האדם וחירותו, ובחופש ההפגנה.
2. אינני חולק על כך שהתכלית – זיהוי מי שנוקט אלימות בהפגנות – היא תכלית ראויה.
3. ואולם קיים אמצעי שפגיעתו פחותה ומשיג את התכלית במידה דומה: דרישת הזדהות רק בחשד סביר לעבירה, ומחיקת הפרטים בתוך 30 יום אם לא נפתחה חקירה.
4. לפיכך אבקש לתקן את סעיף 3 בהתאם, ולהשיב לפנייתי עד יום ___.
```

**Neutrality note.** The analysis does not depend on who demonstrates: the same over-inclusiveness and retention problem arises at protests for or against the government, by settlers or by residents of Arab towns, and the same alternatives answer it.

Rubric self-score for this model: 19/20 (the infringement row would need sourced data, not press quotes, for full marks).
