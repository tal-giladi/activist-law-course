# Module 6 project: Protest legal plan

Write a complete legal plan for a real or realistic protest: the rights analysis, the licence or notice you need with the filled-in Hebrew form, your answer to likely police conditions, the roles and scripts for the day, a legal review of your signs and posts, and the plan for complaints and follow-up. The plan is a dry run unless you are actually organising the protest.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Act in your own name only (or in the name of the group that is organising). This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing: the police procedure and forms may have changed since the 2021 version quoted in 06.2. Do not organise a protest only to complete this project. Never plan to resist police physically.

## What you produce

1. **A protest legal plan** using [protest-legal-plan.md](../templates/protest-legal-plan.md), sections 0–6 (about 4–6 pages).
2. **The filing documents** from [protest-notice.md](../templates/protest-notice.md): Form A or Form B, filled in; the 500+ safety notice if it applies; and a reply to one imagined police condition you would dispute.
3. **A one-page briefing sheet** for stewards and participants: roles, meeting point, sign rules, the detention card, and what to do if someone is hurt.
4. **A complaint-ready kit**: the evidence log from [police-complaint.md](../templates/police-complaint.md), prepared in advance, and a list of which incident would go to מח״ש and which to the Public Complaints Unit.

Time: about 5 hours, spread over a week.

## Steps

1. **Choose the protest.** A real cause you care about, or a realistic one from any part of the political map. Fix the form (vigil, rally, march, convoy), the place, the date and the size.
2. **Rights analysis (06.1).** Fill in section 1: every conflicting interest, vertical or horizontal, the evidence of risk, and the least restrictive condition. Run the mirror test.
3. **Permits (06.2).** Decide: licence, coordination notice, safety notice, Knesset or holy-site permit, owner's consent. Write the deadlines. Fill in the Hebrew form completely.
4. **Conditions.** Predict the three conditions the police are most likely to set; accept the reasonable ones and write a reply to the one you would dispute, with an alternative.
5. **The day (06.3).** Assign every role; write the liaison script and the detention card; plan filming and backup; write the personal kit list.
6. **Messages (06.5).** Review at least five signs, chants or posts; rewrite any "maybe"; write the group's sign rules.
7. **After (06.4).** Prepare the evidence log, the channel decision, the footage-preservation request and the appeal calendar.
8. **Briefing sheet.** One page, plain language, Hebrew or English as your group needs.
9. **Self-assess** with the rubric, then compare with the model answer.

## Self-assessment rubric

Score each row 0, 1 or 2. 14 or more out of 16 means your plan is ready to use; below 10, redo the weakest rows.

| Criterion | 0 — not yet | 1 — partly | 2 — done well |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rights analysis | Argues the cause, not the law | Interests listed, conditions vague | Each interest sorted vertical or horizontal, with evidence and a specific less restrictive condition; mirror test done |
| Licence decision | Wrong or missing | Right answer, no reason | Right answer with s. 83 and AG para. 8; 500+, Knesset and holy-site checks done |
| Filing documents | Missing or half-filled | Complete but late or to the wrong station | Complete, to the event's station, 5+ days ahead, own conditions proposed, proof of receipt planned |
| Reply to conditions | None | Objects without an alternative | Objection based on near certainty and AG section ז, with a concrete alternative and a response date |
| Day-of plan | No roles | Roles but no scripts | Every role named; liaison script; detention card with the 3-hour rule; filming and backup plan; no-physical-resistance rule |
| Message review | None | Texts listed, no legal test | Each text tested against ss. 144ד2, 144ב, CT Law s. 24, s. 192; risky texts rewritten without losing the point |
| Aftermath plan | None | Complaint mentioned only | Channel table, evidence log, footage-preservation request, 60-day appeal calendar, lawyer-first rule |
| Neutrality and safety | Plan targets people or groups; safety ignored | Some checks | Messages target decisions; mirror test applied; medical and legal contacts ready |

<details><summary>Model answer</summary>

This is a condensed model based on a realistic dry run. Your plan will be about your own protest.

**0. Summary.**

Cause: the Ministry of Health's decision to close the night emergency room at a Galilee town's medical centre. Target: the Minister of Health and the hospital's director. Form: a march of about 700 people from the town's central square to the medical centre, with a rally and three short speeches at the gate. Date: Friday 10:00–12:30. Organisers: a residents' committee (two named organisers), liaison: Amal Khoury.

**1. Rights analysis (extract).**

| Interest | Balance | Evidence | Condition proposed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ambulance access to the medical centre | Vertical | The gate is the only ambulance entrance | Rally on the lawn beside the gate; 4-metre corridor kept clear by stewards |
| Friday market traffic on the main street | Vertical | Market until 13:00 | March in the right lane, 25 minutes; return on the pavement |
| Patients' rest | Horizontal | Wards face the lawn | Amplification only for speeches, under 70 dB, no drums |

No intelligence of violence exists; the committee's past three protests ended without incident. Mirror test: the analysis would be the same for a protest demanding that the ER stay closed in favour of a new regional centre.

**2. Permits.** A march of 50+ is a procession → licence required (s. 83; AG para. 8). The speeches concern health policy, not foreign relations or security, but the procession requires the licence anyway. 700 participants → safety notice to the Northern District Commissioner (Safety Law s. 5). Filing: by email to the station operations officer on Thursday a week before; the safety notice is sent the same day and copied to the station and the local authority's director-general.

**Form A (extract).**

```text

5. נושא האירוע: מחאה נגד סגירת חדר המיון הלילי במרכז הרפואי ___.
7. מספר משתתפים משוער: כ-700.
13. מועד תחילת האירוע: יום ו', ___, 10:00. 16. מועד התפזרות: 12:30.
```

**Reply to a condition.** The licence moves the rally to a car park 600 metres from the medical centre. Reply: the condition removes the protest from its audience and target (AG section ז); the risk to ambulance access is fully answered by the corridor and stewards; there is no evidence of a near certainty of harm (para. 13(ו)). Alternative: the lawn beside the gate with the corridor, or the pavement opposite the gate. Answer requested by Wednesday 18:00.

**3. Day-of plan (extract).** Liaison: Amal. Stewards: 25, of whom 6 hold the ambulance corridor. Filming: two people, one at the front, one at the gate, with cloud backup on. Volunteer lawyer on call; first aid: two paramedic volunteers. Meeting point: the pharmacy at the square. Detention card printed on the back of the briefing sheet. Rule: no physical resistance; comply first, complain later.

**4. Messages (extract).**

| Text | Risk | Rewrite |
|---|---|---|
| "The Minister has blood on his hands" (sign) | None: harsh political criticism | Keep |
| "Close the Minister's house the way he closed our ER" (post) | Maybe: could be read as a call to act at a home | "Open our ER — the Minister must answer us" |
| Photo of the hospital director with her home street (post) | High: threat or harassment risk, privacy | Delete; tag the hospital's official account instead |

**5. After.** Evidence log prepared. Channel table: violence → מח״ש; rudeness or no name tag → Public Complaints Unit. Template request to preserve body-camera footage ready. Appeal calendar: 60 days from any closing notice. Lawyer first for anyone detained.

**Rubric self-score for this model:** 15/16. The message-review row loses a point because one chant was not reviewed before the briefing.
