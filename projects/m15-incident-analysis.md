# Module 15 project: Incident analysis

Write a complete, neutral IHL analysis of an invented incident: build the facts with their sources, set out the applicable framework as competing positions, apply the rules to every party, list the evidence gaps, state the competing interpretations fairly, and write a conclusion that goes no further than the evidence. The result is a three-to-four-page analysis that readers on opposite sides would both accept as fair.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> This is a desk exercise on an invented incident. Do not travel to a conflict area, contact armed groups or collect evidence on the ground. The course takes no position on the conflict, and this project does not ask you to reach a verdict on any real event. If you later write about a real incident, act in your own name only; this is not legal advice.

## What you produce

1. **A facts section** (template section 1): a timeline, a graded source table, and a disputed-facts table.
2. **A framework section** (section 2): classification of the conflict and the occupation question as competing positions; which instruments bind each party; any relevant Israeli ruling (lesson 15.3) or ICJ finding (lesson 15.4), each attributed.
3. **A rules card and application** (sections 3–4): each rule quoted exactly and applied to every party.
4. **An evidence-gaps table** (section 5).
5. **A competing-interpretations table** (section 6): at least two questions, each with two attributed readings.
6. **A limited conclusion** (section 7), no more than 150 words.
7. **A fairness note:** half a page recording what each party's representative and an independent researcher would object to, and what you changed.

Time: about 5 hours (facts and framework 1.5 hours, rules and gaps 1.5 hours, interpretations and conclusion 1 hour, fairness review and self-assessment 1 hour).

## The incident (choose A or B)

**Option A.** Your own variation of the Kessar strike from [lesson 15.5](../lessons/module-15/lesson-05.md), with at least three facts changed.

**Option B. The convoy at Gate 4 (invented).** State Norland has controlled the Eastern District since a war twelve years ago. Its army runs the crossings; inside the District, the Valley Front administers the towns, and fighting flares up regularly. Food prices in the District have tripled in a month.

- **06:30.** A convoy of 12 trucks run by an international relief agency enters through Gate 4 after Norland's inspection.
- **07:15.** About 2 km inside, the convoy stops at a junction. A crowd of several hundred gathers around the trucks.
- **07:25.** Norland's army says that armed men opened fire from a nearby building at soldiers guarding a road 400 metres away, that soldiers returned fire toward the building, and that it "regrets any harm to uninvolved civilians". It releases a drone video, 40 seconds long, that shows flashes near the building but not the crowd.
- **The District health office** (run by the Valley Front administration) says 14 people were killed near the trucks and more than 40 wounded, "all of them civilians waiting for food".
- **The relief agency** says its drivers heard shooting "from more than one direction" and that it cannot say who fired. It suspends deliveries for a week.
- **An international NGO**, two weeks later, publishes interviews with six witnesses, four of whom say the shooting came from the direction of the soldiers, and two of whom say armed men in the crowd tried to take over trucks.
- **Satellite images** show the trucks at the junction and the building 300 metres away; they cannot show who fired.

## Steps

1. **Choose A or B** and copy the facts into template section 1. Mark each as established, claimed by whom, or unknown.
2. **Set out the framework** (section 2). For option B, write the competing positions on whether the District is occupied (binary and functional approaches, lesson 15.2) and which rules each position brings in: occupation duties (GC IV arts 55, 59), relief rules, conduct of hostilities, and law enforcement where troops face a crowd (the Sassòli lecture in 15.5).
3. **Fill in the rules card** (section 3) with exact quotes.
4. **Apply the rules to every party** (section 4): Norland's soldiers, the armed men if they existed, the Valley Front administration, and, where relevant, Norland's duties toward relief.
5. **List the evidence gaps** (section 5) and who holds each piece of evidence.
6. **State at least two competing interpretations** (section 6), each with a quoted, attributed source from this module.
7. **Write the limited conclusion** (section 7).
8. **Do the fairness review**: reread as Norland's legal adviser, as the Valley Front's spokesperson and as an independent researcher. Record objections and changes.
9. **Self-assess** with the rubric, then compare with the model answer.

Use the [IHL incident analysis template](../templates/ihl-incident-analysis.md).

## Self-assessment rubric

Score each row 0, 1 or 2. 16 or more out of 20 means the analysis is ready to share as a teaching example; below 12, redo the weakest rows.

| Criterion | 0 — not yet | 1 — partly | 2 — done well |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fact status | Party claims written as facts | Most facts marked, some claims treated as established | Every fact marked established, claimed by whom, or unknown |
| Source grading | No grading | Sources listed without strengths and weaknesses | Each source graded, with its date |
| Framework | One position stated as fact | Positions given but not attributed or dated | Competing positions on classification and occupation, attributed and dated |
| Binding instruments | Treaties cited without status | Status checked for some | Treaty or custom stated for every rule and party |
| Rules quoted | Paraphrased | Quoted, some sources missing | Every rule quoted exactly with source |
| All parties | Only one party analysed | Second party mentioned briefly | Each party's duties applied rule by rule |
| Evidence gaps | None listed | Gaps listed vaguely | Each gap tied to a rule, with who holds the evidence |
| Competing interpretations | None, or only one side | Two readings, one unattributed | At least two questions, each with two attributed readings |
| Limited conclusion | A verdict beyond the evidence | Cautious but vague | States what can and cannot be said and what would change it |
| Fairness review | None | Done from one viewpoint | Done from three viewpoints, with changes recorded |

<details><summary>Model answer</summary>

This is a condensed model for option B. Yours may differ in wording and structure; compare the method, not the sentences.

**1. Facts (extract).**

| Time | Event | Source | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| 06:30 | Convoy enters at Gate 4 after inspection | Relief agency; Norland army | Established (both agree) |
| 07:15 | Convoy stops; crowd of several hundred | Agency; satellite image | Established |
| 07:25 | Armed men fire at soldiers from a building | Norland army; drone video shows flashes only | Claimed by Norland; partly supported (flashes) |
| 07:25 | Soldiers fire toward the building | Norland army | Claimed by Norland (admitted) |
| 07:25 | Shooting toward the crowd from the soldiers' direction | 4 of 6 NGO witnesses | Claimed by witnesses |
| 07:25 | Armed men in the crowd try to take over trucks | 2 of 6 NGO witnesses | Claimed by witnesses |
| — | 14 killed, 40+ wounded, "all civilians" | Health office | Claimed by the health office; status of the dead not verified |

**2. Framework.** *Conflict:* Norland and the Valley Front are in an armed conflict; classification may be disputed, but the rules used below apply in both IAC and NIAC. *Occupation:* on a binary view, the District may not be occupied because the Valley Front governs inside; on a functional view (the approach the ICJ took for Gaza in 2024 and 2025), Norland's control of the crossings brings duties tied to that control, including on relief (GC IV art. 59; customary Rule 55 on humanitarian relief applies either way). *Law enforcement:* where soldiers face a crowd that is not taking part in hostilities, many writers apply a law-enforcement (human-rights) standard on the use of force; others apply conduct-of-hostilities rules when soldiers come under fire. The analysis notes both.

**3–4. Application (extract).**
- *Norland — distinction:* firing at armed men attacking soldiers is directed at persons taking a direct part in hostilities (Rule 6). Firing toward a crowd of civilians is not. Which happened is the core factual dispute.
- *Norland — proportionality and precautions:* if return fire was aimed at the building 300 m from the trucks, what civilian harm was expected near the trucks? Were feasible precautions taken (direction of fire, choice of weapons, warnings)?
- *Norland — relief:* the convoy had passed inspection; duties to allow and facilitate relief (Rule 55; art. 59 on the functional view) frame the security arrangements around convoys.
- *Armed men, if they existed:* firing from near a civilian crowd raises Rules 22 and 97; seizing relief trucks raises the rules on relief.
- *Valley Front administration:* duties to protect civilians under its control (Rule 22), and to give accurate casualty information.

**5. Evidence gaps.** Unedited drone footage covering the crowd (Norland); soldiers' accounts and orders (Norland); ballistic and medical evidence on the dead and wounded (health office, hospitals); identities and status of the dead (health office); independent footage from the crowd (witnesses, open source); the agency's drivers' detailed accounts.

**6. Competing interpretations.**
- *Which force standard applies near a crowd?* Hostilities rules (when soldiers come under fire) vs a law-enforcement standard (crowd control). State each, with the Sassòli lecture as the framework source.
- *Does an occupant's relief duty extend to securing distribution inside an area it does not administer?* Israel's government and Supreme Court, on Gaza, describe duties arising from the conflict and from control of crossings without occupation (*Al-Bassiouni*, *Gisha*); the ICJ (2025) found duties under the law of occupation commensurate with control. Map the Norland case onto both readings without choosing.

**7. Limited conclusion.** "On the public evidence as of [date], it cannot be determined who caused the deaths near the convoy or whether the force used complied with IHL. If soldiers fired only at armed men in the building, and civilian harm near the trucks was neither expected nor caused by their fire, Norland's conduct may have complied with distinction and proportionality; if soldiers fired toward the crowd, it may not have. Unedited video, ballistic and medical evidence would decide it. The duties of armed men (if present) not to endanger the crowd, and of the Valley Front administration toward civilians under its control, are also engaged. No independent body has examined the incident."

**Fairness note (extract).** *Norland's adviser* objected that the first draft called the NGO witnesses "independent"; changed to "witnesses interviewed by an NGO". *The Valley Front's spokesperson* objected that the draft treated the "armed men" as established; changed to "claimed by Norland and two witnesses". *The researcher* objected that the draft did not say the health office does not distinguish fighters; added to the source table.

Rubric self-score for this model: 19/20 (the evidence-gaps row could add how likely each item is to become public).
