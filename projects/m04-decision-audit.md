# Module 4 project: Decision audit

Audit one real decision of a public authority against every ground you learned in this module — authority, considerations, reasonableness and proportionality, fair procedure, and the threshold questions — and finish with a written assessment and a letter that asks for the next step. This audit is the base of the Module 5 project, where you may take the same matter through a Freedom of Information request and a pre-petition letter.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> This project is a dry run. You may send the letter at the end if you wish: act in your own name only (or in the name of the group that is acting). This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing.

## What you produce

1. **A completed decision audit** using the [decision audit template](../templates/decision-audit.md), sections 0 and A–F (about 3–5 pages).
2. **A one-page summary** of the audit: the decision, the strongest ground, the weakest point, and the next step.
3. **A Hebrew letter** to the authority, in your own name, using the [formal letter template](../templates/formal-letter.md) and the paragraph bank in section G of the audit template. It asks for one concrete procedural step (reasons, the material, a hearing, reconsideration) based on your audit.

Time: about 5 hours, spread over a week or two if you wait for documents.

## Steps

1. **Choose the decision.** A real, specific decision of a public body: a refusal, a cancellation, a permit, a condition, an appointment, a fee demand, a policy applied to a specific case. It can be from your issue map (Module 1) or your research memo (Module 2). It must be in writing or published, so you can quote it.
2. **Collect the documents.** The decision itself, the request or application that led to it, any minutes, the enabling law. List what is missing.
3. **Authority (04.1).** Section A: the enabling section quoted, the holder, the decider, delegation, scope, express authority.
4. **Considerations (04.2).** Section B: the law's purpose, a table of considerations, evidence for each extraneous one, fettering and dictation.
5. **Reasonableness and proportionality (04.3).** Section C: weight given to relevant considerations; the four proportionality tests if a right is limited, with at least one concrete less harmful alternative.
6. **Procedure (04.4).** Section D: the five hearing elements, reasons, conflicts of interest, appeal-rights notice.
7. **Threshold (04.5).** Section E: standing, the most affected person, justiciability, prior request, alternative remedy, dates and deadline, forum.
8. **Assess (section F).** Rate each ground strong, arguable, weak or none, with the key evidence. Choose the strongest ground and the next step.
9. **Write the summary page.** In neutral language. If your issue is politically contested, add one sentence on how the same analysis would apply if the political sides were reversed.
10. **Write the letter.** Ask for one concrete thing, cite the relevant section, set a reasonable date, and plan a delivery method that proves receipt (lesson 02.5).
11. **Self-assess** with the rubric, then compare with the model answer.

## Self-assessment rubric

Score each row 0, 1 or 2. 16 or more out of 20 means your audit is ready to use in Module 5; below 12, redo the weakest rows.

| Criterion | 0 — not yet | 1 — partly | 2 — done well |
|---|---|---|---|
| The decision | Vague, or not in writing | Clear, but date or source missing | One sentence; date, decider and source attached |
| Authority | No enabling provision | Provision named but not quoted, or delegation ignored | Quoted section, holder vs decider, delegation and scope checked |
| Considerations | Not analysed, or "extraneous" asserted without basis | Table present, purpose or evidence missing | Purpose cited; every consideration tied to it; evidence or a plan to get it |
| Reasonableness | "Unreasonable" as a label | A consideration named, weight not discussed | Specific consideration and weight, honest about the high threshold |
| Proportionality | Missing where a right is limited | Tests listed but no alternative | All four tests in order with a concrete less harmful alternative, or a reason it does not apply |
| 2026 status of reasonableness | Wrong (for example "abolished") | Correct but one-sided | Correct date and holding of HCJ 5658/23, both positions attributed |
| Procedure | Not checked | Some duties checked | Hearing elements, reasons, conflicts and appeal notice all checked with evidence |
| Threshold questions | Not checked | Standing or exhaustion only | Standing, affected person, justiciability, prior request, alternative remedy, delay dates and forum |
| Assessment and next step | No conclusion | Conclusion without ranking | Grounds ranked with evidence; a realistic next step chosen |
| Letter and limits | Missing, accusatory, or for someone else | Own name, but vague request or no proof-of-receipt plan | Own name, one concrete request with a section and a date, factual tone, proof of receipt |

This condensed model follows the worked example in lesson 04.4. Your audit will be about your own decision and longer.

**0. The decision.** On 3 March the municipality's business licensing department cancelled Moshe's permit to place four tables on the pavement outside his kiosk, effective 10 March, "following complaints and an inspection" (letter attached as נספח 1). Moshe held the permit for six years. He wants it renewed.

**A. Authority.** The municipality regulates use of pavements under its by-law on public spaces (quoted, with the section that lets the municipality grant, condition and cancel permits). The by-law gives the power to "ראש העירייה או מי שהסמיך לכך"; the department director signed. A written authorisation from the mayor exists (published on the municipality's website, dated 2023). Scope covers cancellation. No fee or new limit on a basic right beyond the permit itself. *Conclusion:* authority is not the weak point.

**B. Considerations.** Purpose of the by-law: safe passage on pavements, public order, fair use of public space. Relevant: pavement width, pedestrian safety, noise at night. Stated: "complaints and an inspection" — not specified. Possible extraneous consideration: commercial competition, if the deputy mayor who co-owns a nearby restaurant influenced the decision. Evidence: none yet; requested the complaints, the inspection report and the minutes. *Conclusion:* arguable, depends on documents.

**C. Reasonableness and proportionality.** If the inspection found a passage-width problem, full cancellation gave no weight to six years of compliance and to a simple alternative. Proportionality-style alternative: a condition reducing the tables to three and keeping a marked passage width. Status note: "Reasonableness review was restored when HCJ 5658/23 annulled s. 15(ד1) on 1.1.2024; supporters of the amendment argued the ground allowed too much judicial intervention, and critics of the amendment that it is a key check. Courts intervene only for extreme unreasonableness, so this is a supporting ground." *Conclusion:* arguable, supporting only.

**D. Procedure.** Notice: none. Material: none. Time: the letter allowed seven days, but no opportunity to respond. Open mind: unknown. Reasons: a label only. Conflict of interest: the deputy mayor chairs the business committee and co-owns a restaurant across the street (companies register extract attached); unknown whether he took part. Appeal notice (s. 5): none given. *Conclusion:* strong — no hearing before cancelling an existing permit.

**E. Threshold.** Standing: personal interest (his permit and income). Most affected person: Moshe himself. Justiciability: legal duty to give a hearing. Prior request: letter of 4 March (below). Alternative remedy: the by-law provides no appeal; check the First Schedule for a petition route. Dates: notice received 3 March; petition deadline counted from 3 March (see current numbers). Forum: likely an administrative petition, if the First Schedule item covers municipal permits of this kind — to be confirmed in Module 5.

**F. Assessment.**

| Ground | Strength | Key evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Authority | None | Valid authorisation |
| Considerations | Arguable | Depends on complaints and minutes |
| Reasonableness | Arguable (supporting) | Six years' compliance; a simple alternative |
| Proportionality | Arguable | Fewer tables and a passage condition |
| Procedure | Strong | No notice, material or chance to respond; possible conflict |
| Threshold risks | Low | Prior request sent; clock dates recorded |

Strongest ground: no hearing before cancelling an existing permit. Next step: ask for suspension, the material and a hearing; ask neutrally about conflicts.
