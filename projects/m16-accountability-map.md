# Module 16 project: An accountability map

Choose one incident or one closely defined pattern of alleged serious crimes connected with Israel, the West Bank or Gaza, from any side: an attack on Israeli civilians, a strike in Gaza, settler violence, abuse in detention, hostage-taking. Build the file a careful advocate would prepare before going to any forum: the legal labels tested against the elements, every route to accountability in Israel and abroad, the status of each route as of the day you check, a neutral "who says what" brief, and a dry-run Article 15 communication with its evidence list and consent records. You file nothing as part of this project.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Act in your own name only (or for a person or group that has authorised you). This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing. Do not send material to the ICC, the UN or a foreign prosecutor only to complete this project, and never name a real private person without written consent.

> [!NOTE]
> The course takes no position on the incidents, proceedings or jurisdiction disputes you will write about. Your file is assessed on accuracy, attribution and method, not on its conclusions.

## What you produce

1. **The incident in brief** (half a page): what, where, when, who was harmed, who is alleged to be responsible, and the sources, each dated. Separate what is established from what is alleged.
2. **Legal labels** (one page): for each label used publicly about the incident (by any side), the two-layer test from lesson 16.1 — the underlying act, the context or intent required, the facts for and against, and your conclusion: fits / could fit with more facts / does not fit. Include the Israeli statute, if any.
3. **The accountability map** (a table): every route with its legal basis, who can start it, what it can produce, its status today, and the next step:
   - Israeli criminal routes: police complaint and appeal, or MAG complaint, AG review and High Court (lesson 16.3); the 2026 law if the incident is part of 7 October (lesson 16.4);
   - Israeli oversight: State Comptroller, a commission of inquiry, Knesset (Module 12);
   - the ICC: is the incident within the situation's territory and dates, which warrants exist, the complementarity position (lessons 16.2–16.3);
   - foreign national courts: which states have a link (suspect's or victim's nationality, universal jurisdiction) and any known proceedings (lesson 16.4);
   - UN mechanisms: Special Procedures, the commission of inquiry (Module 14).
4. **"Who says what" brief** (half a page): the position of Israel's government, of the relevant court or UN body, and of at least two non-governmental sources from different perspectives, each quoted exactly with source and date.
5. **Dry-run Article 15 communication** (two to three pages), using the [Article 15 communication template](../templates/article-15-communication.md), Parts A–C: summary, incident table, crimes and elements, domestic proceedings, evidence list.
6. **Evidence and consent annex** (half a page): an evidence log with hashes (Part E of the template) for at least three files, and a consent record for every person you would name (Part D). If the incident is invented, invent the consents too and mark them as such.
7. **Status note** (150 words): the current status of the most important route, dated, written so that readers from opposite sides would accept it as factual.

Time: about 5 hours.

## Steps

1. **Choose** an incident you can document from reliable sources. It may be invented but must be realistic. Avoid incidents where you would have to contact people in danger.
2. **Collect sources** (one hour): official statements (IDF, MFA, police), court records, UN reports, NGO reports from at least two perspectives, and primary material (video, photos, documents). Archive and hash what you keep.
3. **Test the labels** (lesson 16.1).
4. **Build the map** (lessons 16.2–16.4). Check the ICC docket and any foreign proceeding today, not from memory.
5. **Write the "who says what" brief** and ask someone with different views to mark any sentence that reads as a position.
6. **Draft the Article 15 communication** (lesson 16.5) and the annex.
7. **Write the status note.**
8. **Self-assess** with the rubric, then compare with the model answer.

## Self-assessment rubric

Score each row 0, 1 or 2. 12 or more out of 14 means your file is ready to use as a basis for real work; below 8, redo the weakest rows.

| Criterion | 0 — not yet | 1 — partly | 2 — done well |
|---|---|---|---|
| Facts | Facts and allegations mixed; undated | Dated, but sources from one side only | Established and alleged facts separated; dated sources from several perspectives |
| Legal labels | Labels repeated without analysis | Underlying acts checked; context or intent not | Both layers checked for each label; Israeli statute identified; weakest element named |
| Domestic routes | Missing or wrong channel | Right channel, no appeal or review steps | Correct channel (MAG or police), appeal and review steps, deadlines from current numbers |
| International routes | ICC and ICJ confused, or routes listed without status | Routes correct; status not checked today | ICC situation scope, warrants and complementarity; foreign links named by basis; UN routes; all dated |
| Neutrality | Positions written as facts | Attributed, but one side quoted more fairly | Same verbs for all; Israel's government quoted in its own words; checked by a reader with different views |
| Article 15 draft | General statement or news links | Specific but no elements or no domestic steps | Summary, incident table, elements, domestic steps, evidence list with hashes |
| Safety and consent | Missing | Consent recorded, no risk assessment, or originals altered | Consent for every named person, risks stated, originals preserved, hashes re-checked |

This is a condensed model based on an invented incident. Your file will be about your own case.

**1. The incident.** On 3 March 2025, in the early morning, a group of about 15 masked people entered the outskirts of an invented Palestinian village in the South Hebron Hills, set fire to two homes and a car and beat a man, Mahmoud (invented), who was hospitalised with a broken arm. *Established:* fire damage (photos, a fire-service report), injury (hospital record), the time (neighbours' CCTV). *Alleged:* that the attackers came from a nearby outpost (witnesses say they saw them walk back towards it; no identification yet).

**2. Legal labels.** Used publicly: "terror attack" (an Israeli NGO), "pogrom" (a Palestinian NGO), "crime against humanity" (a foreign MP), "self-defence after stone-throwing" (a settlers' WhatsApp group).
- Domestic offences: arson (Penal Law), aggravated assault, possibly a terror offence under the Counter-Terrorism Law 2016 — fits on the facts.
- War crime: settlers are civilians; a war crime needs a nexus to the armed conflict. Arguable only with facts linking the attack to the conflict (e.g. coordination with forces). Could fit with more facts.
- Crime against humanity: needs a widespread or systematic attack on civilians. One incident does not show it; the Commission of Inquiry's 2026 report (A/HRC/62/22) alleges a pattern of settler violence; Israel's government rejects the Commission's findings. Could fit only as part of that alleged pattern.
- "Self-defence": no facts support it; recorded as a claim.
- Israeli statute: no war-crimes or crimes-against-humanity statute; ordinary offences apply.

**4. Who says what.** The Government of Israel: "Israel views the proceedings against it as politically motivated and legally baseless" (MFA ICC page) and says complaints are investigated by the police and MAG. The UN Commission of Inquiry (A/HRC/62/22, para. 74): "a consistent pattern whereby the Israeli authorities ... failed to properly and effectively investigate and punish the perpetrators". Yesh Din, as cited by the Commission: about 93.6% of police investigations in 20 years ended without indictment. A settlers' council spokesman (invented quote from a public statement, dated): residents act in self-defence against attacks. A reader from a settler family checked the brief and asked for the spokesman's statement to be quoted in full rather than summarised; done.

**5. Article 15 draft (summary).** "On 3 March 2025 at about 05:30, at [coordinates], a group of about 15 masked persons set fire to two homes and a car and assaulted a resident, causing a broken arm (E1–E6). Witnesses say the group walked back towards [outpost] (E4, Witness A). The sender asks the Office to consider the incident within the Situation in the State of Palestine as part of an alleged pattern of attacks on civilians. A police complaint was filed (no. Elements: underlying acts (destruction of property, assault) shown; the context element (widespread or systematic attack, or armed-conflict nexus) argued only by reference to the Commission's findings, stated as such.

**6. Annex.** Six files hashed with SHA-256 on 4 March 2025 and re-checked on 13 October 2026 (all match). Consents: Mahmoud agrees to be named and contacted through his lawyer; one neighbour refuses to be named and appears as "Witness A".

**7. Status note.** "On 3 March 2025 masked people burned two homes and a car near [village] and injured a resident. The police opened an investigation and closed it in August 2025 for 'unknown offender'; the victim has appealed, and the appeal was pending on 13 October 2026. A complaint to the MAG about soldiers at the scene is under examination. The UN Commission of Inquiry describes such attacks as part of a pattern of impunity; Israel's government rejects the Commission's findings and says complaints are investigated. No international proceeding concerns this incident."

**Mirror test.** The same file structure serves an Israeli family whose car was attacked with firebombs near a Palestinian village (police complaint, appeal, the 2026 law if linked to 7 October, an Article 15 communication on Palestinian armed groups), or a detainee's family alleging abuse in an IDF facility (MAG, AG, High Court, Special Procedures).

**Rubric self-score for this model:** 13/14. The neutrality row loses a point because the settlers' council quote is invented; in a real file it would be a dated, sourced quote.
