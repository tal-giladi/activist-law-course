# Module 17 project: A documentation kit

Choose one human-rights issue in Israel, East Jerusalem or the West Bank that you can document remotely and from public sources, and build the kit a careful documentation team would use: a documentation plan, an incident log, an interview plan with a consent form, three online items collected and verified to the Berkeley Protocol standard, an evidence register with SHA-256 hashes and a chain of custody, a security plan, and a plan for using the material safely. The project is a dry run: you interview no victim and file nothing. Everything you collect is public, or is your own practice material.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Act in your own name only. This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing anything. Work remotely and from public sources only: do not go to a conflict area, a closed zone or an ongoing event, do not contact suspects, and do not interview victims for this project. Never access anyone's accounts or devices (Computers Law s. 4), never record conversations you are not part of (Wiretap Law s. 2), and do not publish anything about a private person without consent (Privacy Law s. 2).

## What you produce

1. **Documentation plan** (one page): the issue in one sentence; the purpose(s) of the record and who will use it (lesson 17.1); what you will and will not collect; who has access; where you will store it; who else already documents this issue and how you will avoid duplicating their work.
2. **Incident log** (five to eight rows) from public sources: news reports, official statements, court decisions, NGO data, public posts ([incident-log.md](../templates/incident-log.md); lesson 17.1). Facts only, a source and source type for each fact, doubts and a harm check for every row, codes instead of private names.
3. **Interview plan and consent form**: the four "should this interview happen" questions answered for a realistic (invented) witness on your issue, the referral options, eight open questions, and a completed consent form in Hebrew or English ([interview-consent-form.md](../templates/interview-consent-form.md); lesson 17.2). Optional: a 15-minute practice interview with a consenting adult friend about a non-traumatic event, with your list of leading questions rewritten.
4. **Three online items, collected and verified** (one page each): for each, the Berkeley minimum (URL, dated full-page capture, HTML, media, metadata, context, collection data, hashes), the archive decision, and a verification note with all three lenses and a confidence level (lesson 17.3; part B of [evidence-chain-of-custody.md](../templates/evidence-chain-of-custody.md)). At least one item should be a claim from a side you disagree with, verified as fairly as the others.
5. **Evidence register and chain of custody**: parts A, C, D (one practice transfer, for example to a study partner who re-hashes the files), E (one re-check) and F (one stripped or redacted copy) of the chain-of-custody form (lesson 17.4).
6. **Security plan** (one page): the six EFF questions, a table of assets, threats from every side, likelihood and measures, who is responsible, and an emergency procedure (lesson 17.5).
7. **Use plan** (half a page): which legal tool the material could feed — a police or PID complaint (06.4), a letter or petition (Module 5), a Special Procedures communication (14.4), a submission to an international body (16.5) — what consent and redaction each use needs, and what you would never publish.

Time: about 5 hours.

## Steps

1. **Choose the issue.** Any cause from any part of the political map: attacks on Israeli civilians, settler violence, police conduct at protests of any camp, discrimination in services or employment, threats against activists, demolitions, conditions of detention, harassment of journalists.
2. **Write the documentation plan (17.1)** before you collect anything.
3. **Build the incident log (17.1)** from public sources only.
4. **Prepare the interview plan and consent form (17.2).** Do not interview a real victim for this project.
5. **Collect and verify three online items (17.3).** Hash at collection.
6. **Preserve (17.4):** originals in two places, the register, a practice transfer, a re-check and one stripped copy.
7. **Write the security plan (17.5)** and apply at least the basics to your own accounts.
8. **Write the use plan.**
9. **Ask someone with different views** to read the incident log and the verification notes and tell you where they look partisan. Fix what they find.
10. **Self-assess** with the rubric, then compare with the model answer.

## Self-assessment rubric

Score each row 0, 1 or 2. 12 or more out of 14 means your kit is ready to use; below 8, redo the weakest rows.

| Criterion | 0 — not yet | 1 — partly | 2 — done well |
|---|---|---|---|
| Plan and purpose | No stated purpose, or collection started first | Purpose stated; access, storage or existing documenters missing | Purpose, users, collection limits, access, storage and existing documenters all set before collecting |
| Incident log | Labels and opinions in the fact column; no sources | Facts sourced, but doubts or harm checks missing | Acts only; source and type for every fact; doubts and harm check for every row; codes, no private names |
| Interview plan and consent | Plans to interview a child or survivor without training; consent generic | Consent covers some uses; questions partly leading | "Should it happen" answered; referral options; open questions; consent covers each use, identity, recording, risks, withdrawal, storage and end re-confirmation |
| Collection | Screenshots without URL, time or hash | Most of the Berkeley minimum; hashes added later | Full minimum for all three items, hashed at collection, archive decision reasoned |
| Verification | One lens, or a conclusion that fits the cause | Two lenses; confidence not stated | Source, technical and content analysis for each item; confidence level; unverified points listed; one item from the other side handled as fairly |
| Preservation and custody | Originals edited or kept in one place | Hashes and two copies, but no transfer, re-check or derived-file log | Originals untouched in two places; register; transfer with receiver's hash check; re-check; stripped copy logged |
| Security and use | Missing | Security plan generic; or use plan without consent and redaction | Specific assets and threats from every side; basics applied; emergency steps; use plan names the legal tool, consent and redaction for each use |

This is a condensed model based on a realistic dry run. Your kit will be about your own issue.

**0. The issue.** Yonatan Mizrahi, a volunteer with an invented good-governance group in Haifa, documents police conduct towards demonstrators at protests in Haifa in 2025–2026, of every camp: anti-government demonstrators, Arab citizens protesting the war, Haredi anti-draft demonstrators, and right-wing counter-protesters.

**1. Documentation plan.** Purpose: (a) support individuals who choose to file complaints with the Police Investigation Department (PID); (b) a public report to the municipality and a Knesset committee. Users: the group's two coordinators, a volunteer lawyer. Not collected: home addresses, medical records, names of minors, anything from private groups. Storage: encrypted drive at home and encrypted cloud folder; key file separate. Existing documenters: the Association for Civil Rights in Israel's protest work and a Haredi legal-aid hotline; the group will refer people to them and not re-interview anyone they have already interviewed.

**2. Incident log (excerpt, 6 rows).** I-01: "Video shows an officer pushing a woman holding a sign onto the pavement, Horev junction, 14.6.2025, about 20:30 (time from the uploader's post); source: public post by a local news account; documentary; doubts: what happened before the clip not visible; harm check: the woman's face not stored in working copies." I-04: "Police statement says two officers were injured by stones thrown by protesters at the same junction on 3.7.2025; official source; doubts: no independent footage found." Every row describes acts; the words "brutal" and "rioters" appear only inside quotation marks with their sources.

**3. Interview plan.** Invented witness "W-01", an adult demonstrator who was pushed and filmed it. "Should it happen": needed (to fix the time and the officer's unit), safe (adult, at home, by video, no fear of retaliation stated), free (no pressure), right interviewer (no sexual violence or minors involved). Referral options: ERAN 1201, the volunteer lawyer, the PID complaint form. Eight open questions, beginning "Please tell me, in your own words, what happened at the junction." Consent form: uses ticked for the lawyer and the PID complaint only; initials in the public report; audio only; withdrawal until filing. Practice interview with a friend about a parking dispute: three leading questions found and rewritten.

**4. Three online items.** Item E-01 (the Horev junction video): URL, dated PDF, HTML, video, uploader and post ID, comments; hashed at 21:12 on collection day; archived with Save Page Now (no private victim named in the post). Verification: source — earliest upload found, local news account active in Haifa for years; technical — no visible cuts, metadata stripped by the platform; content — the junction matched on street-level imagery, the shadows fit an evening in June, a shop sign matches 2025 imagery. Confidence: confirmed for place and date; what preceded the push unknown. Item E-02: a clip from a right-wing channel claiming "left-wing protesters attacked a policeman" — verified with the same three lenses: genuine, same evening, a different street; the claim about who started it is unverified. Item E-03: the police spokesperson's statement — captured and hashed; claims recorded as the police's account.

**5. Register and custody.** Nine items E-01–E-09; hash lists exported with `Get-FileHash` and saved with the date; transfer of E-01 to the volunteer lawyer by an encrypted link, lawyer's re-hash matched; re-check after one month — all OK; one derived file — a still from E-01 with the woman's face blurred, logged in part F with its own hash.

**6. Security plan.** Assets: witnesses' identities, raw videos, the key file. Threats: harassers from both sides who post about the group, a lost phone, a coordinator who leaves, phishing pretending to be "a new video". Measures: Signal group of three with disappearing messages for coordination; evidence only via the custodian; 2FA (authenticator app) on all accounts; drive shared with named people only; biometric unlock off on protest days. Emergency: contain, preserve, tell sources, contact Access Now (English), report to the police.

**7. Use plan.** W-01's own PID complaint, filed by W-01 with E-01 attached and the hash list; the public report uses only blurred stills and initials, with the police statement quoted next to each incident; nothing is sent to an international body. Never published: faces of demonstrators or bystanders who did not consent, names of officers not yet identified officially.

**Mirror test.** The same kit would serve a group documenting rocket damage to homes in Sderot and the state's response, a Bedouin council documenting demolitions, or a settlers' group documenting attacks on Israeli drivers. The plan, the log, the consent rules, the Berkeley standard and the chain of custody do not depend on the cause.

**Rubric self-score for this model:** 13/14. The verification row loses a point because item E-03 (an official statement) was captured but not verified against independent sources.
