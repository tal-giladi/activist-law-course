# Project: Issue map

Map one real issue you care about to the laws that govern it, the bodies that decide it, and the forum where it can be challenged. This is the first project of the course and the base for many later ones: in Module 2 you will research it, in Module 4 you will audit a decision in it, and in Module 5 you may take it all the way to a petition-ready file.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> This is a desk exercise: you file nothing. Act in your own name only. This is not legal advice — check current rules before you file anything later.

## What you produce

A completed [Issue map](../templates/issue-map.md) (about 2 pages) for one real issue, plus one ruling brief made with the [Ruling brief](../templates/ruling-brief.md) template.

Time: about 3 hours, spread over a few sessions.

## Steps

1. **Pick the issue.** Use the cause you wrote down in lesson 01.1. Narrow it to one concrete situation: a decision, a plan, a rule or a practice, in one place. "Education" is too wide; "the municipality's refusal to open a kindergarten class for children with disabilities in our neighbourhood" works.
2. **State the goal** in one sentence: what concrete result would count as a win?
3. **Check your line (01.1).** Write what you will do in your own name, and what you will not do for others. Name one free service you would turn to if the stakes rise.
4. **Find the sources (01.2, 01.3).** Fill the hierarchy table: one Basic Law right, the main law, at least one regulation or by-law, and one AG guideline or procedure, each with a free link and a section number. Run the source check on the regulation.
5. **Read the key section (01.5).** Break down the one section that gives the decision-maker its power. Note every duty or power word, every exception and any condition. Cite it in the standard form.
6. **Find who decides.** Name the decision-maker, the body that supervises it, and the oversight bodies that could look at it.
7. **Choose the forum (01.4).** For your issue, write the first step (letter, request, objection), the next step if it fails, and the court or tribunal. Cite the section or schedule item that sends it there, and link the relevant line in [current numbers](../references/current-numbers.md).
8. **Read one ruling (01.6).** Find one ruling on a related question (Versa, or the Supreme Court search) and write a one-page brief. If you find nothing directly on point, brief a ruling on the same kind of power or right and say why it is relevant.
9. **List who else is involved,** including groups on more than one side of the issue.
10. **List open questions** and where you will look for the answers in Module 2.

## Self-assessment rubric

Score each row from 0 to 2. 14 or more out of 20 means the map is ready to use in Module 2.

| Criterion | 0 — missing | 1 — partly there | 2 — done well |
|---|---|---|---|
| Issue and goal | Vague topic, no goal | Concrete issue or a goal, not both | One concrete situation and a measurable goal |
| Your line (01.1) | Not addressed | General statement only | Clear own-name plan, what you will not do, and a named free service |
| Sources and links | Fewer than two sources | Several sources, some without links or sections | Four levels of the hierarchy, each with a free link and a section |
| Source check (01.3) | Not done | Enabling section named but not read | Enabling section read and compared with the regulation |
| Key section read (01.5) | Not broken down | Broken down, connecting words missed | Every condition, exception and power word marked; correct citation |
| Decision-makers | Not identified | Decision-maker only | Decision-maker, supervisor and oversight bodies |
| Forum (01.4) | No forum or the wrong one | Right court, no reason or no first step | First step, next step and forum, each with its statute or schedule item |
| Numbers | Numbers written as bare facts | Some links to current numbers | Every fee or deadline linked to current numbers |
| Ruling brief (01.6) | None | Summary without a clear holding | One page with question, holding, result and relevance |
| Balance and open questions | One side only, no questions | Some other actors or some questions | Actors on more than one side and specific open questions with where to look |

<summary>Model answer</summary>

**1. Issue.** The municipality of a mid-sized city has deposited a local outline plan (תכנית מקומית) that would turn a neighbourhood park into a car park with a small commercial building.

**2. Goal.** The plan is amended so that the park stays public open space, or at least half of it does.

**3. My line.** I will file my own objection and my own FOI request, and I will run a public evening explaining the planning process in general terms. I will share a blank objection template for residents to use in their own name. I will not write personalised objections for neighbours or appear for anyone at the hearing. If the case reaches court, I will contact Bimkom or a university clinic first (see Where to Get Help).

**4. Sources.**

| Level | Name and section | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| Basic Law | חוק־יסוד: כבוד האדם וחירותו, s. 11 | Every authority must respect the rights in the Basic Law (relevant mainly to fair procedure and property of neighbours) |
| Law | חוק התכנון והבניה, התשכ״ה–1965, s. 100 ff. (objections), s. 102 (objection period) | Who may object, to which committee, and within what period |
| Regulations | Planning and building regulations on objections and hearings | How an objection is filed and heard; enabling section checked against the law |
| Guideline / procedure | The municipality's published procedure on public participation, if any; a relevant AG guideline found in the database | How the committee says it involves the public |
| Ruling | To be researched in Module 2; for now, a brief of a planning ruling on public open space found on Versa or the Supreme Court search | — |

**5. Key section.** Planning and Building Law, s. 100 (who may object), broken down by paragraph; the objection period in s. 102 is a deadline (see current numbers: 2 months from publication of the deposit, extendable). Connecting words marked: the list of who may object, and the conditions on the committee's power to extend time.

**6. Who decides.** The local planning committee (or the district committee, depending on the plan's type; to check in the plan's documents on Mavat in Module 10). Supervision: the district planning committee and the Ministry of the Interior's Planning Administration. Oversight: the State Comptroller and Ombudsman (local authorities fall within the ombudsman's remit).

**7. Forum.** First step: an objection to the committee within the objection period. Next step: an appeal (ערר) to the appeals committee if the plan is approved, within its deadline (see current numbers). Court: a petition to the Administrative Affairs Court, because planning and building matters are in the first schedule of the Administrative Courts Law. Not the High Court of Justice.

**8. Ruling brief.** One page on a ruling about planning and public open space, with the holding stated as a rule.

**9. Others involved.** Residents for and against (some shop owners want parking); the local council's opposition and coalition members; Bimkom (planning rights) and a local business association; the local newspaper.

**10. Open questions.** Is it a local or district plan? Has the objection period started (date of the last publication)? Are there earlier plans that zoned the park as open space? Where: Mavat (Module 10), the municipality's planning committee minutes (FOI, Module 5), the Open Law Book for the current text of s. 100–102 (Module 2).

This model scores 2 on most rows. Its weakest row is the ruling: a strong map names a specific ruling with its number and holding.
