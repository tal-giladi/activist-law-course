# Module 13 project: International law in an Israeli court

Take one real or realistic Israeli dispute and write a complete international-law section for a petition or a letter to an authority: research every international source, decide each one's route into Israeli law, check treaty status and reservations, prove or test any customary rule, state Israel's official position and answer it, and draft the section in Hebrew. The document stays a draft unless you decide, on your own, to use it.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Act in your own name only (or in the name of the group or amuta that is acting). This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing. Drafting legal documents for others as an occupation or for payment is reserved to lawyers (Bar Association Law s. 20).

## What you produce

1. **A source table** (13.1): at least five international sources about your issue, each with its type, whether it binds Israel, and a link. At least one is an official Israeli source.
2. **A treaty check** (13.2) for every treaty you rely on: Israel's signature and ratification dates, reservations (quoted), and any incorporating statute.
3. **A custom check** (13.3) for every rule you call customary: the rule in one sentence, practice from at least two regions, opinio juris, contrary practice and Israel's position.
4. **A forum note** (13.4): any ICJ proceeding or other international decision on the point, described accurately (contentious or advisory, who started it, the Court's words, Israel's words), or a sentence saying there is none.
5. **The section itself** (13.5): one Hebrew page, built from section 5 of the [international-law argument template](../templates/intl-law-argument.md), that leads with the Israeli ground, names each source's route, quotes exactly, answers the State's position and ends with an Israeli remedy.
6. **A red-team note:** half a page written as the State Attorney's reply to your section, and your one-paragraph answer.

Time: about 5 hours (research 2.5 hours, drafting 1.5 hours, red team and self-assessment 1 hour).

## Steps

1. **Choose the dispute.** Use the decision you challenged in Module 4 or 5, or pick a new one. Political direction does not matter; any cause works. Some ideas: a ministry's refusal to provide accessible information (CRPD), a requisition order in Judea and Samaria (Hague Regulations), a council's failure to secure a road (Hague Regulations, Art. 43), a school placement of a migrant child (CRC), an embassy employee's unpaid wages (state immunity).
2. **Write the Israeli ground** in two sentences: the statute or Basic Law, and the administrative-law principle.
3. **Build the source table** (template section 1). Sort each source into its box.
4. **Check every treaty** on the UN Treaty Collection and Wikisource (section 2). Copy reservations word for word.
5. **Check every customary claim** (section 3). If you cannot find state practice, downgrade the claim.
6. **Check the forums** (section 4). If an ICJ case exists, read its case page and Israel's submission.
7. **Draft the section** (section 5), in Hebrew, one page. Delete the routes you do not use.
8. **Red-team it.** Write the State Attorney's reply in half a page, then your answer.
9. **Self-assess** with the rubric, then compare with the model answer.

## Self-assessment rubric

Score each row 0, 1 or 2. 16 or more out of 20 means your section is ready to go into a draft petition; below 12, redo the weakest rows.

| Criterion | 0 — not yet | 1 — partly | 2 — done well |
|---|---|---|---|
| Israeli ground first | No Israeli ground; international law stands alone | Israeli ground present but buried after the international law | Israeli ground stated first, international law expressly in support |
| Source sorting | Soft law presented as binding | Most sources sorted, one mislabelled | Every source has its correct box and a link |
| Treaty status | Not checked | Ratification checked, reservations or incorporation missing | Dates, reservations (quoted) and incorporation checked, with sources |
| Route for each source | Treaty cited as directly enforceable | Routes named but one is wrong | Custom, legislated treaty, ratified treaty and persuasive sources each on the right route |
| Customary claims | Asserted without evidence | Some evidence, mostly NGO or UN | State practice from two regions, opinio juris, contrary practice, or an Israeli ruling recognising the rule |
| Israeli rulings | None | Cited, but wrong case or wrong proposition | Elon Moreh or Abu Aita for custom; the presumption with its limits (Hasson) |
| Israel's position | Missing | Mentioned without a source | Quoted with a source and answered |
| Attribution and neutrality | Contested claims in your own voice | Mostly attributed | Every contested view attributed; the Court and the State each in their own words |
| Quotation and translation | Paraphrased or unmarked translation | Quoted but translation unmarked | Exact quotes; translations marked as official or free |
| Remedy and red team | Remedy asks a court to "enforce the treaty"; no red team | Israeli remedy, weak red team | Israeli remedy, a realistic State reply, and a convincing answer |

<details><summary>Model answer</summary>

This is a condensed model based on a realistic dry run. Your section will be about your own issue.

**Dispute.** A regional council in the north refuses to install audio announcements and accessible displays at the bus terminal it runs, citing budget priorities. Noa, who is blind, uses the terminal daily. She plans an administrative petition after a pre-petition letter (Module 5).

**Israeli ground.** The Equal Rights for Persons with Disabilities Law and its accessibility regulations (lesson 09.4); the council's discretion must weigh the relevant consideration of equal access (lesson 04.2).

**1. Source table (extract).**

| # | Source | Type | Binding on Israel? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | CRPD Art. 9 (accessibility) | Treaty, ratified 28.9.2012 | Yes, internationally; not legislated |
| 2 | CRPD Art. 4(1) (general obligations) | Treaty | Yes, internationally |
| 3 | CRPD Committee General Comment on accessibility | Interpretation by a treaty body | No; persuasive, attributed |
| 4 | Israel's periodic report to the CRPD Committee | Israel's position | Shows the State's own description of its obligations |
| 5 | A UN Special Rapporteur's report on accessible transport | Soft law | No |

**2. Treaty check.** CRPD, UN Treaty Collection IV-15: signed 30.3.2007, ratified 28.9.2012. Israel's only reservation concerns marriage in Art. 23(1)(a); it does not touch Art. 9. No statute gives the CRPD the force of law (searched Wikisource for "חוק אמנת" and the Knesset database).

**3. Custom check.** Not used: Noa's argument does not need custom, so she does not claim it.

**4. Forum note.** No ICJ proceeding on the point. Noa opens Israel's report to the CRPD Committee in the UN treaty-body database (Module 14) and copies, word for word, how the State describes the 1998 Law and its regulations in relation to the Convention. (This model does not quote the report; use its exact wording and page.)

**5. The section (extract).**

```text
7. המשפט הבינלאומי

7.1 טענות העותרת לעיל מבוססות על חוק שוויון זכויות לאנשים עם מוגבלות, התשנ"ח-1998, ועל חובת המשיבה לשקול את השיקול הרלוונטי של נגישות שוויונית. המשפט הבינלאומי תומך במסקנה זו.

7.2 מדינת ישראל אשררה את האמנה בדבר זכויותיהם של אנשים עם מוגבלויות ביום 28.9.2012. הסתייגותה היחידה של ישראל נוגעת לסעיף 23(1)(א) לאמנה (נישואין), ואינה נוגעת לענייננו.

7.3 סעיף 9(1) לאמנה קובע:
"States Parties shall take appropriate measures to ensure to persons with disabilities access, on an equal basis with others, to the physical environment, to transportation..."
(תרגום חופשי: המדינות שהן צד לאמנה ינקטו צעדים מתאימים כדי להבטיח לאנשים עם מוגבלויות גישה, על בסיס שוויוני, לסביבה הפיזית, לתחבורה...)

7.4 לפי חזקת ההתאמה הפרשנית, יש לפרש את חובות הנגישות שבחוק ובתקנות, ואת שיקול דעתה של המשיבה, באופן העולה בקנה אחד עם התחייבויותיה של ישראל לפי האמנה (ראו, למשל, בג"ץ 2599/00 ית"ד נ' משרד החינוך (2002)).

7.5 בדוח שהגישה מדינת ישראל לוועדת האמנה הציגה המדינה את חוק שוויון זכויות לאנשים עם מוגבלות ואת תקנותיו כך: "[ציטוט מדויק מן הדוח, עמ' ___]". העותרת מבקשת אפוא לפרש את החוק כפי שהמדינה עצמה מציגה אותו.

7.6 לאור האמור, המשפט הבינלאומי מחזק את המסקנה כי החלטת המשיבה לדחות את התקנת אמצעי ההנגשה מטעמי סדרי עדיפויות תקציביים אינה סבירה, ומכאן הסעד המבוקש בפרק 9.
```

**6. Red team.** *State Attorney:* "The CRPD was not incorporated into Israeli law; the petitioner seeks to enforce a treaty through the back door, and budget allocation is the council's discretion." *Answer:* "The petition does not ask the court to enforce the Convention. It relies on the 1998 Law and the regulations; the Convention, which the State itself links to that Law in its report to the Committee, guides their interpretation. The council's discretion must take equal access into account as a relevant consideration."

Rubric self-score for this model: 18/20 (the Israel's-position row is strong; the custom row is not applicable and was scored on the honest decision not to claim custom; the source table loses a point because the General Comment is not cited by number and date).
