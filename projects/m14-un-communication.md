# Module 14 project: A UN communication and engagement plan

Choose one human-rights issue in Israel, East Jerusalem, the West Bank or Gaza that you know first-hand or can document from reliable direct sources, and build the file a UN advocate would prepare: a treaty map, a calendar of every UN opening for the next three years, a ready-to-send Special Procedures communication, a short shadow-report or UPR text with concrete recommendations, a neutral "who says what" brief, and a safety and consent plan. The project is a dry run: you submit nothing unless there is a real case and the people concerned have consented.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Act in your own name only (or for a person or group that has authorised you). This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing. Do not send material to UN bodies only to complete this project, and never name a real person without written consent.

## What you produce

1. **Treaty map** (half a page): the issue in one sentence, the treaties and articles, Israel's ratification dates and reservations, a one-sentence attributed note on territorial scope if relevant, and why no individual complaint is possible ([shadow-report-outline.md](../templates/shadow-report-outline.md), section 1; lesson 14.1).
2. **UN calendar** (a table): for every relevant committee, its last concluding observations and its next step and due date from the OHCHR database; the UPR 5th-cycle stakeholder deadline (provisional) and how you will confirm it; open calls for submissions (lessons 14.2, 14.3, 14.5).
3. **Special Procedures communication** (two to three pages): the full draft in the order of the online form, with the mandates chosen and why, facts in date order with sources, national steps already taken, the request, and a consent record ([special-procedures-communication.md](../templates/special-procedures-communication.md); lesson 14.4). If your issue has no specific recent event, explain why and replace this with a call-for-submissions response to a commission or rapporteur.
4. **Shadow-report summary or UPR text** (one page): either the one-page summary of a shadow report with two issues, each with a question and a recommendation ([shadow-report-outline.md](../templates/shadow-report-outline.md)); or a 600–800-word UPR section with a follow-up table on the 2023 recommendations and two SMART recommendations ([upr-stakeholder-submission.md](../templates/upr-stakeholder-submission.md)).
5. **"Who says what" brief** (half a page): one UN finding or recommendation on your issue (committee, Special Procedure, commission or UPR), Israel's official response, and any other state's position, each quoted exactly with its source (lesson 14.5).
6. **Safety and consent plan** (half a page): who could be identified, what each person agreed to, what will be disclosed to the government and published, how files are stored, and what you will do if someone faces reprisals.
7. **Domestic use** (three lines): how you will use the UN output in Israel — a letter, a Knesset paper (Module 12), a petition argument (lesson 13.5).

Time: about 5 hours.

## Steps

1. **Choose.** Any cause from any part of the political map: detention, policing, protest, religion and state, disability, children, housing and demolitions, the rights of hostages' families, settlers' or Palestinians' rights, women, migrants, the environment.
2. **Map the treaties (14.1)** and check every article against Israel's reservations.
3. **Build the calendar (14.2, 14.3)** from the OHCHR Israel country page and the UPR pages.
4. **Collect evidence**: first-hand statements with consent, documents, court records, official data or FOI answers. No claim may rest only on news reports.
5. **Draft the communication (14.4)** offline in the template. Check the five Code of Conduct tests.
6. **Draft the shadow-report summary or the UPR section (14.2 or 14.3).**
7. **Write the "who says what" brief (14.5)** and ask someone with different views to check that it is neutral.
8. **Write the safety and consent plan.**
9. **Plan the domestic use.**
10. **Self-assess** with the rubric, then compare with the model answer.

## Self-assessment rubric

Score each row 0, 1 or 2. 12 or more out of 14 means your file is ready to use; below 8, redo the weakest rows.

| Criterion | 0 — not yet | 1 — partly | 2 — done well |
|---|---|---|---|
| Treaty map | Treaties guessed or an individual complaint proposed | Treaties right, reservations not checked | Articles checked against reservations; scope attributed; open routes named |
| UN calendar | No dates | Dates for one mechanism only | Every relevant committee with last COs and next step from the database; UPR deadline marked provisional with a confirmation plan |
| Communication: criteria | News-only, abusive or vague | Facts present but undated or unsourced | Dated first-hand facts, neutral language, national steps, a clear request; all five art. 9 tests met |
| Communication: mandate and consent | Wrong mandate, or WGAD/WGEID without the family | Mandate right; consent or disclosure unclear | Mandates justified; consent recorded for disclosure to the government and publication; security concerns stated |
| Shadow report or UPR text | General essay | Issues without questions or recommendations, or over the limit | Article-by-article or follow-up on 2023 recommendations; each issue ends in a question and a SMART recommendation; within limits |
| "Who says what" brief | Findings in your own voice | Attributed, but Israel's response missing | Exact quotes with sources for the UN body, Israel and another state; checked by a reader with different views |
| Safety and domestic use | Missing | Safety plan without storage or reprisal steps; or no domestic use | Consent, storage, reprisal response and a concrete Israeli use of the output |

This is a condensed model based on a realistic dry run. Your file will be about your own issue.

**0. The issue.** Noa Peretz, a volunteer with a parents' forum in Jerusalem (an invented amuta), works on children with disabilities who wait months for a place in a special-education framework. She knows six families first-hand.

**1. Treaty map.** CRPD art. 24 (education) and art. 7 (children with disabilities); CRC arts 23 and 28; ICESCR art. 13. Ratified: CRPD 28 Sep 2012, CRC and ICESCR 3 Oct 1991. Reservations: Israel's only CRPD reservation is on art. 23(1)(a) (marriage); none on the CRC or ICESCR — all cited articles are unreserved. Scope: the issue is inside Israel, so no scope note. No individual complaint is possible: Israel ratified none of the optional protocols.

**2. UN calendar (checked 13 Oct 2026).** CRPD: last COs CRPD/C/ISR/CO/1 (Sept 2023); next LOIPR expected Sept 2034. CRC: last COs CRC/C/ISR/CO/5-6 (Sept 2024); LOIPR date to be set — check monthly; CRC input goes through Child Rights Connect. CESCR: 5th report was due 31 Oct 2024, not shown as submitted. UPR: 5th cycle provisional — stakeholder deadline about 1 Nov 2027 (UPR Info); confirm on the OHCHR UPR page from spring 2027. Special Rapporteur on the rights of persons with disabilities: no open call found on the topic; check the mandate page.

**3. Communication.** Not used: there is no single recent event, and the families want a systemic change rather than an intervention in one case. Replaced by a short response to any future call for input by the Special Rapporteur on the rights of persons with disabilities, drafted in the template with the same facts.

**4. UPR text (720 words).** Follow-up table: two 2023 recommendations on inclusive education found in the UHRI (state and Israel's position recorded). Facts: an anonymised waiting-time log for six families (consent recorded), and Ministry of Education placement figures obtained by an FOI request. SMART recommendations: "Set by 2030 a maximum waiting time of 30 days for a placement decision for children with disabilities, and publish compliance data annually"; "Allocate funding so that every district has an inclusive-education support centre by 2030."

**5. Who says what.** The CRPD Committee's 2023 concluding observations on education (paragraph quoted with symbol); Israel's reply to the list of issues (CRPD/C/ISR/RQ/1) on the Special Education Law amendments (paragraph quoted); one state's 2023 UPR recommendation on inclusive education (from the UHRI). A parent from a different political camp read it and found it neutral.

**6. Safety and consent.** Families are named only as "Family A–F"; each parent signed a consent covering the UN submission and publication without names. The log is stored in an encrypted folder; only Noa and one other volunteer have access. Reprisal risk is low; if a family reports pressure, Noa will inform OHCHR and stop using their details.

**7. Domestic use.** Quote the 2023 CRPD concluding observations in a position paper to the Knesset Education Committee (Module 12); attach the FOI data to a letter to the ministry's director-general; if Israel supports the UPR recommendation in 2028, cite the commitment in every later letter.

**Mirror test.** The same file would serve a Haredi parents' group on special-education places in Haredi schools, a Bedouin council on transport to special-education frameworks in unrecognised villages, or a group of settlers' parents on services in regional councils. The mechanisms and the rules do not depend on the cause.

**Rubric self-score for this model:** 13/14. The communication row loses a point because the file replaced the communication without drafting a full call-for-input response.
