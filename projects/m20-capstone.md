# Module 20 project: Capstone campaign

Run one real campaign on a cause of your choice, from start to finish, with the tools of the whole course, and write it up as a case study. You choose the cause, set a goal that a named decision-maker can grant, map a legal strategy, act in your own name, log every step, communicate within the law, and close with an honest retrospective and a hand-over. This capstone takes the place of the seminar paper that ends a university law degree: it is judged on a real-world strategy, carried out and reviewed, not on an academic argument.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Act in your own name only, or in the name of a group or amuta that has authorised you in writing. This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing: fees and deadlines on [Current Numbers](../references/current-numbers.md), and each tool's lesson. Never file a petition, a claim or a complaint only to complete this project: courts can order costs, and oversight bodies investigate real harm. A step you decide not to take for real stays in the case study as a dry run, with your reasons. Before you publish anything, run the checks in lesson 20.4.

## Requirements

- **One cause, one goal.** A goal that names the decision-maker, the decision and a date (lesson 20.1).
- **At least four tools from at least three families** of the course: information; oversight; pre-litigation and litigation; Knesset and local government; public communication; international (lesson 20.2).
- **At least three real actions** in your own name (or your group's), each with proof in the log. You may count actions from Module 19 (lessons 19.2–19.6) only if they were part of this campaign's plan.
- **A calendar duration of 3–9 months.** Waiting for authorities is part of the work; start early.
- **Fair treatment of the other side.** The case study states the opposing position, attributed, and the official position of any public body involved (plan §5).
- **Privacy.** No ID numbers, home addresses, phone numbers, or names of private people who did not agree, in anything you share.

## What you produce

A case study of about 3,000–5,000 words, plus appendices:

1. **Cause and theory of change** (lesson 20.1): the six-filter table, the goal, the backwards map, the assumptions, in whose name you act — using [theory-of-change.md](../templates/theory-of-change.md).
2. **Legal strategy map** (lesson 20.2): the tools with forum, cost and clock, typical time, expected chance and risk; the deadline spine; the risk register; the budget; the tools you did not use, and why — using [legal-strategy-map.md](../templates/legal-strategy-map.md).
3. **Execution log** (lesson 20.3): every event, with proof and exhibit numbers; weekly reviews; plan versions — using [campaign-execution-log.md](../templates/campaign-execution-log.md).
4. **Public communication** (lesson 20.4): at least one press release or public statement, with the seven-question check and the right-of-reply request — using [press-release.md](../templates/press-release.md) and [pre-publication-checklist.md](../templates/pre-publication-checklist.md).
5. **Retrospective** (lesson 20.5): outcome against goal, assumptions and tools judged, the other side's account, legal lessons, unintended effects, what you would do differently — using [campaign-retrospective.md](../templates/campaign-retrospective.md).
6. **Hand-over or closing pack and safety review** (lesson 20.5).
7. **Exhibits:** the documents you sent and received, numbered, with private details removed.

Time: about 50 hours of hands-on work (planning, writing, filing, meetings, communication, write-up), spread over 3–9 months of calendar time because you wait for authorities, committees and courts.

## Steps

1. **Choose (20.1).** Score two or three causes; set the goal; build the theory of change; get your group's written authorisation if you act for a group.
2. **Map (20.2).** Fill in the tools from the decision matrix; draw the deadline spine; write the risk register and the budget; check the capstone minimum.
3. **Start (20.3).** Set up the log and folders; take the first real action (usually a formal letter or an FOI request); fix your weekly review slot.
4. **Run (20.3).** Act, log the same day, review weekly, adjust the plan when the facts change. Keep every hard deadline.
5. **Communicate (20.4).** At each real milestone, decide whether to publish; check every text; ask the other side for comment.
6. **Close (20.5).** When the goal is reached, refused, or the time is up: retrospective, hand-over or closing, safety and personal-data review, group debrief.
7. **Write the case study.** Introduction (the problem and why it matters), the six parts above in order, and a conclusion of half a page. Cite log lines and exhibits for every factual claim.
8. **Self-assess** with the rubric, then compare with the model answer.

## Self-assessment rubric

Score each row 0, 1 or 2. 17 or more out of 20 means an excellent capstone; 12–16 is a solid pass; below 12, strengthen the weakest rows before you call it done. A campaign that did not reach its goal can still score 20: the rubric judges the strategy, the execution and the honesty of the review, not the outcome.

| Criterion | 0 — not yet | 1 — partly | 2 — done well |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cause and goal | Vague aim; no decision-maker | Goal with a decision-maker but no date or no "reasoned decision" clause | Six filters scored with evidence; goal names decision-maker, decision, date; power traced to a section of law |
| Theory of change | None, or a list of activities | Preconditions without assumptions or indicators | Backwards map; each precondition has an action and a lesson; assumptions tested early and recorded |
| Strategy map | Tools chosen by habit | Tools listed with some deadlines | Matrix data for each tool; deadline spine with hard deadlines first; five sequencing rules met; risk register and budget; reasons for tools not used |
| Real actions within the law | Fewer than three real actions, or an action for another person | Three real actions; some proof missing | At least three real actions in own name, each with proof; s. 20 respected; capstone minimum met |
| Execution log and evidence | Reconstructed afterwards or missing | Logged, but gaps in proof, exhibits or clocks | Same-day lines; proof for every "sent"; numbered exhibits; every clock tracked; weekly reviews with decisions |
| Adapting the plan | The plan never changed despite new facts | Changes made but not explained | Plan versions recorded with the facts that caused them |
| Public communication | None, or a text that breaks a legal limit | Release sent without a recorded check or right of reply | Milestone-based release; seven-question check recorded; right of reply requested; bans and sub judice checked |
| Retrospective | A success story without evidence | Outcome stated; tools or assumptions not judged | Outcome against goal with exhibits; every assumption and tool judged against expectations; legal lessons and unintended effects |
| Hand-over, data and safety | None | Pack or clean-up partly done | Hand-over pack or closing plan complete; filings in own name accounted for; personal data handed over for the same purpose or deleted; safety and wellbeing reviewed |
| Fairness and writing | One-sided; private details exposed | Other side mentioned without attribution | Other side and official positions stated fairly and attributed; private people protected; clear structure with cited log lines |

This is a condensed model for an invented learner. Your case study will be longer and about your own campaign.

**Title.** "Restoring the evening bus: a five-month campaign for an intercity line to a Negev development town."

**Introduction.** In September 2026 the evening intercity bus from Be'er Sheva to the learner's town (Yael, a nursing student) was cut. Students and shift workers lost their only evening connection. Yael ran the capstone in her own name and, from month two, with a residents' group that authorised her in writing.

**1. Cause and theory of change.** Candidates: (a) "public transport in the periphery is neglected" — failed filters 3 and 6; (b) "my own refund for a missed connection" — a personal small claim, not a campaign; (c) the evening line — passed all six. Goal: "By 31 March 2027, the National Public Transport Authority (Ministry of Transport) decides to restore at least one evening departure on weekdays, or gives a reasoned written decision." Power: the Authority's licensing of public-transport lines (researched in a memo, Module 2). Preconditions: the Authority knows the demand in writing; the ridership data is public; the cut has a political cost; refusal faces a legal challenge. Assumptions: (A1) ridership data exists per departure; (A2) the cut was a ministry decision, not the operator's alone; (A3) MKs from more than one party will ask. Other side, attributed: the Ministry's published position that low-occupancy departures are cut to fund high-demand routes.

**2. Strategy map.** Tools: formal letter (02.5, real); FOI request to the Ministry for ridership per departure and the cut decision (05.2, real); FOI reminder and FOI Unit complaint (05.3, real if late); local council resolution through two council members (12.5, real); direct questions through MKs of the new Knesset, one coalition and one opposition (12.4, real); press release at a milestone (20.4, real); pre-petition letter (05.5, dry run unless refused without reasons). Not used: protest (not needed for this goal; would add arrest risk without legal effect); international (no treaty issue that adds anything); small claim (a personal remedy, not the goal). Deadline spine: the petition clock if a decision is published; the council question 7 days before the January meeting; the FOI periods. Risk register: defamation (name the decision, not officials); costs (petition only after a clinic's review); burnout (8 hours a week cap). Budget: FOI fee (see Current Numbers), registered mail, travel to one council meeting.

**3. Execution log (extract).** 01 · 3.11.2026 decision to start. 02 · 5.11 formal letter to the Authority, registered mail (A02–A03). 03 · 5.11 FOI request online, fee paid (A04–A05). 04 · 7.12 day 30, no answer, no extension notice; reminder (A06). 05 · 22.12 FOI Unit complaint (A07). 06 · 6.1.2027 FOI answer: data per departure released (A08) — A1 confirmed; the evening departure carried more passengers than two morning departures that were kept. Weekly review: adjust — ask for "at least one departure" instead of "the old timetable". 07 · 13.1 council member (coalition) proposes a resolution; opposition member co-signs (A09). 08 · 20.1 council passes the resolution unanimously (protocol A10). 09 · 21.1 press release with the FOI figures; Ministry asked for comment on 19.1, answered "the matter is being examined" (A11–A12). 10 · 3.2 two MKs submit direct questions (A13–A14). 11 · 5.2 the formal letter of 5.11 has received no answer for three months — deemed refusal without reasons (s. 6(ב)); pre-petition letter drafted (dry run, A15) and held one week pending the MK answers. 12 · 4.3 the Minister's written answer: a trial evening departure on Mondays and Wednesdays from 1 April (A16). 13 · 1.4 first trial bus runs; Yael rides it and logs the occupancy count with the group (A17).

**4. Communication.** One release, at the FOI milestone, with the seven-question check: one draft sentence ("the official who cut the line ignores the periphery") was changed to an opinion about the decision. Right of reply requested and quoted. Two local news items and a radio interview on the record.

**5. Retrospective.** Outcome: partial — two evenings a week on trial, not every weekday; carried out from 1 April. Assumptions: A1 confirmed (and decisive); A2 confirmed by the Minister's answer; A3 confirmed. Tools: FOI took 62 days and two escalations but produced the decisive figure (expected chance high, actual high); the council resolution was cheap and fast; the press release worked because it carried official data; the direct questions produced the decision; the pre-petition letter was not needed. What I would do differently: file the FOI request two weeks before the formal letter; involve the group from day one; set the trial's evaluation criteria with the Ministry in writing.

**6. Hand-over and safety.** The residents' group takes over monitoring of the trial in its own name: the pack lists the trial's end date, the occupancy counts to collect, the Ministry contact who agreed to be passed on, and the group's own new letter asking for the evaluation criteria. Yael's FOI answers stay hers; she forwards them to the group. The supporters' WhatsApp list stays with the group for the same purpose; Yael's personal spreadsheet is deleted. Safety: one hostile online comment, preserved, no action needed; average 7 hours a week, within the cap.

**Mirror test.** The same structure would serve a campaign by Haredi residents for a women-friendly bus timetable, by a Druze village for a school-bus stop, by settlers for a bullet-proof bus on a dangerous road, or by students in Tel Aviv for night trains.

**Rubric self-score for this model:** 19/20. The "Public communication" row is 2; the "Fairness and writing" row loses a point because the case study quotes the Ministry's position from a press statement but does not check its published budget papers.
