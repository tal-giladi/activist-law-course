# Module 12 project: Position paper and participation file

Choose one bill (or a government draft, תזכיר חוק) or one local-authority decision that affects a cause you care about, and build the file an advocate would bring to the Knesset or the council: a map of where the issue stands, a position paper with proposed wordings, a request to take part in the session, a ready-to-file question for an elected representative, and — for a local issue — a complaint to the authority's commissioner. The project is a dry run unless there is a real session or a real harm to you.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Act in your own name only (or for a group that has authorised you). This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing. In October 2026 the 25th Knesset is in its election recess and few committees sit; date your paper and plan to update it for the 26th Knesset. Do not send material to MKs, council members or commissioners only to complete this project.

## What you produce

1. **Stage map** (half a page): the bill's type, number, every stage passed with dates, its committee, and your verdict for the elections — lapses, or eligible for continuity and who must act (lesson 12.1). For a local issue: the body that decides, the date of the next council meeting, and the protocols where the issue appeared (lesson 12.5).
2. **Position paper** (2–4 pages) in Hebrew, with a one-page summary, numbered requests, arguments each ending in a proposed wording, an answer to the other side, a wording table and sources ([position-paper.md](../templates/position-paper.md); lesson 12.3).
3. **Participation request** to the committee secretariat, and a two-minute statement ([committee-participation-request.md](../templates/committee-participation-request.md); lesson 12.2). For a local issue: a request to the council's committee or the mayor's office to present the paper.
4. **A question for an elected representative**: Part A (MK) or Part B (council member) of [council-question-request.md](../templates/council-question-request.md), with the earlier questions you found (lessons 12.4–12.5).
5. **Lobbying check** (three lines): who in your group acts, whether anyone is paid by a client, and whether a permit is needed (Knesset Law ss. 65–67).
6. **For a local issue:** a complaint to the authority's Public Complaints Commissioner ([municipal-complaint.md](../templates/municipal-complaint.md)) and one FOI request to its FOI officer ([foi-request.md](../templates/foi-request.md)).
7. **Sending plan** (half a page): who receives what, when, and how you will follow up (protocol, next draft, reservation, answer to the question).

Time: about 4 hours.

## Steps

1. **Choose.** A bill or תזכיר from any part of the political map — welfare, religion and state, security, environment, land, education, business, civil rights — or a local decision (a facility, a budget item, a by-law, a service).
2. **Map it (12.1 or 12.5).** Stage, committee, continuity verdict; or decision-maker, council dates, protocols.
3. **Read it.** Bill text and explanatory notes, the latest committee draft, one protocol; or the council protocols and recordings.
4. **Decide your position** in one sentence and choose two to four requests within the bill's subject.
5. **Write the paper (12.3).** Evidence with sources; one FOI request if you need data you do not have.
6. **Write the participation request and the two-minute statement (12.2).**
7. **Draft the question (12.4 or 12.5)** and choose two representatives, from the coalition and the opposition.
8. **Do the lobbying check.**
9. **Local issue:** draft the complaint and the FOI request.
10. **Plan the sending and follow-up.**
11. **Self-assess** with the rubric, then compare with the model answer.

## Self-assessment rubric

Score each row 0, 1 or 2. 12 or more out of 14 means your file is ready to send; below 8, redo the weakest rows.

| Criterion | 0 — not yet | 1 — partly | 2 — done well |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stage map | Stage guessed | Stage right, dates or committee missing | Type, stages with dates, committee; correct lapse or continuity verdict with who must act (or local decision-maker and meeting dates) |
| Paper: summary and requests | No summary | Summary without numbered requests | Page one: who, one-line position, numbered requests |
| Paper: arguments and wordings | General objections | Arguments without wordings, or outside the bill's subject | Problem → sourced evidence → exact wording; all within the bill's subject (s. 85); other side answered |
| Participation request | None | Request without session, interest or entry details | Session, interest (s. 125), what is asked, entry details; statement under two minutes ending in a request |
| Question | Opinion question | Factual but too long or not checked against earlier questions | Factual, right type and length, no private details (s. 47 or Second Schedule s. 36), earlier questions searched, both sides approached |
| Lobbying check and local tools | Missing | Lobbying check only, or local tools with the wrong forum | Correct permit verdict; for local issues, complaint meets ss. 5–7 and FOI goes to the authority's officer |
| Sending and follow-up | None | List of recipients only | Recipients from both sides, legal adviser and secretariat, dates, and a follow-up plan (protocol, reservation, answer) |

This is a condensed model based on a realistic dry run. Your file will be about your own issue.

**0. The issue.** A private bill of the 25th Knesset (invented number פ/4321/25) to require every local authority to broadcast council meetings live and keep the recordings online for five years. It passed its first reading in May 2026 and was in the Interior Committee when the recess began. Submitter: Ruth Ben-David, a volunteer in a residents' forum in a mid-sized city, writing in the forum's name (a registered amuta).

**1. Stage map.** Private bill → preliminary reading (February 2026) → Interior Committee → first reading (May 2026) → back to the Interior Committee for second and third readings (Rules s. 83(א)). Recess since 18 July 2026; elections 27 October 2026. Passed its first reading, so eligible for continuity: one of its three proposers must be re-elected and consent, and the committee of the 26th Knesset that handles local government must propose continuity; a faction may object within two weeks; the plenum decides (Continuity Law ss. 2–6).

**2. Position paper (summary page).** "The forum supports the bill and asks for three changes." (1) Clause 2: add "וכן יפורסם סדר היום של הישיבה, בצירוף החומר שנשלח לחברי המועצה, באתר האינטרנט של הרשות 48 שעות לפני הישיבה" — linking to the 48-hour invitation rule (Second Schedule s. 14). (2) Clause 3: keep recordings for seven years, not five, to match the audit cycle. (3) Clause 5: allow small regional councils to start a year later, with a report to the committee. Evidence: a check of the websites of 20 authorities (own data, method attached) — 7 had not posted recordings required by s. 248ב(2); the s. 248ב text. Answer to the other side: "Small councils fear the cost; request 3 phases it in." Wording table with three rows. Sources listed.

**3. Participation request.** To the Interior Committee secretariat, to be sent once the new committee schedules the bill: Ruth's interest (her forum's monitoring work), a request for two minutes and to submit the paper, her name, ID number and phone for the entry list. Two-minute statement ending: "בסעיף 2 — להוסיף פרסום סדר היום והחומר 48 שעות מראש."

**4. Question.** Direct question (Part A) for an MK on the committee, to the Minister of the Interior: "כמה רשויות מקומיות פרסמו באתר האינטרנט שלהן הקלטות או תמלילים של ישיבות המועצה בשנת 2025, כנדרש בסעיף 248ב לפקודת העיריות, ואילו צעדים ננקטו כלפי עיריות שלא פרסמו?" (factual, no private details). Earlier questions searched in the Knesset open data: none on recordings since 2023. Sent to one coalition and one opposition MK.

**5. Lobbying check.** Ruth and the forum's members are unpaid volunteers: no permit. The forum considered paying a consultant; if it does, the consultant needs a permit and must disclose the forum as the client (Knesset Law ss. 65–68).

**6. Local tool (for her own city).** Ruth's city has not posted the recording of a June meeting. She writes to the council coordinator citing s. 248ב(2); after 30 days with no reply, she drafts an FOI request for the recording and a council member's direct question (Part B). A complaint to the local commissioner is weaker here, because the failure does not harm her directly in the sense of s. 5(ב)(1) — she notes it as a fallback only if her own request is ignored.

**7. Sending plan.** After the 26th Knesset forms its committees: ask a re-elected proposer to consent to continuity; send the paper to the secretariat, the committee's legal adviser, the chair and two members from each side; follow the protocol; if request 1 is rejected, ask an MK to file it as a reservation (s. 86(ב)).

**Mirror test.** The same file would serve a group that opposes the bill — for example a forum of small regional councils that fears the cost — with requests to phase it in or fund it, or a group on a completely different bill, such as rules on synagogue allocations or on protest permits. The method does not depend on the cause.

**Rubric self-score for this model:** 13/14. The local-tools row loses a point because the first draft sent the issue to the complaints commissioner before checking direct harm.
