## Overall

The course meets plan §5 to a high standard. Contested matters (judicial legislation, the territories, ICJ and ICC proceedings) are consistently attributed, with Israel's official position quoted and dated. The mirror-test device keeps examples balanced. No quiz or bank answer requires a political opinion, except m16-052's "centrist" characterisation. The recurring weaknesses:

1. **Labels in course voice** that the course itself forbids: "atrocities" (17.1), "judicial overhaul" (17.1) and "judicial reform" (20.4), "far-right" (bank 17), and a bare "Judea and Samaria" or "Samaria" in worked examples. One global search-and-fix pass would clear these.
2. **One-sided NGO or scholar selection** in four "who says what" tables: 06.1 (Minister's power), 15.2 (Gaza scholars), 16.2 (jurisdiction) and 16.3 (complementarity, where the left is named and the right is generic).
3. **One unsourced contested attribution** (17.1:144 on the Dinah Project) that needs a citation or removal.
