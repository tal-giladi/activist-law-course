# Module 15 — Law of Armed Conflict and Occupation: university equivalent

## University courses

- **Israeli LL.B., elective level.** Israeli faculties teach the law of armed conflict (דיני מלחמה / המשפט ההומניטרי הבינלאומי) as an elective. It is never mandatory. Public international law (משפט בינלאומי פומבי, year 2, 4 ש"ס) comes first. Two examples:
  - Tel Aviv University has listed *The Humanitarian Law of Armed Conflict* (1411-7244-01, Prof. Eyal Benvenisti) as a short course taught in English.
  - At Hebrew University, Prof. Yuval Shany's IHL course is published as a teaching model on the ICRC Casebook site. It covers the principles of distinction, proportionality and military necessity; POW status; the "war on terror"; means and methods of warfare; and enforcement through international criminal law.
- **Israeli seminars.** Seminars and proseminars on Israel and international law recur. Tel Aviv's 2025–26 booklet, for example, has a proseminar *ישראל והמשפט הבינלאומי – סוגיות בוערות* (Israel and International Law – Burning Issues, 3 ש"ס).
- **International LL.M. level.** This is the heart of the Geneva Academy LL.M. Its core course *International Humanitarian Law* carries 12 ECTS, the largest in the programme. There are optionals on the conduct of hostilities, weapons law, non-state armed groups and implementation of IHL.

## What this module covers

- The basics of IHL: the Geneva Conventions and the Hague law, and the difference between international and non-international armed conflict.
- The core principles: distinction, proportionality, precautions and military necessity.
- The law of occupation: the Hague Regulations Art. 42–43, Geneva Convention IV, and the duties of the occupying power.
- The Israeli High Court's case law on the territories (for example *Beit Sourik*, *Ajuri*), and the debate over whether Geneva Convention IV applies.
- The 2004 and 2024 ICJ advisory opinions, with Israel's stated positions alongside them.
- A neutral method for analysing one incident under IHL.

## What we left out

- Classification of conflicts in depth, including transnational conflicts with non-state groups.
- Conduct of hostilities in detail: targeting law, direct participation in hostilities, human shields, and siege and starvation.
- Weapons law: prohibited weapons, Article 36 reviews, and cyber and autonomous weapons.
- Status of persons: combatants, prisoners of war, unlawful combatants (the Israeli Incarceration of Unlawful Combatants Law), and internment.
- Protection of the wounded and sick, medical and humanitarian personnel, and humanitarian access.
- Interaction of IHL with human-rights law: lex specialis and extraterritoriality, in depth.
- The law on the use of force (jus ad bellum) as a separate field.
- Implementation and enforcement: the role of the ICRC, state investigations, and reparations. Module 16 covers part of this.
- Naval and air warfare law, blockade, and neutrality.

## What would make it a whole university course

- **Topics to add.** All of the above, using ICRC Customary IHL Study rules and case studies.
- **Core readings.**
  - Yoram Dinstein, *The Conduct of Hostilities under the Law of International Armed Conflict* (Cambridge UP, latest edition).
  - Yoram Dinstein, *The International Law of Belligerent Occupation* (Cambridge UP, latest edition).
  - Eyal Benvenisti, *The International Law of Occupation* (Oxford UP, 2nd ed. 2012).
  - David Kretzmer and Yaël Ronen, *The Occupation of Justice: The Supreme Court of Israel and the Occupied Territories* (Oxford UP, 2nd ed. 2021).
  - Marco Sassòli, *International Humanitarian Law: Rules, Controversies, and Solutions to Problems Arising in Warfare* (Edward Elgar, 2019).
- **Free sources.**
  - ICRC Casebook *How Does Law Protect in War?* (casebook.icrc.org).
  - ICRC IHL databases (treaties and customary rules).
  - ICRC free e-learning on the basic rules of IHL.
  - UN Audiovisual Library lectures on the law of armed conflict.
  - The Israeli MFA "Israel and international law" pages, for Israel's positions.
- **Rough hours.**
  - An Israeli elective of 2–4 ש"ס is about 26–56 class hours, plus about 60–120 hours of reading, plus exam or paper work, about 100–200 hours in all.
  - The Geneva 12 ECTS core course is about 300–360 hours.
  - This module is roughly 12–15 hours.
