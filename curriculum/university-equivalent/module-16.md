# Module 16 — International Criminal Law and Accountability: university equivalent

## University courses

- **Israeli LL.B., elective level.** International criminal law is a small elective in Israeli faculties. At Tel Aviv University the 2025–26 booklet lists *משפט פלילי בינלאומי וטריבונלים פליליים בינלאומיים* (International Criminal Law and International Criminal Tribunals, 1411-7553-01, 2 ש"ס). Two courses come first:
  - Public international law (משפט בינלאומי פומבי, year 2, 4 ש"ס).
  - Criminal law (דיני עונשין, year 1, mandatory).
- **International LL.M. level.** This is where the subject is taught in full.
  - The Geneva Academy LL.M. has *International Criminal Law* as a core course (6 ECTS).
  - Leiden University's Advanced LL.M. in Public International Law has an International Criminal Law specialisation (60 ECTS in total). Its general courses are public international law, international institutional law, IHL and IHRL. Its specialisation courses are *International Criminal Trials* and *International Crimes*. These cover international crimes, modes of liability, general principles, impediments to enforcement, and international criminal procedure.

## What this module covers

- The four core international crimes: genocide, crimes against humanity, war crimes and aggression, at the level of their definitions.
- The ICC: the Rome Statute, jurisdiction, the Palestine situation, and Israel's position that the Court lacks jurisdiction.
- Complementarity, and Israeli investigation mechanisms: the Military Advocate General, the Turkel Commission follow-up, and the Fact-Finding Assessment Mechanism.
- Universal jurisdiction in foreign courts.
- How civil society sends information to the OTP and other bodies (Article 15 communications).

## What we left out

- The elements of each crime in depth, including the ICC Elements of Crimes and the case law of the ICTY, ICTR and ICC.
- Modes of liability: joint criminal enterprise, co-perpetration, indirect perpetration, command and superior responsibility, aiding and abetting.
- General principles and defences: mens rea, superior orders, duress, self-defence, mistake, and immunities of officials.
- International criminal procedure: investigation, confirmation of charges, trial, the rights of the accused, victims' participation, and reparations.
- History and institutions: Nuremberg and Tokyo, the ad hoc tribunals, hybrid courts, and state cooperation.
- The Eichmann trial and Israeli domestic legislation on international crimes, in depth.
- Transitional justice alternatives: truth commissions, amnesties, and the peace-versus-justice debate.
- Critical perspectives on selectivity and legitimacy.

## What would make it a whole university course

- **Topics to add.** The items above, studied through selected judgments: *Tadić*, *Akayesu*, *Lubanga*, *Katanga*, and *Bemba* (appeal).
- **Core readings.**
  - Antonio Cassese et al., *Cassese's International Criminal Law* (Oxford UP, latest edition).
  - Robert Cryer, Darryl Robinson and Sergey Vasiliev, *An Introduction to International Criminal Law and Procedure* (Cambridge UP, latest edition).
  - William A. Schabas, *An Introduction to the International Criminal Court* (Cambridge UP, latest edition).
  - Gerhard Werle and Florian Jessberger, *Principles of International Criminal Law* (Oxford UP, latest edition).
- **Free sources.**
  - The Rome Statute, Elements of Crimes and ICC judgments (icc-cpi.int).
  - The ICC Legal Tools Database.
  - UN Audiovisual Library lectures on international criminal law.
  - The Israeli MFA paper on the ICC's lack of jurisdiction, for Israel's position.
  - The OTP's own policy papers.
- **Rough hours.**
  - A 2 ש"ס Israeli elective is about 26 class hours, plus about 60 hours of reading, plus about 15 hours of exam preparation, about 100 hours in all.
  - A 6 ECTS LL.M. core course is about 150–180 hours.
  - A full Leiden-style specialisation is far more, about 1,500 hours for the whole year.
  - This module is roughly 12–15 hours.
