# Module 2 — Legal Research and Writing: university equivalent

## University courses

- **Tel Aviv University: ניתוח מקורות וכתיבה משפטית (Source Analysis and Legal Writing), course 1411-9106.** Year 1, mandatory. 3 ש"ס, taught in small groups.
- **Hebrew University: כתיבה ומחקר במשפט (Legal Writing and Research), course 62121.** Year 1, mandatory. 1 ש"ש, 2 נ"ז. Assessed by a written research paper.
- **Later writing requirements.** Most faculties follow up with a proseminar in year 2 and a seminar paper in year 3. At TAU, a proseminar is a condition for entering the seminar.
- **Practical drafting.** Some faculties also have a year-4 practical-skills course on drafting pleadings and letters. Haifa has a mandatory מיומנויות עריכת דין (lawyering skills) course.

## What this module covers

- Finding the current, consolidated text of a law for free: Wikisource, the Knesset database and Reshumot (lesson 02.1).
- Finding rulings for free through the Supreme Court search and Versa, and judging how much weight a ruling has (02.2).
- Secondary sources: Kol Zchut, the Knesset Research and Information Center, free law journals and the Israel Democracy Institute (02.3).
- Building an argument with the IRAC structure (02.4).
- Writing a formal demand letter to an authority under the Administrative Procedure Amendment (Decisions and Reasons) Law 1958 (02.5).
- Affidavits under the Evidence Ordinance ss. 15–17 and CPR 2018 reg. 178 (02.6). This is practitioner material that university writing courses usually skip.

## What we left out

- The paid professional databases (Nevo, Takdin, Dinim) and how lawyers use them, including legislative history and amendment tracking.
- The uniform citation rules (כללי האזכור האחיד במשפט) and footnote practice.
- Academic legal writing: forming a research question, a literature review, outlines and a full 15–25 page research paper. This is the main graded output of the university course.
- Comparative and foreign-law research: US, UK and EU databases, and English-language legal writing.
- Plagiarism and research ethics.
- Library instruction. TAU, for example, requires a one-time library-databases session before the seminar.
- Close case-briefing drills on many rulings, with tutor feedback on drafts.

## What would make it a whole university course

Topics to add:
- A full research paper: choosing a question, building an outline and bibliography, drafting with feedback, and revising.
- Citation according to the uniform citation rules.
- Using Nevo or Takdin through a university or public library terminal.
- 4–6 short writing assignments with feedback, such as a case brief, a statutory analysis, a legal memo and an opinion letter.
- An introduction to comparative research sources.

Core readings and tools:
- כללי האזכור האחיד במשפט, the uniform citation rules agreed by the Israeli law journals.
- The faculty writing guides that law schools publish for first-year students.
- Model memos from ACRI and the Movement for Quality Government. Their petitions and letters are free online.

Free sources:
- Free journals (Mishpatim, Iyunei Mishpat, Mishpat uMimshal, HaMishpat) as models of academic writing.
- The Knesset Research and Information Center papers as models of policy writing.

Rough hours to reach the full course:
- About 40 contact hours.
- About 60–80 hours of writing a research paper.
- About 20 hours of shorter assignments.
- Total: roughly 120–140 hours, against about 10 hours in this module.
