# Module 17 — Documenting Human Rights Violations: university equivalent

## University courses

There is no direct university course for this module. No Israeli LL.B. has a lecture course on human-rights documentation, and most LL.M. programmes teach it only as practical training. The nearest equivalents are listed below.

- **Human rights clinics (קליניקות לזכויות אדם).** These are the closest match inside an Israeli LL.B. Students take testimonies, prepare evidence and draft reports under supervision.
  - Tel Aviv University: *הקליניקה לזכויות אדם* (Human Rights Clinic, 1411-5240-01, 8 ש"ס of practical work, annual).
  - Hebrew University: *זכויות אדם והחברה הישראלית* (62340, 4 credits, annual).
  - Clinics are electives. Bar-Ilan requires one clinic of 5 ש"ס in year 3, but the student chooses which.
- **Evidence — דיני ראיות.** This is the doctrinal background for lesson 17.4: chain of custody, authenticity and admissibility. Tel Aviv: 4 ש"ס, years 3–4. Bar-Ilan: mandatory in year 3, 3 ש"ס.
- **LL.M. practical tracks.** In spring, Geneva Academy LL.M. students complete a "professionalising activity" worth 6 ECTS. One option is the *Geneva IHL Lab*, which involves practical IHL work and open-source research. Another is an internship with OHCHR, the ICRC or a similar body.
- **Professional training (non-degree).** This is the real home of the subject. Examples:
  - UC Berkeley Human Rights Center: its Professional Training Program is built on the *Berkeley Protocol on Digital Open Source Investigations*, published jointly with OHCHR in 2020. The Center also runs a student Investigations Lab.
  - OHCHR monitoring and fact-finding training.
  - WITNESS video-as-evidence training.

## What this module covers

- The principles of documentation: do no harm, accuracy, neutrality and purpose.
- Interviews and informed consent, including how to work with traumatised witnesses at a basic level.
- Open-source and digital evidence under the Berkeley Protocol.
- Preserving and archiving evidence: metadata, hashing, chain of custody, and backups.
- Digital security for activists, using EFF Surveillance Self-Defense.

## What we left out

- Investigative interviewing method in depth: the PEACE model, interviewing children, and interviewing survivors of sexual violence.
- Forensic documentation: medical documentation of torture under the Istanbul Protocol, and forensic pathology and anthropology.
- Advanced open-source techniques: geolocation, chronolocation, satellite imagery analysis, and verification of manipulated media.
- Standards of proof used by fact-finding bodies, and how a commission of inquiry runs.
- Data protection and the legal risks of holding sensitive data, including the Israeli Privacy Protection Law, in depth.
- Witness protection, the psychosocial support and vicarious trauma of investigators, and the security of field teams.
- How documentation is used in litigation, at the ICC and before UN bodies, in detail.

## What would make it a whole university course

- **Topics to add.** The items above, plus a supervised field project. That project is what a clinic year provides.
- **Core readings.**
  - Sam Dubberley, Alexa Koenig and Daragh Murray (eds.), *Digital Witness: Using Open Source Information for Human Rights Investigation, Documentation, and Accountability* (Oxford UP, 2020).
  - The *Berkeley Protocol* (OHCHR and UC Berkeley Human Rights Center, free).
  - OHCHR, *Manual on Human Rights Monitoring* (free).
  - The *Istanbul Protocol* (OHCHR, revised 2022, free).
- **Free sources.**
  - WITNESS, *Video as Evidence Field Guide*.
  - EFF, Surveillance Self-Defense.
  - Berkeley HRC and OHCHR publication pages.
- **Rough hours.**
  - A clinic year is 5–8 ש"ס of practical work, about 200–300 hours including fieldwork.
  - A professional OSINT or monitoring training is about 30–80 hours.
  - This module is roughly 12–15 hours.
