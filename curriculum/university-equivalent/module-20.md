# Module 20 — Capstone Campaign: university equivalent

## University courses

There is no direct university course for this module. No law degree has a "campaign" course. The nearest equivalents are the research seminar and the clinic's year-end project.

- **Seminar paper — סמינריון.** This is the formal capstone of an Israeli LL.B.
  - Tel Aviv University: every student must take a proseminar in year 2. This is a course in academic writing and a condition for the seminar. In year 3 the student takes a seminar of 4 ש"ס and submits an independent seminar paper (עבודה סמינריונית).
  - Bar-Ilan University: two seminars are required, 8 ש"ס in total, in year 3.
  - Seminar topics relevant to this course do exist. Tel Aviv's 2025–26 list includes *משפט ודמוקרטיה*, *בין אפליה לשוויון במשפט* and *משבר האקלים במשפט*.
- **Clinic year-end project.** Many clinics end with a policy paper, a legislative proposal or a strategic litigation file. This is closer in spirit to a campaign than a seminar paper is. At Tel Aviv, clinics are 8 ש"ס of practical work across the year.
- **International equivalent.** The Geneva Academy LL.M. ends with a *final paper* (6 ECTS) written in the summer. Many human-rights LL.M. programmes also offer clinic-based or policy capstones.

The fit is honest but partial. A seminar is graded on research and argument. This capstone is judged on a real-world strategy, carried out and reviewed.

## What this module covers

- Choosing a cause and stating a theory of change.
- A legal strategy map: which tools, forums and bodies, and in what order.
- An execution log of real actions taken.
- Media and public communication within the law, building on Module 8.
- A retrospective and a hand-over to others.

## What we left out

- Academic research method: a literature review, engagement with scholarship, original legal argument, and full citation practice (Israeli uniform citation rules, כללי האזכור האחיד).
- A long-form written thesis of 8,000–15,000 words, assessed by a faculty member.
- The theory of legal mobilisation and cause lawyering, which a seminar on law and social change would cover.
- Strategic litigation as practised by clinics, under supervision: selecting a test case, coalition work with NGOs, and the ethics of representing a cause versus a client.
- An empirical evaluation of impact.

## What would make it a whole university course

- **To match a seminar.** Add a research paper of about 8,000–15,000 words on the campaign's legal question. It should engage with scholarship, be properly cited, and be reviewed by a qualified reader. Writing the year-2 proseminar first, or Module 2 of this course plus extra writing practice, is the preparation.
- **To match a clinic project.** Carry out the campaign inside, or alongside, a clinic or NGO with a supervising lawyer.
- **Core readings** for a law-and-social-change seminar. These are standard works.
  - Stuart A. Scheingold, *The Politics of Rights* (Yale UP, 1974; 2nd ed. University of Michigan Press, 2004).
  - Michael W. McCann, *Rights at Work: Pay Equity Reform and the Politics of Legal Mobilization* (University of Chicago Press, 1994).
  - Austin Sarat and Stuart Scheingold (eds.), *Cause Lawyering: Political Commitments and Professional Responsibilities* (Oxford UP, 1998).
- **Free sources.**
  - Israeli NGO annual reports and strategic-litigation write-ups.
  - Knesset Research Center papers.
  - The faculty's guidance on writing seminar papers.
- **Rough hours.**
  - A 4 ש"ס seminar is about 52 class hours, plus about 150–200 hours of research and writing, about 200–250 hours in all.
  - The Geneva final paper is about 150–180 hours.
  - This capstone is open-ended; plan on about 80–150 hours.
