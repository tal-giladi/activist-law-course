# Module 6 — Protest, Expression and Police: university equivalent

## University courses

No Israeli LL.B. has a mandatory "protest law" course. The material is spread over:

- **First-year constitutional law (משפט חוקתי / דיני חוקה).** The freedom-of-expression and assembly units of the mandatory course: at TAU, course 1411-9102 (4 ש"ס); at HUJI, course 62110 (6 נ"ז).
- **Advanced constitutional electives.** At TAU, חוקתי – סוגיות מתקדמות, course 1411-9202, year 2, 4 ש"ס.
- **Freedom-of-expression seminars, years 3–4.** Example: HUJI חופש הביטוי (Freedom of Expression), seminar 62727, 4 נ"ז. Its first half covers the theory of free expression and its legal protection, with comparative law.
- **Law and protest electives.** Example: HUJI משפט, מחאה ושינוי חברתי (Law, Protest and Social Change), course 62152. It runs from the Black Panthers to the 2011 social protests.
- **Police powers.** These sit in criminal procedure (דיון פלילי), year 3.

## What this module covers

- Freedom of expression and assembly in Israeli law: Kol Ha'am, and Levi v. Southern District Police Commander (HCJ 153/83) (lesson 06.1).
- Permits under the Police Ordinance ss. 83–85, and when one is needed (06.2).
- At the protest: police powers, dispersal, and filming police (06.3).
- Complaints about police conduct to the Police Investigations Department (מח"ש) and the police complaints unit (06.4).
- Social media, signs, and the limits set by the incitement offences (06.5).

## What we left out

- **Theory of free speech:**
  - the truth, democracy and autonomy rationales;
  - the "near certainty" test and its later versions;
  - content-based versus content-neutral regulation.
- **Comparative law:** the US First Amendment (Brandenburg, public forum doctrine) and European Court of Human Rights case law on assembly.
- **Specific speech doctrines:**
  - Hate speech and racism offences (Penal Law s. 144A ff.) as doctrine, not only as a risk.
  - Terror-support and incitement-to-terror offences (Counter-Terrorism Law 2016) in depth.
- **Other branches of expression law:**
  - media law, broadcasting regulation and press freedom;
  - commercial speech;
  - expression in the workplace and by public servants;
  - artistic expression and censorship (film and theatre review, military censorship);
  - academic freedom and expression on campus;
  - the boycott law (Prevention of Damage to the State of Israel through Boycott Law 2011) and its HCJ review.
- **Police law:** the Police Ordinance in full, police discipline, and the structural oversight of policing.

## What would make it a whole university course

The nearest whole course is a freedom-of-expression elective or seminar. To reach it:

Topics to add:
- Free-speech theory and the Israeli tests.
- 15–20 leading rulings on expression and assembly.
- Hate speech and incitement doctrine.
- Censorship and media.
- A comparative unit on the US and the ECHR.
- For a seminar, a 25–30 page paper.

Core readings (real works):
- Aharon Barak, כבוד האדם: הזכות החוקתית ובנותיה (2014), on dignity and its daughter rights, including expression.
- Amnon Rubinstein and Barak Medina, המשפט החוקתי של מדינת ישראל, the freedom of expression chapters.
- Mordechai Kremnitzer and Khalid Ghanayim, הסתה, לא המרדה, an Israel Democracy Institute study on the incitement offences.

Free sources:
- Versa (Kol Ha'am, Levi, and other expression cases in English).
- ACRI's guides on the right to demonstrate.
- Israel Democracy Institute papers on protest and policing.
- The "Right to Protest" course on Amnesty Academy (free account).

Rough hours to reach the full course:
- About 26–52 contact hours (a 2–4 ש"ס elective).
- About 60–80 reading hours.
- Either about 30 hours of exam prep, or about 60 hours for a seminar paper.
- Total: roughly 120–180 hours, against about 10 hours in this module.
