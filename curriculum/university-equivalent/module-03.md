# Module 3 — Constitutional Law and Basic Laws: university equivalent

## University courses

- **Tel Aviv University: משפט חוקתי (Constitutional Law), course 1411-9102.** Year 1, mandatory. 4 ש"ס (about 52 contact hours).
- **Tel Aviv University: חוקתי – סוגיות מתקדמות (Constitutional Law – Advanced Issues), course 1411-9202.** Year 2, 4 ש"ס. Year-2 students must take one advanced course, and this is one of the options.
- **Hebrew University: דיני חוקה (Constitutional Law), course 62110.** Year 1, semester A, 6 נ"ז. It has three parts: foundations (including the history of the constitutional structure), the Basic Laws (Human Dignity and Liberty; Freedom of Occupation), and institutional constitutional law.
- Constitutional law is a first-year mandatory course at every Israeli faculty.

## What this module covers

- Israel's constitution built from Basic Laws: the Harari decision and Bank Mizrahi (lesson 03.1).
- Basic Law: Human Dignity and Liberty and the limitation clause (03.2).
- Proportionality step by step: proper purpose, rational connection, necessity and proportionality stricto sensu (03.3).
- Derived rights: equality, expression, religion and protest (03.4).
- Judicial review and the 2023–2026 constitutional dispute, including HCJ 5658/23, with all sides presented (03.5).

## What we left out

- Institutional constitutional law:
  - the Knesset (elections, immunity, the party system);
  - the government (formation, no-confidence, emergency powers);
  - the President;
  - the State Comptroller.
- Entrenchment, the "constituent authority" theory, and Knesset power to amend Basic Laws (the unconstitutional constitutional amendment doctrine), in depth.
- Basic Law: Freedom of Occupation and its rulings on economic regulation.
- Basic Law: Israel – the Nation State of the Jewish People and HCJ 5555/18, in depth.
- The validity of laws clause, the override clause debate and comparative models (Canada s. 33).
- Social rights: education, health and minimal dignified existence (e.g. the Commitment to Peace and Social Justice case).
- Remedies for unconstitutional laws: suspended invalidity, reading in and severance.
- Constitutional theory: counter-majoritarian objections and dialogue theories.
- Comparative constitutional law: Germany, the US and Canada.
- Rights in detail: property, privacy, movement, and freedom of and from religion.

## What would make it a whole university course

Topics to add:
- An institutions unit covering the Knesset, the government, the President, elections and the State Comptroller.
- A full unit on each major right, with 3–5 leading rulings each.
- The constituent-power debate and remedies.
- 6–8 weeks of comparative and theoretical reading.

Core readings (real, standard works):
- Amnon Rubinstein and Barak Medina, המשפט החוקתי של מדינת ישראל (6th ed., 2005), the standard multi-volume textbook.
- Aharon Barak, מידתיות במשפט (2010); in English, Proportionality: Constitutional Rights and their Limitations (CUP, 2012).
- Aharon Barak, כבוד האדם: הזכות החוקתית ובנותיה (2014).
- Aharon Barak, פרשנות במשפט, vol. 3: פרשנות חוקתית (1994).
- Suzie Navot, The Constitution of Israel: A Contextual Analysis (Hart, 2014). In English and accessible.

Free sources:
- Versa, for leading rulings in English.
- Knesset English translations of the Basic Laws.
- Israel Democracy Institute policy papers on the judicial-review dispute.
- Mishpatim and Iyunei Mishpat articles.

Rough hours to reach the full course:
- About 52 lecture hours (4 ש"ס).
- About 100 reading hours (leading rulings are long).
- About 40 hours of exam prep.
- Total: roughly 190 hours, against about 10–12 hours in this module.
