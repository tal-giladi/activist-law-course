# Module 11 — Civil Litigation on Your Own: university equivalent

## University courses

- **Civil Procedure — סדר דין אזרחי / דיון אזרחי.** The main match.
  - Tel Aviv University: first-year mandatory **פרוצדורה** (Procedure, 1411-9110-01, 4 ש"ס) introduces procedure generally. **דיון אזרחי** (Civil Procedure, 1411-9228-01, 4 ש"ס) is one of the second-year "core courses" (קורסי יסוד), of which a student must take at least five from a list of about ten. In practice almost everyone takes it, because it is needed for the bar exam.
  - Bar-Ilan University: **סדר דין אזרחי** (99-206, 4 ש"ס) is a mandatory third-year course.
- **Evidence — דיני ראיות.** Lesson 11.5 touches it. Tel Aviv lists it among the year 3–4 basic courses (4 ש"ס). At Bar-Ilan it is mandatory in year 3 (99-309, 3 ש"ס).
- Total size of the full equivalent: about 8 ש"ס, that is two semester courses of roughly 52–56 class hours each.

## What this module covers

- The decision to litigate: cost, risk, fee-shifting (הוצאות), and alternatives such as a demand letter, mediation or a complaint.
- Small claims court (בית משפט לתביעות קטנות): jurisdiction ceiling, filing, the hearing, and the limits on lawyers.
- Net HaMishpat (נט המשפט) for unrepresented filers: e-filing, deadlines and service.
- The Civil Procedure Regulations 2018 (תקנות סדר הדין האזרחי, התשע"ט-2018) at the level a self-represented litigant needs: the opening principles (עקרונות יסוד), the statement of claim, the defence, pre-trial, and the duty to try to settle.
- Practical evidence: affidavits, documents, witnesses, and how a hearing day runs.
- Court-fee exemptions (פטור מאגרה) and the Legal Aid Law (חוק הסיוע המשפטי, התשל"ב-1972).

## What we left out

- Jurisdiction theory: subject-matter and territorial jurisdiction (סמכות עניינית / מקומית) in depth, international jurisdiction, and service abroad.
- Joinder of parties and causes of action, third-party notices (הודעה לצד שלישי), and counterclaims beyond the basics.
- Interim relief: temporary injunctions, attachments (עיקולים), Anton Piller-type orders, and security for costs.
- Discovery and disclosure (גילוי ועיון במסמכים, שאלונים) as a doctrinal subject, including privileges.
- Summary procedure (סדר דין מקוצר) beyond a mention, and procedure in magistrates' courts versus district courts.
- Res judicata (מעשה בית דין: השתק עילה / השתק פלוגתא), lis pendens, and limitation (התיישנות).
- Appeals and leave to appeal (ערעור / רשות ערעור), and the doctrine of not interfering with findings of fact.
- Execution of judgments (הוצאה לפועל) and class actions (תובענות ייצוגיות).
- The full law of evidence: admissibility, hearsay, the best-evidence rule, presumptions, burdens of proof, privileges and expert evidence.
- Comparative and theoretical procedure: adversarial versus inquisitorial models and access to justice as theory.

## What would make it a whole university course

- **Topics to add.** Everything in the list above, taught doctrinally through Supreme Court case law on the 2018 Regulations.
- **Core readings.** Use the latest edition of each.
  - Uri Goren, *סוגיות בסדר דין אזרחי*. It is the standard Israeli civil-procedure text; use an edition updated for the 2018 Regulations.
  - Shlomo Levin, *יסודות סדר הדין האזרחי*. Check that the edition you use is current.
  - Yaakov Kedmi, *על הראיות*, for the evidence part.
- **Free sources.**
  - The Regulations and laws on Wikisource (ויקיטקסט) and the Knesset legislation database.
  - Supreme Court rulings through the Judiciary search.
  - Kol Zchut (כל זכות) guides on small claims and fee exemptions.
  - The Judiciary's own guides for unrepresented litigants.
- **Rough hours.**
  - Civil procedure: about 56 class hours, plus about 110 hours of reading and case analysis, plus about 30 hours of exam preparation, about 200 hours in all.
  - Evidence: about another 150 hours.
  - Total: about 350 hours, against roughly 15–20 hours in this module.
