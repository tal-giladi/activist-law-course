# Module 1 — How Israeli Law Works: university equivalent

## University courses

- **Tel Aviv University: משפט ישראלי (Israeli Law), course 1411-9103.** Year 1, mandatory. 4 ש"ס, a full lecture course (about 52 contact hours).
- **Tel Aviv University: חקיקה ורגולציה (Legislation and Regulation), course 1411-9107.** Year 1, mandatory. 4 ש"ס. Module 1 overlaps only with its part on the hierarchy of norms.
- **Hebrew University: מבוא למשפט ישראלי (Introduction to Israeli Law), course 62962.** Year 1, semester A. 2 נ"ז. Its topics are the sources of Israeli law, the court system, the judicial process, and judges and lawyers in Israel.
- At other faculties, the same material sits in a first-year "introduction to law" course (מבוא למשפט / מבוא למשפט הישראלי), which is always mandatory.

## What this module covers

- The sources of Israeli law: the Ottoman and Mandate layers, s. 11 of the Law and Administration Ordinance, Knesset statutes, case law, and the Foundations of Law Act 1980 (lesson 01.2).
- The hierarchy of norms: Basic Laws, statutes, regulations, AG guidelines and procedures (01.3).
- The map of courts and tribunals: Magistrates, District, Supreme Court/HCJ, Administrative Courts, Labour Courts and religious courts (01.4).
- Reading a statute, including the Interpretation Law 1981, definitions and legal Hebrew (01.5).
- The anatomy of a ruling, binding precedent under s. 20 of Basic Law: The Judiciary, and ratio versus obiter (01.6).
- The limits of self-representation and the Bar Association Law monopoly (01.1). This is practical material and not part of the university course.

## What we left out

- Legal history in depth: the Mejelle, Mandate-era English law and how it was absorbed, and the 1980 break with English law.
- Theories of interpretation: purposive interpretation (Barak), textualism and the debate around it.
- The legal profession as an institution: the Bar, ethics, legal education, and the sociology of judges.
- Jewish law (משפט עברי) and Muslim law as sources. These are taught as separate mandatory courses (at TAU, מבוא למשפט עברי / מבוא למשפט מוסלמי).
- Legislative process theory, delegated legislation and the review of regulations. This is the core of TAU's חקיקה ורגולציה.
- The labour, family and religious courts in depth, and ADR (arbitration and mediation).
- Comparative perspective: common law versus civil law, and where Israel sits between them.
- The exam: written problem questions under time pressure.

## What would make it a whole university course

Topics to add:
- A historical unit on the Ottoman, Mandate and early-state layers of law, with the 1980 Foundations of Law Act debate.
- Statutory interpretation as a full unit: purposive interpretation, its critics, and 6–8 leading cases.
- Delegated legislation, the principle that primary arrangements belong in statute (הסדרים ראשוניים), and the review of regulations.
- The legal profession, legal ethics basics, and ADR.
- Law and society in Israel: Jewish/democratic tensions and religious jurisdiction in personal status.

Core readings (real, standard works):
- Aharon Barak, פרשנות במשפט, vol. 1 (general theory of interpretation), and his פרשנות תכליתית במשפט (2003).
- Aharon Barak, שופט בחברה דמוקרטית (2004).
- Menachem Mautner, משפט ותרבות בישראל בפתח המאה העשרים ואחת (2008).
- Assaf Likhovski, Law and Identity in Mandate Palestine (2006), for the Mandate layer. Likhovski teaches TAU's משפט ישראלי.

Free sources:
- Free Israeli law journals: Mishpatim (HUJI), Iyunei Mishpat (TAU), Mishpat uMimshal (Haifa).
- The Knesset legislation database and Hebrew Wikisource, for statutes.
- Versa (Cardozo), for English translations of leading rulings.

Rough hours to reach the full course:
- About 50 lecture hours (the equivalent of a 4 ש"ס course).
- About 80–100 reading hours.
- About 30 hours of exam prep, practising problem questions.
- Total: roughly 160–180 hours, against about 10 hours in this module.
