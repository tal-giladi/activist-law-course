# Module 13 — International Law Foundations: university equivalent

## University courses

- **Public International Law — משפט בינלאומי פומבי.** This is the direct match. Every Israeli law faculty teaches it.
  - Tel Aviv University: one of the second-year core courses (1411-9223-01, 4 ש"ס). A student must take at least five from a list of about ten. *Advanced Issues in Public International Law* (סוגיות מתקדמות במשפט בינלאומי פומבי) follows in years 3–4.
  - Bar-Ilan University: a second-year mandatory course (99-107, 4 ש"ס). A student may swap one of four year-2 courses for electives, and this is one of them.
- **Practical companion.** Tel Aviv offers *International Law in Israeli Courts – Legal Writing in English* (המשפט הבינלאומי בבתי-משפט ישראליים – כתיבה משפטית באנגלית, 1411-7857-01, 2 ש"ס of practical work). It matches lesson 13.5 almost exactly.
- **International equivalent.** In LL.M. programmes this is the foundation course. The Geneva Academy LL.M. in International Humanitarian Law and Human Rights opens with *Public International Law* (6 ECTS core).

## What this module covers

- What international law is, its sources, and who its subjects are (Statute of the ICJ, Art. 38).
- Treaties: making them, reservations, interpretation and termination, at the level of the Vienna Convention on the Law of Treaties.
- Customary law: state practice and opinio juris, using the ILC 2018 conclusions.
- The International Court of Justice: contentious cases, advisory opinions, and jurisdiction by consent.
- International law inside Israeli courts:
  - custom applies directly;
  - treaties need incorporation;
  - the presumption of conformity.

## What we left out

- States and recognition, statehood criteria, and self-determination as doctrine.
- Territory, boundaries, and the law of the sea (only touched in Module 15).
- Jurisdiction (prescriptive and enforcement) and immunities of states and officials. Universal jurisdiction is in Module 16.
- State responsibility in full: attribution, countermeasures, and the ILC Articles on State Responsibility.
- Use of force and the UN Charter (Arts. 2(4) and 51), collective security, and the Security Council.
- International organisations law, diplomatic and consular law, and nationality.
- International dispute settlement beyond the ICJ: arbitration and investment tribunals.
- International economic and environmental law, and the theory and critical approaches (TWAIL, feminist approaches).

## What would make it a whole university course

- **Topics to add.** All of the above, with the major ICJ cases read in full: *Nicaragua*, *North Sea Continental Shelf*, *Barcelona Traction*, *Reparation for Injuries*, and *Jurisdictional Immunities*.
- **Core readings.**
  - Malcolm N. Shaw, *International Law* (Cambridge UP, latest edition).
  - James Crawford, *Brownlie's Principles of Public International Law* (Oxford UP, 9th ed. 2019).
  - Antonio Cassese, *International Law* (Oxford UP, 2nd ed. 2005).
- **Free sources.**
  - UN Audiovisual Library of International Law lectures (legal.un.org/avl).
  - ICJ judgments and advisory opinions (icj-cij.org).
  - ILC texts and commentaries (legal.un.org/ilc).
  - Israel's Ministry of Foreign Affairs page on Israel and international law, for the Israeli positions.
- **Rough hours.** The core course is about 56 class hours, plus about 120 hours of reading, plus about 30 hours of exam preparation, about 200 hours in all. With the advanced course and the Israeli-courts workshop it comes to about 350 hours, against roughly 12–15 hours in this module.
