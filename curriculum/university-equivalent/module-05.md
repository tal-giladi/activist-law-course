# Module 5 — Acting Against the Authorities: university equivalent

## University courses

No single LL.B. course matches this module. It combines several:

- **The procedural half of administrative law (משפט מנהלי / דיני מנהל ציבורי).** At TAU this is course 1411-9224, year 2, 4 ש"ס. At HUJI it is 62819, 6 נ"ז. HUJI's administrative-law syllabus lists "administrative adjudication, including the HCJ and threshold grounds" (השיפוט המינהלי לרבות בג"ץ ועילות הסף) as a core unit.
- **Freedom of information law.** It is usually taught as a unit inside administrative law or in public-law electives and seminars, not as its own course.
- **Clinical courses.** Example: TAU's הקליניקה לזכויות אדם (Human Rights Clinic), a year 3–4 elective listed as 8 hours of practical work (עבודה מעשית). In clinics, students draft real FOI requests, pre-petition letters and petitions under supervision.
- **TAU פרוצדורה (Procedure), course 1411-9110.** Year 1, mandatory, 4 ש"ס. It gives the general procedural background.

## What this module covers

- Choosing the right tool: a decision tree from FOI to complaint to petition (lesson 05.1).
- The Freedom of Information Law 1998: the request, the exemptions in ss. 8–9, fees and deadlines (05.2–05.3).
- The FOI appeal path to the Administrative Court under the First Schedule of the Administrative Courts Law 2000 (05.3).
- The Public Complaints Commissioner and the State Comptroller (05.4).
- The pre-petition letter, which exhausts remedies (05.5).
- Administrative petitions and HCJ petitions filed without a lawyer, step by step (05.6–05.7).

## What we left out

- The jurisdiction split between the HCJ and the Administrative Courts, in depth, and its case law.
- The theory of threshold doctrines:
  - delay (שיהוי);
  - lack of clean hands;
  - alternative remedy;
  - ripeness;
  - theoretical petitions.
- Remedies and interim relief in administrative adjudication: interim orders, conditional orders, and declaratory relief.
- Costs, including public-interest costs, and the risk of paying them.
- Evidence in petitions: affidavits, the status of factual disputes, and cross-examination.
- The Ombudsman's theory and comparative models, and the State Comptroller's constitutional status.
- Freedom of information as a constitutional right, and comparative FOI law (US FOIA, UK FOIA 2000).
- Class actions against authorities under the Class Actions Law 2006, Second Schedule item 11.
- Tort claims against the state.

## What would make it a whole university course

The nearest whole course is administrative procedure plus a clinic. To reach it:

Topics to add:
- A full unit on threshold doctrines, with leading rulings.
- Interim relief and costs.
- Public-interest litigation strategy.
- Class actions against authorities.
- A supervised clinic or volunteer placement to file real documents, as in a university clinic.

Core readings (real works):
- Zeev Segal, הזכות לדעת באור חוק חופש המידע (2000), the standard work on FOI.
- Itzhak Zamir, הסמכות המינהלית. The later volumes cover judicial review and its procedure.
- Daphne Barak-Erez, משפט מינהלי. Its procedural volume covers administrative adjudication.
- The High Court of Justice Procedure Regulations 1984 and the Administrative Courts (Procedure) Regulations 2000.

Free sources:
- The Government FOI Unit site on gov.il.
- Guides from the Movement for Freedom of Information (התנועה לחופש המידע).
- Published petitions from ACRI, the Movement for Quality Government and HaMoked.

Rough hours to reach the full course:
- About 40 hours of extra study for the procedural doctrine.
- About 100–200 hours of a supervised clinic or placement.
- Total: roughly 150–250 hours, against about 12–15 hours in this module.
