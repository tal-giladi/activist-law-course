# Module 10 — Planning, Land and Environment: university equivalent

## University courses

- **Planning and Building Law (דיני התכנון והבנייה).** An elective in years 3–4.
  - Tel Aviv University: course 1411-9350, an advanced course of 2 ש"ס.
  - Hebrew University: course 62842. It covers constitutional issues in planning, compensation for planning harm, and the right to object to a plan and to initiate one. It assigns Karmon and Alterman's 2011 study of the right to object.
- **Environmental Law (דיני סביבה / דיני איכות הסביבה).** An elective in years 3–4.
  - Tel Aviv University: courses 1411-9314 and 1411-9332, offered in 2 ש"ס and 4 ש"ס versions.
  - Hebrew University: course 62409, which covers local and global aspects.
- **Property Law (דיני קניין).** The background course. At TAU it is a year-2 core course, 4 ש"ס.
- **Local Government Law (משפט השלטון המקומי).** Related. At TAU it is course 1411-9312, a year 3–4 core course of 4 ש"ס.
- **Clinics.** Example: TAU's environmental justice clinic (הקליניקה לצדק סביבתי).

## What this module covers

- The planning system map: the national, district and local committees and the plan hierarchy (lesson 10.1).
- Reading a plan on Mavat (10.2).
- Writing an objection under s. 100 of the Planning and Building Law 1965 (10.3).
- The hearing and the appeal: the appeals committee and the administrative petition (10.4).
- Environmental laws and how to complain (10.5).

## What we left out

**Planning law doctrine:**
- the types of plans and their legal force;
- National Infrastructure Committee (ות"ל) and fast-track regimes, in depth;
- building permits, deviations and exemptions;
- planning enforcement: the 2017 enforcement reform (Amendment 116), demolition orders and administrative fines.

**Money and land:**
- compensation for planning harm (s. 197) and betterment levy (היטל השבחה);
- expropriation for public needs, including the Karsik line and return of expropriated land;
- Israel Land Authority policy and land allocation.

**Environmental law:**
- the Clean Air Law 2008, the Prevention of Hazards Law, water and coastal protection (Coastal Environment Protection Law 2004), and environmental class actions;
- environmental justice theory, climate law, and international environmental law.

**Other:**
- Land Law 1969 basics: ownership, leases and the Land Registry.
- The planning reform debates and comparative planning law.

## What would make it a whole university course

The nearest whole course is a planning-law course plus an environmental-law course, built on property law.

Topics to add:
- Full planning doctrine: plans, permits and enforcement.
- Compensation and betterment levy.
- Expropriation.
- Land allocation.
- Environmental statutes and enforcement tools.
- Environmental class actions.
- Climate law basics.

Core readings (real works):
- Karmon and Alterman, "The Voice Is Heard?" (Hebrew study on the right to object before planning institutions, Center for Urban and Regional Studies, 2011). It is assigned in HUJI 62842.
- Alon Tal, Pollution in a Promised Land: An Environmental History of Israel (2002).
- The Planning and Building Law 1965 and the Land Law 1969, read in full. Practitioners rely on the standard multi-volume planning-law treatises.

Free sources:
- Mavat (the national planning portal) and the Planning Administration site.
- The Ministry of Environmental Protection.
- Bimkom guides on objections.
- Adam Teva V'Din publications.
- Mishpat uMimshal and Iyunei Mishpat articles on planning.

Rough hours to reach the full courses:
- **Planning law (2 ש"ס):** about 26 lecture hours, 50 reading hours and 20 hours of exam prep.
- **Environmental law (4 ש"ס):** about 52 lecture hours, 80 reading hours and 30 hours of exam prep.
- Total: roughly 260 hours, against about 10 hours in this module.
