# Module 7 — Criminal Law and Procedure for Activists: university equivalent

## University courses

- **Criminal law (דיני עונשין), the general part.** Year 1, mandatory everywhere.
  - Tel Aviv University: course 1411-9109, 4 ש"ס.
  - Hebrew University: courses 62114 and 62201.
- **Criminal procedure (דיון פלילי / סדר דין פלילי).** Usually year 3.
  - Tel Aviv University: course 1411-9341, 4 ש"ס, one of the year 3–4 core courses (קורסי יסוד; students need at least 16 ש"ס from that list).
  - Hebrew University: course 62306. Its units include the criminal investigation, the right to silence and to counsel, search and seizure, detention for investigation and remand ("מעצר ימים"), closing files, and detention until the end of proceedings.
- **Evidence (דיני ראיות).** Year 3–4.
  - Tel Aviv University: course 1411-9334, 4 ש"ס.
  - Haifa: a mandatory year-4 course.
- **Clinics.** Example: TAU הקליניקה לזכויות בהליך הפלילי (Clinic for Rights in Criminal Proceedings).

## What this module covers

- The offences activists are most often charged with: disturbing public order, assaulting or obstructing a police officer, trespass, and illegal assembly (lesson 07.1).
- Detention, arrest and rights under the Criminal Procedure (Enforcement Powers – Arrests) Law 1996 (07.2).
- Interrogation: the right to silence, the right to a lawyer, and the Public Defender (07.3).
- Remand hearings and release conditions (07.4).
- After the case: closed files, criminal records and police records under the Criminal Register and Rehabilitation Law 1981 (07.5).

## What we left out

- **The general part of criminal law:**
  - the elements of an offence: the conduct element (actus reus) and the mental element (mens rea);
  - attempt, complicity and incitement as forms of liability;
  - defences: self-defence, necessity, duress and de minimis;
  - punishment theory and sentencing (Amendment 113).
- **The trial itself:**
  - the indictment, the pre-trial hearing (s. 60A), plea bargains and conditional arrangements;
  - trial procedure, cross-examination and appeal;
  - the prosecution's discretion and the Attorney General's role in political cases.
- **Evidence and exclusion:**
  - search and seizure warrants and phone searches, in depth;
  - the exclusionary rule (Issacharov, CrimA 5121/98);
  - the evidence law of confessions (s. 12 of the Evidence Ordinance), privileges and hearsay.
- **Special regimes:**
  - juvenile procedure;
  - administrative detention;
  - security offences and military courts. Module 15 touches on these for the territories.
- **Theory:** criminal-law theory, criminalisation, and over-criminalisation of protest.

## What would make it a whole university course

Two courses together: criminal law (general part) plus criminal procedure.

Topics to add:
- **Criminal law:** a full general-part unit (elements, mental states, attempt, complicity, defences).
- **Procedure:** the full arc from complaint to appeal, sentencing, and search and seizure.
- **Evidence:** the exclusionary rule and confessions.
- **Leading rulings:**
  - Dagani (HCJ 465/75), cited in HUJI's arrest unit;
  - Issacharov;
  - the main remand and release cases.

Core readings (real, standard works):
- Yaakov Kedmi, על סדר הדין בפלילים, the standard practitioner treatise on criminal procedure.
- Yaakov Kedmi, על הראיות.
- S. Z. Feller, יסודות בדיני עונשין, on the general part.
- The Criminal Procedure Law [Consolidated Version] 1982 and the Arrests Law 1996, read in full.

Free sources:
- The Office of the Public Defender site.
- ACRI's "your rights at arrest" guides.
- Kol Zchut pages on criminal records.
- Mishpatim and Iyunei Mishpat articles on remand and the exclusionary rule.

Rough hours to reach the full courses:
- **Criminal law:** about 52 lecture hours, 100 reading hours and 40 hours of exam prep.
- **Criminal procedure:** about 52 lecture hours, 90 reading hours and 40 hours of exam prep.
- Total: roughly 370 hours, against about 10–12 hours in this module.
