# Module 14 — The UN Human Rights System: university equivalent

## University courses

There is no mandatory Israeli LL.B. course on the UN human rights system. The closest matches are electives and clinics, and the standard LL.M. course on International Human Rights Law.

- **Israeli LL.B., elective level.**
  - Hebrew University offers *זכויות אדם והחברה הישראלית* (Human Rights and Israeli Society, course 62340), an annual course worth 4 credits. Its first part covers the universality of rights, the international conventions and the international mechanisms. Its main part covers human rights in Israel.
  - Tel Aviv University runs *הקליניקה לזכויות אדם* (Human Rights Clinic, 1411-5240-01, 8 ש"ס of practical work, annual).
  - In every faculty, public international law (משפט בינלאומי פומבי, year 2, 4 ש"ס) gives the background.
- **International LL.M. level.**
  - The Geneva Academy LL.M. in International Humanitarian Law and Human Rights has a core course, *International Human Rights Law* (6 ECTS).
  - The same programme has an optional course, *The Law and Politics of International Human Rights Monitoring* (3 ECTS). This optional course is the closest academic match to this module.
- Total size of the full equivalent: about 9 ECTS, that is a core IHRL course plus a monitoring course.

## What this module covers

- The core UN treaties Israel ratified, and its reservations and declarations.
- Treaty bodies, the reporting cycle, and how to write a shadow report.
- The Universal Periodic Review: the cycle, stakeholder submissions, and recommendations.
- Special Procedures: who they are and how to send a communication.
- The Human Rights Council and its commissions of inquiry, explained so that the learner knows what they can and cannot do.

## What we left out

- Doctrine of the rights themselves: the content of civil, political, economic, social and cultural rights, and the general comments.
- Theory: universality versus cultural relativism, the foundations of rights, and the critiques.
- Individual complaint procedures (Optional Protocols) and their jurisprudence. Israel has not accepted the individual-complaint procedures, so the module skips them.
- Regional systems: the European Court of Human Rights, the Inter-American system and the African system, often taught comparatively.
- Extraterritorial application of human-rights treaties and their relation to IHL. Module 15 touches this only briefly.
- Derogation in emergencies, limitations analysis, and positive obligations.
- Business and human rights, the UN Guiding Principles, and non-state actors.
- The politics and reform debates of the Human Rights Council, and a sustained analysis of how effective it is.

## What would make it a whole university course

- **Topics to add.** The topics above, taught through treaty-body general comments and views, and leading ECtHR cases for comparison.
- **Core readings.**
  - Olivier De Schutter, *International Human Rights Law: Cases, Materials, Commentary* (Cambridge UP, latest edition).
  - Daniel Moeckli, Sangeeta Shah and Sandesh Sivakumaran (eds.), *International Human Rights Law* (Oxford UP, latest edition).
- **Free sources.**
  - OHCHR, *Working with the United Nations Human Rights Programme: A Handbook for Civil Society*.
  - ISHR Academy modules on the treaty bodies, UPR, Special Procedures and the Council (academy.ishr.ch).
  - The UN Audiovisual Library human-rights lectures.
  - The OHCHR treaty-body database and UHRI.
- **Rough hours.**
  - A 6 ECTS core course is about 150–180 hours: about 40 class hours, about 90 hours of reading, and exam or paper work.
  - The monitoring course adds about 75–90 hours.
  - Total: about 250 hours, against roughly 12–15 hours in this module.
