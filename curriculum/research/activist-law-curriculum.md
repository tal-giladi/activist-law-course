# Self-taught Law Degree — Human Rights & International Affairs track

This curriculum mirrors a typical Israeli LL.B. (3.5 years, about 135 credit hours at TAU or HUJI), squeezed into a part-time self-study plan of about 10–12 hours a week. After it comes a practical stage that replaces the 12-month internship (התמחות). The bar exam is not included.

You will not get a degree or a licence to represent other people. You can represent yourself (in court and before authorities), file requests and complaints, and lead or advise a group.

## Toolkit used throughout

**Israeli laws, free:**
- Hebrew Wikisource ("ספר החוקים הפתוח"), he.wikisource.org.
- Nevo has a free tier for laws.
- English translations of the Basic Laws: main.knesset.gov.il.

**Israeli rulings:**
- Supreme Court search on supreme.court.gov.il.
- Versa (Cardozo), versa.cardozo.yu.edu. Free English translations of leading Israeli Supreme Court rulings.

**Free Israeli law journals.** Most articles are free PDFs:
- Mishpatim (HUJI)
- Iyunei Mishpat (TAU)
- Mishpat uMimshal (Haifa)
- HaMishpat (Reichman / COLMAN)
- Mivzakei Hearot (Bar-Ilan)

**Practical rights guides:**
- Kol Zchut, kolzchut.org.il.
- ACRI guides, acri.org.il.

**Free international courses** (verify the current run on the platform):
- Coursera, Leiden "International Law in Action" series (ICJ, ICC).
- Coursera, Leiden "International Humanitarian Law in Theory and Practice".
- Coursera, Case Western "Introduction to International Criminal Law".
- edX, UCLouvain "International Human Rights" plus the International Law MicroMasters (4 courses).
- edX, Harvard "Justice" (Sandel) and "Contract Law: From Trust to Promise to Contract" (Fried).
- Coursera, Yale "Constitutional Law" (Amar). US law, but the reasoning carries over.
- Amnesty Academy, academy.amnesty.org. Short courses with certificates.
- Inner Temple Library list of 200+ free legal courses and lectures: https://www.innertemplelibrary.org.uk/news/learn-free-law-courses-lectures-online/

**Gap.** Free Hebrew video lectures that teach Israeli doctrine are rare. For Israeli doctrine, you read the statute, the 3–6 leading rulings and 1–2 journal articles for each subject. That is how first-year students actually study for exams anyway.

## Year 1 — Foundations (the Israeli first-year core)

**1.1 Introduction to law and legal methods**
- Covers: the sources of Israeli law (Ottoman and Mandate layers, Knesset statutes, case law, Jewish law), how courts are built, and how to read a ruling.
- Read: Foundations of Law Act 1980. Courts Law 1984.
- Practice: summarise 5 rulings, writing the facts, question, holding and reasoning for each.

**1.2 Legal research and writing**
- Covers: finding a law and its amendments, finding rulings, the structure of a legal argument, citation.
- Practice: write a one-page legal memo every week.

**1.3 Constitutional law**
- Read: Basic Law: Human Dignity and Liberty. Basic Law: Freedom of Occupation. Basic Law: The Judiciary. Basic Law: The Government. Basic Law: Nation-State.
- Rulings:
  - Kol Ha'am (HCJ 73/53), free speech.
  - Bank Mizrahi (CA 6821/93), the constitutional revolution.
  - Ka'adan (HCJ 6698/95), equality.
  - Nation-State Law ruling (HCJ 5555/18).
  - Reasonableness-amendment ruling (HCJ 5658/23, Jan 2024).
- Course: Yale Constitutional Law, for comparison.

**1.4 Contracts**
- Read: Contracts (General Part) Law 1973. Contracts (Remedies) Law 1970.
- Rulings: Apropim (CA 4628/93), on interpreting contracts. Good faith (section 39).
- Course: Harvard "Contract Law" (edX).

**1.5 Torts**
- Read: Civil Wrongs Ordinance (negligence, ss. 35–36). Defamation Law 1965. This one is critical for activists because of SLAPP suits.
- Rulings: Vaknin (CA 145/80), duty of care. Defamation and public-interest defence cases.

**1.6 Criminal law**
- Read: Penal Law 1977, general part. Offences activists meet in practice: incitement, disturbing public order, assaulting a police officer, trespass.
- Rulings: protest-related criminal cases.

**1.7 Legal philosophy and justice**
- Course: Harvard "Justice" (edX). Read Hart and Dworkin in summaries.

**1.8 Jewish law and the Israeli context (optional)**
- How religious law operates in family law and in the religious courts.

## Year 2 — Public law & procedure (the activist's core)

**2.1 Administrative law** — the most important course for activists.
- Covers: authority, discretion, reasonableness, proportionality, the right to a hearing, exhausting remedies, standing.
- Rulings:
  - Dapei Zahav (HCJ 389/80), reasonableness.
  - Ressler (HCJ 910/86), standing and justiciability.
  - Deri-Pinhasi (HCJ 3094/93), appointments.
- Read: Administrative Courts Law 2000. Freedom of Information Law 1998.

**2.2 Civil procedure**
- Read: Civil Procedure Regulations 2018. High Court of Justice Procedure Regulations. Administrative Courts Procedure Regulations.
- Covers: pleadings, deadlines, interim relief, costs.

**2.3 Criminal procedure and police powers**
- Read: Criminal Procedure (Arrest) Law 1996. Police Ordinance ss. 83–85, which cover demonstration licensing. Search powers.
- Practice: your rights during detention and arrest, using ACRI's guide.

**2.4 Evidence**
- Read: Evidence Ordinance.
- Covers: affidavits, admissibility, documenting events. Includes how to film police lawfully.

**2.5 Equality and anti-discrimination law**
- Read: Equal Employment Opportunities Law 1988. Prohibition of Discrimination in Products and Services Law 2000. Equal Rights for Persons with Disabilities Law 1998. Women's Equal Rights Law 1951.

**2.6 Privacy and digital rights**
- Read: Protection of Privacy Law 1981, including Amendment 13 (2025). Surveillance and online speech.

**2.7 Labour law (intro)**
- Covers: the right to organise, the right to strike, the Labour Courts.

**2.8 Property, planning and land**
- Read: Planning and Building Law 1965 (objections, s. 100). Land Law 1969. Israel Land Authority basics.

---

## Year 3 — International law & human rights

**3.1 Public international law**
- Covers: sources, treaties, state responsibility, the ICJ.
- Course: Leiden "International Law in Action: the ICJ" plus UCLouvain MicroMasters.

**3.2 International human rights law**
- Covers: ICCPR, ICESCR, CEDAW, CRC, CAT, CERD (Israel ratified all six). Treaty bodies, UPR, Special Procedures.
- Courses: UCLouvain "International Human Rights" (edX). Global Campus of Human Rights MOOC. ISHR Academy, academy.ishr.ch.

**3.3 International humanitarian law (laws of war) and occupation**
- Covers: Geneva Conventions, Hague Regulations, belligerent occupation.
- Course: Leiden IHL (Coursera). ICRC e-learning, icrc.org, free.
- Israeli rulings:
  - Elon Moreh (HCJ 390/79).
  - Public Committee Against Torture (HCJ 5100/94).
  - Beit Sourik (HCJ 2056/04).
  - Targeted Killings (HCJ 769/02).
- ICJ: Wall Advisory Opinion (2004). Advisory Opinion on the Occupied Palestinian Territory (July 2024).

**3.4 International criminal law**
- Covers: the ICC, genocide, crimes against humanity, complementarity. Israel is not a party to the Rome Statute.
- Course: Case Western ICL (Coursera). Leiden "International Law in Action: the ICC".

**3.5 International law in Israeli courts**
- Covers: how Israeli courts absorb customary law versus treaties (treaties need legislation). Use of international law in petitions.

**3.6 International organisations and diplomacy**
- Covers: the UN system, the EU and its human-rights-defender guidelines, how NGOs lobby internationally.

## Year 4 (half year) — Seminar & specialisation

- **Seminar paper:** 20–30 pages on one real issue, using Israeli and international sources. Aim to publish it as a blog post or a submission to an NGO.
- **Choose 2 electives:**
  - Freedom of expression and protest.
  - Refugee and immigration law (Entry into Israel Law).
  - Rights of persons with disabilities.
  - Environmental law.
  - Women's rights.
  - Minority rights.
  - Corruption and good governance.

## Practical stage (replaces the internship, 6–12 months)

Each module needs a real output, not just reading.

**P1. Court observation.** Hearings are public. Attend 10 hearings: 3 at Magistrates court, 3 at the Administrative Court, 2 at the High Court (בג"ץ), and 2 criminal remand hearings. Write a short report on each.

**P2. Freedom of Information.** File 5 requests. Take at least one through a complaint to the Government FOI Unit.

**P3. Oversight complaints.** File complaints with the Public Complaints Commissioner. Also with the police investigations department (מח"ש) or the Privacy Authority, as relevant.

**P4. Planning objection.** Write one complete objection with an affidavit. Try a real one if a plan affects you.

**P5. Knesset.** Follow one bill through committee. Send a position paper and attend a session.

**P6. Mock filings.**
- Write a full pre-petition letter to an authority. This is the exhaustion-of-remedies step (מיצוי הליכים).
- Write a full administrative petition, using real petitions published by ACRI, HaMoked and the Movement for Quality Government as models.
- Get feedback from a university legal clinic.

**P7. Real self-represented case.** Small claims, an FOI petition, or a consumer claim, filed through Net HaMishpat.

**P8. International.** Write one UN Special Procedures communication. Contribute a section to an NGO shadow report or UPR submission.

**P9. Volunteer placement, 3+ months.** Options: ACRI, the Movement for Freedom of Information, the Movement for Quality Government, Bimkom, Yesh Din, B'Tselem, Kav LaOved, HIAS/ASSAF, or a university clinic. This is the closest thing to a real internship.

**P10. Capstone.** Run one campaign from start to finish: research → FOI → complaint → petition or Knesset → media → international. Document it as a case study.

Step-by-step procedures for P2–P8 are in [activist-law-practical.md](activist-law-practical.md).
