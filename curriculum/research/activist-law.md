# Activist law — learning to act without paid lawyers

Goal: understand Israeli law, human rights law and international law well enough to file, petition, complain and report yourself. Not a law degree.

## Option 1 — Learn the basics (free, self-paced)

- **Kol Zchut (כל זכות)** — kolzchut.org.il. Plain-Hebrew guides to rights and procedures. Best first stop for anything Israeli.
- **ACRI (האגודה לזכויות האזרח)** — acri.org.il. Guides on protest rights, police, privacy, freedom of expression.
- **Open Law Book (ספר החוקים הפתוח)** — he.wikisource.org. Full text of Israeli laws free. Start with Basic Law: Human Dignity and Liberty, Basic Law: Freedom of Occupation, the Freedom of Information Law 1998, Administrative Courts Law 2000.
- **Court rulings** — supreme.court.gov.il has free search of Supreme Court / High Court (בג"ץ) decisions.
- **International human rights (English):**
  - Amnesty Academy — free short online courses with certificates (academy.amnesty.org).
  - edX "International Human Rights" (UCLouvain, Olivier De Schutter) — the strongest single course; currently self-paced/archived, check for a re-run. https://classcentral.com/mooc/1340/edx-international-human-rights
  - Global Campus of Human Rights MOOC "Promoting and Protecting Human Rights: a Global Overview" — free. https://gchumanrights.org/education/e-learning/moocs/promoting-and-protecting-human-rights-a-global-overview/how-to-enrol.html
  - Class Central human-rights listing for current free courses: https://classcentral.com/subject/human-rights
- **Reading outline** — Berkeley syllabus on human rights and civil rights in Israel, useful as a study plan: https://jsp-ls.berkeley.edu/sites/default/files/ls_190.3_fa_21.docx.pdf

## Option 2 — Tools in Israeli law you can use yourself

Individuals (not companies) may represent themselves in Israeli courts.

1. **Freedom of Information requests** — any resident/citizen can ask any public authority for information, no reason needed. If refused or ignored, you can petition the Administrative Court under section 17. Government FOI unit: https://www.gov.il/en/Departments/Units/freedomof_information_maya — the Movement for Freedom of Information (hofesh.org.il) has templates.
2. **Complaints to oversight bodies (free)** — State Comptroller & Ombudsman (נציב תלונות הציבור), Police Investigations Dept (מח"ש), Privacy Protection Authority, Equal Employment Opportunities Commission, Ombudsman for judges.
3. **Planning objections (התנגדויות)** — anyone affected can object to a building/zoning plan at the local/district committee. Very effective for local activism.
4. **Knesset** — anyone can send position papers to committees and ask to attend sessions; you can also ask an MK to file a parliamentary question (שאילתה).
5. **High Court petition (עתירה לבג"ץ)** — individuals can file pro se against a government action. There is a filing fee and strict format rules; standing is relatively broad ("public petitioner"). Realistic only after practicing with 1–4.
6. **Free legal help when you get stuck** — university legal clinics (TAU, HUJI, Haifa, Reichman), the Justice Ministry Legal Aid Administration (סיוע משפטי), ACRI, and NGOs by topic.

## Option 3 — International mechanisms open to individuals and NGOs

Israel has NOT accepted individual complaints under the UN treaties (no ICCPR Optional Protocol), so the routes are reports and communications:

- **UN Special Rapporteurs (Special Procedures)** — anyone can submit a communication about a violation via the OHCHR online form.
- **Treaty body shadow reports** — when Israel is reviewed (ICCPR, ICESCR, CEDAW, CRC, CAT, CERD), civil society can submit parallel reports.
- **Universal Periodic Review (UPR)** — stakeholder submissions before Israel's review at the Human Rights Council.
- **ICC** — anyone can send information to the Prosecutor (Article 15 communication).
- **Advocacy at embassies / EU** — EU guidelines on human rights defenders give access to EU missions.

## Option 4 — Structured learning with a community

- Al-Haq international law orientation (Ramallah, in person, historically limited eligibility): https://hic-net.org/es/al-haq-international-law-course/
- Hebrew University summer course on Israeli law (paid, 60 hours, exam): https://overseas.huji.ac.il/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Tentative-Syllabus_2026Mishpatim_Australiatentative2.pdf
- Volunteer with an NGO (ACRI, Movement for Freedom of Information, Movement for Quality Government, Yesh Din, B'Tselem, Bimkom for planning). Fastest way to learn real procedure.

## Suggested path

1. Month 1: Kol Zchut + read the two Basic Laws + Amnesty Academy course.
2. Month 2: file 2–3 Freedom of Information requests on something you care about.
3. Month 3: edX/Global Campus course; send one ombudsman/comptroller complaint.
4. Then: planning objection or Knesset position paper; consider a UN communication or shadow report with an NGO.
5. Only after that: pro se petition to Administrative Court / High Court.

Could also become a Tal's Academy course ("Activist law for non-lawyers").
