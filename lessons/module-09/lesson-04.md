# 09.4 · Disability Rights and Accessibility Complaints

The Equal Rights for Persons with Disabilities Law gives two separate rights in public life: not to be refused or served worse because of a disability, and to find places and services actually accessible. In this lesson you learn which duty was broken, which tool fits — a Commission complaint, a query, a demand letter or a claim — and how the defences work, and you write an accessibility complaint with evidence.

## Why it matters

Most accessibility problems are solved without a court: a complaint to the Commission leads to an inspection, a warning, an accessibility order or an administrative fine. But many complaints fail because they are vague ("the place is not accessible"), go to the wrong address, or ignore the exemptions the law gives existing buildings and small businesses. A precise complaint with photos and measurements gets a ramp built; a vague one gets a form letter. Disability crosses every community — Haredi neighbourhoods, Arab towns, kibbutzim, settlements — and accessibility campaigns are among the few that unite activists from all sides.

## The law

> מי שעיסוקו במתן שירות ציבורי, בהפעלת מקום ציבורי או בהספקת מוצר, לא יפלה אדם מחמת מוגבלותו בכל אחד מאלה, לפי הענין:

*Gist:* a service provider, operator of a public place or supplier may not, because of disability, refuse access, service or a product, set irrelevant conditions, or give service on worse terms (s. 19ו(א)(1)–(3)); s. 19ו(ד) says this section alone does not require accessibility works. ([s. 19ו](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_שוויון_זכויות_לאנשים_עם_מוגבלות#סעיף_19ו))

> מקום ציבורי יהיה נגיש לאנשים עם מוגבלות, בהתאם להוראות לפי סימן זה או להוראות הנגישות לפי חוק התכנון והבניה, לפי הענין.

*Gist:* a public place must be accessible under the regulations and the planning-law accessibility rules; s. 19יא says the same for a public service, and the regulations set what and by when. ([s. 19ח(א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_שוויון_זכויות_לאנשים_עם_מוגבלות#סעיף_19ח))

> בית משפט רשאי לפסוק בשל עוולה לפי סעיף קטן (א) פיצויים בסכום שלא יעלה על 50,000 שקלים חדשים, בלא הוכחת נזק, בשל עוולה –

*Gist:* up to ₪50,000 without proof of damage, for discrimination under s. 19ו or for breach of the accessibility regulations; the sum is updated once a year from the February 2005 index (s. 19נא(ו)), roughly ₪73,000 by our calculation for 2026 (see [Current Numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)). ([s. 19נא(ב)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_שוויון_זכויות_לאנשים_עם_מוגבלות#סעיף_19נא))

> הוא פנה לנציבות זמן סביר לפני הגשת התביעה בשאילתה;

*Gist:* one of the bars to compensation without proof of damage for an accessibility breach: the defendant itself sent a query to the Commission a reasonable time before the claim. Others: it is complying with an accessibility order or a written undertaking, it was fined or warned, or another claim on the same matter is being carried out. ([s. 19נא(ג)(3)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_שוויון_זכויות_לאנשים_עם_מוגבלות#סעיף_19נא))

> הנציב ישיב לשאילתה בתוך 60 ימים מיום קבלתה.

*Gist:* anyone may ask the Commissioner whether a place or service is covered, whether an exemption or alternative arrangement applies, and whether an accessibility order was issued; the answer is due within 60 days. ([s. 19מד(ג)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_שוויון_זכויות_לאנשים_עם_מוגבלות#סעיף_19מד))

## How it works in practice

### Which duty was broken?

| What happened | Duty | Section |
|---|---|---|
| "We don't serve people in wheelchairs", "you can't come in with the dog", "bring someone who can hear" | Non-discrimination in service | 19ו (and 19ו1 for service animals) |
| Steps with no ramp, no accessible toilet, a website a screen reader cannot use, no hearing loop | Accessibility of a place or service | 19ח, 19יא and the regulations |
| Not hired, no reasonable adjustment at work | Employment | 8 (Labour Court, s. 14) |

The first is like the services law (lesson 09.2), with similar presumptions (s. 19סה). The second depends on the accessibility regulations and their timetables, and on exemptions.

### The defences in accessibility cases

- **Exemptions** for existing places and services (s. 19יג): engineering impossibility confirmed by a licensed accessibility expert; undue burden (type of place, scope, cost, outside funding, an accessible alternative nearby, turnover); harm to historical, archaeological, architectural or natural value; a fundamental change of the place.
- **Gradual timetables** (for example s. 19כ for health services): check whether the deadline for this place has passed.
- **Bars on compensation without proof of damage** (s. 19נא(ג)): an accessibility order being complied with, an undertaking or administrative fine, a previous claim being carried out, or the defendant's own query to the Commission.

This is why a query can be useful *before* you sue: it tells you whether the place is covered, exempt, or under an order.

### The Commission complaint

From the gov.il service page (opened 13 October 2026):

- **Who may complain:** about an employer, a service provider, or anyone required to make adjustments. Free.
- **How:** the online form, email pniotnez@justice.gov.il, fax 02-6462881, or post to P.O.B. 34255, Steiner branch, Jerusalem 9134100. Phone \*6763 (Sunday–Thursday 8:00–16:00).
- **What to attach:** earlier correspondence, documents about the disability, photographs, plans.
- **What happens:** a handler contacts you within 10 business days and updates you every 21 business days. Handling takes up to 53 working days, plus 17 if an accessibility inspector must visit. If an offence or civil wrong is found, the Commission may prosecute or sue; a civil claim in your matter needs your consent.
- **The Commission's tools:** accessibility orders (s. 19מג), investigation (s. 19מה), administrative fines and warnings (ch. ט, since 2022), and claims in its own name or yours (ss. 12, 19נג).

See [Current Numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md) for the response times and the compensation ceiling.

### A precise complaint

A complaint gets action when it lets an inspector find the problem without calling you:

- the exact address, floor and entrance;
- what is missing or wrong, with measurements (step height, door width, slope) and photos with a ruler or tape;
- the date and time you tried to use it, and what happened;
- the business or body responsible (owner or operator: s. 19ח(ג));
- what you ask for: an inspection, an accessibility order, a temporary solution;
- whether you also asked the business directly, and its answer.

## Worked example

**Situation.** Nimrod, 58, a former paratrooper who uses a wheelchair since an injury, lives in Kiryat Shmona. The bank branch in his neighbourhood moved in 2025 to a new building with three steps at the entrance and a bell "for assistance" that nobody answers. Staff tell him to "use the app or go to the branch in the next town".

1. **Duties.** A bank branch is a public place giving a public service. Two issues: accessibility of the entrance (19ח), and possibly worse service (19ו(א)(3)) if he is always sent away.
2. **New or existing?** The branch moved into a new building in 2025, so arguments about gradual timetables for old buildings are weaker; he notes the date of the move.
3. **Evidence.** Photos of the steps with a tape measure (17 cm each), the bell, the sign; notes of two visits with dates and the staff's words; a screenshot of the bank's accessibility statement on its website.
4. **Letter to the bank.** He writes to the branch manager and the bank's accessibility coordinator (רכז נגישות, s. 19מב), asking for a ramp or lift within 30 days and an interim arrangement.
5. **Complaint to the Commission.** After 30 days without a solution he files the complaint online with the photos, the letter and the reply, asking for an inspection and an accessibility order.
6. **Claim?** He keeps the option of a claim with compensation without proof of damage; before filing he would check, by a query, whether the bank has an exemption or an order already (s. 19נא(ג)).

## Do it yourself

### You need

- A real place or service near you that is not accessible, or the fictional case below.
- A tape measure and a phone camera (for a real place; take photos from public areas only — lesson 09.5 and lesson [08.4](../module-08/lesson-04.md)).
- The template [accessibility-complaint.md](../../templates/accessibility-complaint.md).
- 50 minutes.

Fictional case: Huda, who is blind, arrives with her guide dog at a restaurant in Nazareth for her sister's engagement dinner. The manager says, "No animals, it's a health rule," and offers to tie the dog outside. The other guests go in. The restaurant's online menu is a scanned image a screen reader cannot read.

### Steps

1. Split the case into duties: which part is discrimination (19ו, 19ו1) and which is accessibility (19יא)?
2. For each, write the evidence you have and what you still need.
3. Write the likely defence (for example "health rules", or an exemption for the website) and your answer.
4. Choose the tools: a letter to the business, a Commission complaint, a query, a claim. Write the order and why.
5. Fill in the complaint template for one of the two issues, with measurements or screenshots described as attachments.
6. Put the dates in a calendar: the Commission's contact within 10 business days, the 53-working-day mark.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Filing is optional; a dry run is enough. If you file for real: act in your own name only (or with the written consent of the person affected). This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing.

### Template

- [accessibility-complaint.md](../../templates/accessibility-complaint.md) — a Hebrew complaint to the Commission and an optional query (שאילתה) under s. 19מד.

### How you know it worked

- Your complaint names the place precisely, says what is wrong with numbers or screenshots, and asks for specific action.
- You separated refusal of service (19ו) from missing adjustments (19ח, 19יא).
- You anticipated an exemption or a bar to compensation and answered it.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| "The place is not accessible" with no details | Form reply; no inspection | Resubmit with address, measurements, photos | Use the precise-complaint checklist |
| Suing for an accessibility breach without checking for an order or exemption | No compensation without proof of damage (s. 19נא(ג)) | Ask for actual damage or an injunction instead | Send a query first |
| Claiming under the services law | Wrong cause of action | Amend to the disability law | Disability = disability law |
| Photographing customers or staff inside | Privacy exposure (08.4) | Delete faces; photograph the barrier only | Photograph the barrier from public areas |
| Complaining for someone without consent | The Commission may not act; a claim needs consent | Get written consent | Act in your own name or with consent |
| Waiting for the Commission before preserving evidence | Barrier fixed temporarily, then returns | Keep dated photos | Document first, complain second |

## Review

- The disability law forbids refusing or worsening service because of disability (s. 19ו) and requires places and services to be accessible under the regulations (ss. 19ח, 19יא).
- The Commission handles complaints for free, can inspect, issue accessibility orders, impose fines and sue with your consent.
- A query (s. 19מד) tells you within 60 days whether a place is covered, exempt or under an order.
- Compensation without proof of damage exists, with an index-linked ceiling, but s. 19נא(ג) bars it in several situations.
- Precise evidence — address, measurements, photos, dates — is what makes a complaint work.
