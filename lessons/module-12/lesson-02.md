# 12.2 · Committees and How the Public Takes Part

By the end of this lesson you will be able to find the Knesset committee that handles your issue, ask its secretariat to let you take part in a session, prepare a short statement and a written submission, and use protocols and broadcasts to follow what was said.

## Why it matters

Knesset committees are where bills are rewritten, where ministries are questioned and where a citizen can actually speak. The Rules of Procedure let a committee chair invite "anyone who has knowledge or an interest" in the subject, and in practice committees hear residents, parents, farmers, patients, settlers' and Bedouin councils, business owners and NGOs from every side. A clear request, a two-minute statement that answers the committee's question, and a short written paper often get more attention than a demonstration outside.

## The law

> יושב ראש ועדה רשאי להזמין לישיבה כל מי שיש לו ידע או עניין בנושא שהוועדה דנה בו, לשמוע את דעתו ולבקש ממנו מידע.

*Gist:* the chair may invite anyone with knowledge of, or an interest in, the subject, to hear their view and ask them for information. ([תקנון הכנסת, s. 125(א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/תקנון_הכנסת#סעיף_125))

> ישיבות הוועדות הן פומביות, אלא אם כן החליטה הוועדה אחרת או אם פרוטוקול הישיבה חסוי כמפורט בפרק זה; יושב ראש ועדה רשאי להגביל את הנוכחות באולם הוועדה, כולה או חלקה

*Gist:* committee sessions are public unless the committee decides otherwise or the protocol is confidential, but the chair may limit attendance in the room. ([s. 118(א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/תקנון_הכנסת#סעיף_118))

> פרוטוקולים של ישיבות ועדות יהיו פתוחים לעיון הציבור ויפורסמו באתר האינטרנט של הכנסת, בכפוף להוראות סעיף זה.

*Gist:* protocols are open to the public and published on the Knesset website, except for the Foreign Affairs and Defense Committee and other confidential sessions. ([s. 120(א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/תקנון_הכנסת#סעיף_120))

> יושב ראש ועדה רשאי להתיר למי שזומן להתייצב לפני ועדה או שהוזמן אליה להופיע לפני הוועדה שלא בנוכחות אחרים ... אם ביקש זאת לפני תחילת הישיבה

*Gist:* an invitee may ask, before the session starts, to speak without the public present; the chair decides. ([s. 126](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/תקנון_הכנסת#סעיף_126))

> כל דובר רשאי לבקש לתקן שיבושים, שגיאות והשמטות בדבריו כפי שנרשמו בפרוטוקול, בפנייה בכתב למחלקת הפרוטוקולים

*Gist:* every speaker may ask the protocol department in writing to correct errors in how their words were recorded. ([s. 119(ג)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/תקנון_הכנסת#סעיף_119))

## How it works in practice

### Which committee

Section 100 of the Rules lists the permanent committees and their subjects: for example Finance; Economy; Foreign Affairs and Defense; Interior and Environmental Protection; Constitution, Law and Justice; Education, Culture and Sports; Labour and Welfare; Health; State Control; Status of Women and Gender Equality; Immigration, Absorption and Diaspora Affairs; Science and Technology; National Security. Some subject areas were changed by temporary provisions for the 25th Knesset only, and the 26th Knesset may set up special committees of its own. A bill goes to the committee named when it passed the preliminary or first reading (lesson 12.1). A topic without a bill reaches a committee as a quick debate or a motion for the agenda that an MK raised.

### The two ways in

Research by the Israel Democracy Institute describes two routes, and practice has not changed much:

1. **By invitation.** The chair, a member or the committee staff invites organisations and experts. Become known to the staff: send short, useful information before a session.
2. **By your own request.** The agenda is posted on the committee page, usually about a week ahead (it can change at short notice). Write to the committee secretariat (the e-mail and phone are on the committee page) and ask to take part. IDI found such requests are rarely refused; **whether you speak, and for how long, is at the chair's discretion**.

### The request

Short, specific, sent early. Use the [committee participation request template](../../templates/committee-participation-request.md):

- Session date and agenda item (bill number or debate title).
- Who you are and why you have knowledge or an interest (s. 125): resident, parent, practitioner, an amuta, a local council.
- What you ask: to attend, to speak for two or three minutes, to submit a written paper (attach it, or say when).
- Your full name and ID number for the entry list, and your phone. Ask the secretariat to confirm that your name is on the list for the Knesset entrance.

### Speaking

- Sessions are tightly timed. Expect two to three minutes, sometimes less; the chair may cut you off.
- Open with who you are and **one** sentence of position. Then two facts the committee does not have. Close with a concrete request: "In clause 4, replace 30 days with 60 days."
- Answer the question the committee is discussing, not the one you wish it was discussing.
- Bring a one-page summary for the members and staff; send the full paper by e-mail before the session (lesson 12.3).
- If speaking in public could expose you to harm (for example a victim of a crime or a whistleblower), ask before the session to appear without the public present (s. 126).

### Following without attending

- **Broadcasts.** Many sessions are broadcast on the Knesset Channel and the Knesset website.
- **Protocols.** Published on the Knesset website (s. 120) and searchable on Open Knesset. Check what you said, and ask for corrections in writing (s. 119(ג)).
- **Committee documents.** Drafts and materials are on the committee's page for the session; ask the secretariat for what is missing.

### In the election recess

Committees meet in the recess only with permission (Rules s. 112), and in summer 2026 an "agreements committee" approves sessions case by case (IDI, July 2026). Few sessions are held, and new committees are only formed after the new Knesset convenes. Use this time to prepare your paper and a list of who to contact once the committees of the 26th Knesset are formed.

### Lobbyist rules

If you speak for your own cause, as a volunteer, or as an employee of the organisation you represent, you do not need a lobbyist permit. A person paid by a client to persuade MKs does (lesson 12.4).

## Worked example

**Situation.** The Education Committee schedules a session on a bill that changes daycare subsidies so that they depend on both parents working or studying. Rivka, a mother of five in Beit Shemesh whose husband studies in a kollel, and Noam, a single father from Haifa who studies part time, both want to be heard.

1. **Committee page.** The agenda lists the session for Monday, with the bill number and invitees from the Finance and Labour ministries.
2. **Requests.** On Tuesday of the week before, each sends the secretariat a short request: the session, who they are, their interest (each would lose the subsidy under clause 3), a request for two minutes and to submit a one-page paper, full name and ID number for entry.
3. **Paper.** Each attaches one page: the facts of their family's case, the numbers (subsidy amount, hours of study), and a specific change to clause 3. Rivka proposes recognising kollel study hours; Noam proposes a lower minimum of study hours for single parents.
4. **Session.** Both are added to the list. Rivka speaks for two minutes; Noam's turn does not come because time runs out. He e-mails his paper to the committee's legal adviser after the session and asks that it be placed on the committee's page.
5. **Protocol.** Rivka finds an error in the protocol ("17 hours" instead of "70 hours") and asks the protocol department in writing to correct it.

**Mirror test.** The same steps fit a secular parents' forum asking the same committee to tighten the criteria, or an Arab local council asking for daycare centres in towns with few places. The committee hears all of them under the same rule.

## Do it yourself

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Act in your own name only (or for a group that has authorised you). This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing. The default here is a dry run: send the request only if there is a real session on your issue.

### You need

- A bill or topic from lesson 12.1 and the committee that handles it.
- [committee-participation-request.md](../../templates/committee-participation-request.md).
- 60 minutes.

### Steps

1. Find the committee's page, its secretariat e-mail and phone, and its last three sessions.
2. Open one protocol of a session on your topic. Note who was invited, who spoke, for how long, and what the chair asked.
3. Watch ten minutes of a broadcast of a committee session (any topic) to see the pace.
4. Fill the request template for a real or a realistic upcoming session.
5. Write a two-minute statement: who you are, one-sentence position, two facts, one specific request. Time yourself reading it aloud.
6. List what you will send in writing before the session (lesson 12.3).

### Template

[committee-participation-request.md](../../templates/committee-participation-request.md) — a Hebrew request to the committee secretariat to attend, speak and submit a paper, with an entry-list block and a checklist.

### How you know it worked

- A secretariat reading only your first three lines knows which session, who you are and what you ask.
- Your statement takes under two minutes and ends with a request the committee can act on.

### Self-check

- [ ] Right committee and session; bill number or debate title.
- [ ] Interest or knowledge stated (s. 125).
- [ ] Request: attend, speak, submit a paper.
- [ ] Entry details given; phone number for confirmation.
- [ ] Statement timed; ends with a specific request.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Asking on the morning of the session | Not on the entry list; cannot enter | Ask the secretariat by phone; follow by broadcast and send the paper | Write as soon as the agenda is posted |
| Reading a long speech | Cut off before your request | State the request first next time; send the rest in writing | Two-minute statement, request at the end and in the paper |
| Talking about a different issue | Chair stops you; no effect | Bring it back to the clause under discussion | Read the bill and the agenda item |
| No written paper | Your points vanish after the session | E-mail it to the secretariat and the legal adviser afterwards | Send a one-page summary before the session |
| Personal attacks on officials or MKs | Removed from the room; credibility lost; defamation risk (Module 8) | Apologise; stick to facts | Criticise decisions, not people |
| Expecting a fixed right to speak | Disappointment | Ask to submit in writing and to be invited next time | Know that speaking is at the chair's discretion |

## Review

- Committees do the real work on bills and oversight; the chair may invite anyone with knowledge or an interest (s. 125).
- Sessions are public and protocols are published, except for confidential sessions (ss. 118, 120).
- Ask the secretariat early, in writing, with the session, your interest, what you ask and your entry details.
- Speak briefly and end with a specific request; back it with a written paper.
- In the election recess few committees sit; prepare now for the 26th Knesset.
