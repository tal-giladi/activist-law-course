# 18.1 · Acting as a Group: Informal Group or Amuta

Most campaigns start as a few people in a WhatsApp group. At some point someone asks: "Should we open an amuta?" By the end of this lesson you will be able to answer that question for your own group with five practical tests, explain what registration changes in law, and write a short group agreement that keeps an unregistered group safe and working until — or instead of — registering.

## Why it matters

A group of residents, parents or volunteers can do almost everything this course teaches without any legal form: each member can file FOI requests, objections, complaints and petitions in their own name, and the group can sign joint letters. Registration costs money and creates permanent duties, so many groups register too early and then fail to file reports.

But an unregistered group has no legal existence. It cannot open a bank account, sign a contract, receive tax-credited donations or sue in its own name. Whoever signs or holds the money does so personally and carries the risk. When money starts to flow, or the campaign will last for years, those gaps become real problems: arguments over money, a member who leaves with the donations, a hall rental the treasurer is personally liable for.

Choosing the form on purpose, and writing down the rules either way, prevents most of these fights.

## The law

### Who may found an amuta, and for what

> שני בני אדם או יותר, שהם בגירים, החפצים להתאגד כתאגיד למטרה חוקית שאינה מכוונת לחלוקת רווחים בין חבריו ושמטרתו העיקרית אינה עשיית רווחים, רשאים לייסד עמותה; העמותה תיכון עם רישומה בפנקס העמותות.

*Gist:* two or more adults may found an amuta for a lawful purpose that is not aimed at sharing profits among members; the amuta exists from its registration. ([חוק העמותות, s. 1](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_העמותות))

> לא תירשם עמותה אם מטרה ממטרותיה שוללת את קיומה של מדינת ישראל או את אופיה הדמוקרטי, או אם יש יסוד סביר למסקנה כי העמותה תשמש מסווה לפעולות בלתי חוקיות.

*Gist:* the Registrar refuses registration if an objective negates the existence of the State of Israel or its democratic character, or if the amuta is likely to be a cover for unlawful activity. (s. 3)

### What registration changes

> מהיום שצויין בתעודת הרישום כיום הרישום תהיה העמותה תאגיד, כשר לכל זכות, חובה ופעולה משפטית.

*Gist:* from the registration date the amuta is a legal person, able to hold rights and duties and to act in law (sign, own, sue, be sued). (s. 8)

> לכל עמותה יהיה תקנון כמפורט בפרק זה; דין התקנון כדין חוזה בין העמותה לבין חבריה ובינם לבין עצמם.

*Gist:* every amuta has bylaws, which bind the amuta and its members like a contract. (s. 9)

> עמותה אינה רשאית לבצע חלוקת רווחים לחבריה, במישרין או בעקיפין, לרבות למייסד העמותה

*Gist:* an amuta may not distribute profits to its members or founders, directly or indirectly. (s. 34ג(א))

Other rules you will meet in lessons 18.2–18.3: every amuta must have a general assembly, a committee (ועד) and an audit committee (s. 19); its full name, ending in "(ע״ר)", must appear on everything it publishes (s. 8א).

## How it works in practice

### Three forms, compared

| | Informal group | Amuta (ע״ר) | Public-benefit company (חל״צ) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Legal person | No; members act personally | Yes (s. 8) | Yes (Companies Law) |
| Set-up | Nothing to file; write a group agreement | Online application to the Registrar, a fee of about ₪900 (see [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)), bylaws | Companies Registrar; used mostly by larger or institutional bodies |
| Bank account | Only in a member's name | In the amuta's name | In the company's name |
| Tax-credited donations (s. 46) | No | Possible after approval as a public institution (lesson 18.3) | Possible after approval |
| Yearly duties | None by law | Assembly, financial and verbal reports, annual fee (lesson 18.3) | Similar, under the Companies Law |
| Who is liable | The members who signed or acted | The amuta (members are not liable for its debts as such) | The company |
| Ending it | Agree to stop | Voluntary dissolution or striking off | Liquidation |

Ottoman associations (אגודות עותמאניות) still exist from before 1980, but new groups register as amutot. This module deals with amutot.

### Five tests: should we register?

1. **Money.** Will the group receive or spend more than small, one-off sums? Donations, grants, a hall, printing, legal fees? Money in a private account is the most common source of disputes and suspicion.
2. **Contracts and property.** Will the group rent, hire, buy equipment or employ anyone? Without a legal person, the signer is personally bound.
3. **Duration.** Will the work last more than a year or outlive the founders? An amuta survives changes of people; a WhatsApp group does not.
4. **Credibility and access.** Do funders, authorities or partners require a registered body? Grants, a proper-management certificate (lesson 18.3), the FOI fee exemption for amutot (lesson 05.2), donors who want a tax receipt.
5. **Capacity to comply.** Do you have at least three reliable people, someone who can keep books, and a few hours a month for meetings, minutes and reports? An amuta that does not file becomes "inactive" and can be struck off (lesson 18.3).

If tests 1–4 are mostly "no", stay informal and write a group agreement. If two or more are clearly "yes" and test 5 is "yes", plan to register (lesson 18.2). If 1–4 are "yes" but 5 is "no", look for an existing amuta with similar goals that will host your project (a "fiscal sponsor" arrangement, written down in an agreement) before creating a new one.

### What an informal group can and cannot do

- **Can:** meet, campaign, publish, protest (with a licence where needed, lesson 06.2), send joint letters signed by members, attend council meetings and Knesset committees, and have each member file in their own name.
- **Cannot:** sign contracts, own property, open an account, receive tax-credited donations or sue as a group. Any of these is done by an individual member, personally.
- **Watch:** if one member files documents "for" other members, or advises them on their personal cases as a regular activity, Bar Association Law s. 20 may apply (lesson 18.4). Joint letters signed by everyone, or each member filing their own copy, are the safe pattern.

### The group agreement

A one-page written agreement turns unspoken assumptions into rules: the group's purpose; who the members are and how people join or leave; how decisions are made; who holds money, in which account, and how spending is approved and reported; who may speak or sign in the group's name; what happens to money and materials if the group ends or registers. It does not create a legal person, but it is evidence of what members agreed, and it becomes the first draft of your amuta's bylaws if you register. Use the [group agreement template](../../templates/group-agreement.md).

## Worked example

**Situation.** Eight parents of children with disabilities in a northern town campaign for an accessible school bus. They have a WhatsApp group, a Facebook page and ₪4,000 collected in cash at a fundraising evening, now in the account of Rina, one of the parents. A local business offers ₪15,000 if it gets "a receipt for tax purposes". A disability-rights fund invites them to apply for a grant of ₪60,000 — for registered amutot only.

**The five tests.**

1. Money: yes — and already sitting in a private account.
2. Contracts: the grant would pay for a part-time coordinator: yes.
3. Duration: the parents expect two to three years of work: yes.
4. Credibility: the fund requires an amuta, and the business wants a s. 46 receipt: yes. (A new amuta cannot give that receipt at once; lesson 18.3 explains why.)
5. Capacity: five parents are willing to serve; one is a bookkeeper: yes.

**Decision.** Register an amuta. Meanwhile:

- They sign a group agreement that day: Rina holds the ₪4,000 as trustee for the group, spending over ₪300 needs two signatures in the WhatsApp group, a monthly balance is posted, and the money moves to the amuta's account once it exists.
- They tell the business honestly that a tax receipt is not yet possible.
- They divide the work for lesson 18.2: name, objectives, bylaws, three founders.

**Mirror test.** The same tests serve a group of residents opposing a quarry, a settlement's volunteer emergency team, a mosque's youth programme or a group of secular students running a Shabbat culture series. The form follows the money and the duration, not the cause.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- The group you work with (or one you would like to start), and 60 minutes.
- [Group agreement template](../../templates/group-agreement.md).
- The [Amutot Law on Wikisource](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_העמותות), open at ss. 1–9.

### Steps

1. Write one sentence on what your group is for and how long you expect it to work.
2. Score the five tests, yes or no, with one line of evidence each.
3. Decide: informal group, new amuta, or hosting by an existing amuta. Write two lines of reasons.
4. Fill in the group agreement template for your group as it is today, even if you decided to register: purpose, members, decisions, money, spokespeople, ending.
5. Mark in the agreement every clause you would later move into amuta bylaws.

### Template

[Group agreement (הסכם התארגנות לקבוצה לא רשומה)](../../templates/group-agreement.md) — a Hebrew one-page agreement with English notes, for an unregistered group acting in its own members' names.

### How you know it worked

- Your decision follows from the tests, not from habit ("everyone opens an amuta").
- The agreement names one person who holds money and the rule for approving spending.
- It says who may speak and sign for the group, and that everything else is signed by members personally.

### Self-check

- [ ] Five tests scored with evidence.
- [ ] Decision and reasons written.
- [ ] Group agreement filled in: purpose, members, decisions, money, spokespeople, ending.
- [ ] Clauses to move into future bylaws marked.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Group money in a member's private account with no written rule | Disputes, suspicion, money lost when the member leaves | Write a group agreement now; post a balance; move the money when you register | Agree on money rules before the first donation |
| Promising donors a tax receipt before approval | Donor misled; the amuta cannot issue a s. 46 receipt | Tell donors the truth; apply for approval when eligible (lesson 18.3) | Check approval status before fundraising |
| Registering an amuta with two people and no plan for reports | Inactive status, debts of fees, striking off | Recruit members; adopt a compliance calendar (lesson 18.3) | Score the capacity test honestly first |
| One member files and argues personal cases "for" the others | Possible unlicensed practice (s. 20) | Each member files in their own name; joint letters signed by all | Use the patterns in lesson 18.4 |
| Signing a hall rental "for the group" | The signer is personally liable | Agree in writing how members share the cost | Sign contracts only once there is an amuta, or share the risk in the agreement |

## Review

- An informal group can campaign and file in members' names, but it is not a legal person: whoever signs or holds money does so personally.
- An amuta is a legal person from registration (s. 8); its bylaws bind it and its members like a contract (s. 9); it may not distribute profits (s. 34ג).
- Five tests decide the form: money, contracts, duration, credibility, and capacity to comply.
- If you stay informal, write a group agreement on purpose, decisions, money, spokespeople and ending.
- The form follows the money and the time horizon, not the cause.
