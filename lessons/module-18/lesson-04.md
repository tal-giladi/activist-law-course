# 18.4 · What Non-Lawyers May and May Not Do for Others

Lesson 01.1 gave you three questions — for whom, what act, and how — to place any act on the map. This lesson goes deeper: what exactly makes help to others "an occupation" or "for payment", which bodies and acts the law reserves, where other laws let non-lawyers represent people, what the Supreme Court and the Bar have said about organisations that help the public, and how to turn all of that into a written policy your group can follow. You will finish by writing that policy and testing it against ten real-life requests.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Not legal advice — this is our reading of the law text; no lawyer reviewed this lesson. It explains what the statute, one Supreme Court judgment and one Bar Association ethics decision say, as we understand them. If your group plans an organised service for the public, or anyone faces a complaint about unlicensed practice, get advice from a lawyer or a university legal clinic (lesson 18.5).

## Why it matters

Activists who learn the law become the person everyone asks. A tenants' group gets eviction letters, a protest movement gets arrested members, a disability forum gets families fighting the National Insurance Institute. Helping is the point of activism, and most help is lawful. But the line between explaining the law and practising it is drawn by a criminal offence, and organised groups cross it more easily than individuals, because what they do is repeated and structured — exactly what "an occupation" means.

Crossing the line hurts the people you help as well: a non-lawyer has no professional insurance, no duty of confidentiality protected by law, and no disciplinary body behind them. Lawyers' communications with clients are privileged (Bar Association Law s. 90); yours are not.

## The law

### The rule and its two triggers

> הפעולות המנויות להלן, לא יעשה אותן דרך עיסוק, או בתמורה אף שלא דרך עיסוק, אלא עורך דין; ואלה הפעולות:

*Gist:* only a lawyer may do the listed acts as an occupation, or for consideration even when not as an occupation. ([חוק לשכת עורכי הדין, s. 20](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_לשכת_עורכי_הדין))

Read the sentence slowly. There are **two independent triggers**:

- **"דרך עיסוק"** — doing it as a regular activity. Payment is not needed: a free weekly advice desk is an occupation.
- **"בתמורה אף שלא דרך עיסוק"** — doing it for any consideration, even once. Money is not needed either: a share of the compensation, "expenses", a membership fee or a donation tied to the help can all be consideration.

An act that meets **neither** trigger — a single, unpaid favour — is outside the words of s. 20. That is a narrow gap, not a business model.

### The four reserved acts

> (1) ייצוג אדם אחר וכל טיעון ופעולה אחרת בשמו לפני בתי משפט, בתי דין, בוררים וגופים ואנשים בעלי סמכות שיפוטית או מעין שיפוטית;

*Gist:* representing another person, or arguing or acting in their name, before courts, tribunals, arbitrators and judicial or quasi-judicial bodies.

> (2) ייצוג אדם אחר וכל פעולה אחרת בשמו לפני – משרד ההוצאה לפועל; לשכת רישום הקרקעות; ... רשם החברות; ... פקיד השומה ונציג מס ההכנסה לענין פקודת מס הכנסה; ...

*Gist:* representing another person before a closed list of authorities: the Execution Office, the Land Registry, the condominium registrar, the registrars of companies, partnerships, co-operatives, patents, designs and trademarks, and the income-tax, land-appreciation-tax and estate-tax authorities.

> (3) עריכת מסמכים בעלי אופי משפטי בשביל אדם אחר, לרבות ייצוג אדם אחר במשא ומתן משפטי לקראת עריכת מסמך כזה;

*Gist:* drafting documents of a legal character for another person, including negotiating for them toward such a document.

> (4) ייעוץ וחיווי דעת משפטיים.

*Gist:* legal advice and legal opinions.

Notice what items (1)–(2) do **not** cover: writing to a ministry, a municipality or an FOI officer is not representation before a court or a listed authority. But writing a demand letter or a complaint **for** someone may still be "drafting a document of a legal character for another person" (item 3), and telling them what to claim is advice (item 4).

### The savings: where other rules apply

> הוראות סעיף 20 אינן פוגעות באלה: ... (2) ייצוג לפני בתי דין דתיים ולפני בתי דין צבאיים; (3) ייצוג לפני בית משפט, בית דין, גוף או אדם אחר שהייצוג לפניהם מוסדר בחיקוק, לרבות הוראות סעיף 236 לפקודת מס הכנסה; ... (6) ייצוג של ארגון עובדים או מעבידים או של חבר בהם על ידי נציגו של ארגון כזה, בבוררות לעניני עבודה או בקשר להסכם עבודה; (7) חיווי דעת משפטי על ידי אדם שנתבקש לכך על ידי עורך דין או על ידי רשות מרשויות המדינה;

*Gist:* s. 20 does not affect the Attorney General's powers, representation before religious and military courts, bodies whose own law regulates representation, patent and trademark agents, accountants' lawful functions, workers' and employers' organisations in labour arbitration, a legal opinion requested by a lawyer or a state authority, and certain foreign lawyers in international arbitration. (s. 21(1)–(8))

The best-known item (3) rule for activists is the small claims court:

> על אף הוראות סעיף קטן (א), יכול אדם, באישור בית המשפט לתביעות קטנות, לייצג בעל דין בבית משפט לתביעות קטנות, אם בעל הדין ייפה את כוחו לכך וייפוי הכוח הוגש לבית המשפט לתביעות קטנות; אין לייצג אדם לפי סעיף קטן זה – (1) אם המייצג עוסק בייצוג בדרך קבע; (2) אם הייצוג נעשה במהלך הרגיל של עסקיו של המייצג; (3) אם הייצוג נעשה בתמורה.

*Gist:* with the court's approval and a filed power of attorney, a non-lawyer may represent a party in small claims — but not regularly, not in the course of business, and not for payment. ([חוק בתי המשפט, s. 63(ב)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_בתי_המשפט))

### The offence

> מי שאינו עורך דין ועושה פעולה שנתייחדה לפי חוק זה לעורכי דין, דינו – קנס כאמור בסעיף 61(א)(2) לחוק העונשין.

*Gist:* a non-lawyer who does a reserved act is liable to a fine at the Penal Law s. 61(a)(2) level. (s. 96; the amount is on [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)) Pretending to be a lawyer is a separate offence, punishable by up to a year in prison (s. 97). Anyone may hire a lawyer to represent them before any state or local authority (s. 22).

## How it works in practice

### What the Supreme Court said: the Medical Rights Centre case

In **ע״א 4223/12 המרכז למימוש זכויות רפואיות בע״מ נ׳ לשכת עורכי הדין בישראל** (judgment of 25 June 2014; Justices Arbel, Amit and Shoham), the Bar sued a **for-profit company** that helped clients, for payment, claim benefits from the National Insurance Institute, insurers and other bodies. The Jerusalem District Court had found that it trespassed on the profession and restricted its activity; the Supreme Court allowed the appeal in part. As we read the free summaries and text of the judgment:

- **Neutral information is not prohibited.** Publishing forms, legislation or rulings, without guidance about a particular person's case, does not cross the line.
- **Choosing the claim is advice.** Deciding which rights a person has, classifying their claim or choosing the legal route for them requires real legal judgment, and is legal advice reserved to lawyers.
- **Form help must stay technical.** Help with forms is acceptable when it is technical — for example for people with language difficulties or no internet access — and limited to fixed, factual fields; adding legal framing in free text crosses the line.
- **Quasi-judicial bodies count.** National Insurance medical committees are quasi-judicial, so representing someone before them is reserved (s. 20(1)); some help on the medical side was allowed, but legal arguments (fair procedure, legal causation) were not.
- **Facts decide.** The Court stressed that the line is drawn case by case.

The company charged its clients; your group probably does not. But the Court's line between information and advice does not depend on payment, and an organised service that repeats itself every week may be "an occupation" even when it is free.

### What the Bar said: the 2009 ethics decision on amutot and clinics

In February 2009 the Bar's National Ethics Committee published a decision, adopted by the Bar's Central Committee, on non-profits, amutot and legal clinics that represent clients for free (Ethics Bulletin 29). This is the Bar's position, binding on lawyers, not a court ruling. In summary:

- A body that represents clients without payment must be organised **like a law office**: its managers and the people who act must be **lawyers**; interns and students act only under close supervision.
- **No consideration of any kind** — not success fees, not legal costs, not membership fees, not payments from private lawyers to whom clients are referred.
- The body must act as an **independent legal unit**, not a department of its funder; policy on which cases to take may be set with the funder, but each case is run only on professional judgment and **loyalty to the client** — including when the client's wishes do not fit the organisation's cause.

### The working line: information or advice?

| Signal | General information (fine) | Individual advice (reserved) |
|---|---|---|
| Whose facts? | The law in general, examples, typical situations | This person's facts, applied to the law |
| What do you say? | "The law says X; the deadline is Y; here is the official guide" | "In your case, claim X; you will win; do not appeal" |
| Who chooses? | The person decides, after reading | You decide for them, or they rely on your choice |
| What do you write? | Blank templates, checklists, explanations | Their letter, claim, appeal or contract, in your words |
| Before whom? | Workshops, websites, public meetings | Speaking for them before a court or quasi-judicial body |

A disclaimer ("this is not legal advice") does not turn advice into information. What matters is what you actually do.

### What the group itself may do

When an amuta writes **its own** FOI request, objection or petition, signed by its authorised committee members, it acts for itself, not for "another person": that is the group's self-representation, and this course teaches it. Arguing for an amuta **in court** is different: the Civil Procedure Regulations 2018 let a party act "בעצמו" or through a lawyer (reg. 170), but they contain no rule like old reg. 30 of 1984, which let a corporation's manager act for it. Plan to have a lawyer for an amuta in the regular courts, and note that an amuta cannot file in small claims at all (lesson 11.1). Helping **members** with their personal cases is helping other people, and the rules above apply.

### A policy beats improvisation

Groups that help the public should decide in advance, in writing:

1. What we offer: information, workshops, blank templates, technical help, accompaniment, referral.
2. What we never do: advise on a person's case, draft their documents in our words, speak for them before a court or quasi-judicial body, take any payment or benefit for help.
3. What we say: a standard opening line and a referral list (lesson 18.5).
4. Who checks: one person reviews new kinds of help against the policy; organised legal services are run only by lawyers, on the 2009 model, or by a clinic.

Use the [helping-others boundaries card](../../templates/helping-others-boundaries.md).

## Worked example

**Situation A.** Salim, an activist in an unrecognised Bedouin village in the Negev, is known for understanding planning law. Families bring him demolition orders. One offers ₪500 "for your time" if he writes the family's request to the court to delay the demolition and comes to the hearing to speak for them.

**Situation B (mirror).** Noa coordinates a support network for right-wing protesters arrested at demonstrations. Every Saturday night her WhatsApp hotline gets questions from detainees' parents: "What should he say in the interrogation?", "Should we accept the release conditions?", "Can you come to the remand hearing and speak for him?"

**The analysis is the same for both.**

1. **Triggers.** Salim: ₪500 is consideration — trigger 2, even once. Noa: an organised weekly hotline is "an occupation" — trigger 1, even unpaid.
2. **Acts.** Writing the family's court request is drafting a legal document for another (s. 20(3)); speaking at the hearing is representation before a court (s. 20(1)). Telling a detainee what to say, or whether to accept conditions, is individual legal advice (s. 20(4)).
3. **What each does instead.**
   - Salim refuses the money. He runs an evening for the village on how demolition orders and planning objections work in general (Module 10), shares blank templates "for use in your own name", helps families collect their own documents, and refers each family to a planning-rights NGO or a clinic with lawyers, or to Legal Aid (lesson 11.6). He can sit in the public seats at the hearing.
   - Noa's hotline gives a standard message: the right to silence and to a lawyer in general terms (Module 7), the Public Defender's number and a list of volunteer lawyers. Questions about a specific case go straight to a lawyer. The network recruits lawyers who run a duty roster, on the 2009 model.
4. **Result.** The same help reaches people faster and more safely, and neither activist commits an offence.

### Steps

1. List the five most common requests people bring to your group.
2. For each, apply the analysis: trigger (occupation? consideration?), act (s. 20(1)–(4)), any s. 21 saving or special rule (for example small claims s. 63(ב)).
3. Sort the ten requests below as "information — fine", "technical help — fine with care", or "reserved — refer", with one line of reasoning each:
   1. A workshop on how to file an FOI request.
   2. Typing a neighbour's handwritten complaint word for word, because she cannot use a computer.
   3. Telling a tenant whether her landlord's eviction notice is valid.
   4. Sending a WhatsApp list of templates and Kol Zchut links to the group.
   5. Speaking for a member at a National Insurance medical committee.
   6. With the court's approval and a power of attorney, representing your brother, unpaid, once, in a small claim.
   7. Charging ₪200 for "help with paperwork" on planning objections.
   8. Reviewing a friend's draft appeal and rewriting its legal arguments.
   9. The amuta's coordinator filing the amuta's own FOI request.
   10. A volunteer lawyer advising members at a weekly clinic the amuta hosts, free, under the lawyer's own responsibility.
4. Fill in the boundaries card for your group: offers, never-dos, standard opening line, referral list, who checks.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| A free weekly "legal advice desk" run by non-lawyers | "An occupation": possible offence under s. 96, even unpaid | Turn it into an information session plus referral, or have lawyers run it | Write the group's policy before opening any service |
| Asking for "a small contribution" or membership for help | Consideration: one act is enough | Stop; return payments | No payment or benefit of any kind for help |
| Speaking for someone at a medical or appeals committee | Representation before a quasi-judicial body (s. 20(1)) | Help them prepare to speak for themselves; refer to a lawyer | Check whether the body decides rights |
| Rewriting someone's appeal in your words | Drafting a legal document for another (s. 20(3)) | Give the blank template and general guidance; they write it | Their words, their signature |
| "This is not legal advice" before advising anyway | The label does not change the act | Stop at general information and refer | Judge the act, not the label |
| Calling yourself "the group's legal adviser" | Misleads the public; close to impersonation (s. 97) | Change the title: "coordinator", "information volunteer" | Never use titles that suggest a lawyer |
| Arguing for the amuta in the Magistrates' Court without a lawyer | The court may refuse; the amuta's case suffers | Ask for time to appoint a lawyer | Budget for a lawyer for the amuta's own lawsuits |

## Review

- Section 20 has two independent triggers: "as an occupation" (even free) and "for consideration" (even once).
- The reserved acts are representing another before courts and quasi-judicial bodies or before listed authorities, drafting legal documents for another, and legal advice; s. 96 makes doing them an offence.
- Section 21 and laws like Courts Law s. 63(ב) open specific doors: small claims representation with approval, unpaid, not regular.
- The Medical Rights Centre case (ע״א 4223/12): neutral information and technical form help are allowed; choosing a person's claim and representing before quasi-judicial bodies are not.
- The 2009 Bar decision: a body that represents the public must be run by lawyers, take nothing in return, and be loyal to each client. Write your group's helping-others policy.
