# 18.5 · Using Lawyers Smartly: Clinics, Pro Bono and Legal Aid

This course teaches you to act alone, and that includes knowing when not to. By the end of this lesson you will be able to choose the right kind of legal help for each part of a problem, find free help beyond Legal Aid, brief a lawyer so that an hour of their time is enough, and agree in writing on exactly what they will do — so that you keep control of your case and your budget.

## Why it matters

The choice is rarely "lawyer or no lawyer". A self-represented petitioner may need one hour of a lawyer's review before filing; a group may need a clinic for its members' personal cases while it runs its own campaign; an amuta may need a lawyer only for the one lawsuit in which it is a party (lesson 18.4). Activists who use lawyers this way get better results for less money, and stay within the s. 20 limits when people ask them for help.

Free help also exists far beyond state Legal Aid, but each service has its own subjects, eligibility tests and waiting times. Knowing them before a crisis saves the days you will not have during one.

## The law

### You may act alone, or through a lawyer

> כל פעולה בבית משפט הנדרשת מבעל דין או המותרת לו לפי דין, רשאי הוא לעשותה בעצמו או בידי עורך דין שמינה לכך כדין, והוא כשאין הוראה מפורשת אחרת בכל דין.

*Gist:* a party may do any act in court personally or through a lawyer appointed for it. ([תקנות סדר הדין האזרחי, reg. 170](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/תקנות_סדר_הדין_האזרחי))

> בעל דין שמייצגו עורך דין רשאי לבטל את ייפוי הכוח של עורך דינו, ובלבד שנמסרה הודעה על כך לבית המשפט ולכל שאר בעלי הדין; עורך דין רשאי להפסיק את הייצוג ברשות בית המשפט בלבד.

*Gist:* you may end a lawyer's mandate at any time by notice to the court and the other parties; the lawyer may stop representing you only with the court's permission. (reg. 172)

> אדם שייפה כוחו של עורך דין זכאי להיות מיוצג על ידיו בפני כל רשויות המדינה, רשויות מקומיות וגופים ואנשים אחרים הממלאים תפקידים ציבוריים על פי דין

*Gist:* anyone who appoints a lawyer may be represented by them before every state and local authority and public body. ([חוק לשכת עורכי הדין, s. 22](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_לשכת_עורכי_הדין))

### Confidentiality and fees

> דברים ומסמכים שהוחלפו בין לקוח לבין עורך דין ויש להם קשר עניני לשירות המקצועי שניתן על ידי עורך הדין ללקוח, לא יגלה אותם עורך הדין בכל הליך משפטי, חקירה או חיפוש, מלבד אם ויתר הלקוח על חסינותם.

*Gist:* what you and your lawyer exchange about the service is privileged: the lawyer may not disclose it in any proceeding, investigation or search unless you waive it. (s. 90) Conversations with a non-lawyer activist have no such protection.

> המועצה הארצית של הלשכה רשאית לקבוע תעריף מינימלי לשכר טרחה בעד שירותי עורכי דין, שיהיה בבחינת המלצה לחברי הלשכה, אך לא יחייבם.

*Gist:* the Bar may set a recommended minimum fee tariff, which does not bind lawyers. (s. 81) Fees are agreed between you and the lawyer.

Section 21(7) also lets a lawyer ask a non-lawyer for a legal opinion: an activist with expertise can work **for** a lawyer on a case, as an expert, without breaching s. 20.

## How it works in practice

### The help ladder

Start at the top and move down only when the rung above does not fit.

| Rung | What it gives | Who qualifies | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| Act alone with this course | Your own filings, with templates | Everyone | Modules 2–12 |
| Public Defender | Criminal defence, including at remand hearings | Detainees and defendants by the law's tests (lesson 07.3) | Ask the police or court at once |
| State Legal Aid | A lawyer in listed civil matters, free or for a small fee | Income and asset tests, or no test for some subjects (lesson 11.6) | Ministry of Justice district offices |
| Bar pro bono "שכר מצווה" | A volunteer lawyer in civil matters, free | Low-income people not eligible for Legal Aid, by the programme's tests | Kol Zchut gives its hotline, 1-700-505-500 (check before use) |
| University legal clinics | Students supervised by lawyers; individual cases and public-interest litigation | Depends on the clinic's subjects and capacity | Tel Aviv, Hebrew University, Haifa, Bar-Ilan, Ono and others (links below) |
| Public-interest organisations | Advice, representation, amicus briefs, test cases in their field | Their cause and criteria | [Where to Get Help](../../references/where-to-get-help.md) |
| Private lawyer, limited scope | One hour's review, one letter, one hearing | Anyone who pays | Agreed in writing |
| Private lawyer, full representation | The whole case | Anyone who pays | Agreed in writing |

### When a lawyer is worth it

- **Criminal exposure** (arrest, interrogation, charge): always ask for the Public Defender or a lawyer first.
- **Your amuta is a party** to a lawsuit in the regular courts (lesson 18.4).
- **The other side has lawyers and costs are at stake**: a defamation suit, a large civil claim, a petition where costs may be ordered against you.
- **A deadline you cannot calculate** or a procedural step you have not seen before.
- **Other people's cases** your group is asked to handle (lesson 18.4): refer them.

Otherwise, consider a **limited-scope** use: you prepare the file, the lawyer reviews it for an hour and you file in your own name.

### Briefing a lawyer: the one-page brief

Lawyers bill by time; clinics and NGOs have queues. A clear brief gets you a faster, cheaper and better answer.

1. **Who you are** and who else is involved (names, roles; for a group, who instructs the lawyer).
2. **The question** in one sentence: "Can we petition against X, and by when?"
3. **What you want** to achieve, and what you are not willing to do.
4. **Chronology**: dated facts, each with a document number.
5. **What you already did**: letters, FOI requests, complaints, replies.
6. **Deadlines** you know of, and any hearing dates.
7. **Documents**: numbered, in one PDF or folder.

Use the issue map from Module 1 ([issue-map.md](../../templates/issue-map.md)) for points 2–3 and your research memo (Module 2) if you have one.

### The engagement: get it in writing

Before work starts, agree in writing (an e-mail is enough for a short task):

- **Scope**: exactly what the lawyer does — "review the draft petition and comment in writing" — and what they do not do.
- **Fee**: fixed, hourly (with an estimate and a cap) or free; expenses; VAT.
- **Costs risk**: who pays if a court orders costs against you.
- **Decisions**: you decide on filing, settling and appealing; the lawyer advises.
- **Reporting**: how and when the lawyer updates you; who the lawyer's contact in your group is.
- **Ending**: either side may end the engagement; in court, ending needs notice or the court's permission (reg. 172).

If something goes wrong, raise it with the lawyer first. Complaints about a lawyer's conduct go to the Bar's district ethics committee (ועדת האתיקה המחוזית).

### When the lawyer is the group's

A group's lawyer is loyal to the client — the person or amuta that hired them — not to the cause in general (the 2009 Bar decision, lesson 18.4). Decide in advance, by a committee decision, who instructs the lawyer, and make sure members with personal cases get their own advice rather than relying on the group's lawyer.

## Worked example

**Situation.** Forty families in a public-housing block in Jerusalem — Haredi, secular and Arab tenants — receive letters from the housing company about a renovation project that would move them out for two years. Their tenants' committee, an informal group with a group agreement (lesson 18.1), meets.

1. **Split the problem.** The collective issue (the project's legality and the consultation) is the committee's campaign. Each family's tenancy terms and compensation are personal cases.
2. **Committee acts alone.** It sends FOI requests for the project's approvals (lesson 05.2), attends the planning hearing (Module 10) and writes a joint letter signed by all families.
3. **Personal cases: referral, not advice.** The committee shares a referral sheet (lesson 18.4): Legal Aid for eligible families (lesson 11.6), the Bar's pro bono programme for others, and a Jerusalem university clinic that handles housing. It does not tell any family what its rights are.
4. **Limited-scope lawyer for the group.** The committee drafts a pre-petition letter (lesson 05.5) and pays a public-law lawyer for a two-hour review, agreed by e-mail: fixed fee, written comments within a week, the committee signs and sends the letter itself.
5. **If it escalates.** The lawyer's review shows that a petition would face a costs risk and complex planning questions. The committee approaches a public-interest planning organisation and a clinic to take the petition; it prepares the one-page brief and chronology so that the intake takes one meeting.

**Mirror test.** The same ladder serves a moshav fighting a regional council's land allocation, a Druze village's committee contesting a planning scheme, or a group of small business owners challenging a municipal by-law.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- The issue you have used in this module (or in Module 1), with its documents.
- [Where to Get Help](../../references/where-to-get-help.md), the clinic links below, and your boundaries card from lesson 18.4.
- 75 minutes.

### Steps

1. Split your issue into the parts you will do alone, the parts that need free help, and the parts that may need a paid lawyer. Write one line of reasons for each.
2. Build your group's referral map: for each common need on your boundaries card, find one free service and its current contact details. Check each clinic's subjects on its website.
3. Write a one-page brief for a lawyer on one part of your issue, with a dated chronology and numbered documents.
4. Write a limited-scope engagement e-mail: scope, fee and cap, costs risk, who decides, reporting, ending.
5. Write the three questions you will ask in the first ten minutes of the meeting.

### Template

No new template. Use [Issue map](../../templates/issue-map.md) for the brief's question and goal, and the referral list in [Helping-others boundaries card](../../templates/helping-others-boundaries.md) (Part E) for your referral map.

### How you know it worked

- A stranger can read your brief in five minutes and state your question and deadline.
- Your engagement e-mail says what the lawyer will **not** do.
- Each common need on your referral map has a free route with a checked contact.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Calling a lawyer the day before a deadline | No time to review; higher fee or refusal | File what you can; ask the court for an extension | Brief a lawyer as soon as a deadline appears |
| No written scope | Bills for work you did not want; gaps nobody covers | Agree the scope in writing now | One engagement e-mail before work starts |
| Sending the lawyer 200 unsorted pages | Expensive reading time; key facts missed | Send the one-page brief and chronology | Number documents and summarise first |
| Forwarding the lawyer's advice to the whole group or the press | Privilege may be waived; the other side learns your strategy | Limit circulation; ask the lawyer what can be shared | Agree who receives legal advice |
| The group's lawyer advising members on personal cases "on the side" | Conflicts of interest; members rely on the wrong advice | Refer members to their own help | Decide who the client is |
| Applying only to Legal Aid and giving up when refused | Missed free alternatives | Try pro bono, clinics and NGOs; appeal a Legal Aid refusal (lesson 11.6) | Build the referral map in advance |

## Review

- You may act in court personally or through a lawyer (reg. 170), and may end a lawyer's mandate by notice (reg. 172).
- Free help goes well beyond Legal Aid: the Public Defender, the Bar's "שכר מצווה", university clinics and public-interest organisations.
- A lawyer is worth it for criminal exposure, the amuta's own lawsuits, costs risk and unfamiliar deadlines; otherwise consider limited-scope review.
- A one-page brief, a chronology and numbered documents make every hour of legal help count; agree scope, fee, costs and decisions in writing.
- What you tell your lawyer is privileged (s. 90); what you tell an activist friend is not.
