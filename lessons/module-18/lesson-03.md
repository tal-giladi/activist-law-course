# 18.3 · Running an Amuta: Proper Management, Donations and Reporting

Most amutot that get into trouble do so not because of what they campaign for but because they missed a report, a fee or a meeting. By the end of this lesson you will be able to build your amuta's yearly compliance calendar, explain what the proper-management certificate depends on, handle donations — including the rules on donations from foreign state entities — correctly, and tell donors honestly whether they can get a tax credit.

## Why it matters

An amuta's credibility rests on its paperwork. Funders, the Tax Authority, the FOI fee exemption (lesson 05.2) and many government support schemes depend on the proper-management certificate, and the certificate depends on reports filed on time. An amuta that does not pay its fee or file its financial report within 90 days of the deadline may be treated as having stopped operating, and can be struck off. Opponents of any cause, from any side, look first at the reports on GuideStar; a missing report is an easy headline.

The rules on foreign state funding are also a frequent subject of public debate, so an activist needs to know exactly what the law requires today — and what was only proposed.

## The law

### The yearly cycle

> אסיפה כללית רגילה של חברי העמותה תתקיים במועדים הקבועים בתקנונה ולא פחות מאחת לשנה.

*Gist:* an ordinary general assembly at least once a year. ([חוק העמותות, s. 20(א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_העמותות))

> הדו״ח הכספי המקורי או העתקו המאושר כדין יוגש לרשם לא יאוחר מיום 30 ביוני בשנה שלאחר תום תקופת הדו״ח, חתום בידי שנים מחברי הועד, ורשאי הרשם, להאריך את המועד להגשתם.

*Gist:* the financial report is filed with the Registrar by 30 June of the following year, signed by two committee members; the Registrar may extend. (s. 36(ד)) The committee prepares it for each tax year, gives it to the audit committee at least two weeks before the assembly, and the assembly approves it (s. 36(א)). A verbal report on the amuta's activity is filed with it (s. 37א). Above a turnover threshold (s. 19(ג), indexed yearly) the report must be audited by an accountant (s. 37(א)).

> עמותה שהתקיים בה אחד מאלה יראו אותה כעמותה שחדלה לפעול: (1) לא שילמה אגרה שחובה עליה לשלם לפי חוק זה, תוך תשעים ימים מהיום האחרון שנקבע לתשלומם; (2) לא הגישה לרשם את הדו״ח הכספי תוך תשעים ימים מהיום האחרון שנקבע לכך בסעיף 36;

*Gist:* an amuta that has not paid a fee, or not filed its financial report, within 90 days of the deadline is treated as having stopped operating, and the Registrar may strike it off. (s. 59(א)–(ב))

Section 38 lists what else must be filed online, signed by two committee members: changes of address or of committee and audit members, decisions on signatories, bylaw changes, notice of any lawsuit against the amuta or a committee member — each within two weeks (s. 38(ב)(1)). Members may inspect the registers, minutes and reports at any reasonable time (s. 39(א)); the public may inspect what was filed with the Registrar (s. 39(ב)).

### Donations from foreign state entities

A "foreign state entity" (ישות מדינית זרה) includes a foreign state, a union of states such as the EU, their organs and local authorities, the Palestinian Authority, and corporations set up or controlled by them (s. 36א(א)). Donations from private foreign individuals or private foundations are not covered.

> עמותה שמחזורה עולה על 300,000 שקלים חדשים, תציין בדוח הכספי לפי סעיף 36, אם קיבלה או לא קיבלה בשנה שאליה מתייחס הדוח הכספי, תרומות מישויות מדיניות זרות ששוויין המצטבר עולה על סכום של 20,000 שקלים חדשים

*Gist:* in its annual financial report, an amuta with turnover above ₪300,000 states whether it received more than ₪20,000 from foreign state entities that year, with the donor, amount, purpose and conditions of each donation, and publishes the details on its website. (s. 36א(ב)–(ג))

> נתמך שקיבל תרומה מישות מדינית זרה, יגיש לרשם, בתוך שבוע מתום אותו רבעון שבו התקבלה התרומה, דוח בטופס מקוון שיקבע שר המשפטים (להלן – דוח רבעוני)

*Gist:* any amuta that received a donation from a foreign state entity files an online quarterly report within a week after the end of that quarter, with the donor, amount, purpose and conditions. ([חוק חובת גילוי, s. 2](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_חובת_גילוי_לגבי_מי_שנתמך_על_ידי_ישות_מדינית_זרה)) No minimum amount appears in s. 2.

The 2016 amendment added s. 5א: an amuta whose **main** source of funding in the last reported year was foreign state entities must say so in a form filed by the reporting date, and then state it prominently in its public advertising, in letters and e-mails to public servants and elected officials, and in reports it distributes; its representative must tell a Knesset committee chair before speaking. Every amuta must make every effort to find out whether a donation comes from a foreign state entity (s. 6). Breaching the quarterly-report and labelling duties is a strict-liability offence with a fine (Amutot Law s. 64א(א)(8)–(9)).

### The donation tax credit

> אדם שתרם בשנת מס פלונית סכום העולה על 180 שקלים חדשים לקרן לאומית, או למוסד ציבורי כמשמעותו בסעיף 9(2) שקבע לענין זה שר האוצר באישור ועדת הכספים של הכנסת, יזוכה מהמס שהוא חייב בו באותה שנה בשיעור של 35% מסכום התרומה – אם הוא יחיד

*Gist:* an individual who donates to an approved public institution gets a tax credit of 35% of the donation, above a yearly minimum and up to a ceiling (both indexed). ([פקודת מס הכנסה, s. 46(א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/פקודת_מס_הכנסה)) The credit exists only for donations to an institution **approved** under s. 46; the approval can be cancelled for missing reports or improper books (s. 46(א1)).

## How it works in practice

### The compliance calendar

The fee amounts and thresholds are on [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md).

| When | Task | Who | Law |
|---|---|---|---|
| January–31 March | Pay the annual fee at the reduced early rate (a much lower fee applies in the first two years and for small amutot that filed their verbal report) | Treasurer | Fees Regulations, Schedule item 2 |
| After 31 March | Higher fee; unpaid 90 days after the year's deadline → "stopped operating" risk | Treasurer | s. 59(א)(1) |
| Within a week after each quarter (by about 7 Jan, 7 Apr, 7 Jul, 7 Oct) | Quarterly report, only if a foreign-state-entity donation arrived in the quarter | Treasurer | Disclosure Law s. 2 |
| January–April | Close the books for last year; prepare the financial report (audited if above the threshold) and the verbal report | Committee, bookkeeper, accountant | ss. 35–37א |
| At least 2 weeks before the assembly | Give the financial report to the audit committee | Committee | s. 36(א) |
| April–June | Ordinary general assembly: reports, audit committee's conclusions, approval, elections if due; signed minutes | Chair, secretary | ss. 20, 23, 36 |
| By 30 June | File online: financial report, verbal report, assembly minutes approving them, audit committee's recommendation (and accountant's opinion, if any) | Two committee members | ss. 36(ד), 37א, 38(א)(3) |
| Within 2 weeks of any change | Committee or audit members, address, digital address, signatories, bylaws, a lawsuit against the amuta | Secretary | s. 38 |
| After filing | Apply for (or renew) the proper-management certificate | Committee | Registrar's guidelines |

### The proper-management certificate

The proper-management certificate (אישור ניהול תקין) is issued by the Registrar, usually for the next calendar year, after the amuta has filed its annual documents in full and the Registrar is satisfied that it complies with the law and uses its assets for its objectives. It is not a section of the Amutot Law but an administrative certificate under the Registrar's published guidelines. It unlocks:

- the FOI fee exemption for amutot (lesson 05.2);
- most government and local-authority support and many private funders;
- an application for s. 46 approval.

Check any amuta's status, reports and certificate on [GuideStar](https://www.guidestar.org.il/). The Registrar's guidelines and the application route are on the Corporations Authority's gov.il pages.

### Donations, step by step

1. **Record every donation**: donor, date, amount, method, purpose, conditions. Cash only with a receipt.
2. **Ask where it comes from.** For grants from foundations, agencies or "programmes", check whether the source is a foreign state, a union of states, their agency or a body they control (s. 6). Ask the donor in writing and keep the answer.
3. **Foreign state entity?** Quarterly report within a week after the quarter; note it for the annual report and the website; check at year end whether such donations were your **main** funding (s. 5א) — if so, prepare labels for publications, letters to officials and Knesset appearances.
4. **Receipts.** Issue an ordinary receipt. Only an amuta with a valid s. 46 approval may issue a receipt for the tax credit; never write "מוכר לצורכי מס" without it.
5. **Restricted donations.** Use money given for a purpose for that purpose; changing objectives later requires accounting for assets and commitments to donors (s. 11(ב)).

### Section 46 approval

Approval is given by the Tax Authority under s. 46 and its procedure (application form 5245, gov.il). In practice it requires a track record of activity and reports, a valid proper-management certificate and bylaws that send assets to a similar public institution on dissolution (see the [bylaws checklist](../../templates/amuta-bylaws-checklist.md)); a brand-new amuta cannot get it immediately. Until then, tell donors plainly that no tax credit is available.

### Legislation status in October 2026 (check before relying on it)

- **The foreign-funding bill.** A private bill (P/5222/25, "הצעת חוק העמותות (תיקון — תרומה מישות מדינית זרה)", MK Ariel Kallner) would tax donations from foreign state entities at 80% and limit court access for organisations funded mainly that way (IDI, June 2025). It passed a preliminary reading in February 2025; the Knesset database shows it at "preparation for first reading" (status last updated March 2025). Supporters say foreign governments should not buy political influence in Israel; critics, including IDI, say it would cripple civil-society and human-rights organisations. Because it never passed a first reading, it **lapses** with the 25th Knesset (lesson 12.1); a new Knesset would have to start again.
- **A government bill on financial sanctions** under the Amutot Law (Amendment 20, "עיצומים כספיים") was tabled for first reading in December 2025 and had not passed it; it too lapses unless re-tabled.
- **The law in force** is the Amutot Law s. 36א and the 2011 Disclosure Law as amended in 2016, described above.

## Worked example

**Situation 1.** "שומרי הקרקע בגליל (ע״ר)", a land-use watchdog amuta founded three years ago, files petitions against illegal construction. Turnover ₪420,000, all from Israeli and American private donors.

- **Calendar.** Its treasurer pays the annual fee in February at the reduced early rate. In March the accountant audits the 2025 accounts (the turnover is below the audit threshold, but the committee chose an audit because a funder asked). The general assembly meets on 20 May; the audit committee received the report on 1 May; minutes are signed the same evening; everything is filed online on 10 June.
- **Donations.** No foreign state entity, so no quarterly reports; the annual report states "no donations from foreign state entities" (s. 36א(ב)(1) — turnover above ₪300,000). An American family foundation is private, not a state entity.
- **Tax credit.** It received s. 46 approval last year, so its receipts carry the approval number.
- **Certificate.** It applies for a certificate for 2027 after filing, and posts the link on its website.

**Situation 2 (mirror).** "בית משותף לחינוך דו־לשוני (ע״ר)", a coexistence-education amuta with turnover ₪900,000, receives a €40,000 EU grant in February and a grant from a European government's development agency in August. It files quarterly reports by 7 April and by 7 October (Disclosure Law s. 2), lists both in its annual report and on its website (s. 36א), and checks at year end: foreign state money was 35% of its income, so it is **not** "mainly" funded that way and no labelling under s. 5א is needed. If the share had passed half, its flyers, its letters to the municipality and its representative at a Knesset committee would have had to say so.

Both amutot follow the same law. Neither the cause nor the donors' politics changes the duties; only the source and the size of the money do.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- Your amuta (or the dry-run amuta from lesson 18.2), or a real amuta you choose on GuideStar.
- [GuideStar](https://www.guidestar.org.il/), [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md), and the [Disclosure Law](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_חובת_גילוי_לגבי_מי_שנתמך_על_ידי_ישות_מדינית_זרה).
- 75 minutes.

### Steps

1. On GuideStar, open one real amuta. Note: last financial and verbal report filed, proper-management certificate status, s. 46 approval, turnover. Is anything late?
2. Build a twelve-month compliance calendar for your amuta, using the table above, with a named person for each task.
3. Write a one-page donations procedure: what you record, how you check for foreign state entities, who files the quarterly report, how receipts are worded with and without s. 46 approval.
4. Classify these donations: (a) ₪5,000 from a German citizen living in Berlin; (b) ₪30,000 from a fund of a European Union delegation; (c) ₪10,000 from a company fully owned by a foreign government; (d) ₪2,000 from an Israeli kibbutz. For each, write which duties it triggers.
5. Write two sentences you would say to a donor who asks for a tax receipt before you have s. 46 approval.

### Template

[Amuta bylaws checklist](../../templates/amuta-bylaws-checklist.md) — check that your bylaws contain the dissolution clause that s. 46 approval usually needs.

### How you know it worked

- Your calendar has every row of the table, with dates and names.
- Step 4: (b) and (c) are foreign state entities (quarterly report; annual-report details; website if over the s. 36א thresholds); (a) and (d) are not.
- Your donor sentences promise nothing you cannot deliver.

<details>
<summary>Reference answer for step 4</summary>

(a) A private individual abroad: not a foreign state entity; ordinary record and receipt. (b) The EU and its organs are a "union of states": quarterly report within a week after the quarter; annual report and website details if the amuta's turnover is above ₪300,000 and such donations exceed ₪20,000 in the year. (c) A corporation more than half controlled by a foreign state is a foreign state entity (s. 36א(א)(3)): same duties as (b). (d) Israeli body: not covered.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Annual fee paid late | Higher fee; after 90 days, "stopped operating" and striking-off risk (s. 59) | Pay at once; ask the Registrar to restore if struck off | Pay in January–March; calendar reminder |
| Financial report filed without assembly approval or with one signature | Filing incomplete; no proper-management certificate | Hold the assembly; file with two signatures and the minutes | Follow the order: audit committee → assembly → filing |
| EU or foreign-government grant not reported quarterly | Strict-liability offence (s. 64א(א)(8)); public criticism | File the late report now | Check the source of every grant (s. 6) |
| "Tax-deductible" on receipts without s. 46 approval | Misleading donors; donors' credits rejected | Correct the receipts and tell donors | Check your approval status first |
| Assuming a lapsed bill is law, or that a proposed bill is dead forever | Wrong advice to the board; missed preparation | Check the Knesset database for the current stage | Check status at the start of each Knesset (lesson 12.1) |
| Committee member also on the audit committee | Breach of s. 32; audit worthless | Elect a separate audit committee | Check roles at every election |

## Review

- The yearly cycle: annual fee early in the year, an assembly at least once a year, financial and verbal reports filed by 30 June with two signatures, and changes reported within two weeks.
- Missing a fee or the financial report by 90 days after the deadline can make the amuta "stopped operating" and lead to striking off (s. 59).
- The proper-management certificate follows full, timely reports and unlocks the FOI fee exemption, most public funding and s. 46 approval.
- Any donation from a foreign state entity triggers a quarterly report; larger amounts also appear in the annual report and on the website; amutot funded mainly that way must label their publications and letters (s. 5א).
- The s. 46 tax credit (35% for individuals) needs the Tax Authority's approval; a new amuta must not promise it.
