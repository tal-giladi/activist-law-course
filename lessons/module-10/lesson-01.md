# 10.1 · The Planning System Map

By the end of this lesson you will be able to look at any building or land-use project — a tower, a road, a quarry, a new neighbourhood, a house extension next door — and say which planning body decides it, at what stage the public can still speak, and where an objection, an appeal and a court challenge would go.

## Why it matters

In Israel almost nothing is built without a plan (תכנית) and then a permit (היתר). Both are decided by planning institutions under the Planning and Building Law 1965, and both have short, fixed windows in which the public may object. Most people hear about a project when the fences go up — by then the plan was approved years ago and the objection period has long closed.

Residents fighting a tower, farmers whose fields are crossed by a new road, an unrecognised Bedouin village whose homes do not appear on any plan, a settlement waiting for its plan to be approved, an NGO that wants the law enforced against illegal building, an environmental group opposing a power station: they all use the same system. The first skill is the map: who decides, when, and where you go next.

## The law

The law sets out who decides objections, by the level of the plan:

> המועצה הארצית תדון ותכריע בהתנגדויות לתכנית מיתאר מחוזית; הועדה המחוזית תדון ותכריע בהתנגדויות לתכנית שהופקדה על ידיה; הועדה המקומית תדון ותכריע בהתנגדויות לתכנית שבסמכותה.

*Gist:* objections are decided by the body that deposited the plan: the National Council for district outline plans, the district committee for its plans, the local committee for plans within its own powers. ([Planning and Building Law s. 105](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_התכנון_והבניה#סעיף_105))

> תכנית מפורטת או תכנית מיתאר מקומית, הכוללת אך ורק אחד או יותר מהנושאים המפורטים להלן, היא תכנית בסמכות הועדה המקומית:

*Gist:* a local committee may approve a plan only if it deals **only** with the topics listed in s. 62א (for example building lines, height without adding total floor area, merging plots, more public areas); anything else goes to the district committee. ([s. 62א(א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_התכנון_והבניה#סעיף_62א))

> (1) לדון ולהחליט בערר על החלטה של ועדה מקומית, של רשות רישוי מקומית או של ועדה משותפת למספר ועדות מקומיות שבמחוז אחד, בכל ענין שבו ניתנת בחוק זה זכות ערר על החלטותיהן

*Gist:* each district has an appeals committee that hears appeals against local committees and local licensing authorities wherever the law gives a right of appeal. ([s. 12ב(א)(1)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_התכנון_והבניה#סעיף_12ב))

> תכנון ובניה – (א) עניני תכנון ובניה לפי חוק התכנון והבניה, התשכ״ה–1965, למעט לפי פרק י׳: פיקוח, אכיפה ועונשין ... ולמעט החלטות שענינן תכנית מיתאר ארצית או מחוזית והחלטות שר הפנים או שר האוצר.

*Gist:* most planning decisions are challenged in the Administrative Court; national and district outline plans, ministers' decisions and the enforcement chapter are left out, so challenges to them go to the High Court. ([Administrative Courts Law, First Schedule item 10](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_בתי_משפט_לענינים_מינהליים))

## How it works in practice

### The ladder of institutions

| Level | Institution | Typical plans and decisions | Objection or comment goes to |
|---|---|---|---|
| National | National Council (המועצה הארצית) and its subcommittees | National outline plans (תמ״א), appeals against district committees | National outline plans have no formal objection stage; public comments are heard in the Council's discussions (Kol Zchut, citing HCJ 288/00) |
| National, special | National Infrastructure Committee (ות״ל) | Roads, railways, power lines, gas, water infrastructure | "השגה" (a challenge), not an objection, heard by an investigator; the Government approves the plan |
| National, special | Preferred Housing Compounds Committee (ותמ״ל) | Large housing plans on declared compounds (temporary law) | Objection to the ותמ״ל; **anyone** may object |
| District | District committee (Jerusalem, North, Haifa, Centre, Tel Aviv, South) | District outline plans, most new neighbourhoods, towers that add floor area, land-use changes | District committee (its objections subcommittee), copy to the local committee |
| Local | Local committee (one town, or a joint committee for several) | Plans limited to the s. 62א topics; all building permits, הקלות and non-conforming uses | Local committee, copy to the district committee |
| Appeals | District appeals committee (ועדת ערר) | Appeals against local committee decisions on plans and permits | — |

An **independent local committee** (ועדה מקומית עצמאית) has wider plan powers. If you want to appeal its plan decision as an objector, you first need the appeals committee chair's leave (s. 112(א1)); lesson 10.4 covers this.

### The life of a plan

```mermaid
flowchart LR
  A[Preparation and submission] --> B[Discussion: decision to deposit]
  B --> C[Deposit published: newspapers, sign, websites]
  C --> D[Objection period]
  D --> E[Hearing of objections]
  E --> F[Decision: approve, change or reject]
  F --> G[Appeal window]
  G --> H[Approval published: plan in force]
  H --> I[Building permit, הקלות, non-conforming use]
```

Where the public can act:

1. **Before deposit.** Attend or follow the committee's public sessions and read its protocols (s. 48ד requires them to be published online). Write to the committee and the planners. You have no formal right to be heard yet, but early letters shape the plan.
2. **At deposit.** The formal objection (lesson 10.3). The notice is published in newspapers, on the internet, on boards in the neighbourhood and on a sign at the site (ss. 1א, 89, 89א).
3. **After the decision.** Appeal (lesson 10.4).
4. **At the permit stage.** An objection to a request for a הקלה or a non-conforming use, after a 15-day notice to the neighbours and in the newspaper (s. 149; lesson 10.3).
5. **After construction.** Complaints to the enforcement units, petitions demanding enforcement or challenging demolition (lesson 10.4).

### Where the map changes

- **ות״ל plans** (national infrastructure). Anyone entitled to object under s. 100 may file a השגה within the period set in s. 76ג (see [Current Numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)); an investigator hears it, and the Government, not the committee, gives final approval.
- **ותמ״ל plans** (preferred housing). Special objection rules in the 2014 law: anyone may object; an investigator usually hears the objections. The law is temporary; under s. 34(ג) it applies to plans submitted to the committee within six years of 8.8.2021. Check whether it has been extended.
- **Judea and Samaria.** Planning there is under Jordanian planning law and military orders, run by the Civil Administration's Higher Planning Council, not by the 1965 Law. Some of its decisions go to the Administrative Court in Jerusalem (Fourth Schedule item 2); offences and enforcement, regional and outline plans, and the military commander's decisions are excluded and go to the High Court. Objection periods and forms differ: read the notice.
- **Unrecognised villages.** Where there is no approved plan, there is nothing to get a permit under. Residents and planning NGOs use objections to regional plans, alternative plans and petitions to seek recognition; others use complaints and petitions to demand enforcement. Same system, opposite aims.

### Who to call

- The local committee's engineer's office (מהנדס הוועדה) and its website, for local plans and permits.
- The district committee's secretariat, for district plans and the objection deadlines.
- The Planning Administration's technical support, 074-7697777, for Mavat and the online forms.

## Worked example

**Situation.** Residents of a 1960s street in Petah Tikva learn that a developer plans to replace six four-storey buildings with two 28-storey towers under an urban-renewal (פינוי-בינוי) plan. Some residents want it; others fear traffic and the loss of a small public garden. Ronit Ashkenazi wants to know who decides and when she can object.

1. **Which body?** The plan adds hundreds of housing units and a large amount of floor area and changes land use (part of the garden). That goes beyond the s. 62א list, so it is a **district committee** plan (Centre District). If it only moved building lines or changed heights without adding floor area, the local committee could approve it.
2. **Which stage?** On Mavat (lesson 10.2) the plan's status shows "discussed — decision to deposit with conditions". It is not deposited yet: the objection clock has not started.
3. **What now?** Ronit reads the district committee protocol of the deposit discussion and writes to the planners about the garden before deposit. She signs up for alerts so she sees the deposit notice the day it appears.
4. **At deposit.** Objection to the district committee, copy to the local committee, within the s. 102 period (lesson 10.3).
5. **If rejected.** Leave from the district committee chair to appeal to the National Council (lesson 10.4). An Administrative Court petition is possible against the final planning decision.
6. **The other side.** Residents who want the towers can also be heard: anyone who may be harmed if an objection is accepted gets a chance to respond before the decision (s. 106(ב)). The map is the same for both camps.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- Your home address, or the address of a project you care about.
- [mavat.iplan.gov.il](https://mavat.iplan.gov.il/), your municipality's website and the [issue-map template](../../templates/issue-map.md).
- 45 minutes.

### Steps

1. Find your **local committee**. Is it a committee for one town, a regional or joint committee, or an independent one? (Search the municipality website for "הוועדה המקומית לתכנון ובניה".)
2. Find your **district committee** and the **appeals committee** for that district.
3. Find one plan near you in any status. Note its number, the body that decides it (column "סמכות" on Mavat) and its status.
4. Place the plan on the timeline above. Which participation step is open now?
5. Ask: is this a special track (ות״ל, ותמ״ל, Judea and Samaria, a national outline plan)? If yes, write down how the route differs.
6. Write the full route on the issue map: objection → hearing → appeal → court, with the body at each step.

[issue-map.md](../../templates/issue-map.md): use one row per step of the route, with the institution and its contact details.

### How you know it worked

- For your plan you can name the deciding body, the appeal body and the court, and explain why.
- You know whether the objection window is open, not yet open, or closed.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Waiting for the bulldozers | The plan was approved years ago; no objection is possible | Look for a later permit, הקלה or enforcement route | Follow deposits in your area (lesson 10.2) |
| Objecting to the local committee about a district plan | Filed with the wrong body; may be ignored or late | Re-file at once with the right body, copy to the other | Check "סמכות" on the plan page |
| Treating a ות״ל plan like an ordinary plan | Wrong document, wrong body, wrong deadline | File a השגה under s. 76ג before its deadline | Check the committee name on the notice |
| Assuming the 1965 Law applies in Judea and Samaria | Wrong forum, wrong deadlines | Read the Higher Planning Council's notice; check the court with the Fourth Schedule | Map the special tracks first |
| Ignoring the other camp | Surprised at the hearing by residents who support the plan | Prepare answers to their interests | Remember s. 106(ב): they may be heard too |

## Review

- Plans are decided at three levels — national, district, local — and objections go to the body that deposited the plan (s. 105).
- A local committee may approve only plans limited to the s. 62א topics; bigger changes are district plans.
- The public's formal window opens at deposit; before it you can follow protocols and write; after it you appeal.
- ות״ל, ותמ״ל, national outline plans and Judea and Samaria have their own tracks.
- Most planning challenges go to the Administrative Court; outline plans, ministers' decisions and enforcement go to the High Court.
