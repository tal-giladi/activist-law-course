# 10.5 · Environmental Laws and Complaints

By the end of this lesson you will be able to document an environmental hazard, report it to the body that must deal with it, follow up in writing, take part in the permit decisions behind it, and — if the authorities and the polluter do nothing — send the 60-day notice that opens the way to a citizen's injunction suit.

## Why it matters

Smoke from waste burned in open fields, a factory's night-time smell, sewage in a stream, asbestos left after a demolition, noise from a quarry: environmental hazards hit residents of every kind of community, Jewish and Arab, urban and rural, on both sides of the Green Line. The law gives individuals more than the right to complain. A citizen may sue a polluter directly for an injunction, without proving negligence, after giving the authorities 60 days to act. Approved environmental organisations may sue too, and may file group claims. Most cases never get that far: a well-documented complaint, followed up in writing and backed by an FOI request, usually moves the authority. But the authority knows the next step exists, and so should you.

## The law

> מפגע סביבתי – זיהום אויר, רעש, ריח, זיהום מים, זיהום מי־ים, זיהום על ידי פסולת, ... והכל כשהם בניגוד לחיקוק, לצו, לתכנית, לרשיון עסק או לכל היתר או רשיון אחר, או שיש בהם פגיעה בבריאותו של אדם או גרימת סבל ממשי לאדם;

*Gist:* an environmental hazard is pollution, noise, smell, waste and similar harms that break a law, order, plan, business licence or permit — or that harm a person's health or cause real suffering. ([1992 Law s. 1](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_למניעת_מפגעים_סביבתיים_(תביעות_אזרחיות)))

> (א) לבקשת מי שנפגע או עלול להיפגע ממפגע סביבתי או לבקשת גוף או עמותה כאמור בסעיף 6 רשאי בית משפט לתת צו המורה למי שגורם או עומד לגרום מפגע סביבתי ... (1) להימנע מהמעשה ... או להפסיק לעשותו; (2) לתקן את המעוות ...; (3) לעשות ככל הדרוש כדי למנוע הישנותו

*Gist:* anyone harmed or likely to be harmed, or a listed body, may ask the court (the Magistrates' Court, s. 1) to order the polluter to stop, to restore the previous state, and to prevent a repeat; negligence need not be proved (s. 4). ([s. 2](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_למניעת_מפגעים_סביבתיים_(תביעות_אזרחיות)))

> לא יגיש אדם תובענה לפי סעיף 2, אלא לאחר שעברו 60 ימים מהיום שהוא הודיע לרשות ולגורם המפגע על כוונתו להגיש תובענה, והתקיימו שניים אלה: (1) גורם המפגע לא נקט צעדים ממשיים ...; (2) הרשות לא נקטה צעדים ממשיים על־פי סמכויותיה ...

*Gist:* before suing you must notify both the authority (the Minister of Environmental Protection or delegate; for nature and trees, other authorities) and the polluter, and wait 60 days in which neither takes real steps. ([s. 5](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_למניעת_מפגעים_סביבתיים_(תביעות_אזרחיות)))

> (ה) כל אדם רשאי להגיש לממונה הערות לעניין טיוטת היתר פליטה בתוך 45 ימים ממועד פרסום ההודעה לפי סעיף קטן (ב); הערות שהוגשו יפורסמו באתר האינטרנט של המשרד.

*Gist:* anyone may comment on a draft emission permit within the period after the Ministry publishes it; the comments are published and must be considered before the permit is granted. ([Clean Air Law s. 21(ה)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_אוויר_נקי))

> מעשה או מחדל בניגוד להוראות לפי חוק זה, הם עוולה אזרחית

*Gist:* breaching the Clean Air Law is a civil wrong; bodies entitled under the 1992 Law may sue for it, with the consent of a specific person who was harmed (s. 71). ([s. 70](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_אוויר_נקי))

## How it works in practice

### Step 1: document

- **Log** each event: date, time, duration, what you smelled, saw or heard, wind direction, photos or video with timestamps, and who else noticed.
- **Data.** Download readings from the nearest station on [air.sviva.gov.il](https://air.sviva.gov.il/) for the same hours.
- **Health.** Note medical visits linked to the events (keep the documents yourself; share only what you choose).

### Step 2: report to the right body

The Ministry's own table of who handles what (updated 7.6.2026):

| Hazard | Who handles it | Contact |
|---|---|---|
| Industrial smell, smoke or burning (source not visible), chemical smell | Ministry environment hotline | \*6911 (24/7; emergencies extension 5) or 073-2733220 |
| Hazardous materials spill or exposure | Fire and Rescue + environment hotline | 102 and \*6911 |
| Asbestos after a fire or demolition | Environment hotline | \*6911 |
| Pesticide misuse | Environment hotline | \*6911 |
| Sewage or water leaks in open areas | Environment hotline | \*6911 |
| Sewage in built areas | Municipal hotline or water corporation | 106 |
| Noise | Police and municipal hotline | 100 or 106 |
| Injured wildlife | Nature and Parks Authority hotline | \*3639 |

Write down the call reference number. Then send a **written complaint** by the Ministry's online public-inquiries form or by e-mail to pniot@sviva.gov.il, with your log and exhibits, using the [environmental-complaint template](../../templates/environmental-complaint.md). A written request triggers the duty to decide and give reasons (lesson 02.5).

### Step 3: follow up and get the file

- After 45 days without a reply, send a reminder (Decisions and Reasons Law; lesson 05.3 for the FOI side).
- **FOI request** (lesson 05.2) to the Ministry or the local authority: inspection reports, the plant's business licence conditions, its emission permit, enforcement letters, monitoring results.

### Step 4: use the decision points

- **Emission permits.** Large plants need an emission permit under the Clean Air Law. The application is published on the Ministry's site; when the Ministry intends to grant it, it publishes the draft and anyone may comment within the period in s. 21(ה) (see [Current Numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)). The Ministry may hold a public hearing.
- **Planning.** If the plant needs a new plan, an expansion or a permit, object (lessons 10.3–10.4). Where a plan for a plant needing an emission permit is deposited, the objection and the permit comments may run together, with the later deadline (Clean Air Law s. 23(ד)(5)).

### Step 5: the citizen's suit (1992 Law)

1. **Notice.** Send the 60-day notice of intent to sue, by registered mail, to the polluter **and** to the Minister of Environmental Protection (or the competent authority) — Part B of the template.
2. **Wait** 60 days. If either the polluter or the authority takes real steps, the suit is not open.
3. **Sue** in the Magistrates' Court for an order to stop, restore and prevent (s. 2). The court weighs the harm to you and the public against the harm an order would cause (s. 3).
4. **Who.** You, if harmed or likely to be harmed; the bodies in the Schedule; and a registered amuta whose main aim is environmental protection if at least one member could sue (s. 6(ג)).
5. **Group.** A harmed person or such a body may sue for a group harmed by the same hazard (s. 10). Separately, the Class Actions Law 2006 allows class actions over an environmental hazard (Second Schedule item 6), usually with a lawyer. Which route fits is a question for an environmental clinic or NGO.
6. **Repeat hazard.** If the hazard returns within two years of the order, you may sue again without notice and claim compensation (s. 9).

Court fees and procedure for self-represented people are covered in Module 11.

## Worked example

**Situation.** Every few nights, smoke from burning waste in fields between a moshav and a neighbouring Arab town fills both communities. Dana Levi, from the moshav, and Kamal Haj Yahia, from the town, meet in a residents' WhatsApp group and decide to act together.

1. **Document.** For three weeks both keep logs (time, smell, wind); Dana films the fire from the road; Kamal downloads PM2.5 readings from the nearest station showing spikes on the same nights.
2. **Report.** Each event is reported to \*6911; they keep 11 reference numbers. Kamal also calls the fire service when the fire is near homes.
3. **Written complaint.** A joint letter to the Ministry's district office and the regional council, signed by 40 residents of both communities, with the logs and the data; they ask for enforcement against the landowner, a site inspection and a written answer within 45 days.
4. **FOI.** A request to the Ministry for inspection reports on the site for the past two years.
5. **No real action.** After 45 days, a reminder; the Ministry answers that "the matter is under examination". The FOI answer shows two inspections and no enforcement.
6. **Notice.** Dana and Kamal send the 60-day notice under s. 5 to the landowner and to the Minister, attaching the evidence, and send a copy to an environmental organisation listed in the Schedule.
7. **Outcome.** Within the 60 days the Ministry issues an administrative order and the landowner fences the site. The notice did its job without a suit.

**Mirror test.** The same steps serve residents of a Palestinian village complaining about sewage from a nearby settlement's treatment plant, or a settlement's residents complaining about waste burning near their homes: document, report, write, FOI, notice — each to the authority that has the power in that area.

## Do it yourself

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Act in your own name only. This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing. Do not trespass to document a hazard: film from public places. If there is a fire or a hazardous-materials event, call 102 first.

### You need

- A real or realistic environmental hazard near you.
- [environmental-complaint.md](../../templates/environmental-complaint.md); the [FOI request template](../../templates/foi-request.md).
- 75 minutes.

### Steps

1. Identify the hazard and, using the table, the body that handles it.
2. Set up a log (the template has one) and fill in at least three entries (real or realistic).
3. Download air or other monitoring data for the same dates, if relevant.
4. Write the complaint (template Part A): facts, the law the hazard breaches, what you ask for, a 45-day answer date.
5. Write one FOI question for the inspection and permit file.
6. Check the Ministry's site for an emission permit or draft permit for the source; note any comment period.
7. Draft the 60-day notice (template Part B), addressed to the polluter and the Minister. Do not send it in this exercise.
8. Write a one-paragraph decision: which route (complaint, objection, permit comments, notice and suit, NGO or class action) fits this hazard, and why.

### Template

[environmental-complaint.md](../../templates/environmental-complaint.md) — Part A: complaint to the Ministry or the local authority; Part B: 60-day notice under s. 5 of the 1992 Law; an evidence log; a checklist.

### How you know it worked

- The complaint names the hazard, the source, the dates and the law, and asks for specific action by a date.
- The notice goes to both the polluter and the authority and states your intention to sue under s. 2.
- You can explain why you chose your route.

### Self-check

- [ ] My log has dates, times, descriptions and evidence for each event.
- [ ] I reported to the right body and kept reference numbers.
- [ ] My written complaint asks for specific action and a reply date.
- [ ] I know the 60-day notice goes to both the polluter and the authority.
- [ ] I know the suit is in the Magistrates' Court and needs no proof of negligence.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Only phone complaints, no written record | No duty to answer in writing; nothing to show a court | Send a written complaint with the reference numbers | Always follow a call with a letter |
| Notice sent only to the polluter | The 60 days did not start against the authority; suit premature | Send the notice to the authority and count again | Address both in one letter |
| Suing before 60 days pass | The claim may be dismissed | Withdraw and refile after the period | Calendar day 61 |
| Missing the emission-permit comment period | The permit is granted without your input | Ask the Ministry to consider late comments; use FOI and complaints | Check the Ministry's site for draft permits near you |
| Trespassing to film the source | Criminal and civil risk; evidence attacked | Stop; use public vantage points and official data | Document from public places |
| Accusing a named owner of crimes in public posts | Defamation risk (Module 8) | Correct the post | Report facts to the authority; say "the site", not "a criminal" |

## Review

- Document first: logs, timestamps, official monitoring data.
- Report to the body that handles the hazard (\*6911 for most environmental hazards), then complain in writing and use FOI.
- Comment on draft emission permits and object to plans: decisions are made there.
- Under the 1992 Law anyone harmed, listed bodies and environmental amutot may sue in the Magistrates' Court for an injunction, without proving negligence, after a 60-day notice to the polluter and the authority.
- Group claims (1992 Law s. 10) and class actions (2006 Law) exist; get advice on which fits.
