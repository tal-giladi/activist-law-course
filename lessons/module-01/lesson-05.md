# 01.5 · Reading a Statute

A statute is a precise machine: every word, comma and sub-number carries meaning. In this lesson you learn how an Israeli law is built, the dozen Hebrew connecting words that change what a section means, how to cite a provision and how to count a deadline, and you finish by taking apart the main law of your own cause with a reading sheet.

## Why it matters

Most mistakes in letters and petitions come from misreading a section: treating "רשאי" (may) as "must", missing an exception that starts with "למעט" (except), or counting a deadline from the wrong day. Authorities' lawyers read every word. If you read the same way, your letters become hard to dismiss, and you will often find that the law gives you more than the authority admits.

## Key terms

| עברית | Transliteration | English |
|---|---|---|
| סעיף / סעיף קטן | se'if / se'if katan | section / subsection, for example 20 and 20(א) |
| פסקה / פסקת משנה | piska / piskat mishne | paragraph (1) / sub-paragraph (א) inside a subsection |
| הגדרות | hagdarot | definitions section |
| תוספת | tosefet | schedule: a list at the end of the law |
| רשאי / חייב | rashai / chayav | may (discretion) / must (duty) |
| לרבות / למעט | lerabot / lema'et | including / except |
| ובלבד ש… | u-vilvad she… | provided that (a condition) |
| על אף / בכפוף ל… | al af / bechafuf le… | notwithstanding / subject to |
| דינו | dino | "his penalty is" (introduces a criminal penalty) |

## The law

### How a section is built

Courts Law s. 60(a), the small-claims jurisdiction, shows the numbering:

> (א) בית משפט לתביעות קטנות ידון בתביעה אזרחית שהגיש יחיד –
> (1) לתשלום סכום שאינו עולה על 30,000 שקלים חדשים …;
> (2) למתן צו להחלפת מצרך או לתיקונו או לביטול עסקה …

*Gist:* the small claims court hears a civil claim filed by an individual (natural person) for payment up to a set sum, or for an order to replace or repair goods or cancel a deal.

Read it as: section 60, subsection (א), paragraphs (1) and (2). The word "יחיד" (an individual) is a condition: a company or an amuta cannot sue there. The sum in the text is linked to the index; the current ceiling is on [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md). Source: [חוק בתי המשפט](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_בתי_המשפט).

### Counting periods

> (א) מקום שנקבעה תקופה קצובה במספר ימים או שבועות מיום פלוני, אותו יום לא יבוא במנין.

*Gist:* when a period is set in days or weeks from a certain day, that day itself is not counted.

> (ג) במנין ימי תקופה יבואו גם ימי מנוחה, פגרה או שבתון שעל פי חיקוק, זולת אם הם הימים האחרונים שבתקופה.

*Gist:* rest days and holidays count, unless they fall at the very end of the period.

Source: [חוק הפרשנות](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הפרשנות), s. 10. Procedural rules in specific courts may add their own counting rules; Module 11 covers them.

### Definitions travel

The Interpretation Law's definitions (s. 3) apply to every law unless the law says otherwise. A law's own definitions section usually comes first (often s. 1, "הגדרות"). Always read it before the operative sections.

## How it works in practice

### The anatomy of a law

1. **Title and year:** "חוק …, התשנ״ח–1998". The year in the title is the year it was passed.
2. **Amendment trail:** on Wikisource, a box at the top lists every amendment with its publication in the official gazette (ספר החוקים), and each section shows "תיקון: …" with the years it changed.
3. **Definitions:** usually s. 1 or s. 2.
4. **Operative sections:** who must or may do what, and how.
5. **Exceptions and savings:** "שמירת דינים", "סייג", "למעט".
6. **Penalties:** "דינו – …".
7. **Regulations power:** "השר רשאי להתקין תקנות" — this tells you which regulations to look for (lesson 01.3).
8. **Schedules (תוספות):** lists that the sections refer to, such as the first schedule of the Administrative Courts Law (lesson 01.4).

### The connecting words

| Word | What it does | Reading tip |
|---|---|---|
| חייב / "יעשה" / "ידון" | Creates a duty | The authority has no choice; ask why it did not act |
| רשאי / מוסמך | Gives a power or discretion | It may refuse, but it must use its discretion lawfully (Module 4) |
| לרבות | Widens the term | The list is not closed |
| למעט / אלא אם | Cuts out an exception | Check whether you fall into it |
| ובלבד ש… | Adds a condition | The power exists only if the condition is met |
| על אף | Overrides another rule | This section wins over the one it names |
| בכפוף ל… | Gives way to another rule | The other rule wins |
| "או" vs "ו" | Alternatives vs cumulative conditions | One of them is enough vs all of them are needed |

### Citing a provision

Hebrew legal writing cites the smallest unit, then the law's full title: "סעיף 20(3) לחוק לשכת עורכי הדין, התשכ״א–1961". After the first full citation you may write "החוק" or a short name you defined. Module 2 (lesson 02.4) covers citation in full.

### Always check you have the current text

Wikisource marks repealed text with strike-through and notes amendments. Before you rely on a section, check its "תיקון" note and, for anything recent, the Knesset database (lesson 02.1).

## Worked example

Miriam volunteers with a disability-rights group. An elderly neighbour with limited mobility asks Miriam to appear for her in small claims court against a pharmacy that refused to make an entrance accessible. Can she?

**Step 1 — Find the section.** Courts Law, chapter on small claims, s. 63, "ייצוג".

**Step 2 — Read the structure.** Subsection (א): a lawyer may represent only with the court's permission, for special reasons. Subsection (ב): "על אף הוראות סעיף קטן (א)" — notwithstanding (א) — a person may represent a party with the court's approval, if the party gave a power of attorney filed with the court.

**Step 3 — Read the exceptions.** Subsection (ב) continues: "אין לייצג אדם לפי סעיף קטן זה" if the representative (1) represents as a regular practice, (2) does it in the ordinary course of business, or (3) does it for payment. The three paragraphs are alternatives: any one of them bars her.

**Step 4 — Apply.** Miriam is unpaid, does not do this regularly, and it is not her business. With a filed power of attorney and the court's approval, she may represent her neighbour in small claims.

**Step 5 — Connect to lesson 01.1.** This is a special rule (Bar Association Law s. 21(3) leaves room for laws that regulate representation). It does not let Miriam represent anyone in any other court, and if her group starts doing this regularly, paragraph (1) applies.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- The main law of your cause from lesson 01.2, open on Wikisource.
- The statute reading sheet template, 40 minutes.

### Steps

1. Write the full title and year. Note the date of the latest amendment from the box at the top.
2. Find the definitions section. Copy two definitions that matter for your cause.
3. List the chapters (פרקים) by name.
4. Pick the one section that matters most to your cause. Break it into subsections and paragraphs, one per line.
5. Circle every connecting word from the table above and write what each one does in that section.
6. Find whether the law has a regulations power and a schedule. Note the section numbers.
7. Find a deadline in the law (or use the FOI 30-day answer period, Freedom of Information Law s. 7(b)). Count it from a real date: your request is received on a Thursday; which day is the last day? Use Interpretation Law s. 10.
8. Write the full Hebrew citation of your key section.

### How you know it worked

Your key section is broken down so that someone else can see every condition and exception at a glance. Your citation names the smallest unit and the full title with the year. Your deadline count does not include the starting day.

<details>
<summary>Deadline check</summary>

A request received on Thursday 1 October 2026 with a 30-day period: 1 October is not counted (s. 10(a)), so day 1 is 2 October and day 30 is 31 October. Saturdays and holidays inside the period count (s. 10(c)). But 31 October 2026 is a Saturday, a rest day at the very end of the period, so it does not count and the last day becomes Sunday 1 November.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reading "רשאי" as a duty | You demand something the authority may lawfully refuse | Argue about how it used its discretion instead | Mark every duty and power word before you write |
| Missing an exception after "למעט" | Your letter relies on a rule that does not cover you | Re-read the full section, to the last paragraph | Read every section to its end, including the schedule it cites |
| Reading an old, repealed version | You cite law that no longer exists | Check the amendment notes and the Knesset text | Check "תיקון" notes every time |
| Counting the first day | You file one day late | Ask the court to extend time, explaining the error | Apply Interpretation Law s. 10(a) |
| Vague citation ("according to the law") | The reader cannot check you; you look unsure | Add section, subsection and full title | Cite the smallest unit every time |

## Review

- A law has a title, an amendment trail, definitions, operative sections, exceptions, penalties, a regulations power and schedules.
- Connecting words decide the meaning: duty or power, including or except, conditions and overrides.
- Cite the smallest unit and the full title with the year.
- When counting days, the starting day is not counted, and rest days count unless they are the last days.
- Always check you are reading the current version.
