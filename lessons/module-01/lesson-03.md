# 01.3 · The Hierarchy of Norms

Not every rule weighs the same. A Basic Law outranks a law, a law outranks a regulation, and a regulation outranks a ministry's internal procedure. In this lesson you learn the order, how to check that a lower rule really has a source above it, and you finish by building a hierarchy ladder for your own cause.

## Why it matters

Many of the battles activists win are won on the hierarchy. A municipality enforces an internal procedure that no law authorises. A minister's regulation goes further than the law that allowed it. A clerk says "those are the rules" about a page on a website that is not a rule at all. If you know which norm wins and how to trace a rule to its source, you can ask the right question in your letter: "Under which section did you act?"

## The law

### The Interpretation Law's vocabulary

> ”חיקוק“ – חוק או תקנה;
> ”תקנה“ – הוראה שניתנה מכוח חוק והיא בת־פעל תחיקתי.

*Gist:* an "enactment" is a law or a regulation; a "regulation" is a legislative rule made under the power of a law.

> ”הוראת מינהל“ – הוראה או מינוי – לרבות הודעה, מודעה, רשיון, היתר וכיוצא באלה – שניתנו בכתב מכוח חוק ואינם בני־פעל תחיקתי;

*Gist:* an "administrative instruction" (a notice, licence, permit and so on) is also given under a law, but it is not legislation.

Source: [חוק הפרשנות](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הפרשנות), s. 3. The key words in both definitions are "מכוח חוק" (under the power of a law): below the statute, every rule needs a source above it.

### The limitation clause

> אין פוגעים בזכויות שלפי חוק־יסוד זה אלא בחוק ההולם את ערכיה של מדינת ישראל, שנועד לתכלית ראויה, ובמידה שאינה עולה על הנדרש או לפי חוק כאמור מכוח הסמכה מפורשת בו.

*Gist:* rights under this Basic Law may be infringed only by a law that fits the state's values, for a proper purpose, to no greater extent than necessary, or under such a law by express authorisation in it (Basic Law: Human Dignity and Liberty, s. 8).

> אין בחוק־יסוד זה כדי לפגוע בתקפו של דין שהיה קיים ערב תחילתו של חוק־היסוד.

*Gist:* the Basic Law does not affect the validity of law that existed before it came into force in 1992 (s. 10).

> כל רשות מרשויות השלטון חייבת לכבד את הזכויות שלפי חוק־יסוד זה.

*Gist:* every government authority must respect the rights in this Basic Law (s. 11).

Source: [חוק־יסוד: כבוד האדם וחירותו](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק-יסוד:_כבוד_האדם_וחירותו); [English translation (Knesset)](https://main.knesset.gov.il/EN/activity/documents/BasicLawsPDF/BasicLawLiberty.pdf).

## How it works in practice

### The ladder

| Rank | Norm | Who makes it | What it can do |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Basic Laws (חוקי יסוד) | The Knesset | Set the structure of government and basic rights |
| 2 | Laws and ordinances (חוקים, פקודות) | The Knesset (ordinances: the Mandate) | Anything within the Basic Laws; a law limiting a protected right must meet s. 8 |
| 3 | Regulations (תקנות), municipal by-laws (חוקי עזר) | A minister, or a local council, under a section in a law | Only what the enabling section allows |
| 4 | Administrative instructions, permits, orders | An authority, under a law | Apply the law to a case |
| 5 | AG guidelines, ministry procedures (הנחיות, נהלים) | The Attorney General, a ministry or an authority | Guide how officials use their powers; they cannot create new powers |

Rulings are not a rung of their own: they interpret every rung, and Supreme Court rulings bind lower courts (lesson 01.2).

### Three checks you can always make

1. **Source check.** Every regulation opens with its source, in a formula such as "בתוקף סמכותי לפי סעיף … לחוק …" (by my power under section … of the … Law). Open that section and check that it really allows what the regulation does.
2. **Conflict check.** A regulation that contradicts a law, or a procedure that contradicts a regulation, loses. You can ask the authority to explain the conflict in writing.
3. **Rights check.** If a law or a decision limits a right in a Basic Law, it must meet the limitation clause. Lessons 03.2 and 03.3 teach how courts apply it.

### Two contested points, presented from both sides

- **Judicial review of laws.** In *Mizrahi Bank* (1995) the Supreme Court held that Basic Laws have constitutional status and that a court can void a law that breaks the limitation clause. Supporters call this the "constitutional revolution" that protects rights; critics say the Knesset never meant to give courts that power. Both views are presented in Module 3.
- **Review of Basic Laws themselves.** In HCJ 5658/23 (1 January 2024), 12 of 15 justices held that the Court may review Basic Laws in extreme cases, and 8 of 15 voided the amendment that limited the reasonableness ground. The government and some justices argued the Court has no such power. There is still no Basic Law that sets how Basic Laws are made.

### AG guidelines and procedures

The Attorney General's guidelines (on gov.il, filterable by chapter) state the law as the government's legal adviser reads it. For decades the government has treated them as binding on its ministries; since 2023 some ministers have challenged that, and the issue has been before the High Court (Module 3). For you, they are a powerful tool: if a ministry departs from a published guideline or its own published procedure, you can ask it to explain why.

## Worked example

A city council debates a by-law (חוק עזר) on whether grocery stores may open on Shabbat. One group of residents wants stores closed; another wants them open. Both want to know what the council can actually do.

**Step 1 — Find the enabling law.** By-laws are made under a law that gives local councils power to make them (in most cities, the Municipalities Ordinance). Each side opens the by-law, reads its opening formula, and finds the section it relies on.

**Step 2 — Source check.** Each side reads that section: does it allow the council to regulate opening hours, and on what grounds?

**Step 3 — Conflict check.** Is there a national law (for example on hours of work and rest) that already regulates the same point? A by-law cannot contradict it.

**Step 4 — Approval and rights.** Some by-laws need a minister's approval before they take effect, and a by-law that limits freedom of occupation must be justified. Both sides note these as questions for their letters.

**Result:** each side writes to the council asking under which section it acts, and whether the minister's approval was obtained. Neither side argues "it's unfair" alone: both argue from the hierarchy. (The facts here are a training scenario, not a summary of any one city's case.)

## Do it yourself

### You need

- Your cause sentence and the three laws from lesson 01.2.
- The [Open Law Book](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/ספר_החוקים_הפתוח), the [AG guidelines database](https://www.gov.il/he/Departments/DynamicCollectors/legal-advisor-guidelines) (opens in a normal browser), 40 minutes.

### Steps

1. **Top rung.** Pick the Basic Law right your cause touches (for example dignity, liberty, property, privacy, or freedom of occupation). Copy the section number from the Basic Law on Wikisource.
2. **Law rung.** Pick the main law from lesson 01.2.
3. **Regulation rung.** On the law's Wikisource page, look for links to regulations made under it (often listed as "תקנות" at the bottom or on the Open Law Book index). Open one and copy its opening formula "בתוקף סמכותי לפי סעיף …".
4. **Source check.** Open the enabling section in the law. Write one line: does it allow what the regulation does?
5. **Bottom rung.** Search the AG guidelines database or the ministry's website for a guideline or procedure (נוהל) on your topic. Note its number and date.
6. Draw your ladder: four rungs, each with a name, a section number and a link.

### How you know it worked

Every rung has a working link. The regulation's opening formula names a section, and you opened that section and wrote whether it supports the regulation. If you could not find a regulation or a guideline, you wrote down where you looked; that is a valid result too.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Arguing against a procedure without checking its source | You miss the strongest argument: it may have no source | Ask in writing under which section the procedure was issued | Always run the source check |
| Citing a website FAQ as if it were law | The authority or the court ignores it | Find the law, regulation or published guideline behind it | Quote only enactments, guidelines and rulings |
| Attacking a pre-1992 law under the Basic Law | The argument fails because of s. 10 | Argue that the law should be interpreted in light of the Basic Law instead | Check the law's date before invoking the limitation clause |
| Presenting the judicial review debate as settled | You lose credibility with readers who disagree | Present both positions, attributed | Quote rulings and official positions, not slogans |

## Review

- Basic Laws, then laws and ordinances, then regulations and by-laws, then administrative instructions, then guidelines and procedures.
- Below the statute, every rule needs a source: "מכוח חוק". Find the enabling section and read it.
- A law that limits a right in Basic Law: Human Dignity and Liberty must meet the limitation clause in s. 8, but s. 10 protects laws from before 1992.
- Judicial review of laws and of Basic Laws is contested; present both sides.
- AG guidelines and published procedures are tools: ask an authority to explain any departure from them.
