# 01.4 · The Map of Courts and Tribunals

Filing in the wrong forum is one of the most common ways to lose before you start: the case is struck out, time runs out, and sometimes you pay costs. In this lesson you learn the map of Israeli courts and tribunals, how to find which one hears a dispute, and you finish by mapping three disputes in your own cause to their forum and appeal route.

## Why it matters

The same problem can belong to very different forums. A refusal to release information goes to one court, a cabinet decision to another, a dismissal from work to a third, and a small money claim to a fourth. Each has its own fee, deadline and rules about who may appear. The forum also shapes your strategy: the High Court of Justice may hear a case of public importance quickly, while an appeals committee may be the only place you can win a planning dispute on the facts.

## The law

### Who holds judicial power

> (א) אלה בתי המשפט שסמכות שפיטה נתונה בידיהם: (1) בית המשפט העליון; (2) בית משפט מחוזי; (3) בית משפט שלום; (4) בית משפט אחר שנקבע בחוק כבית משפט;
> (ב) סמכות שפיטה נתונה גם בידי אלה: (1) בית דין דתי; (2) בית דין אחר; (3) רשות אחרת והכל כפי שנקבע בחוק.

*Gist:* the courts are the Supreme, District and Magistrates' Courts and other courts set by law; religious courts, other tribunals and other bodies also judge, as set by law (Basic Law: The Judiciary, s. 1).

### The High Court of Justice

> בית המשפט העליון ישב גם כבית משפט גבוה לצדק; בשבתו כאמור ידון בענינים אשר הוא רואה צורך לתת בהם סעד למען הצדק ואשר אינם בסמכותו של בית משפט או של בית דין אחר.

*Gist:* the Supreme Court also sits as the High Court of Justice and hears matters where it sees a need to give relief for the sake of justice and that are not within the jurisdiction of another court or tribunal (s. 15(c)).

Section 15(d)(2) lets it order state authorities, local authorities and anyone with public functions under law to act or to refrain from acting. Source: [חוק־יסוד: השפיטה](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק-יסוד:_השפיטה).

### The Administrative Affairs Court

> בית משפט לענינים מינהליים ידון באלה – (1) עתירה נגד החלטה של רשות או של גוף המנוי בתוספת הראשונה בענין המנוי בתוספת הראשונה …

*Gist:* the Administrative Affairs Court hears petitions against decisions of authorities on the subjects listed in the first schedule to the law (Administrative Courts Law, s. 5).

Source: [חוק בתי משפט לענינים מינהליים](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_בתי_משפט_לענינים_מינהליים). The first schedule is the list you check: if your subject is on it, start there, not at the High Court.

### Appeal

> פסק דין של בית משפט בערכאה ראשונה ניתן לערעור בזכות, להוציא פסק דין של בית המשפט העליון.

*Gist:* a first-instance judgment can be appealed as of right, except a Supreme Court judgment (Basic Law: The Judiciary, s. 17).

### Where to find the rest

In the [Courts Law](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_בתי_המשפט), search the page for:

- s. 51 — the Magistrates' Court: crimes with up to seven years' prison, and civil claims up to a ceiling (see [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md));
- s. 40 — the District Court: everything not given to the Magistrates' Court, administrative matters, and appeals from the Magistrates' Court;
- s. 55 — the Local Affairs Court (municipal by-law and planning offences);
- s. 60 and s. 63 — small claims, and who may represent there.

In the [Labour Court Law](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_בית_הדין_לעבודה), s. 24 gives the regional labour courts exclusive jurisdiction over disputes between employees and employers.

## How it works in practice

### The map

| Forum | Hears | Typical activist use |
|---|---|---|
| Supreme Court as High Court of Justice (בג״ץ) | Petitions against state bodies not given to another court; cabinet and ministerial decisions | Challenging a government decision or a law (Module 5, lesson 05.7) |
| Supreme Court as court of appeal | Appeals from District Courts; a further hearing (דיון נוסף) in larger panels | Rarely your first step |
| District Court | Large civil claims, serious crimes, appeals from Magistrates' Courts | Appeals; defamation claims above the ceiling |
| Administrative Affairs Court (in the District Court) | Petitions on subjects in the first schedule (for example FOI, planning, local authorities) | FOI petitions, planning, municipal decisions (lesson 05.6) |
| Magistrates' Court | Most criminal cases; civil claims up to a ceiling | Defence after a protest arrest (Module 7) |
| Small claims court (in the Magistrates' Court) | Money claims by individuals up to a ceiling | Consumer and discrimination claims (Modules 9, 11) |
| Local Affairs Court | By-law and planning offences | Fines for signs, stands, building offences |
| Labour courts | Employment disputes; some social security matters | Dismissal for activism, workplace discrimination |
| Family courts | Family matters | Rarely activist work |
| Religious courts (Jewish, Muslim, Christian, Druze) | Marriage, divorce and some personal-status matters, by statute | Religious-freedom causes meet them here |
| Tribunals and appeals committees (ועדות ערר and others) | Specific subjects set by law, for example planning appeals | Planning objections and appeals (Module 10) |
| Military courts | Soldiers; and in the West Bank, people charged under military orders | Covered in Modules 7 and 15 |

The amounts, fees and deadlines for each forum are kept in one place: [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md).

### Four questions to choose the forum

1. **Is it a crime, a private dispute, or a decision of an authority?**
2. **If it is an authority's decision:** is the subject listed in the first schedule of the Administrative Courts Law? If yes, the Administrative Affairs Court. If not, and it is a state body, the High Court of Justice.
3. **Does a special law send it elsewhere?** Labour, family, religious, planning appeals and other tribunals come first when a law gives them jurisdiction, sometimes exclusive.
4. **If it is money:** how much, and are you an individual? That decides between small claims, Magistrates' and District Court.

Before any court, ask whether there is a step you must take first, such as an objection, an internal appeal or a demand letter. Courts expect you to use them (exhaustion of remedies, lesson 04.5).

## Worked example

Residents of a neighbourhood near an industrial zone in Haifa Bay campaign about air pollution. Four disputes come up.

| Dispute | Question that decides | Forum |
|---|---|---|
| The Ministry of Environmental Protection does not answer an FOI request for emission data | FOI is a listed subject in the first schedule | Administrative Affairs Court, after the FOI steps (Module 5) |
| A plant worker is fired after speaking at a residents' meeting | Employee–employer dispute; Labour Court Law s. 24 | Regional Labour Court |
| A resident claims ₪5,000 from the plant for cleaning soot off her car | An individual's money claim below the small-claims ceiling | Small claims court |
| The cabinet decides to extend the plant's operation | A government decision, not in the first schedule | High Court of Justice |

The group learns that one campaign needs four different forums, each with its own fee, deadline and rules, and that the first schedule and the special laws decide most of them.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- Your cause and your hierarchy ladder from lesson 01.3.
- The [Administrative Courts Law](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_בתי_משפט_לענינים_מינהליים) open at the first schedule (search the page for "תוספת ראשונה").
- 35 minutes.

### Steps

1. Write three disputes that could arise in your cause: one with a public authority, one money or private dispute, and one of your choice.
2. For each, answer the four questions above in writing.
3. For the authority dispute, check the first schedule item by item. Copy the item number if your subject is there, or write "not listed".
4. Name the first forum and the appeal route (for example: Magistrates' Court, then District Court on appeal).
5. Next to each forum, write the line from [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md) that applies (fee, ceiling or deadline), or "not listed yet".

### How you know it worked

Each dispute has a forum, the statute section or schedule item that sends it there, and an appeal route. For the authority dispute you can show that you checked the first schedule. If two forums seemed possible, you wrote down why you chose one.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Petitioning the High Court on a subject listed in the first schedule | The petition is dismissed or transferred; time is lost | Refile in the Administrative Affairs Court, checking the deadline | Check the first schedule before choosing |
| Suing an employer in the Magistrates' Court | The court lacks jurisdiction; the case is struck out | Refile in the Labour Court | Ask "is this employee against employer?" first |
| Skipping the planning appeals committee | The court sends you back, or the deadline passes | File the appeal at the committee at once | Check whether a special tribunal comes first |
| Filing a large claim in small claims | The court may transfer it or you lose the excess | Reduce the claim or file in the Magistrates' Court | Compare the amount to current numbers |
| Going to court before writing to the authority | The petition fails for not exhausting remedies | Send a demand letter and wait for the answer | Always ask "what step comes first?" |

## Review

- Courts: Supreme (also as the High Court of Justice), District (also as the Administrative Affairs Court) and Magistrates' (also small claims and local affairs); plus labour, family, religious, military courts and tribunals.
- The first schedule of the Administrative Courts Law decides whether a petition against an authority starts in the District Court or the High Court.
- Special laws send some disputes to their own forum, often exclusively.
- First-instance judgments can be appealed as of right; check the route before you file.
- Fees, ceilings and deadlines live on the current numbers page.
