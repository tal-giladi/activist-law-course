# 01.2 · Where Israeli Law Comes From

Israeli law is built in layers: a little Ottoman law, a good deal of British Mandate law, religious law in personal matters, and on top of it all the Knesset's statutes and the Supreme Court's rulings. In this lesson you learn to recognise each layer, to see how a judge fills a gap when no law answers a question, and you finish by tracing the laws behind your own cause.

## Why it matters

When you find the rule that governs your issue, it may be called a "פקודה" (ordinance) from 1929, a "חוק" (law) from 2018, or a ruling from 1953. Activists often assume an old ordinance is weaker than a Knesset law, or that a court ruling is "only an opinion". Both mistakes lose arguments. Knowing where each layer comes from tells you how much weight it carries, where to find its current text, and what a court will do if nothing written answers your question.

## Key terms

| עברית | Transliteration | English |
|---|---|---|
| פקודה | pkuda | ordinance: a law from the British Mandate period (or the first months of the state) |
| נוסח חדש | nusach chadash | new version: an official Hebrew re-enactment of a Mandate ordinance |
| חוק | chok | law (statute) passed by the Knesset |
| הלכה פסוקה | halacha psuka | binding precedent: a legal rule laid down by a court |
| היקש | hekesh | analogy: applying a rule from a similar case |
| לאקונה | lacuna | a gap: a question no law or precedent answers |
| דין | din | "law" in the broad sense defined by the Interpretation Law |
| משפט עברי | mishpat ivri | Jewish law as a source of legal principles |

## The law

### Continuity in 1948

> המשפט שהיה קיים בארץ־ישראל ביום ה׳ באייר תש״ח (14 במאי 1948) יעמוד בתקפו, עד כמה שאין בו משום סתירה לפקודה זו או לחוקים האחרים שיינתנו על ידי מועצת המדינה הזמנית או על פיה, ובשינויים הנובעים מתוך הקמת המדינה ורשויותיה.

*Gist:* the law in force on 14 May 1948 stayed in force, except where it conflicted with the new state's laws (Law and Administration Ordinance, s. 11).

Source: [פקודת סדרי השלטון והמשפט](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/פקודת_סדרי_השלטון_והמשפט), s. 11.

### What counts as "law"

The Interpretation Law defines "דין" (law) to include, among others:

> (3)(א) אקט של הפרלמנט הבריטי או דבר המלך במועצתו או חלק מהם, או תקנות לפיהם, ודיני המשפט המקובל ועקרוני היושר של אנגליה, כפי תקפם במדינה;
> (ב) דינים עותומניים כפי תקפם במדינה;

*Gist:* British acts, the common law and equity of England, and Ottoman laws are part of Israeli law to the extent they are still in force.

The same section says "חוק" means "חוק של הכנסת או פקודה", so an ordinance is a law just like a Knesset statute. Source: [חוק הפרשנות](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הפרשנות), s. 3.

### Cutting the cord to English law, and filling gaps

The Foundations of Law Act, 1980 repealed Article 46 of the Palestine Order in Council, which had sent courts to English common law and equity whenever local law was silent. Section 2(b) keeps the law that was absorbed before 1980. Section 1 now says what a court does when nothing answers the question:

> ראה בית המשפט שאלה משפטית הטעונה הכרעה, ולא מצא לה תשובה בדבר חקיקה, בהלכה פסוקה או בדרך של היקש, יכריע בה לאור עקרונות החירות, הצדק, היושר והשלום של המשפט העברי ומורשת ישראל.

*Gist:* if a legal question has no answer in legislation, in precedent or by analogy, the court decides it in light of the principles of freedom, justice, equity and peace of Jewish law and Israel's heritage.

The current wording follows a 2018 (תשע״ח) amendment. Source: [חוק יסודות המשפט](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_יסודות_המשפט).

### Precedent

> (א) הלכה שנפסקה בבית משפט תנחה בית משפט של דרגה נמוכה ממנו.
> (ב) הלכה שנפסקה בבית המשפט העליון מחייבת כל בית משפט, זולת בית המשפט העליון.

*Gist:* a court's ruling guides the courts below it, and a Supreme Court ruling binds every court except the Supreme Court itself (Basic Law: The Judiciary, s. 20).

Source: [חוק־יסוד: השפיטה](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק-יסוד:_השפיטה).

## How it works in practice

### The five layers

| Layer | What it looks like | Examples you will meet in this course |
|---|---|---|
| Ottoman (before 1917) | Few rules left, mostly in land law | Land categories in old land claims |
| British Mandate (1917–1948) | "פקודה", often later re-issued as "[נוסח חדש]" | Police Ordinance [New Version] 1971 (protest permits, Module 6); Torts Ordinance [New Version] |
| Religious law | Applies by statute in personal status | Marriage and divorce of Jews in the rabbinical courts; other communities' religious courts |
| Knesset legislation (1949–) | "חוק", "חוק־יסוד", and regulations (תקנות) made under them | Freedom of Information Law 1998, Basic Law: Human Dignity and Liberty 1992 |
| Case law | Rulings, especially of the Supreme Court | Kol Ha'am (1953), Mizrahi Bank (1995) |

### Reading the title tells you the layer

- **"פקודת …"** means a Mandate ordinance. If it says "[נוסח חדש]", the binding text is the Hebrew re-enactment (Interpretation Law, s. 24).
- **"חוק …, התש״ן–1990"**: the Hebrew year and the civil year show when the Knesset passed it.
- **"חוק־יסוד: …"** is a Basic Law, the top layer (lesson 01.3).
- **"תקנות …"** are regulations made by a minister under a law (lesson 01.3).

### Where to read the current text

The free [Open Law Book on Wikisource](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/ספר_החוקים_הפתוח) has most laws in full, with amendments marked. The Knesset's National Legislation Database is the official record. Module 2 (lesson 02.1) teaches both in depth.

### The gap-filling order in practice

Courts almost never reach the last step of s. 1, because legislation, precedent and analogy usually give an answer. When you build an argument (Module 2), follow the same order: the statute first, then binding rulings, then similar cases, and only then general principles.

## Worked example

Avi organises a weekly protest outside a minister's home against a plan to cut national-service benefits. A friend tells him: "Protest permits come from an old British ordinance, so it doesn't really bind anyone any more."

**Step 1 — Find the source.** On the Open Law Book, Avi finds "פקודת המשטרה [נוסח חדש], התשל״א–1971". The word "פקודה" shows it began as a Mandate ordinance; "[נוסח חדש]" shows the Knesset period re-issued it in Hebrew in 1971.

**Step 2 — Check its weight.** The Interpretation Law defines "חוק" as a Knesset law or an ordinance. The ordinance is a law, with the same force as any statute.

**Step 3 — Check for limits.** Being a law, it sits below the Basic Laws. Courts read police powers over protests in light of freedom of expression, as in Kol Ha'am (1953), which binds lower courts under Basic Law: The Judiciary s. 20.

**Step 4 — Decide.** Avi plans as if the ordinance fully applies, and keeps the rulings on freedom of protest ready in case the police use it too broadly. Module 6 covers when a permit is actually required.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- The one-sentence cause you wrote in lesson 01.1.
- The [Open Law Book](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/ספר_החוקים_הפתוח) and 35 minutes.

### Steps

1. In the Open Law Book index, find three laws or ordinances that touch your cause. Try the obvious words first (for example "תכנון", "חינוך", "משטרה", "מידע", "עמותות", "שוויון").
2. For each one, write: the full title, the year, whether it is "פקודה", "חוק", "חוק־יסוד" or "תקנות", and whether it says "[נוסח חדש]".
3. Assign each one to a layer from the table above.
4. Search each page for a section that gives someone power to decide (look for "רשאי" (may) or "מוסמך" (authorised)). Copy that section number.
5. Write two lines: if no law answered a question in your cause, what order would a court follow to fill the gap?

You have three entries, each with a working Wikisource link, a layer, and a section number that gives someone a power. Your gap-filling answer lists legislation, precedent, analogy, then the principles in s. 1, in that order.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Treating an ordinance as weaker than a statute | Your argument ignores a binding rule | Re-read Interpretation Law s. 3: "חוק" includes "פקודה" | Check the title and the definition before you argue |
| Quoting the old English text of a re-issued ordinance | You cite a version that is no longer binding | Use the "[נוסח חדש]" Hebrew text | Always open the current Wikisource or Knesset text |
| Jumping straight to "justice and equity" | The court ignores an argument that skipped the statute and precedent | Rebuild the argument in the s. 1 order | Statute, precedent, analogy, principles, every time |
| Assuming English case law still binds | Out-of-date argument | Use Israeli rulings; English cases persuade at most | Remember Article 46 was repealed in 1980 |

## Review

- Law in force on 14 May 1948 stayed in force unless it conflicted with the new state's law.
- Ottoman, Mandate, religious, Knesset and case-law layers all still matter; an ordinance is a law.
- The Foundations of Law Act ended the automatic pull of English law and set the gap-filling order: legislation, precedent, analogy, principles.
- Supreme Court precedent binds every court except the Supreme Court itself.
- A law's title tells you its layer and where to look for its current text.
