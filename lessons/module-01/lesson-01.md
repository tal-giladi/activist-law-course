# 01.1 · What This Course Can and Cannot Make You

By the end of this lesson you will know exactly what this course gives you, where the legal line runs between acting for yourself and practising law without a licence, and when you should stop and get a lawyer. You will finish by sorting real situations onto the right side of that line and writing your own safety list.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> This free course teaches you to act for yourself in Israeli and international legal processes. It is not a law degree, does not license you to practise law, and is not legal advice. What this lesson says about the Bar Association Law is our reading of the law text, not legal advice and not a court ruling. Check current rules before you act, and act in your own name only.

## Why it matters

Israeli law lets you represent yourself almost everywhere: you can write to a ministry, file a Freedom of Information request, object to a building plan, sue in small claims court or petition the High Court of Justice in your own name, without a lawyer. This course teaches you to do those things well.

The same law reserves some acts to lawyers when they are done **for another person**. Activists run into this line all the time, because people around them ask for help: "Can you write my letter too?", "Can you come to court for me?", "What should I do about my eviction?" Helping in the wrong way can be a criminal offence, and it can also hurt the person you meant to help, because a wrongly drafted document has no lawyer's insurance, duty of confidentiality or professional responsibility behind it.

So the first skill in this course is knowing where the line is.

## The law

The core rule is section 20 of the Bar Association Law, 1961. Its opening words and the four reserved acts:

> הפעולות המנויות להלן, לא יעשה אותן דרך עיסוק, או בתמורה אף שלא דרך עיסוק, אלא עורך דין; ואלה הפעולות:

*Gist:* only a lawyer may do the acts listed below as an occupation, or for payment even when not as an occupation.

> (1) ייצוג אדם אחר וכל טיעון ופעולה אחרת בשמו לפני בתי משפט, בתי דין, בוררים וגופים ואנשים בעלי סמכות שיפוטית או מעין שיפוטית;

*Gist:* representing another person, or arguing or acting in their name, before courts, tribunals, arbitrators and quasi-judicial bodies.

Item (2) does the same for representation before a list of authorities, such as the Execution Office (הוצאה לפועל), the Land Registry and the tax assessor.

> (3) עריכת מסמכים בעלי אופי משפטי בשביל אדם אחר, לרבות ייצוג אדם אחר במשא ומתן משפטי לקראת עריכת מסמך כזה;

*Gist:* drafting documents of a legal character for another person, including negotiating for them toward such a document.

> (4) ייעוץ וחיווי דעת משפטיים.

*Gist:* giving legal advice and legal opinions.

Section 96 makes breaking the rule an offence:

> מי שאינו עורך דין ועושה פעולה שנתייחדה לפי חוק זה לעורכי דין, דינו – קנס כאמור בסעיף 61(א)(2) לחוק העונשין.

*Gist:* a non-lawyer who does an act reserved to lawyers is liable to a fine at the level set in Penal Law s. 61(a)(2).

Section 21 lists exceptions; for example, s. 21(2) leaves out "ייצוג לפני בתי דין דתיים ולפני בתי דין צבאיים" (representation before religious and military courts), and s. 21(3) leaves out bodies whose own law regulates who may represent before them. One example of such a law is the small claims court rule in Courts Law s. 63(b), which lets a non-lawyer represent a party with the court's approval, but not:

> (1) אם המייצג עוסק בייצוג בדרך קבע;
> (2) אם הייצוג נעשה במהלך הרגיל של עסקיו של המייצג;
> (3) אם הייצוג נעשה בתמורה.

*Gist:* not if the representative does this regularly, as part of their business, or for payment.

Full texts: [Bar Association Law](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_לשכת_עורכי_הדין) (search the page for "ייחוד פעולות המקצוע"), [Courts Law](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_בתי_המשפט) (search for "ייצוג"). The fine amounts are in [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md).

## How it works in practice

### Three questions that place any act on the map

This is our reading of the text. No ruling interpreting these sections has been verified for this course yet, so treat it as a careful working rule.

1. **For whom?** Acting for yourself is outside s. 20. You may sign, file, argue and appear in your own name.
2. **What act?** Representation, drafting a legal document for someone else, and individual legal advice are reserved. Explaining what the law says in general (what this course does), pointing to a public guide, or sharing a template for people to fill in themselves is general legal information.
3. **How?** The ban applies when the act is done "דרך עיסוק" (as an occupation) or "בתמורה" (for payment). A single unpaid favour is outside the words of the section, but organised, repeated help, even for free, can look like an occupation. A 2009 Bar ethics decision took a strict view of amutot offering free legal services. Module 18 covers groups and NGOs in depth.

| Usually fine | Grey zone: stop and check | Reserved to lawyers |
|---|---|---|
| Filing your own FOI request, objection or petition | Helping friends again and again, as a regular service | Representing someone else in court for money |
| Explaining in a workshop what the FOI Law says | Writing "your" version of a neighbour's letter for them | Drafting a contract, will or claim for someone, for a fee |
| Sharing a blank template "for use in your own name" | Telling one person what they should do in their case | Running a paid "appeals service" without a lawyer |
| A group of residents each signing their own objection | Collecting money "for expenses" while preparing documents | Legal opinions given as a business |

### When to stop and get help

Acting alone saves money, but some situations carry real risk:

- a criminal investigation, arrest or charge (the Public Defender may represent you; see Module 7);
- a threat of a defamation suit (Module 8);
- a case where you could be ordered to pay the other side's costs (הוצאות);
- strict deadlines you are unsure how to count, or a case that is already in court with a lawyer on the other side.

Free help exists: Legal Aid, the Public Defender, university clinics and NGOs across the spectrum. They are listed in [Where to Get Help](../../references/where-to-get-help.md).

### What this course gives you

A certificate of completion, skills, templates for use in your own name, and a portfolio of real filings if you do the practicum. It does not give you a degree, the right to sit the bar exam, or the right to act for others.

## Worked example

Yossi, a retired teacher from a moshav in the Galilee, is fighting the cancellation of a bus line. He files his own Freedom of Information request to the Ministry of Transport and a letter to the regional council. Neighbours hear about it and ask him to "do the same for them". One offers ₪100 per letter.

**Step 1 — For whom?** His own request and letter are self-representation. Fine.

**Step 2 — What act?** Writing a personalised letter of demand to an authority for a neighbour is drafting a document of a legal character for another person (s. 20(3)).

**Step 3 — How?** For ₪100 a letter, it is "בתמורה". Doing it for twenty neighbours, even free, starts to look "דרך עיסוק".

**What Yossi does instead:**

- He runs a free evening at the community centre explaining what the FOI Law and the duty to give reasons say, in general terms.
- He shares a blank template marked "for use in your own name", and each neighbour fills it in and signs it.
- He suggests the residents also send one joint letter, signed by all of them, as a group speaking for itself.
- He refuses the ₪100 and sends the one neighbour facing a personal fine to Legal Aid.

Result: the same pressure on the authority, and no reserved act done for others.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- 30 minutes and a notebook or document.
- The [Bar Association Law on Wikisource](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_לשכת_עורכי_הדין), open at s. 20.

### Steps

1. Read s. 20 in full on Wikisource, including item (2) with its list of authorities, and s. 21.
2. Sort each situation below into "own name", "general information" or "reserved — do not do". Write one line of reasoning for each, using the three questions.
   1. You file a planning objection against a tower next to your house.
   2. You post a video explaining how the 30-day FOI deadline works.
   3. A friend pays you ₪300 to write her appeal against a parking fine.
   4. Your protest group asks you to appear in court for a member who was arrested.
   5. You send your neighbours a blank objection template "for use in your own name".
   6. You tell a stranger on Facebook exactly what to write to win his eviction case.
   7. Fifty residents each sign their own copy of an objection they wrote together at a meeting.
   8. You open a weekly paid "help desk" for welfare appeals, with no lawyer involved.
3. Write your personal safety list: three situations in which you will get a lawyer, a clinic or Legal Aid, with the phone number or website of one free service from [Where to Get Help](../../references/where-to-get-help.md).
4. Write one sentence naming the cause you care about. You will use it in every lesson of this module and in the project.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Drafting letters or claims for others for money | Possible offence under s. 96; no protection for the person you "helped" | Stop, return any money, send them to a clinic or Legal Aid | Share blank templates for use in their own name only |
| Appearing in court "for" a friend | The court may refuse to hear you; possible offence | Let the friend appear for themselves or get the Public Defender | Help people prepare to speak for themselves |
| Treating general information as advice about a specific case | The person relies on you and misses a deadline or remedy | Tell them clearly you are not giving advice, and refer them | Talk about what the law says, not what they should do |
| Acting alone in a criminal matter | Statements and choices that are hard to undo | Ask for the Public Defender or a lawyer at once | Keep your safety list with you |

## Review

- You may act for yourself almost everywhere; this course teaches you to do it well.
- Bar Association Law s. 20 reserves representing others, drafting legal documents for others and legal advice to lawyers, when done as an occupation or for payment; s. 96 makes it an offence.
- Three questions place any act: for whom, what act, and how (occupation or payment).
- Sharing general information and blank templates for use in people's own names is the safe way to help.
- Some situations call for a lawyer, a clinic or Legal Aid; know which before you need them.
