# 01.6 · Reading a Ruling

A judgment (פסק דין) can run from three pages to three hundred, but every one has the same skeleton. In this lesson you learn to find your way through that skeleton, to decode case numbers, and to separate the rule that binds from everything else the judges said, and you finish by writing a one-page brief of a real ruling.

## Why it matters

Rulings are where the law comes alive. Your letters and petitions will be stronger when you can quote the holding of a relevant ruling accurately, and weaker if you quote a minority opinion or a passing remark as if it were the law. Authorities' lawyers will notice. Reading rulings also shows you what judges actually care about: facts, procedure, and whether the petitioner did everything required first.

## Key terms

| עברית | Transliteration | English |
|---|---|---|
| פסק דין / החלטה | psak din / hachlata | judgment (final) / decision (interim or procedural) |
| עותר / משיב | oter / meshiv | petitioner / respondent |
| מערער / תובע / נתבע | me'arer / tove'a / nitba | appellant / plaintiff / defendant |
| הלכה | halacha | holding: the legal rule the case decides, which binds as precedent |
| אמרת אגב (אוביטר) | imrat agav (obiter) | a remark in passing, not binding |
| דעת רוב / דעת מיעוט | da'at rov / da'at mi'ut | majority opinion / minority (dissenting) opinion |
| צו על תנאי / צו מוחלט | tzav al tnai / tzav muchlat | order nisi (show-cause order) / order made absolute |
| הוצאות | hotza'ot | costs: money one side is ordered to pay the other |
| הרכב | herkev | the panel of judges |

## The law

> (א) הלכה שנפסקה בבית משפט תנחה בית משפט של דרגה נמוכה ממנו.
> (ב) הלכה שנפסקה בבית המשפט העליון מחייבת כל בית משפט, זולת בית המשפט העליון.

*Gist:* a holding of a court guides the courts below it; a holding of the Supreme Court binds every court except the Supreme Court itself (Basic Law: The Judiciary, s. 20).

Source: [חוק־יסוד: השפיטה](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק-יסוד:_השפיטה). The key word is "הלכה": only the rule the case decides binds, not every sentence in the judgment.

## How it works in practice

### Decoding the case number

A case number has three parts: the type of proceeding, a running number and the year.

| Prefix | Meaning | Court |
|---|---|---|
| בג״ץ | High Court of Justice petition | Supreme Court |
| ע״א / ע״פ | civil appeal / criminal appeal | Supreme Court (or District Court on appeal) |
| רע״א / רע״פ | leave to appeal, civil / criminal | Supreme Court |
| עע״ם | administrative appeal | Supreme Court, on an Administrative Affairs Court judgment |
| דנג״ץ / דנ״א | further hearing on a High Court / civil appeal ruling | Supreme Court, larger panel |
| עת״מ | administrative petition | District Court as Administrative Affairs Court |
| ת״א / ת״ק | civil claim / small claim | Magistrates' or District Court / small claims court |

Older and Supreme Court numbers look like "73/53" (case 73 of 1953) or "5658/23" (case 5658 of 2023). Lower courts now use a number–month–year form, for example "עת״מ 35192-09-24" (an administrative petition opened in September 2024).

### The skeleton of a judgment

1. **Header:** court, case number, panel (הרכב), parties, counsel, date of hearing and of judgment.
2. **Summary or opening:** many recent Supreme Court judgments open with a short paragraph saying what the case is about and the result. Read it first.
3. **Facts (העובדות):** what happened, as the court found it.
4. **Arguments (טענות הצדדים):** each side's position.
5. **Discussion (דיון):** the law, the precedents, the analysis. The holding lives here.
6. **Result (סוף דבר / התוצאה):** petition granted or dismissed, order made absolute or cancelled, and costs.
7. **Other opinions:** in a multi-judge panel, each judge may write. Look for "אני מסכים" (I agree), "דעתי שונה" (my view differs) and the final paragraph that states the result "כאמור בפסק דינו של …" (as in the judgment of …).

### Finding the holding

Ask: what legal question did the court have to answer to reach this result, and what rule did it apply? That rule, at the level of generality the court used, is the holding. Everything else (wider reflections, hypothetical cases, comments on issues the court did not need to decide) is a remark in passing. A minority opinion is never the holding, even when it is well reasoned.

### Where to read rulings for free

- **English:** [Versa](https://versa.cardozo.yu.edu/), the Cardozo Israeli Supreme Court Project, has free translations of landmark opinions.
- **Hebrew:** the Supreme Court's free search at [supremedecisions.court.gov.il](https://supremedecisions.court.gov.il/) covers rulings from about 1997 on. Lesson 02.2 teaches how to search it.
- Nevo's case-law pages are paid; this course never sends you there.

## Worked example

**The ruling:** *Kol Ha'am Co. Ltd. v. Minister of Interior*, HCJ 73/53, decided 16 October 1953. Two newspapers of the Communist Party, Kol Ha'am (Hebrew) and Al-Ittihad (Arabic), were suspended by the Minister of Interior under the Press Ordinance after publishing articles attacking government policy.

**Step 1 — Decode the header.** בג״ץ 73/53: a High Court of Justice petition from 1953. Two petitions were heard together (73/53 and 87/53). Panel: Agranat, Sussman and Landau JJ. Respondent: the Minister of Interior, represented by the Attorney General.

**Step 2 — Find the question.** The Ordinance allowed suspension of a newspaper whose publication was "likely to endanger the public peace". Does "likely" mean a mere tendency, or something stronger?

**Step 3 — Find the holding.** The Minister may suspend a newspaper only where there is a "near certainty" (probability, not a bad tendency) that the publication will endanger the public peace, after weighing the danger against freedom of expression. Applying that test, the court found the Minister had exceeded his power in both cases.

**Step 4 — Find the result.** "Orders nisi made absolute": the suspension orders were cancelled.

**Step 5 — Remarks in passing.** The long discussion of democracy and of the Declaration of Independence as a guide to interpretation is famous and often quoted, but the binding core is the near-certainty test for this power.

**Step 6 — Why it still matters.** The near-certainty test has been applied in later cases on expression and protest, across the political spectrum, from left-wing newspapers in 1953 to protests of every kind since (Module 6).

## Do it yourself

### You need

- About 60 minutes and the ruling brief template.
- One ruling from this list (all free on Versa, in English), or a Hebrew ruling on your own cause from the Supreme Court search:
  - *Kol Ha'am v. Minister of Interior*, HCJ 73/53 (freedom of the press) — <https://versa.cardozo.yu.edu/opinions/kol-haam-co-ltd-v-minister-interior>
  - *Ka'adan v. Israel Land Administration*, HCJ 6698/95 (equality in land allocation) — <https://versa.cardozo.yu.edu/node/167>
  - *Ressler v. Minister of Defence*, HCJ 910/86 (standing; draft deferrals for yeshiva students) — <https://versa.cardozo.yu.edu/opinions/ressler-v-minister-defence>
  - *Movement for Quality Government v. Knesset*, HCJ 5658/23 (review of a Basic Law amendment; official abstract) — <https://versa.cardozo.yu.edu/node/704>

### Steps

1. Read the header and decode the case number, the court, the panel and the parties.
2. Read the abstract or opening and the result first, so you know where the judgment is going.
3. Write the legal question in one sentence.
4. Write the facts in no more than five lines.
5. Find the holding and write it in one or two sentences, in your own words. Copy one short quote that states it.
6. Note any majority and minority, and one remark in passing.
7. Write two lines on how this ruling could help, or limit, your own cause.

### How you know it worked

Your brief fits on one page. Someone who has not read the ruling can tell from it which court decided what, on which question, with what result, and which sentence is the binding rule. Your holding is a rule, not a story: it would decide a similar future case.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Quoting a minority opinion as "the court held" | Your credibility drops; the argument fails | Quote the majority, or say clearly "in a minority opinion" | Check who joined which opinion before you quote |
| Quoting a remark in passing as binding | The other side shows it is not a holding | Rebuild the argument on the holding, use the remark as support | Ask "did the court need this to decide the case?" |
| Citing a ruling that was later reversed in a further hearing | You rely on law that no longer holds | Search for a later דנג״ץ or דנ״א on the same case | Search the case number before citing it |
| Citing a Nevo link | The reader hits a paywall | Use Versa or the Supreme Court search | Use only free sources |
| Reading only the summary in the news | You miss the conditions in the holding | Read the opening, the holding and the result yourself | Always read the judgment, at least the key parts |

## Review

- Every judgment has a header, facts, arguments, discussion, a result and, in panels, several opinions.
- A case number tells you the court, the type of proceeding and the year.
- Only the holding (הלכה) binds, and Supreme Court holdings bind every court except the Supreme Court.
- Minority opinions and remarks in passing can persuade, but never quote them as the law.
- Versa and the Supreme Court search are free; Nevo case law is not.
