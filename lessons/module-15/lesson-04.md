# 15.4 · ICJ Advisory Opinions and Israel's Positions

Lesson 13.4 explained how the International Court of Justice works and what an advisory opinion is. This lesson looks at what the Court actually said in its three opinions on Israel and the territories (2004, 2024 and 2025), what the judges who disagreed said, what Israel's government argued and answered, and where *South Africa v. Israel* stands. You finish by writing a short summary of one finding, with every claim attributed and every paragraph numbered.

> [!NOTE]
> These opinions concern an active conflict and are strongly contested. The course reports the Court's findings, the dissenting and separate opinions, Israel's official positions and scholars' views, each in their own words. It does not say which is right.

## Why it matters

Few legal texts are quoted more, and more loosely, than these three opinions. Supporters cite them as "the law"; opponents dismiss them as "political". Both often skip the actual wording, the vote, the separate opinions and the limits of what was asked. Activists who can say "by eleven votes to four, the Court was of the opinion that … ; Judges Tomka, Abraham and Aurescu disagreed because … ; Israel's government responded that …" are taken seriously by courts, journalists and officials on every side.

## The law

### 2004: the wall

> The construction of the wall being built by Israel, the occupying Power, in the Occupied Palestinian Territory, including in and around East Jerusalem, and its associated régime, are contrary to international law;

*Gist:* adopted by fourteen votes to one (Judge Buergenthal against) (para. 163(3)A). The Court found GC IV applicable (para. 101), the settlements contrary to art. 49(6) (para. 120) and self-defence under Charter art. 51 not relevant (para. 139).

### 2024: policies and practices

> Is of the opinion that the State of Israel's continued presence in the Occupied Palestinian Territory is unlawful;

*Gist:* by eleven votes to four (Vice-President Sebutinde, Judges Tomka, Abraham, Aurescu against) (para. 285(3)). Also: an obligation to end that presence "as rapidly as possible" (11–4); to "cease immediately all new settlement activities, and to evacuate all settlers" (14–1); to make reparation (14–1); and duties of non-recognition for states and the UN (12–3) (para. 285(4)–(9)).

### 2025: the UN, UNRWA and other bodies

> to agree to and facilitate by all means at its disposal relief schemes on behalf of the population of the Occupied Palestinian Territory so long as that population is inadequately supplied, as has been the case in the Gaza Strip, including relief provided by the United Nations and its entities, in particular the United Nations Relief and Works Agency for Palestine Refugees in the Near East, other international organizations and third States, and not to impede such relief;

*Gist:* by ten votes to one (Vice-President Sebutinde against) (para. 223(3)(b)). Unanimous findings include the duty to ensure essential supplies, to respect relief personnel, the prohibition of forcible transfer and deportation, ICRC visits to detainees and the prohibition of starvation as a method of warfare (para. 223(3)(a), (c)–(f)); findings (4)–(8) on human rights, cooperation with the UN and UN privileges and immunities were adopted 10–1.

Full texts: [2004 (A/ES-10/273)](https://documents.un.org/api/symbol/access?s=A/ES-10/273&l=en&t=pdf); [2024 (A/78/968)](https://documents.un.org/api/symbol/access?s=A/78/968&l=en&t=pdf); [2025 summary](https://www.un.org/unispal/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/196-20251022-sum-01-00-en.pdf); case pages [131](https://www.icj-cij.org/case/131), [186](https://www.icj-cij.org/case/186), [196](https://www.icj-cij.org/case/196).

## How it works in practice

### 2004: who says what

| Point | The Court | Disagreement or response |
|---|---|---|
| Should the Court answer at all? | Yes, 14–1 | Judge Buergenthal: the Court "did not have before it the requisite factual bases for its sweeping findings", especially on Israel's "legitimate right of self-defence, military necessity and security needs" (Declaration, paras 1, 3) |
| Does GC IV apply? | Yes (para. 101) | Judge Buergenthal agreed that IHL "including the Fourth Geneva Convention" applies (para. 2); Israel's government disputes de jure application (lesson 15.2) |
| Israel's Supreme Court | — | Weighs the opinion but is not bound by it, because it rested on a different factual basis (*Mara'abe*, para. 74) |

### 2024: who says what

| Point | The Court | Disagreement or response |
|---|---|---|
| Gaza after 2005 | Obligations "commensurate with the degree of its effective control" (paras 93–94) | Israel's government: no effective control since 2005 (lesson 15.2) |
| Settlements | Contrary to GC IV art. 49(6) (para. 119); the régime established in violation of international law (para. 155) | Joint opinion of Judges Tomka, Abraham and Aurescu: *agrees* that "the general and systemic practice of establishment and development of settlements in the West Bank is contrary to Article 49" (para. 3) |
| Annexation | Policies and practices "amount to annexation of large parts" of the territory (para. 179) | Joint opinion: only East Jerusalem and the Area C settlements were annexed |
| Is the occupation itself unlawful? | Yes, 11–4 (para. 285(3)) | Joint opinion: "not convinced", the Court failed to distinguish the rules on conducting an occupation from those on the use of force, and ordered withdrawal "without any prior guarantee, particularly regarding its security" (paras 1, 5); the Oslo Accords were "not duly taken into account" |
| Israel's government | — | Before the opinion: the request is "an abuse of international law and the judicial process", "a clear distortion of the history and present reality", and ignores the Israeli–Palestinian agreements to negotiate permanent status (Written Statement, 24 July 2023). After it, the Prime Minister: "The Jewish people are not occupiers in their own land"; the MFA spokesperson: "completely detached from the reality of the Middle East" (as reported 19 July 2024) |
| Scholars | — | Yuval Shany and Amichai Cohen (Lawfare, 2024) see "deep disagreement underlying the consensus" and note that most judges treated Israel's security concerns as too abstract. Marko Milanovic (ICLQ, 2025) supports the conclusion that the occupation is unlawful but finds the reasoning underdeveloped, noting that "the Opinion does not mention self-defence" |

### 2025: who says what

| Point | The Court | Disagreement or response |
|---|---|---|
| Gaza | Israel's control, and so its obligations under the law of occupation, "increased significantly" after 7 October 2023 (paras 85–87) | Israel's government: no occupation (lesson 15.2); Sean Watts (Lieber, 2025) questions the legal basis of applying obligations "à la carte" |
| Security | Security "is not a free-standing exception"; limits on obligations "must be grounded in a specific rule" (paras 88–90) | Vice-President Sebutinde: the Court "did not sufficiently consider the host State's stated security concerns", including alleged Hamas infiltration of UNRWA, human shields and the militarisation of hospitals and schools (separate opinion, as summarised by the Court) |
| UNRWA | Information "not sufficient to establish UNRWA's lack of neutrality"; Israel "has not substantiated its allegations" about a significant part of its staff; an occupant is "in principle free to choose" the organisations it works through (paras 110–124) | Israel at the General Assembly, October 2025: Israel "categorically rejects the advisory opinion, particularly its conclusions concerning UNRWA"; earlier, its February 2025 statement called the proceedings biased and politically motivated |

### South Africa v. Israel: status on 13 October 2026

This is a contentious case under the Genocide Convention (lesson 13.4), not an advisory opinion. As of 13 October 2026 the [case page](https://www.icj-cij.org/case/192) shows:

- provisional-measures orders of 26 January, 28 March and 24 May 2024, which remain in force;
- orders extending the time-limit for Israel's Counter-Memorial (14 April and 20 October 2025), and an order of 21 May 2026 fixing time-limits for a Reply and a Rejoinder: the written phase continues;
- many declarations of intervention under Statute art. 63, including, in March 2026, Paraguay, the Netherlands, Iceland, Namibia, the United States, Hungary and Fiji; Colombia withdrew its declaration on 22 September 2026;
- no hearing on the merits scheduled and no finding on whether genocide occurred.

Israel's submissions are on [israelihl.mfa.gov.il/icj](https://israelihl.mfa.gov.il/icj). Module 16 covers international criminal accountability. Re-check the case page before you write about it.

### Five rules for writing about these opinions

1. Name the kind of proceeding and the requesting body.
2. Quote the operative paragraph, with its number and the vote.
3. Give the main separate or dissenting view on the same point.
4. Give Israel's position from an official source, with its date.
5. Do not merge findings: "the settlements breach art. 49(6)" (14–1 in substance) is a different finding from "the occupation itself is unlawful" (11–4).

## Worked example

Two groups each write one paragraph about the 2024 opinion for their newsletters, and a third, a student debating society, asks a volunteer to check both.

**Group A (a pro-Palestinian solidarity group):** "The ICJ ruled that Israel must withdraw immediately and that all states must sanction Israel."
*Check:* the opinion is advisory; it said Israel must end its presence "as rapidly as possible" (11–4), not "immediately"; "immediately" appears in the finding on new settlement activity (14–1); the opinion speaks of non-recognition and non-assistance (12–3), not of "sanctions". *Fixed:* "In an advisory opinion of 19 July 2024, the ICJ was of the opinion, by 11 votes to 4, that Israel's continued presence in the territory is unlawful and should end as rapidly as possible. Israel rejected the opinion."

**Group B (a pro-Israel advocacy group):** "Even the ICJ's own judges rejected the opinion, and the Court ignored the settlements question."
*Check:* the judges in the minority on the "unlawful presence" finding (Tomka, Abraham, Aurescu) agreed that the settlement practice breaches art. 49 and that many policies are unlawful; the Court addressed settlements at length (paras 115–155). *Fixed:* "Four of fifteen judges voted against the finding that the occupation itself is unlawful. Three of them wrote that the Court ignored Israel's security and the Oslo framework, while agreeing that the settlement practice breaches art. 49."

**What both show.** Each fixed version is shorter, accurate, and harder for opponents to attack.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- The 2024 opinion (A/78/968), the joint opinion of Judges Tomka, Abraham and Aurescu, and Israel's 2023 Written Statement, all linked in the sources.
- About 100 minutes: 30 minutes for the assigned reading, 40 minutes to build the matrix, 30 minutes to write.

### Steps

1. Choose one finding: 2004 para. 163(3)A, 2024 para. 285(3) or (5), or 2025 para. 223(3)(b).
2. Copy the operative wording exactly, with the paragraph number and the vote.
3. Find the paragraphs of the opinion that give the reasons for that finding and note their numbers.
4. Find what a dissenting or separate opinion said about the same point, and quote one sentence.
5. Find Israel's official position on the same point (a written statement, a GA statement, or an MFA document), with its date.
6. Find one scholar or NGO view on each side and note it in one line each.
7. Write a 150–200 word summary using the five rules above. Ask someone who disagrees with you politically to read it and tell you which sentence they would object to; fix it if their objection is about accuracy.

### Template

Use section 2 ("Applicable framework") and section 6 ("Competing interpretations") of the [IHL incident analysis template](../../templates/ihl-incident-analysis.md) to store the matrix.

### How you know it worked

Your summary quotes the operative paragraph with its number and vote, gives the main separate opinion and Israel's position, and a reader from either side accepts it as accurate even if they reject the finding.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Calling the 2024 opinion a "ruling" that orders Israel | Wrong kind of proceeding; easy to rebut | "Advisory opinion … was of the opinion that" | Name the proceeding first |
| Merging the settlement finding with the "unlawful presence" finding | Misstates the votes and the dissent | Quote each operative paragraph separately | Copy para. 285 by number |
| Saying the dissenting judges "rejected the opinion" | Misleading; they agreed on much of it | Quote what they agreed and disagreed with | Read the joint opinion's paras 1–5 |
| Quoting Israel's position from a news report only | Risk of misquotation | Use the MFA site or the UN record, and say when you rely on a report | Keep a dated source for every position |
| Saying South Africa's case "found genocide" | False; no merits decision exists | Describe provisional measures and the written phase | Check the case page on the day you write |

## Review

- 2004: the wall and its régime contrary to international law (14–1); GC IV applicable; Judge Buergenthal said the Court lacked the facts on Israel's security.
- 2024: Israel's continued presence unlawful (11–4); end settlement activity and evacuate settlers (14–1); Judges Tomka, Abraham and Aurescu agreed the settlement practice breaches art. 49 but rejected the "unlawful presence" finding, citing security and the Oslo framework.
- 2025: duties to facilitate relief, including through UNRWA (10–1), and unanimous duties on supplies, relief workers, transfers, ICRC visits and starvation; Israel "categorically rejects" it.
- *South Africa v. Israel* is in its written phase; provisional measures remain; there is no merits finding.
- Quote the paragraph, the vote, the disagreement and Israel's position, each with its source.
