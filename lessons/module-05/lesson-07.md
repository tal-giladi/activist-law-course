# 05.7 · High Court Petition, Self-Represented

By the end of this lesson you will be able to decide when a matter belongs in the High Court of Justice (בג״ץ), draft a petition for an order nisi in your own name with its supporting affidavit, and plan the filing, fee, service and hearing — while avoiding the threshold grounds on which most self-represented petitions fail.

## Why it matters

The High Court of Justice is the forum for decisions of the Government and ministers, regulations, national policy, the security forces and most matters no other court hears. Anyone may petition it without a lawyer, and it hears unrepresented petitioners every week. Most such petitions are dismissed at the threshold: the petitioner did not ask the authority first, waited too long, has no standing, or should have gone to the Administrative Court. A well-prepared petition gets past the threshold, and many end with the State changing its decision before judgment.

## The law

> (ד) מבלי לפגוע בכלליות ההוראות שבסעיף קטן (ג), מוסמך בית המשפט העליון בשבתו כבית משפט גבוה לצדק –
> (2) לתת צווים לרשויות המדינה, לרשויות מקומיות, לפקידיהן ולגופים ולאנשים אחרים הממלאים תפקידים ציבוריים על פי דין, לעשות מעשה או להימנע מעשות מעשה במילוי תפקידיהם כדין, ואם נבחרו או נתמנו שלא כדין – להימנע מלפעול;

*Gist:* the HCJ can order any state or local authority, official or other body with public functions under law to act or to refrain from acting — subject to s. 15(ג), which sends to other courts what they can hear. ([חוק-יסוד: השפיטה, s. 15(ד)(2)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק-יסוד:_השפיטה#סעיף_15))

> העתירה תפרט את פרטיו האישיים של העותר ואת הנימוקים, שבעובדה ושבחוק, שעליהם היא מתבססת, ותהא ערוכה לפי טופס 1 שבתוספת וכתובה על צד אחד של הגליון עם שוליים של חמישה סנטימטרים לפחות.

*Gist:* the petition gives your personal details and the factual and legal grounds, follows Form 1, and is written on one side of the page with margins of at least 5 cm. ([תקנות סדר הדין בבית המשפט הגבוה לצדק, reg. 2](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/תקנות_סדר_הדין_בבית_המשפט_הגבוה_לצדק#סעיף_2))

> העותר יציין בעתירה מען להמצאה בתחומה של המדינה; מסירה למען זה תהיה המצאה כדין.

*Gist:* you must give an address for service inside Israel. ([reg. 3](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/תקנות_סדר_הדין_בבית_המשפט_הגבוה_לצדק#סעיף_3))

> כל עתירה תהא נתמכת בתצהיר לפי טופס 2 שבתוספת והוא יצורף אל העתירה או יירשם בסופה; על עובדות שהן בתחום ידיעתו האישית של העותר יצהיר שהן נכונות, ועל עובדות אחרות יצהיר שהן נכונות לפי מיטב ידיעתו ואמונתו.

*Gist:* every petition is supported by an affidavit (Form 2), attached or at its end, distinguishing facts you know personally from facts you believe. ([reg. 4](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/תקנות_סדר_הדין_בבית_המשפט_הגבוה_לצדק#סעיף_4))

> העתירה תובא לפני שופט של בית המשפט העליון והוא רשאי ליתן את הצו או להורות על הזמנת העותר לפניו או להעביר את העתירה להרכב של שלושה; הועברה העתירה להרכב של שלושה, רשאי ההרכב ליתן את הצו או להורות על הזמנת העותר לפניו, ואם היה סבור שהעתירה אינה מראה על פניה עילה רשאי הוא לדחותה על יסוד האמור בה, ללא הזמנת העותר;

*Gist:* a judge, or a panel of three, reads the petition first and may grant an order, call a hearing, or dismiss it on its face without hearing you; reg. 6 allows costs to the State for such a dismissal. ([reg. 5](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/תקנות_סדר_הדין_בבית_המשפט_הגבוה_לצדק#סעיף_5))

> (1) עתירה נגד החלטה של רשות או של גוף המנוי בתוספת הרביעית, הפועלים ביהודה והשומרון (להלן – האזור), בעניין המנוי בתוספת הרביעית

*Gist:* certain decisions of authorities in Judea and Samaria (FOI, some planning, entry and exit matters) go to the Administrative Court in Jerusalem, not the HCJ. ([חוק בתי משפט לענינים מינהליים, s. 5א(א)(1)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_בתי_משפט_לענינים_מינהליים#סעיף_5א))

## How it works in practice

### Is it an HCJ matter?

1. Is it in the First Schedule (or, for Judea and Samaria, the Fourth Schedule) and not excluded? → Administrative Court (lesson 05.6), not the HCJ.
2. Is the main remedy about **regulations** (making, cancelling, declaring void)? → HCJ, even in a First Schedule subject (s. 5(1)).
3. Is it a decision of the **Government, a minister, or a national body** that the First Schedule item excludes? → HCJ.
4. Is there another court or tribunal (labour court, appeals board, a statutory appeal)? → there, not the HCJ (s. 15(ג)).

### Threshold grounds (lesson 04.5)

| Ground | What the court asks | What your petition shows |
|---|---|---|
| Exhaustion (מיצוי הליכים) | Did you ask the authority and give it time? | Your letters and the answers, as exhibits (lesson 05.5) |
| Delay (שיהוי) | Did you wait too long, harming others or reliance? | A timeline showing you acted promptly |
| Standing (זכות עמידה) | Are you harmed, or a serious public petitioner on a matter of public importance? | Your interest; why no better-placed petitioner exists |
| Alternative remedy | Is another court or procedure available? | Why the HCJ is the only forum |
| Justiciability and general petitions | Is it a concrete legal dispute? | A specific decision, a specific remedy |

There is no fixed day count for an HCJ petition. Courts apply the delay doctrine; treat 45 days from the decision as your own target and file as soon as the pre-petition deadline passes.

### The document (Form 1 practice)

Follow the template [hcj-petition.md](../../templates/hcj-petition.md):

1. Heading: "בבית המשפט העליון בשבתו כבית משפט גבוה לצדק", "בג״ץ ___/__" (given on filing), the parties with addresses for service (reg. 3).
2. "עתירה למתן צו על תנאי" and the remedies, in the customary wording: an order directed to the respondents to come and show cause why they should not do X, or why decision Y should not be cancelled.
3. Optionally, a separate request for an interim order (reg. 19), with its own affidavit.
4. Introduction, parties, facts with exhibits, exhaustion of remedies, legal grounds, why the HCJ (and not another court), other proceedings.
5. The fee (item 24) or an exemption request.
6. Signature and date; the affidavit (Form 2) attached or at the end (reg. 4).
7. Exhibits.

Write on one side of the page with wide margins (reg. 2). Keep it short and specific; long, angry or general petitions are dismissed on their face.

### Respondents

Name the decision-maker (the minister, the ministry, the authority) and anyone who may be harmed if you win. Where the respondent is a body the Attorney General may represent, the court summons the Attorney General (reg. 7(ב)); in practice the State Attorney's HCJ department answers for state respondents.

### Fee and exemption

The HCJ petition fee is item 24 of the Court Fees Regulations' schedule (amount on [Current Numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md); indexed every 1 January). The exemption route is the same as in lesson 05.6 (reg. 14: a request with an affidavit on assets and six months of income, filed with the petition). The registry will not open a file without the fee or an exemption request.

### Filing and service

- **Where:** the Supreme Court in Jerusalem. How an unrepresented petitioner files online in the Supreme Court, and how the fee is paid there, could not be confirmed from an official page for this lesson. Before you file, call the judiciary information centre (*3852) or the Supreme Court registry and ask: "How does an unrepresented petitioner file a בג״ץ petition — through Net HaMishpat or at the registry — and how is the fee paid?" Write the answer and the date in your file.
- **Service:** after filing, serve every document you file on every other party the same day (reg. 8א); the Civil Procedure Regulations' service chapter applies (reg. 21א). If an order nisi or interim order is given, the court arranges service of the order with your petition copies (reg. 8).

### What happens next

| Stage | Rule |
|---|---|
| A judge reads the petition: may dismiss on its face, call a hearing, or ask the respondents for a response | regs 5, 7 |
| Respondents' written response (if summoned), served on you at least three days before the hearing | reg. 7(ב1) |
| Hearing; the court may, with the respondent's consent, decide as if an order nisi had been given | reg. 7(ג) |
| Order nisi → respondent's answering affidavit (Form 3) within the time set | reg. 9 |
| Skeleton arguments three days before the hearing | reg. 16 |
| Final judgment: absolute order, or dismissal; costs possible | regs 6, 21 |

Court recess periods are not counted in the regulations' deadlines (reg. 19א). Many petitions end when the State files an update notice that it changed its decision, and the petition is deleted as redundant — often with costs in the petitioner's favour.

## Worked example

**Situation (fictional provision).** A law passed in 2019 required the minister responsible to issue regulations on accessible formats for public notices within two years. In 2026 there are still no regulations. Dana, who is blind, cannot read municipal and ministry notices that affect her.

1. **Forum.** The main remedy is an order to make regulations, which s. 5(1) of the Administrative Courts Law excludes from the Administrative Court → HCJ.
2. **Exhaustion.** Dana wrote to the minister and the ministry's legal adviser on 1 March asking for a timetable; a reminder on 20 April; on 5 May the ministry answered that the regulations are "in preparation", without a date. Pre-petition letter on 10 May with a 14-day response date; no answer.
3. **Standing.** Dana is personally affected; she also attaches two letters from others in her situation.
4. **Delay.** The failure is continuing, and she acted within weeks of the last answer.
5. **Remedy.** An order directing the minister to show cause why he should not issue the regulations within a time the court sets.
6. **Respondents.** The minister and the ministry; the Attorney General will be summoned.
7. **Fee.** Dana receives a disability pension; she files an exemption request with an affidavit on assets and income.
8. **Filing.** She calls *3852 to confirm the filing route for an unrepresented HCJ petitioner, files on 3 June, and serves the State Attorney's office the same day.
9. **Outcome.** The court orders a response within 45 days; the State files an update notice with a timetable for the regulations; the court keeps the petition pending until they are signed.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- A matter that passes the "Is it an HCJ matter?" test, real or realistic (the module project may give you one).
- Your pre-petition letter and the answer (lesson 05.5).
- The templates: [hcj-petition.md](../../templates/hcj-petition.md) and [affidavit.md](../../templates/affidavit.md).
- 90 minutes.

### Steps

1. Run the four-question forum test and write the answer with its source.
2. Run the threshold table: exhaustion, delay, standing, alternative remedy, justiciability. Fix any gap before drafting.
3. Write the remedies as "לבוא וליתן טעם, אם יש בידם, מדוע לא…".
4. Fill in the HCJ template: parties with addresses for service, facts with exhibits, exhaustion, grounds, why the HCJ.
5. Write the affidavit: personal knowledge, then belief with its source.
6. Decide the fee: item 24 or an exemption request.
7. Write the call you will make to *3852 about the filing route, and the service plan.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> In this lesson the petition is a dry run. If you ever file for real: act in your own name only. This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing, including the filing route and fee with the court.

### Template

[hcj-petition.md](../../templates/hcj-petition.md) — a Hebrew HCJ petition skeleton for an order nisi, with a threshold checklist; [affidavit.md](../../templates/affidavit.md) for Form 2.

### How you know it worked

- You can say in one sentence why no other court can hear your matter.
- Each threshold ground has a paragraph and an exhibit answering it.
- The petition is short enough that a judge reading it on the first day sees the decision, the defect and the remedy on page one.

### Self-check

- [ ] Forum: not in the First or Fourth Schedule, or excluded; no other court or tribunal.
- [ ] Exhaustion documented; delay explained; standing stated.
- [ ] Remedies as an order nisi to show cause.
- [ ] Personal details and an Israeli address for service (regs 2–3).
- [ ] Affidavit attached or at the end, knowledge separated from belief (reg. 4).
- [ ] Fee item 24 or exemption request; filing route confirmed by phone.
- [ ] Service plan for the same day as filing (reg. 8א).

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| HCJ petition in a First Schedule matter | Dismissed or transferred; fee and time lost | Re-file in the Administrative Court within its deadline | Run the forum test first |
| No prior demand | Dismissed at the threshold, costs to the State (reg. 6) | Send a pre-petition letter, then re-file | Exhaust remedies and attach the proof |
| Waiting months after the decision | Dismissed for delay | Explain every gap in a timeline | File within weeks |
| A general petition against a policy with no specific decision | Dismissed as non-justiciable or too general | Identify a concrete decision or failure | Ask for one specific order |
| No affidavit, or not certified | File not accepted, or struck | File a certified affidavit | Book the certifier early |
| Assuming the online route works as in the District Court | Missed filing day | Go to the registry or call *3852 at once | Confirm the route days before filing |
| Long, emotional text | Dismissed on its face | Rewrite as facts, rules, remedy | One page to the decision, defect and remedy |

## Review

- The HCJ hears what no other court can hear; First and Fourth Schedule matters go to the Administrative Court, but remedies about regulations go to the HCJ.
- The petition asks for an order nisi; it gives your details, an Israeli address for service and the factual and legal grounds, on one side of the page (regs 2–3).
- A supporting affidavit, separating knowledge from belief, is required (reg. 4).
- Exhaustion, delay, standing and the alternative-remedy rule decide most self-represented petitions before the merits.
- Fee item 24 or an exemption request; confirm the Supreme Court filing route by phone; serve every filing the same day (reg. 8א).
