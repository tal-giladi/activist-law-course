# 05.3 · When FOI Fails: Reminders, Complaints and Petitions

By the end of this lesson you will be able to respond the right way when an FOI request is ignored, delayed or refused: send a reminder that cites the law, complain to the Government FOI Unit when the body is a ministry, read a refusal for its weak points, and keep the 45-day petition deadline safe while you do.

## Why it matters

Many FOI requests are answered late, partly or not at all. Most of those are solved by one firm reminder or a complaint to the FOI Unit. When they are not, the law gives you a petition to the Administrative Court — and the court can order disclosure even of exempt information when the public interest is stronger (s. 17(ד)).

The trap is time. A refusal starts a 45-day petition clock. Learners who wait for a complaint to be answered, or who keep sending reminders after a written refusal, discover that the clock ran out.

## The law

> (ו) החליטה הרשות הציבורית לדחות את בקשת המבקש, כולה או חלקה, תשלח למבקש הודעה בכתב שתפרט את נימוקי ההחלטה, ותיידע את המבקש על זכותו לעתור נגד ההחלטה לפי הוראות חוק בתי משפט לענינים מינהליים, התש״ס–2000.

*Gist:* a refusal, full or partial, must be in writing, give reasons and tell you of your right to petition. ([חוק חופש המידע, s. 7(ו)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_חופש_המידע#סעיף_7))

> (א) עתירה על החלטת רשות ציבורית לפי חוק זה תידון בבית משפט לענינים מינהליים, אלא אם כן הוצאה לגבי המידע נושא העתירה תעודת חיסיון לפי סעיף 44 לפקודת הראיות [נוסח חדש], התשל״א–1971; הוצאה תעודת חיסיון כאמור, תידון העתירה לפני בית המשפט העליון.

*Gist:* FOI petitions go to the Administrative Court, unless a minister signed a privilege certificate, in which case they go to the Supreme Court. ([s. 17(א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_חופש_המידע#סעיף_17))

> (ד) על אף הוראות סעיף 9, רשאי בית המשפט להורות על מתן מידע מבוקש, כולו או חלקו ובתנאים שיקבע, אם לדעתו הענין הציבורי בגילוי המידע, עדיף וגובר על הטעם לדחיית הבקשה, ובלבד שגילוי המידע אינו אסור על פי דין.

*Gist:* the court may order disclosure despite a s. 9 exemption when the public interest outweighs the reason for refusal, unless the law forbids disclosure. ([s. 17(ד)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_חופש_המידע#סעיף_17))

> (ב) לא נקבע מועד כאמור, תוגש העתירה בלא שיהוי, לפי נסיבות הענין, ולא יאוחר מארבעים וחמישה ימים מיום שההחלטה פורסמה כדין, או מיום שהעותר קיבל הודעה עליה או מיום שנודע לעותר עליה, לפי המוקדם.

*Gist:* a petition must be filed without delay and no later than 45 days from the earliest of publication, notice or knowledge of the decision; the court may extend this (reg. 3(ג)) and may reject a late-in-spirit petition even inside the 45 days (reg. 4). ([תקנות בתי משפט לענינים מינהליים (סדרי דין), reg. 3](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/תקנות_בתי_משפט_לענינים_מינהליים_%28סדרי_דין%29#סעיף_3))

> על החלטת הממונה לפי תקנות אלה רשאי המבקש לערער, בתוך 30 ימים מיום מסירת החלטת הממונה, לבית משפט השלום שבתחום שיפוטו נמצא משרדו של הממונה.

*Gist:* a dispute about fees goes to the Magistrates' Court within 30 days, not to the Administrative Court. ([תקנות חופש המידע (אגרות), reg. 8](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/תקנות_חופש_המידע_%28אגרות%29#סעיף_8))

## How it works in practice

### Day 30: what you check

| Situation | What it means | Your move |
|---|---|---|
| A written, reasoned extension notice arrived in time | Lawful, up to 30 more days (s. 7(ב)); a further extension up to 60 days needs the head of the authority and reasons (s. 7(ג)) | Note the new date; check the reasons are real |
| A one-line "we need more time" with no reasons | Not a proper extension | Reminder citing s. 7(ב) |
| Silence | The authority breached s. 7(ב); no fees for handling and production any more (reg. 6א) | Reminder now; complaint and petition plan |
| Partial release, the rest refused | A refusal of the rest under s. 7(ו); the 45-day clock started on receipt | Read the reasons; decide on a petition within 45 days |
| Full refusal | Same | Same |

### The reminder

Short and firm: the date of the request, the date of payment, the s. 7(ב) deadline that passed, a demand for an answer within 7 days, a note that handling fees can no longer be charged (reg. 6א), and the next step (complaint, petition). Send it to the FOI officer with a copy to the head of the authority (the director general, or the mayor for a local authority, s. 2).

### The FOI Unit complaint (ministries only)

The Government FOI Unit in the Ministry of Justice supervises government ministries and their auxiliary units. Its published procedure says you may complain to it for an answer not given in the statutory times "and other matters". File through the complaint service on gov.il or by email to the unit (contact details are in its procedure). It is free. The Unit contacts the ministry; many requests are answered within weeks.

For local authorities, government companies and other bodies the FOI Unit has no supervisory role. Your options there are a reminder to the head of the body, a complaint to the Public Complaints Commissioner if you are directly harmed (lesson 05.4), and a petition.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> No complaint stops the petition clock. If you have a written refusal, count 45 days from the day you received it and decide about the petition inside that time, whatever the complaint's status.

### Silence and the 45 days

When the authority stays silent, there is no written decision to count from. The cautious practice is to treat the end of the lawful answer period (30 days, or the extended date) as the point you knew of a refusal, and to petition within 45 days of it — or to send one reminder with a short final date and count from that date. Courts dismiss FOI petitions for delay (reg. 4), so do not let silence run for months.

### Reading a refusal

For each item refused, write:

1. The section relied on (s. 8 or 9) — is it named? A bare "the information is exempt" is not a reasoned decision.
2. Is it a "must not" (s. 9(א)) or a "may withhold" (s. 9(ב)) ground? For "may" grounds the authority must use discretion and weigh your interest and the public interest (s. 10).
3. Could part be released with redactions (s. 11)? Was that considered?
4. Is there a carve-out? For example, s. 9(ב)(6) on trade secrets excludes information on substances released into the environment and noise, smell and radiation measurements.
5. Does the information concern a third party? The authority must notify them; they have 21 days to object, and those days do not count toward the FOI deadlines (s. 13).
6. Why does the public interest outweigh the reason for refusal (s. 17(ד))?

These notes become the "grounds" section of your petition (lesson 05.6).

### Where the petition goes and what it costs

FOI decisions are item 2 of the First Schedule: "חופש המידע – החלטה של רשות לפי חוק חופש המידע". File in the District Court (sitting as an Administrative Court) of the district where the decision was made (reg. 2(א)); for most ministries that is Jerusalem. The fee is the administrative-petition fee (see [Current Numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)); no reduced fee for FOI petitions was found in the fee schedule — check with the court's information line before filing. Lesson 05.6 teaches the petition itself.

## Worked example

**Situation.** A Conservative (Masorti) congregation in a northern town asks the Ministry of Religious Services, under the FOI Law, for the criteria and the list of grants paid to communities for ritual baths (mikva'ot) in 2024–2026. Rachel, the congregation's chair, files in her own name.

1. **Request** filed online on 2 February; fee paid the same day. Day 30 is 4 March.
2. **4 March.** Nothing. Rachel sends a reminder to the FOI officer, copy to the director general: request, payment, s. 7(ב), reg. 6א, an answer within 7 days.
3. **12 March.** Still nothing. She files a complaint with the Government FOI Unit (the ministry is a government ministry). She also writes 45 days from 4 March — 18 April — in red, as her cautious petition date.
4. **28 March.** The ministry releases the criteria but refuses the list of grants, citing "privacy" (s. 9(א)(3)) without explanation.
5. **Reading the refusal.** The grants go to communities and institutions, not to private persons, so privacy is doubtful; names of officials can be redacted (s. 11); the public interest in equal distribution of public money is strong (s. 17(ד)). The new 45-day clock runs from 28 March: 12 May.
6. **Next step.** A short pre-petition letter (05.5) asking for the list within 10 days, and a draft petition to the Jerusalem District Court (05.6). Either outcome is on time.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- Your FOI request from lesson 05.2 (real or a dry run), or an invented answer: silence, an unreasoned extension, or a partial refusal.
- The template: [foi-unit-complaint.md](../../templates/foi-unit-complaint.md) (includes the reminder).
- 45 minutes.

### Steps

1. Write the timeline: receipt, payment, day 30, any extension, any answer.
2. Classify the situation with the "Day 30" table.
3. Write the reminder from the template.
4. If the body is a ministry or auxiliary unit, write the FOI Unit complaint. If not, write which other route you would take.
5. If there is a refusal, fill in the six-question refusal check for each refused item.
6. Calculate the petition deadline (45 days) and mark it in red.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Sending is optional; a dry run is enough. If you send for real: act in your own name only. This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reminders for months after a written refusal | Petition late; dismissed | Ask the court to extend (reg. 3(ג)) with good reasons | Count 45 days from the refusal and act inside them |
| Complaining to the FOI Unit about a municipality | No supervisory power; time lost | Remind the mayor, use the Commissioner, or petition | Check the type of body first |
| Petitioning the Administrative Court about a fee | Wrong forum | Appeal to the Magistrates' Court within 30 days (reg. 8) | Fees go to the Magistrates' Court |
| Accepting "the information is exempt" | A weak refusal stands | Demand reasons and the section (s. 7(ו)) | Ask for reasoned refusals in the original request |
| Ignoring a privilege certificate | Petition filed in the wrong court | Re-file in the Supreme Court (s. 17(א)) | Check the refusal for a certificate under s. 44 |
| Arguing only "I have a right to know" | The court weighs nothing new | Show the public interest and partial release | Build the s. 10, s. 11 and s. 17(ד) arguments |

## Review

- On day 30, check for a written, reasoned extension; without one, send a reminder citing s. 7(ב) and reg. 6א.
- The FOI Unit takes complaints about ministries and their units only; it is free and often effective.
- A written refusal starts the 45-day petition clock; no reminder or complaint pauses it.
- Read every refusal for the section, its type, partial release, carve-outs, third parties and the public interest.
- FOI petitions go to the Administrative Court (First Schedule item 2); with a privilege certificate, to the Supreme Court; fee disputes, to the Magistrates' Court.
