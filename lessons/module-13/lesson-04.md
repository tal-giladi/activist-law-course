# 13.4 · The International Court of Justice

The International Court of Justice in The Hague is the United Nations' court: it decides disputes between states that agreed to its jurisdiction and gives advisory opinions to UN bodies. In this lesson you learn who can bring a case, when the Court has jurisdiction, what binds and what only advises, and how Israel has engaged with the Court, and you finish by reading a live case page and building its docket timeline.

## Why it matters

The ICJ is often in the Israeli news: the 2004 advisory opinion on the separation barrier, the 2024 and 2025 advisory opinions on the territories and on UN bodies, and South Africa's case against Israel under the Genocide Convention. Activists on all sides quote these proceedings, and many quotes are wrong: an advisory opinion is called a "ruling", a provisional-measures order is called a "verdict", or a "case" is said to be filed by an NGO. If you know how the Court works, you can read its documents yourself, describe them accurately, and explain both the Court's findings and Israel's responses without mixing them up.

## The law

### The Court and its judges

> The International Court of Justice shall be the principal judicial organ of the United Nations.

*Gist:* the ICJ is a UN organ; its Statute is part of the Charter (UN Charter, Art. 92).

> All Members of the United Nations are ipso facto parties to the Statute of the International Court of Justice.

*Gist:* every UN member, Israel included, is a party to the Statute, but that alone does not give the Court jurisdiction over it (Charter, Art. 93(1)).

> The Court shall consist of fifteen Members, no two of whom may be nationals of the same State.

*Gist:* fifteen judges, elected for nine years (Statute, arts 3(1) and 13(1)).

Sources: [UN Charter, Chapter XIV](https://www.un.org/en/about-us/un-charter/chapter-14); [ICJ Statute](https://www.icj-cij.org/statute).

### Jurisdiction rests on consent

> 1. The jurisdiction of the Court comprises all cases which the parties refer to it and all matters specially provided for in the Charter of the United Nations or in treaties and conventions in force.
> 2. The states parties to the present Statute may at any time declare that they recognize as compulsory ipso facto and without special agreement, in relation to any other state accepting the same obligation, the jurisdiction of the Court in all legal disputes concerning: a. the interpretation of a treaty; b. any question of international law; c. the existence of any fact which, if established, would constitute a breach of an international obligation; d. the nature or extent of the reparation to be made for the breach of an international obligation.

*Gist:* the Court hears a case only if both states consented: by a special agreement, by a treaty clause, or by optional-clause declarations on both sides (Statute, Art. 36(1)–(2)).

### Provisional measures

> The Court shall have the power to indicate, if it considers that circumstances so require, any provisional measures which ought to be taken to preserve the respective rights of either party.

*Gist:* early in a case the Court may order interim measures; it later held such orders binding (Statute, Art. 41(1)).

### Compliance

> Each Member of the United Nations undertakes to comply with the decision of the International Court of Justice in any case to which it is a party.

> If any party to a case fails to perform the obligations incumbent upon it under a judgment rendered by the Court, the other party may have recourse to the Security Council, which may, if it deems necessary, make recommendations or decide upon measures to be taken to give effect to the judgment.

*Gist:* judgments bind the parties; enforcement goes through the Security Council (Charter, Art. 94).

### Advisory opinions

> The General Assembly or the Security Council may request the International Court of Justice to give an advisory opinion on any legal question.

*Gist:* UN organs, not states or NGOs, ask for advisory opinions (Charter, Art. 96(1); Statute, Art. 65(1)). Advisory opinions are not binding as such, though they carry legal authority.

## How it works in practice

### Three kinds of output

| Output | Who starts it | Who is bound | Example involving Israel |
|---|---|---|---|
| Judgment in a contentious case | A state, against another state | The parties, in that case only (Art. 59) | *Israel v. Bulgaria* (filed 1957): in 1959 the Court found it had no jurisdiction |
| Provisional-measures order | A party, during a contentious case | The parties | *South Africa v. Israel*: orders of 26 January, 28 March and 24 May 2024 |
| Advisory opinion | The General Assembly, Security Council or another authorised body | No one, as such | 9 July 2004 (wall); 19 July 2024 (policies and practices in the OPT); 22 October 2025 (UN and other bodies in and in relation to the OPT) |

### The jurisdiction test

Before you say "the ICJ could rule on this", answer four questions:

1. **Are both sides states?** NGOs, individuals, companies and armed groups cannot be parties (Art. 34(1)).
2. **Is there a special agreement?** Both states may agree to send a specific dispute to the Court.
3. **Is there a treaty clause?** Many treaties send disputes about them to the ICJ. The Genocide Convention (Art. IX) is the clause South Africa invoked against Israel. Israel ratified the Genocide Convention on 9 March 1950 without a reservation to Art. IX; in Israel's own words to the General Assembly in 2025, this was its "unreserved consent to the Court's jurisdiction under the Convention".
4. **Are there optional-clause declarations on both sides?** The ICJ lists 75 states with declarations in force; Israel is not among them, so this route is not open against Israel.

### Reading a case page

Every ICJ case page has tabs: Overview, Institution of proceedings, Written proceedings, Oral proceedings, Orders, Judgments, Press releases. The press releases summarise each step in plain language. For *South Africa v. Israel* ([case 192](https://www.icj-cij.org/case/192)), as of 13 October 2026, the page lists the provisional-measures orders of 2024, orders fixing and extending time-limits (5 April 2024; 14 April 2025; 20 October 2025), and an order of 21 May 2026 fixing time-limits for a Reply and Rejoinder. The case is in its written phase; no merits hearing has been scheduled. Check the page again before you write about it: dates change.

### Who says what: the Court and Israel

| Topic | The Court | Israel's official position |
|---|---|---|
| 2004 opinion on the wall | The construction of the wall and its associated régime were contrary to international law | Israel filed a written statement on 30 January 2004 arguing the Court should decline to give an opinion; Israel's Supreme Court later addressed the opinion in *Mara'abe* (Module 15) |
| 2024 and 2025 opinions | Opinions given at the General Assembly's request (resolutions 77/247 and 79/232) | Israel's 2023 written statement argued that the request distorts both history and law; its 2025 statement called the proceedings biased and politically motivated and urged the Court to decline; in 2025 Israel told the General Assembly it "categorically rejects" the 2025 opinion |
| *South Africa v. Israel* | Provisional measures ordered in 2024; the case continues | Israel argues the case misuses the Court and that it complies with international law; its submissions are on [israelihl.mfa.gov.il/icj](https://israelihl.mfa.gov.il/icj) |

Human-rights groups and many states cite the opinions as authoritative statements of the law; Israel and some other states contest the proceedings and their conclusions. In this course you quote the Court's words, the State's words, and others' words, each with its source, and draw your own conclusions only about procedure.

## Worked example

A farmers' cooperative in the north says a factory across the border in the fictional State R pollutes a shared river. Its members ask: "Can we take State R to the ICJ?"

**Step 1 — Parties.** The cooperative cannot be a party. Only Israel could bring a case against State R (Art. 34(1)).

**Step 2 — Jurisdiction.** No special agreement exists. The cooperative searches the UN Treaty Collection for a treaty both states ratified that has an ICJ clause and covers river pollution, and finds none. Israel has no optional-clause declaration. So the ICJ has no jurisdiction, even if Israel wanted to sue.

**Step 3 — Other routes.** The members turn to tools they can use: a complaint to the Ministry of Environmental Protection (lesson 10.5), a request to the Foreign Ministry to raise the matter diplomatically, and a written question through an MK (lesson 12.4).

**Step 4 — How they describe it.** In their newsletter they write: "The ICJ hears only cases between states that consented to its jurisdiction. Israel and State R have not, so we are pursuing domestic and diplomatic routes." Accurate, short and not misleading.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- An internet connection and the ICJ website.
- One hour for the assigned lecture, 20 minutes for one Israeli submission and 20 minutes for the docket.

### Steps

1. Open an ICJ case page involving Israel: [case 192](https://www.icj-cij.org/case/192), [186](https://www.icj-cij.org/case/186), [196](https://www.icj-cij.org/case/196) or [131](https://www.icj-cij.org/case/131).
2. Write the case's full name, whether it is contentious or advisory, and who started it (a state, or which UN organ by which resolution).
3. Build a timeline table: every filing, order, hearing and opinion or judgment, with its date.
4. For a contentious case, write the basis of jurisdiction; for an advisory case, the question asked.
5. Open Israel's submission on [israelihl.mfa.gov.il/icj](https://israelihl.mfa.gov.il/icj) for the same proceeding and write two sentences on Israel's main argument.
6. Read the Court's press release for the latest step and write two sentences on what the Court decided or did, using its own words.
7. Run the four-question jurisdiction test on a hypothetical dispute from your own cause.

### Template

Record the docket and both positions in section 4 ("International forums") of the [international-law argument template](../../templates/intl-law-argument.md).

### How you know it worked

Your timeline has exact dates from the ICJ page, the right kind of proceeding, Israel's position and the Court's words, each with its source. You never call an advisory opinion a "judgment against Israel" or a provisional-measures order a "verdict".

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Calling an advisory opinion a binding "ruling against Israel" | Factually wrong; your opponents correct you | Say "advisory opinion" and quote its words | Check whether the case is contentious or advisory |
| Saying an NGO "filed a case at the ICJ" | Wrong; only states and UN organs start proceedings | Name the state or UN organ | Read "Institution of proceedings" on the case page |
| Treating a provisional-measures order as a finding on the merits | Overstates what the Court decided | Describe it as interim measures pending judgment | Read the order's operative part |
| Writing only the Court's view, or only the State's | Reads as advocacy; breaks this course's neutrality | Add the other side with its source | Use the "who says what" table |
| Using old dates for a live case | Out-of-date claims | Re-open the case page | Note the date you checked |

## Review

- The ICJ has fifteen judges and hears disputes only between states that consented to its jurisdiction.
- Jurisdiction comes from a special agreement, a treaty clause, or optional-clause declarations; Israel has no optional-clause declaration, but accepted the Genocide Convention's Art. IX without reservation.
- Judgments and provisional-measures orders bind the parties; advisory opinions, requested by UN organs, are not binding as such.
- Israel has engaged with the Court as a party and by written statements, and contests several recent proceedings.
- Quote the Court and the State in their own words, with dates checked on the case page.
