# 11.1 · Should You Go to Court? Cost, Risk and Alternatives

Going to court is a tool, not a goal: it costs money, months and nerves, and losing can mean paying the other side's costs. In this lesson you learn to weigh a civil claim against cheaper routes — a demand letter, negotiation, mediation, a regulator — and to choose the right civil court if you do sue. You finish with a written go/no-go memo for one dispute of your own.

## Why it matters

Activists end up in civil disputes all the time: a hall that cancels an event and keeps the deposit, a printer who delivers flyers after the rally, a counter-protester who smashes equipment, a business that discriminates, a neighbour who sues over a sign. Most of these are small money disputes that you can bring yourself. But a claim filed in anger, against the wrong defendant, in the wrong court, without evidence, can end with a costs order against you and a public loss. Ten minutes of honest cost-and-risk thinking before you file is the cheapest legal advice you will ever get.

## The law

> בית משפט לתביעות קטנות ידון בתביעה אזרחית שהגיש יחיד –
> (1) לתשלום סכום שאינו עולה על 30,000 שקלים חדשים;

*Gist:* the Small Claims Court hears civil money claims filed by an individual, up to a ceiling that is linked to the index and updated every January (see [Current Numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)). ([Courts Law s. 60(א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_בתי_המשפט#סעיף_60))

> בית המשפט רשאי לא לדון בתביעה או בתביעה־שכנגד ולהעבירן לבית משפט שלום, או לבית המשפט המוסמך לדון בהן, לפי העניין, אם ראה שהדיון בבית משפט לתביעות קטנות אינו מתאים להן, או אם ראה שהתביעה הוגשה בידי תובע שכבר הגיש באותה שנה באותו בית משפט יותר מחמש תביעות.

*Gist:* the judge may send a case to the regular Magistrates' Court if it does not suit small claims, or if the claimant has already filed more than five claims that year in the same court. ([s. 60(ב)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_בתי_המשפט#סעיף_60))

> האמור בסעיף 60 אין בו כדי למנוע –
> (1) בעל דין מהגיש תביעה כאמור בו לבית משפט אחר במקום להגישה לבית משפט לתביעות קטנות;

*Gist:* small claims is an option, not an obligation; you may always file in the regular court instead. ([s. 61](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_בתי_המשפט#סעיף_61))

> חיוב בעל דין בתשלום הוצאות נועד לשפות את בעל הדין שכנגד על הוצאותיו בהליך בהתחשב בתוצאותיו, במשאבים שנדרשו לניהולו ובהתנהלות בעלי הדין.

*Gist:* in the regular courts, costs compensate the other side for what the case cost them, judged by the outcome, the resources it needed and how each side behaved. ([Civil Procedure Regulations 2018, reg. 151(א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/תקנות_סדר_הדין_האזרחי#סעיף_151))

> בתום הדיון יפסוק בית המשפט הוצאות סבירות והוגנות, זולת אם מצא שקיימים טעמים מיוחדים שלא לחייב בהוצאות כאמור.

*Gist:* at the end of the case the court awards reasonable costs unless there are special reasons not to — the loser usually pays. ([reg. 152](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/תקנות_סדר_הדין_האזרחי#סעיף_152))

The texts were checked on Hebrew Wikisource on 13 October 2026. The general limitation period for a civil claim is seven years ([Limitation Law s. 5](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_ההתיישנות#סעיף_5)); some laws set shorter periods (lesson 09.3 shows one).

## How it works in practice

### Step 1: what do you actually want?

Write it in one line. Money back? Compensation? An apology? A policy change? A precedent? A court can give money and a few orders (in small claims: replacing or repairing a product, or cancelling a deal, s. 60(א)(2)). It cannot make anyone apologise sincerely or change a city's policy in a small claim. If what you want is a policy change by an authority, Module 5's tools usually fit better.

### Step 2: the ladder of options

Climb only as high as you need:

| Rung | When it fits | Cost and time | Lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| Talk or write informally | The other side may simply not know | Free, days | — |
| Demand letter (מכתב דרישה) | Almost always before suing; it often settles the matter and shows the judge you tried | Registered mail, 1–2 hours, 14 days' wait | 11.2, template below |
| Regulator or ombudsman | Banks, insurers, phone companies, public bodies | Free, weeks to months, no compensation in most cases | 05.4 |
| Mediation (גישור) | Ongoing relationships: neighbours, a venue you will use again | A first meeting may be free inside the court process; private mediators charge | 11.4 |
| Small Claims Court | You are an individual, the claim is within the ceiling, the facts are simple | Fee about 1% of the claim, one hearing, a few months | 11.2 |
| Magistrates' Court | Above the ceiling, a company or amuta is the claimant, or the case is complex | Fee about 2.5% (paid in two halves), longer, costs risk higher | 11.4 |

See [Current Numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md) for the ceiling and the fees.

### Step 3: who is the claimant?

Only an **individual** (יחיד) may file in the Small Claims Court. A self-employed person (עוסק פטור or מורשה) counts as an individual; a company, partnership or amuta does not (Kol Zchut, January 2026). So if your amuta paid the hall, the amuta must sue in the Magistrates' Court, while if you paid personally, you may file a small claim. The **defendant** can be anyone: a person, a company, an amuta, a municipality, the State.

### Step 4: the cost and risk sheet

| Item | Small claims | Magistrates' Court |
|---|---|---|
| Court fee | 1% of the claim, with a minimum | 2.5% of the claim, with a minimum, half on filing and half before the first hearing |
| Lawyer | Not allowed without the court's permission (s. 63) | Allowed; not required |
| Your time | Writing the claim, one hearing day | Pleadings, preliminary hearings, affidavits, cross-examination, months to years |
| If you lose | The court may order you to pay the other side's expenses; amounts are usually modest | Costs follow the event (reg. 152) and lawyers' fees are included (reg. 153) |
| If you win and they do not pay | Execution Office (הוצאה לפועל) — easier if you have the defendant's ID or company number | Same |

The exemption from the fee for people who cannot pay is in lesson 11.6.

### Step 5: honest chances

Ask three questions:

1. **Evidence.** Can you prove each fact with a document, a photo, a message or a witness who will come? If the case is "my word against theirs", your chances are close to a coin toss.
2. **The law.** Which rule gives you the right — a contract, a consumer law, the services law (09.2), a tort? Name it (lesson 02.4).
3. **Collectability.** Will the defendant be able to pay? A judgment against a company that closed is a piece of paper.

### Step 6: think about the second-order effects

- **Counterclaims.** The defendant may file a counterclaim (תביעה שכנגד) against you (small claims reg. 5).
- **Publicity.** Suing can bring the dispute into the public record. If you plan to publish about the other side, read lessons 08.1–08.2 first.
- **Time limits.** Do not wait years: the limitation period protects the defendant, and evidence fades sooner.

## Worked example

**Situation.** Shira, a volunteer in a campaign for a new neighbourhood in a West Bank (Samaria) community, ordered 20,000 flyers from a Jerusalem print shop for a rally on 3 June, paying ₪6,800 from her own credit card. The shop promised delivery by 31 May in a WhatsApp message. The flyers arrived on 5 June, after the rally. The shop refuses a refund: "You received the goods."

1. **What she wants.** Her money back, or most of it. She does not need a precedent.
2. **The rule.** A breach of the agreed delivery date; she can rely on the shop's own message.
3. **Claimant.** She paid personally, so she may file a small claim. Had the campaign's amuta paid, it would have to use the Magistrates' Court.
4. **Ladder.** First a demand letter (template below) giving 14 days and offering to return the flyers. If no answer, a small claim in Jerusalem (where the shop is).
5. **Cost and risk.** Fee about ₪70 (1% of ₪6,800, rounded up to ₪5); one hearing day; low costs risk. Evidence: the WhatsApp promise, the receipt, the delivery note dated 5 June, a photo of the boxes.
6. **Decision.** Go, but after the letter. She diarises the 14-day deadline and the claim date.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- A real dispute you are part of (money, a defective service, damage to your property), or the fictional one below.
- The template [pre-action-demand-letter.md](../../templates/pre-action-demand-letter.md).
- 45 minutes.

Fictional dispute: a community hall in Haifa cancelled your lecture on disability rights two days before the event and kept your ₪2,400 deposit "under the contract". The contract you signed says the deposit is returned if the hall cancels.

### Steps

1. Write what you want in one line.
2. Name the legal rule that gives you the right (contract clause, consumer law or other).
3. Decide who the claimant is (you or a body) and who the defendant is (the legal entity, with its number).
4. Place your dispute on the ladder: which rung first, which next, and why.
5. Fill the cost-and-risk sheet with your numbers, using [Current Numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md).
6. List every fact and the proof you have for it; mark facts without proof.
7. Write a one-page memo: go / not yet / no, with the date of each next step.

### Template

- [pre-action-demand-letter.md](../../templates/pre-action-demand-letter.md) — מכתב דרישה לפני הגשת תביעה. You will send it (or dry-run it) in lesson 11.2.

### How you know it worked

- Your memo names the claimant, the defendant (legal name), the rule, the court and the amount.
- Every fact in it has a proof next to it, or is marked "no proof yet".
- Your decision would still make sense if you lost and had to pay modest costs.

- [ ] I know that only an individual may file a small claim, and that I may always choose the regular court instead.
- [ ] I can estimate the fee for my claim in both courts.

- [ ] I tried, or planned, a demand letter before suing.
- [ ] I checked that the defendant can be found and can pay.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| The amuta files a small claim | Not allowed: only an individual may file | File in the Magistrates' Court, or the person who paid files | Check who paid and who signed |
| Suing for more than the ceiling in small claims | The claim does not fit; the court may transfer it, or you waive the excess | Reduce the claim, or file in the Magistrates' Court | Decide the amount before choosing the court |
| Suing without a demand letter | The defendant may settle on day one, and you paid a fee and lost months for nothing | Send a letter now and ask for a short postponement | Send the letter first |
| "My word against theirs" | Close to a coin toss | Look for messages, receipts, witnesses | List proof per fact before you decide |
| Ignoring costs risk in the regular court | A costs order can exceed what you claimed | Settle, or narrow the claim | Price the risk on day one |
| Waiting years | Limitation, lost evidence | File promptly | Diarise the limitation date |

## Review

- Decide what you want first; a court gives money and a few orders, not policy changes.
- Climb the ladder: letter, regulator, mediation, small claims, Magistrates' Court.
- Only an individual may file a small claim; anyone can be sued there, and you may always choose the regular court (ss. 60–61).
- In the regular court the loser usually pays reasonable costs (regs 151–152).
- Check evidence, the legal rule and collectability before you file.
