# 14.5 · The Human Rights Council and Commissions of Inquiry

By the end of this lesson you will be able to explain how the UN Human Rights Council works and why Israel's relationship with it is contested, read a commission of inquiry report and Israel's response side by side without taking a side, and choose a realistic way for you or your group to engage.

## Why it matters

The Human Rights Council is the political body above the mechanisms of this module: it runs the UPR (14.3), appoints the Special Procedures (14.4) and creates commissions of inquiry. Its resolutions and reports are quoted in courts, parliaments and the media around the world, including in Israel. It is also the UN body with which Israel's relationship is most disputed. An activist who uses UN material — from any side — has to know what a Council resolution or a commission report is, who wrote it, how it was mandated and what Israel says about it, or the material will be misused or dismissed.

## The law

> Decides further that the Council shall consist of forty-seven Member States, which shall be elected directly and individually by secret ballot by the majority of the members of the General Assembly

*Gist:* the Council is a body of 47 states elected by the General Assembly — a political body, unlike the expert committees of lesson 14.2. ([GA res. 60/251, para. 7](https://docs.un.org/A/RES/60/251))

> Item 7. Human rights situation in Palestine and other occupied Arab territories

*Gist:* since 2007 the Council's permanent agenda has a standing item on this situation; it is the only item about a specific country. ([HRC res. 5/1, agenda](https://ap.ohchr.org/documents/E/HRC/resolutions/A_HRC_RES_5_1.doc))

> Decides to urgently establish an ongoing independent, international commission of inquiry, to be appointed by the President of the Human Rights Council, to investigate in the Occupied Palestinian Territory, including East Jerusalem, and in Israel all alleged violations of international humanitarian law and all alleged violations and abuses of international human rights law leading up to and since 13 April 2021, and all underlying root causes of recurrent tensions, instability and protraction of conflict

*Gist:* the special session of 27 May 2021 created a commission with an "ongoing" mandate covering both the Palestinian territory and Israel, and root causes. ([HRC res. S-30/1, para. 1](https://docs.un.org/A/HRC/RES/S-30/1))

> A complaint procedure is being established to address consistent patterns of gross and reliably attested violations of all human rights and all fundamental freedoms occurring in any part of the world and under any circumstances.

*Gist:* separately from the public debates, anyone may send the Council a confidential complaint about a consistent pattern of gross violations, after domestic remedies, unless they are ineffective. ([HRC res. 5/1, para. 85](https://ap.ohchr.org/documents/E/HRC/resolutions/A_HRC_RES_5_1.doc))

## How it works in practice

### The Council in one table

| Feature | How it works |
|---|---|
| Members | 47 states, three-year terms, elected by the General Assembly. Israel has never been a member; it takes part as an observer state. |
| Sessions | At least three regular sessions a year in Geneva (about February–April, June–July, September–October), plus special sessions. |
| Agenda | Ten standing items. Item 4: situations that require the Council's attention (any country). Item 7: Palestine and other occupied Arab territories. |
| Output | Resolutions (adopted by vote or consensus), which create or renew mandates — Special Procedures, commissions of inquiry, fact-finding missions — and request reports. |
| Civil society | NGOs with ECOSOC consultative status may make oral statements and hold side events; anyone may brief states and answer calls for input. |

### Commissions of inquiry

A commission of inquiry is a small panel of independent experts, with a secretariat in OHCHR, created by a Council resolution to establish facts, identify responsibility and report. It has no power to compel witnesses, no court, and cannot enter a country without consent. Its reports are findings, not judgments.

**The Independent International Commission of Inquiry on the Occupied Palestinian Territory, including East Jerusalem, and Israel:**

- Created by resolution S-30/1 on 27 May 2021, with an "ongoing" mandate.
- Commissioners named in UN Geneva's January 2026 announcement: Srinivasan Muralidhar (chair), Florence Mumba and Chris Sidoti. Check the current list on the OHCHR commission page.
- Its report A/HRC/62/22 (8 May 2026) covers killings and harm by non-state actors: settler attacks, and Palestinian armed groups in Gaza and the West Bank, including executions by Hamas-affiliated forces. The commission says it sent one request for information and access to the Government of Israel, and one each to the State of Palestine and the de facto authorities in Gaza (para. 3). Other reports, including a June 2026 paper on children and earlier findings that Israel rejects, are listed on its page.
- It runs open **calls for submissions** on the OHCHR commission page; anyone may send information.

### Who says what about the Council and the commission

| Who | Position (attributed, in their words where quoted) |
|---|---|
| **Israel**, at the adoption of its UPR outcome (3 Oct 2023, UN Geneva summary) | Recognised "the critical role of international mechanisms", but protested "institutional bias": being "the only country in the world with a dedicated Human Rights Council agenda item, the infamous item seven", a disproportionate number of resolutions and special sessions, and "the only country subjected to a permanent Commission of Inquiry with an effectively unlimited mandate". |
| **Israel**, Foreign Minister's letter to the Council President (Feb 2025, per ASIL) | Israel would not take part in the Council, citing "institutional bias"; called item 7 inherently discriminatory and the Council a "political tool". Israel is an observer state, not a member. |
| **Israel**, response to the commission (MFA, 14 June 2026) | Challenges the commission's "entire framework", including its composition, mandate and methodology, and lists what it calls errors and omissions in its paper on children. |
| **United States**, Executive Order 14199 (4 Feb 2025) | The United States "will not participate in the UNHRC", stating that it "has protected human rights abusers by allowing them to use the organization to shield themselves from scrutiny". |
| **States speaking in the item 7 debate** (4 Oct 2023, UN Geneva summary) | Many described violations in the occupied territory and said "it was important to keep item seven on the agenda", calling it a vital instrument for documenting violations. |
| **Other states in the same debate** | Some said item 7 showed "a long legacy of disproportionate focus on Israel", was "ineffective and needed to be removed". |

The [ICJ advisory opinions](../module-13/lesson-04.md) and the law of occupation are taught in Module 15. Here the skill is attribution: write "the commission found…", "Israel's Foreign Ministry responded…", never either in your own voice.

### How you can engage

| Route | Who | What it takes |
|---|---|---|
| Answer a call for submissions (commission, Special Procedure, OHCHR report) | Anyone | Facts, sources, consent; deadline on the call page |
| Oral statement at a session | NGOs with ECOSOC status (others join through them) | About 90 seconds to 2 minutes; register through the NGO; read the ISHR guide |
| Brief states on a resolution or a UPR | Anyone | A one-page brief to missions in Geneva or embassies in Israel |
| Confidential complaint procedure | Anyone with direct or reliable knowledge | A consistent pattern of gross violations, not a single case; domestic remedies tried unless ineffective; slow and confidential |
| Use Council output at home | Anyone | Quote resolutions and reports, attributed, with Israel's response, in letters, Knesset papers or petitions (lesson 13.5) |

## Worked example

**Situation.** Two groups each want to use the commission's May 2026 report A/HRC/62/22. "Shomrei HaGvul" (invented), a residents' group from a kibbutz near Gaza, wants to cite its findings on Hamas-affiliated forces in a letter to MKs about victims' support. "Ein Lev" (invented), a Palestinian-Israeli human-rights group in Jaffa, wants to cite its findings on settler attacks in a letter to the police.

1. **Read the source.** Both download A/HRC/62/22 and note its mandate (S-30/1), its date and its method (para. 3: remote interviews, open-source material, a call for submissions; one request for information and access sent to Israel).
2. **Quote exactly.** Each copies the paragraph it relies on with the paragraph number.
3. **Attribute.** "The UN commission of inquiry found, in its May 2026 report…". Not "it is proven that…".
4. **Add the response.** Each adds one sentence on Israel's official position on the commission (MFA, June 2026), with the link. A reader who disagrees can check both.
5. **Domestic ask.** Shomrei HaGvul asks the Knesset Health Committee for a session on long-term care; Ein Lev asks the police for data on investigations under FOI (lesson 05.2). The UN report supports the ask; it is not the ask.

**Mirror test.** The method is the same for a group citing the commission against Israel's policies and for a group citing the same commission's findings on Hamas. The discipline — exact quote, attribution, the other side's official position, a domestic ask — does not change.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- One commission of inquiry report or Council resolution related to your issue (A/HRC/62/22 is a good start), and Israel's official response if there is one (MFA Human Rights bodies page).
- 75 minutes.

### Steps

1. Write down the document's symbol, date, mandate (which resolution), and author.
2. Choose two paragraphs relevant to your issue. Copy them exactly, with paragraph numbers.
3. Find Israel's official position on that report or body. Copy one or two sentences exactly, with the link.
4. Build a two-column "who says what" table: the body's finding, Israel's response. Add a third column if another state or body responded.
5. Write a 150-word neutral summary for a reader who knows nothing. Ask someone with different views to read it and point out any sentence that sounds like your opinion; fix it.
6. Pick one engagement route from the table above and write the next concrete step, with a date.

### Template

No template for this lesson. Use the two-column table from step 4; if you then send information to a commission or a Special Procedure, use [special-procedures-communication.md](../../templates/special-procedures-communication.md) as your checklist for facts and consent.

### How you know it worked

- Every claim in your summary has a "who": the commission, Israel's Foreign Ministry, a named state.
- A reader from the other side of the debate agrees that your summary is accurate, even if they disagree with the finding.
- Your next step is something you can do on your own, with a date.

### Self-check

- [ ] Symbol, date, mandate and author recorded.
- [ ] Exact quotes with paragraph numbers.
- [ ] Israel's official response quoted and linked.
- [ ] Neutral summary checked by someone with different views.
- [ ] One engagement route and next step chosen.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Writing a commission's finding as fact in your own voice | Dismissed as partisan; possible defamation risk if it names people (Module 8) | Rewrite with attribution and the response | "The commission found…; Israel responded…" |
| Treating a Council resolution as binding law | Wrong claim, easily rebutted | Describe it as a political decision of 47 states | Check what kind of document it is |
| Calling every UN report "the UN" | Confuses the Council, the commission, OHCHR and the treaty bodies | Name the exact author and symbol | Record the symbol and mandate first |
| Planning to speak at the Council without ECOSOC status | Not admitted to speak | Partner with an accredited NGO or brief states instead | Read the ISHR oral-statement guide |
| Sending a single case to the complaint procedure | Not admissible; it addresses patterns | Send it to a Special Procedure (14.4) | Match the case to the tool |
| Using only one side's sources | Readers stop trusting your material | Add the other official position | Use the "who says what" table every time |

## Review

- The Human Rights Council is a political body of 47 elected states; it creates and renews Special Procedures and commissions of inquiry and runs the UPR.
- Item 7 is the only country-specific standing item; Israel and some states call it discriminatory, while other states call it vital for documenting violations.
- The commission of inquiry created by resolution S-30/1 has an ongoing mandate covering the Palestinian territory and Israel; its findings are not judgments, and Israel rejects its framework.
- Anyone can answer calls for submissions, brief states, or use the confidential complaint procedure; oral statements need an ECOSOC-accredited NGO.
- Always attribute: the body's finding, Israel's official response, and other states' positions, each with its source.
