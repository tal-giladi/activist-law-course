# 14.3 · The Universal Periodic Review

By the end of this lesson you will be able to explain how every UN member state, Israel included, is reviewed by other states, find what Israel was asked and how it answered last time, and plan a stakeholder submission for Israel's next review that is accepted rather than rejected on form.

## Why it matters

The Universal Periodic Review (UPR) is the one UN process in which Israel is questioned by other governments rather than by independent experts, and in which Israel itself says, recommendation by recommendation, whether it supports or only "notes" what it was asked. Supported recommendations become commitments that Israeli ministries report on and that activists can quote back at them in letters, Knesset committees and petitions. The process is open to any organisation, with no UN accreditation, but its form and deadline rules are strict: a submission that is late or over the word limit is simply not considered.

## The law

> Undertake a universal periodic review, based on objective and reliable information, of the fulfilment by each State of its human rights obligations and commitments in a manner which ensures universality of coverage and equal treatment with respect to all States; the review shall be a cooperative mechanism, based on an interactive dialogue, with the full involvement of the country concerned

*Gist:* the General Assembly told the new Human Rights Council to review every state, equally, in a cooperative dialogue with that state. ([GA res. 60/251, para. 5(e)](https://docs.un.org/A/RES/60/251))

> Additional, credible and reliable information provided by other relevant stakeholders to the universal periodic review which should also be taken into consideration by the Council in the review. The Office of the High Commissioner for Human Rights will prepare a summary of such information which shall not exceed 10 pages.

*Gist:* stakeholder information is one of the three documents of every review; OHCHR condenses all stakeholder submissions into a short summary. ([HRC res. 5/1, annex para. 15(c)](https://ap.ohchr.org/documents/E/HRC/resolutions/A_HRC_RES_5_1.doc))

> Written submissions should not exceed 2815 words in the case of individual submissions, to which additional documentation can be annexed for reference. Submissions by coalitions of stakeholders should not exceed 5630 words.

*Gist:* the word limits are part of the binding form rules — a submission that breaks them is not considered. ([OHCHR technical guidelines, para. 15](https://upr-info.org/sites/default/files/documents/2015-03/ohchr_submissions_technical_guidelines_en.pdf); also listed on [Current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md))

> The UPR mechanism does not provide for confidentiality and is conducted on the basis of public documents.

*Gist:* every accepted submission is published with the submitter's name, so mention individual cases only if no one is put at risk. (Technical guidelines, paras 24–25)

## How it works in practice

### The review in five steps

1. **Three documents.** Israel's national report; a UN compilation of what treaty bodies and Special Procedures said; and the OHCHR summary of stakeholder submissions (yours goes here).
2. **Working Group dialogue.** A three-and-a-half-hour public session in Geneva. Any state may speak and make recommendations. Watch it later on UN Web TV.
3. **Outcome report.** Lists every recommendation and which state made it.
4. **Israel's response.** Israel answers each recommendation: **supported** or **noted** (states cannot "reject" formally, but "noted" means no commitment).
5. **Adoption** at a later Human Rights Council session, where NGOs with UN accreditation may make short statements. Then implementation, an optional mid-term report, and the next cycle about four and a half years later.

### Israel's UPR record

| Cycle | Review | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1st | December 2008 | — |
| 2nd | October 2013 | Israel suspended its relations with the Council in 2012 and did not appear on 29 January 2013, the first state to miss its review; the Council postponed it, and Israel was reviewed on 29 October 2013 (UPR Info) |
| 3rd | January 2018 | Outcome A/HRC/38/15 |
| 4th | 9 May 2023, 43rd session | National report A/HRC/WG.6/43/ISR/1; outcome A/HRC/54/16. Of 320 recommendations, 156 supported, 154 noted, 10 with additional clarification (adopted 3 October 2023) |
| 5th | About April–May 2028 (provisional) | UPR Info lists a stakeholder deadline of about 1 November 2027, a national report about 1 February 2028, and the Working Group about April 2028; its session list shows session 57, May 2028. Confirm on the OHCHR UPR page. |

**Who says what at the 2023 adoption (UN Geneva summary, 3 October 2023).** Israel's delegation said it supported 171 recommendations in whole or in part, recognised "the critical role of international mechanisms", and protested "institutional bias" at the Council — "the infamous item seven" and a commission of inquiry "with an effectively unlimited mandate" (see lesson 14.5). Some states praised Israel's cooperation and its steps on children, persons with disabilities and women; others said Israel had rejected recommendations on settlements, the use of force and the blockade.

### Finding past recommendations

- **Universal Human Rights Index** (uhri.ohchr.org): filter by country "Israel", mechanism "UPR", and theme. Each entry shows the recommending state, the cycle and Israel's position.
- **UPR Info's Israel page**: a database of recommendations and responses, plus the documents of each cycle.
- **The outcome report and its addendum** (A/HRC/54/16 and Add.1): Israel's position on each recommendation, sometimes with an explanation.

### Writing a stakeholder submission

The OHCHR technical guidelines make the form and deadline rules **binding**. In short (numbers also on [Current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)):

- **Length:** individual submission up to 2,815 words; joint (coalition) submission up to 5,630 words. Footnotes do not count, but they are ignored in the summary. The cover page does not count.
- **Form:** Word file only; numbered paragraphs and pages; a cover page that clearly identifies the submitter, and a short paragraph about the organisation.
- **Language:** a UN official language, preferably English, French or Spanish.
- **Where and when:** only through the online UPR submissions system (uprdoc.ohchr.org, free account), by 23:59 on the deadline posted on the OHCHR UPR page — indicatively about five to eight months before the session. Late submissions are not considered, and revisions are not accepted.
- **Content (advisory, but it decides whether you are quoted):** written for the UPR; covers the period since the last review; starts from **implementation of the recommendations from the last cycle**; first-hand information first; a few concrete recommendations, each linked to an issue. Do not list Israel's treaties and committee findings — OHCHR's UN compilation already does.

Use the [UPR stakeholder submission template](../../templates/upr-stakeholder-submission.md).

### After you submit: getting a state to say it

The summary of all stakeholders is ten pages, so your words may shrink to a sentence. The real impact comes when a **state** makes your recommendation in the Working Group. Practitioners therefore:

- send a one-page "advocacy factsheet" with two or three recommendations to the Geneva or Tel Aviv embassies of states that care about the topic, a few months before the review;
- take part in UPR Info's pre-sessions in Geneva (about a month before the review), where NGOs brief diplomats;
- write recommendations in "SMART" form: one action, a time frame, something measurable ("Adopt, by 2030, a national plan to…").

## Worked example

**Situation.** "Kol Achim" (invented), a coalition of Ethiopian-Israeli community groups, wants the 5th-cycle review to address policing.

1. **Last cycle.** In the UHRI it searches the 2023 recommendations for racial profiling and police conduct and notes which ones Israel supported.
2. **Timing.** UPR Info lists a stakeholder deadline of about 1 November 2027. The coalition diarises a check of the OHCHR UPR page every month from April 2027.
3. **Facts.** It collects its own anonymised incident log (with consent), data from FOI requests to the police (lesson 05.2), and two public reports of the Department for Investigations of Police Officers.
4. **Structure.** A joint submission (up to 5,630 words): introduction; follow-up table on each 2023 recommendation (implemented, partly, not); two issues; four recommendations.
5. **Recommendation.** "Publish annually, from 2029, data on stops and searches broken down by ethnic origin."
6. **Advocacy.** In early 2028 it sends a one-page factsheet to five embassies that made similar recommendations in 2023, and applies for a UPR Info pre-session.

**Mirror test.** The same route is used by a forum of hostages' families asking the state to strengthen support services for released hostages, by a settlers' regional council on equal access to services, by a Bedouin council on recognised villages, or by a Haredi organisation on religious freedom. The form rules are identical for all.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- Your issue and treaty map (lesson 14.1).
- The UHRI (uhri.ohchr.org) or the UPR Info Israel page.
- [upr-stakeholder-submission.md](../../templates/upr-stakeholder-submission.md).
- 90 minutes.

### Steps

1. In the UHRI, find every 2023 UPR recommendation to Israel on your issue. Copy the text, the state, and Israel's position (supported or noted).
2. For two of them, write one line each: implemented, partly implemented, or not — with a source.
3. Draft the cover page and the organisation paragraph from the template.
4. Write a 600–800-word draft of one section: the issue, what changed since 2023, two pieces of first-hand evidence.
5. Write two SMART recommendations.
6. Run a word count and check the form list in the template.
7. List three states that made recommendations on your topic in 2023 and could be approached in 2028.

### Template

[upr-stakeholder-submission.md](../../templates/upr-stakeholder-submission.md) — an English skeleton for an individual or joint UPR submission, with a cover page, a follow-up table, a recommendation formula and a form checklist based on the OHCHR technical guidelines.

### How you know it worked

- Every claim about the last cycle cites the recommendation and Israel's response.
- Your recommendations could be read aloud by a diplomat without editing.
- Your word count leaves room for the remaining sections under the limit.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Submitting after the posted deadline | Not considered | Turn it into an advocacy factsheet for states | Check the OHCHR UPR page monthly from about a year before |
| Over the word limit, or a PDF instead of Word | Not considered (binding form rules) | Cut and resubmit only if the deadline has not passed | Count words; save as Word |
| Listing treaties and committee findings | Wasted words; OHCHR already compiles them | Replace with first-hand facts | Start from the last cycle's recommendations |
| Vague recommendations ("respect human rights") | Not taken up by any state | Rewrite as one action, a time frame, a measure | Use the SMART formula |
| Naming individuals at risk | Published with your name; danger to them | Ask OHCHR to remove it only in extreme cases | Anonymise from the start |
| Stopping at submission | One line in a ten-page summary | Brief embassies; join a pre-session | Plan advocacy for the months before the review |

## Review

- The UPR reviews every state, by other states, about every four and a half years; Israel's 4th review was on 9 May 2023 and its 5th is provisionally due around April–May 2028.
- Three documents: the national report, the UN compilation, and the summary of stakeholder submissions.
- Israel answers each recommendation as supported or noted; in 2023, 156 of 320 were supported.
- Form and deadline rules are binding: word limits, Word format, the online system, the posted deadline.
- Impact comes when a state adopts your recommendation, so plan embassy briefings, not only the submission.
