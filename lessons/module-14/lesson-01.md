# 14.1 · The Core Treaties Israel Ratified

By the end of this lesson you will know which UN human-rights treaties bind Israel, what Israel reserved, why there is no UN individual complaint against Israel, and which UN routes are still open — and you will have built a one-page treaty map for an issue you care about.

## Why it matters

Every UN tool in this module starts from one question: which obligation is Israel being asked to meet? A shadow report, a UPR recommendation or a letter to a Special Rapporteur that cites the wrong treaty, an article Israel reserved, or a complaint procedure Israel never accepted is easy to dismiss. Activists across the spectrum use these treaties — a disability group citing the CRPD on accessible voting, a Haredi parents' group citing the CRC on education funding, a hostage family citing the CAT, a Bedouin council citing CERD on recognised villages, a secular couple citing the ICCPR on civil marriage. Knowing the map tells you in five minutes which door is open and which is locked.

## The law

International material is quoted in English. Israel's reservations are quoted from the UN Treaty Collection.

> Each State Party to the present Covenant undertakes to respect and to ensure to all individuals within its territory and subject to its jurisdiction the rights recognized in the present Covenant, without distinction of any kind, such as race, colour, sex, language, religion, political or other opinion, national or social origin, property, birth or other status.

*Gist:* the ICCPR binds the state towards everyone "within its territory and subject to its jurisdiction" — the phrase at the centre of the scope dispute below. (ICCPR art. 2(1); [UN Treaty Collection, IV-4](https://treaties.un.org/Pages/ViewDetails.aspx?src=TREATY&mtdsg_no=IV-4&chapter=4&clang=_en))

> The States Parties to the present Covenant undertake to submit reports on the measures they have adopted which give effect to the rights recognized herein and on the progress made in the enjoyment of those rights.

*Gist:* every state party must report periodically to the committee — this reporting duty is what shadow reports plug into (lesson 14.2). (ICCPR art. 40(1))

> With reference to Article 23 of the Covenant, and any other provision thereof to which the present reservation may be relevant, matters of personal status are governed in Israel by the religious law of the parties concerned. To the extent that such law is inconsistent with its obligations under the Covenant, Israel reserves the right to apply that law.

*Gist:* Israel's ICCPR reservation keeps religious personal-status law (marriage and divorce) even where it conflicts with the Covenant. ([UN Treaty Collection, IV-4](https://treaties.un.org/Pages/ViewDetails.aspx?src=TREATY&mtdsg_no=IV-4&chapter=4&clang=_en))

> In accordance with article 28 of the Convention, the State of Israel hereby declares that it does not recognize the competence of the Committee provided for in article 20.

*Gist:* Israel excluded the Committee against Torture's confidential inquiry procedure. ([UN Treaty Collection, IV-9](https://treaties.un.org/Pages/ViewDetails.aspx?src=TREATY&mtdsg_no=IV-9&chapter=4&clang=_en))

> A State Party to the Covenant that becomes a Party to the present Protocol recognizes the competence of the Committee to receive and consider communications from individuals subject to its jurisdiction who claim to be victims of a violation by that State Party of any of the rights set forth in the Covenant.

*Gist:* individual complaints exist only against states that join the optional protocol (or make the matching declaration) — Israel has not. (ICCPR First Optional Protocol, art. 1)

## How it works in practice

### The seven treaties

Data from the OHCHR Treaty Body Database and the UN Treaty Collection, checked on 13 October 2026.

| Treaty | Israel ratified | Monitoring committee | Israel's reservations and declarations | Individual complaints against Israel |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CERD — racial discrimination | 3 Jan 1979 | CERD | Not bound by art. 22 (disputes to the ICJ) | No (no art. 14 declaration) |
| ICCPR — civil and political rights | 3 Oct 1991 | Human Rights Committee (CCPR) | Art. 23 (personal status); art. 4 notice: the 1948 state of emergency, derogating from art. 9 (arrest and detention) | No (OP1 not ratified) |
| ICESCR — economic, social and cultural rights | 3 Oct 1991 | CESCR | None | No (OP not ratified) |
| CEDAW — discrimination against women | 3 Oct 1991 | CEDAW | Art. 7(b) (women as religious-court judges); art. 16 (personal-status laws); not bound by art. 29(1) | No (OP not ratified) |
| CAT — torture | 3 Oct 1991 | CAT | Does not recognise art. 20 (inquiries); not bound by art. 30(1) | No (no art. 22 declaration) |
| CRC — rights of the child | 3 Oct 1991 | CRC | None. Also ratified the optional protocols on children in armed conflict (2005) and on the sale of children (2008) | No (OP3 not ratified) |
| CRPD — persons with disabilities | 28 Sep 2012 | CRPD | Art. 23(1)(a) (marriage, to the extent of personal-status laws) | No (OP not ratified) |

Israel is not a party to the Convention on Enforced Disappearance, the Migrant Workers Convention, the Optional Protocol to the CAT (OPCAT, prison-visit mechanism) or the ICCPR's Second Optional Protocol (death penalty).

How to read a reservation and check status yourself is in [lesson 13.2](../module-13/lesson-02.md). Here the point is practical: before you cite CEDAW art. 16 or ICCPR art. 23 on marriage, say that Israel reserved on it — and argue from articles it did not reserve on (equality, art. 26; privacy, art. 17) or from the committees' calls to withdraw the reservation.

### No complaints: what that means for you

Because Israel accepted none of the individual-complaint procedures, **no person can file a UN treaty-body complaint against Israel** — not an Israeli citizen, not a resident of the West Bank or Gaza, not a foreign national. The Committee against Torture again invited Israel to make the art. 22 declaration in December 2025 (CAT/C/ISR/CO/6, para. 55); Israel has not done so.

The routes that stay open, and the lessons that teach them:

| Route | Who can use it | Needs Israel's consent? | Lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| Comment on the state's draft report | Anyone; the Ministry of Justice invites NGOs | Run by the state | 14.2 |
| Shadow report to a treaty body | Anyone, no UN status needed | No | 14.2 |
| Universal Periodic Review submission | Any stakeholder | No | 14.3 |
| Communication to a Special Procedure | Anyone (two working groups: victims, families or representatives only) | No | 14.4 |
| Information to a commission of inquiry; Human Rights Council complaint procedure | Anyone | No | 14.5 |

None of these produces a binding judgment. They produce questions to the state, recommendations and public records — which you then use in Israeli forums (lesson 13.5: the presumption that Israeli law is read in line with Israel's international obligations).

### Where the treaties apply: two positions

| Who | Position |
|---|---|
| **Israel** (5th ICCPR report, 2019, paras 22–24) | The Covenant applies to state territory and is implemented "throughout the State of Israel"; "the Convention, which is territorially bound, does not apply, nor was it intended to apply, to areas beyond a state's national territory." Israel adds that its courts refer to the ICCPR in West Bank cases. |
| **Committee against Torture** (CAT/C/ISR/CO/6, 2025, paras 8–9), citing other treaty bodies and the ICJ | Regrets Israel's position and "reaffirms that the Convention applies to all territory under the jurisdiction of the State Party, including the Occupied Palestinian Territory," including in armed conflict and occupation. |

The ICJ's view is covered in [lesson 13.4](../module-13/lesson-04.md). For your work: a submission about the West Bank or Gaza will be answered by Israel on this ground first. State the dispute in one neutral sentence and move on to the facts.

### Not law in Israel unless legislated

None of the seven treaties was enacted as a whole by the Knesset. In an Israeli court they work through the presumption of compatibility (lesson 13.5), not as a direct cause of action. At the UN they are the yardstick.

## Worked example

**Situation.** Dana and Yossi, a secular couple from Haifa, cannot marry in Israel without a religious ceremony. Dana's cousin Amira, a Muslim woman from Tamra, is in a long divorce case in the Sharia court. Both want to raise personal-status law at the UN.

1. **Find the treaties.** ICCPR art. 23 (marriage) and arts 2, 26 (equality); CEDAW art. 16 (marriage and family) and art. 2; ICESCR art. 10 (family).
2. **Check reservations.** ICCPR art. 23 and CEDAW art. 16 are reserved. Arts 2 and 26 ICCPR and art. 2 CEDAW are not.
3. **Check complaint routes.** None: Israel accepted no individual complaints. Their cases cannot go to a committee as individual complaints.
4. **Pick the open route.** CEDAW reviews Israel's 7th report (session 94, 2027, to be confirmed — lesson 14.2), so a joint shadow report on personal status is timely; the UPR (lesson 14.3) is next.
5. **Frame the ask.** "The Committee may wish to ask Israel whether it will review its reservation to art. 16 and how it ensures equality under art. 2 for women who cannot obtain a divorce." The reservation is named, not ignored.

**Mirror test.** The same map serves an Orthodox women's organisation working on get refusal (מסורבות גט), a Haredi organisation arguing that changes to religious courts would harm religious freedom (ICCPR art. 18), or a Druze community group on religious autonomy. The treaty check is identical.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- An issue you care about (from your module 1 issue map, if you have one).
- The OHCHR Israel ratification page and the UN Treaty Collection (links in Learn more and the sources).
- 50 minutes.

### Steps

1. Write your issue in one sentence: who is affected, by what, where.
2. List two or three treaties that cover it. Open each treaty's text and copy the article numbers that fit.
3. For each article, check Israel's reservations and declarations on the UN Treaty Collection. Mark "reserved" or "not reserved".
4. Note whether your issue is inside Israel, in East Jerusalem, or in the West Bank or Gaza, and write one neutral sentence on the scope dispute if it applies.
5. Open the Israel country page in the OHCHR database and note, for each committee you listed, the date of the last concluding observations.
6. Choose one open route from the table above and say why it fits.

### How you know it worked

- Every article you cite is checked against Israel's reservations.
- Your map does not propose an individual complaint to a committee.
- Someone with opposite politics could read your scope sentence and agree that it is accurate.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Citing a reserved article as if it binds Israel | Israel's reply points to the reservation; your argument collapses | Acknowledge the reservation; argue from unreserved articles or ask the committee to press for withdrawal | Check the UN Treaty Collection before citing |
| Planning an individual complaint to a committee | Rejected: Israel accepted no complaint procedure | Use a shadow report, the UPR or a Special Procedure | Check the "individual complaints" column first |
| Writing "Israel signed" when you mean "ratified" | Looks careless; signing alone does not bind | Correct the date and the verb | Copy dates from the UN record |
| Stating the scope question in your own voice ("the treaties obviously apply…") | Dismissed as partisan by one side | Attribute: "Israel's position is…; the Committee's position is…" | Use the two-position table |
| Citing a treaty as Israeli law in a court petition | Weak: it is not incorporated | Use the presumption of compatibility (lesson 13.5) | Separate the UN argument from the domestic one |

## Review

- Israel ratified seven core treaties: CERD (1979), ICCPR, ICESCR, CEDAW, CAT, CRC (all 1991) and CRPD (2012).
- Its reservations concern mainly religious personal-status law (ICCPR 23, CEDAW 7(b) and 16, CRPD 23(1)(a)), the CAT inquiry procedure, and dispute clauses; it notified an ICCPR art. 4 derogation on detention.
- Israel accepted no individual-complaint procedure, so the open routes are shadow reports, the UPR, Special Procedures and the Human Rights Council's mechanisms.
- Israel holds that the treaties apply only to its national territory; the treaty bodies hold that they apply wherever Israel has jurisdiction. Attribute both.
