# 14.4 · Special Procedures: Sending a Communication

By the end of this lesson you will be able to choose the right UN Special Rapporteur or Working Group for a situation, prepare a communication they can act on — with consent, sources and a clear request — and read how Israel answers such letters, so you can draft your own in a dry run.

## Why it matters

Special Procedures are independent experts appointed by the Human Rights Council — Special Rapporteurs, Independent Experts and Working Groups — each with a theme (torture, freedom of expression, human-rights defenders, the right to health, arbitrary detention…) or a country. Anyone can write to them. Israel does not need to have ratified anything, you do not need to exhaust Israeli remedies, and there is no lawyer or fee. If the information is credible and within the mandate, the experts may write to the Government of Israel, ask for answers within about 60 days, and later publish the letter and the reply. For a detained activist, a threatened journalist or a community facing a sudden demolition or law, this is the fastest UN route.

## The law

> (a) The communication should not be manifestly unfounded or politically motivated;
> (b) The communication should contain a factual description of the alleged violations of human rights;
> (c) The language in the communication should not be abusive;
> (d) The communication should be submitted by a person or a group of persons claiming to be victim of violations or by any person or group of persons, including non-governmental organizations, acting in good faith in accordance with principles of human rights, and free from politically motivated stands or contrary to, the provisions of the Charter of the United Nations, and claiming to have direct or reliable knowledge of those violations substantiated by clear information;
> (e) The communication should not be exclusively based on reports disseminated by mass media.

*Gist:* five tests decide whether experts act on your letter: facts not slogans, polite language, a good-faith sender with direct or reliable knowledge, and more than press clippings. ([HRC res. 5/2, Code of Conduct, art. 9](https://ap.ohchr.org/documents/E/HRC/resolutions/A_HRC_RES_5_2.doc))

> Mandate-holders may resort to urgent appeals in cases where the alleged violations are time-sensitive in terms of involving loss of life, life-threatening situations or either imminent or ongoing damage of a very grave nature to victims that cannot be addressed in a timely manner by the procedure under article 9 of the present Code.

*Gist:* urgent appeals are for life-threatening or very grave imminent harm; everything else is a letter of allegation. (Code of Conduct, art. 10)

## How it works in practice

> [!WARNING]
> Sending information to the UN can expose you and the people you write about. Get written consent; tell OHCHR if anyone faces a security risk; keep your files encrypted (Module 17). If someone is threatened or punished for cooperating with the UN, OHCHR collects reprisal cases (reprisals@ohchr.org, per OHCHR calls for information — check the OHCHR reprisals page before sending). Never collect testimony in a dangerous place; this course teaches remote and open-source work only.

### Is this the right tool?

| Fits a communication | Better elsewhere |
|---|---|
| A specific, recent or imminent event: detention, threat, attack, eviction, a bill about to pass | A general long-term pattern with no new event: shadow report (14.2) or UPR (14.3) |
| You have first-hand facts or reliable direct sources | You have only news articles |
| The person concerned consents | The person refuses, or cannot be asked safely |
| Speed matters | You want a binding remedy: Israeli courts (Module 5) |

Communications also go to non-state actors and companies in some mandates, and experts may comment on draft legislation through "other letters".

### Choosing the mandate

- Find the closest **thematic** mandates on the OHCHR Special Procedures pages (for example torture; freedom of expression; peaceful assembly and association; human-rights defenders; freedom of religion; adequate housing; rights of persons with disabilities; violence against women; arbitrary detention).
- There is also a **country** mandate, the "Special Rapporteur on the situation of human rights in the Palestinian territories occupied since 1967" (its official title). Thematic mandates can be addressed for situations anywhere, including inside Israel.
- You may send the same case to several mandates and ask for a **joint** communication — explain why. Some mandates receive more than 100 submissions a week (ISHR), so short and timely wins.
- **Two exceptions:** the Working Group on Arbitrary Detention and the Working Group on Enforced or Involuntary Disappearances accept cases only from the person concerned, the family, or their representatives (including an NGO acting for them).

### The online form

OHCHR "highly recommends" the online form at [spsubmission.ohchr.org](https://spsubmission.ohchr.org/) because it lets OHCHR track your case (English, French or Spanish). Its sections:

1. **Start** and **Submission type** (an individual, a group, legislation or policy).
2. **Victims:** name, age, gender, residence, or a description of the affected group.
3. **Submitter:** you or your organisation, with contact details.
4. **Disclosure:** consent to disclose names to the government, and to publish them in the public report to the Council.
5. **Case info:** what happened, when, where, the law or policy involved, what was done nationally (complaints, petitions, police), and what you ask the experts to do.
6. **Perpetrators:** who, with titles, if known.
7. **Submit.** You get a reference and a password. **A draft can be saved for 24 hours only**, then it is lost — write the text offline first with the [communication template](../../templates/special-procedures-communication.md).

Alternatives: e-mail to the mandate (OHCHR lists contacts on each mandate page; urgent cases have used urgent-action@ohchr.org) or post to OHCHR-UNOG, 8-14 Avenue de la Paix, 1211 Geneva 10, Switzerland.

### Consent and disclosure

- **By default the experts' letter to the government names the victims.** Names are withheld only if your submission makes clear there are security concerns.
- Names of victims under 18 and of victims of sexual violence are not published in the public reports.
- Without the consent of the people or organisations named, ISHR advises that the information will not be considered.
- Record consent in writing: what will be sent, to whom (UN, Government of Israel, public report), and that the person may withdraw before you submit.

### What happens next

1. Automatic acknowledgement — it does **not** mean action was taken. You will usually not be told whether a letter was sent.
2. OHCHR staff screen the case and may write to you for more details.
3. If the experts act, they write to the Government of Israel (through its Geneva mission) and usually ask for a reply within 60 days; faster if a press release is planned.
4. After the deadline the letter, and any reply, are published in the [communications database](https://spcommreports.ohchr.org/) and listed in the joint communications report to each Council session.
5. Keep OHCHR informed: release, new threats, a court ruling.

### What a reply from Israel looks like

Israel publishes some replies. For example, its reply to communication AL ISR 10/2024 from the Special Rapporteur on torture (16 May 2024) sets out the legal framework for detention after 7 October 2023, oversight and investigations, and states that Israel acts in accordance with international and domestic law. Its reply to the joint communication AL ISR 1/2024 (7 February 2024) rejects the allegations as unsubstantiated and based on unreliable sources, and says the mandate-holders failed to address sexual violence committed by Hamas. Reading a communication and the reply side by side teaches you what facts a state can contest — and what it cannot.

## Worked example

**Situation.** Eliyahu, 17, from a West Bank outpost, has been held for three months under an administrative detention order. His mother, Tzipi, says he was never questioned and that his lawyer cannot see the evidence. Judicial review of the order did not release him.

1. **Tool.** A specific, ongoing detention: a communication fits. A shadow report would be too slow.
2. **Mandate.** Working Group on Arbitrary Detention (the family may submit), and the Special Rapporteur on counter-terrorism and human rights (security detention), jointly. Because he is a minor, the experts will not publish his name.
3. **Consent.** Tzipi signs a consent form; Eliyahu confirms through his lawyer. They agree his name may be disclosed to the government.
4. **Facts.** Dates of the order and extensions, the court decisions (with case numbers), the lawyer's statement on access to evidence. No media reports as the main source.
5. **Request.** Ask the experts to seek information from Israel on the legal basis, the evidence, judicial review and alternatives to detention for a minor.
6. **Submission.** Text written offline in the template, pasted into the form in one sitting, reference and password saved.

**Mirror test.** The same steps fit a Palestinian student from Hebron held in administrative detention, an Arab-Israeli journalist arrested over social-media posts (freedom of expression mandate), or a Haredi yeshiva student detained in a protest (peaceful assembly mandate). The criteria do not change with the cause.

## Do it yourself

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Act in your own name only (or for a person who asked you to). This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing. The default here is a dry run: do not submit unless there is a real case and the person concerned has consented.

### You need

- A real situation you know first-hand, or a realistic invented one.
- [special-procedures-communication.md](../../templates/special-procedures-communication.md).
- The communications database (spcommreports.ohchr.org).
- 90 minutes.

### Steps

1. In the communications database, filter by "Israel" and open one communication and, if published, Israel's reply. Note the mandates, the request and how the reply answers each point.
2. Decide whether your situation fits a communication (use the table above). If not, write which tool fits.
3. Choose one to three mandates and write one sentence each on why.
4. Fill the template offline: victims, submitter, disclosure and consent, facts in date order, national steps already taken, perpetrators, your request.
5. Check the five Code of Conduct tests against your draft.
6. Write the consent form text in the language of the person concerned.
7. Stop here unless the case is real and consented. If it is, open the online form only when your text is final.

### Template

[special-procedures-communication.md](../../templates/special-procedures-communication.md) — an English draft that follows the sections of the OHCHR online form, with a consent record, a Code of Conduct check and a follow-up log.

### How you know it worked

- A reader who sees only your first paragraph knows who, what, when, where, and what you ask.
- Every fact has a date and a source; no fact rests only on a news report.
- Consent covers exactly what you will disclose and to whom.

### Self-check

- [ ] Situation fits a communication; mandates chosen with reasons.
- [ ] WGAD or WGEID case comes from the person, family or representative.
- [ ] Facts in date order with sources; national remedies listed.
- [ ] Consent recorded; security concerns stated if names must be withheld.
- [ ] Text written offline; 24-hour draft limit planned for.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Relying only on news articles | Fails art. 9(e); not acted on | Add first-hand statements, documents, court records | Collect direct evidence first |
| Naming people without consent | Not considered; risk to them | Withdraw or correct through OHCHR | Written consent before drafting |
| Typing directly into the form and leaving it | Draft lost after 24 hours | Start again from your saved text | Write offline in the template |
| Sending a detention case to WGAD as an unrelated NGO | Not admissible for that working group | Get the family's authorisation | Check the two working-group exceptions |
| Abusive or political language | Fails art. 9(a) or (c) | Rewrite as neutral facts | Describe acts, not opinions |
| Expecting a ruling or quick news | Frustration | Send updates; use the published letter in Israeli advocacy | Know that letters are confidential until published |

## Review

- Anyone may write to the Special Procedures; no ratification, no exhaustion of remedies and no fee are needed.
- The Code of Conduct (art. 9) requires facts, good faith, polite language and more than media reports; urgent appeals (art. 10) are for grave imminent harm.
- The online form asks for victims, submitter, disclosure, case information and perpetrators; drafts last 24 hours.
- Names go to the government by default; get consent and state any security concern.
- Letters and replies are published in the communications database after about 60 days; Israel publishes some of its replies.
