# 07.2 · Detention, Arrest and Your Rights

By the end of this lesson you will be able to tell, in the moment, whether you are detained or arrested, know what the police must tell you and do for you from the street to the station, count the hours until you must see a judge, and carry a filled-in arrest card and a support person who knows what to do.

> [!WARNING]
> If you are arrested, ask for a lawyer at once; if you have none, ask for the Public Defender. This lesson teaches your rights so that you can use them and recognise when they are broken; it does not replace a lawyer. Never resist an arrest physically, even one you think is unlawful (lesson 07.1). This is not legal advice.

## Why it matters

The first hours after an arrest decide a lot: whether you are released from the station or spend the night in a cell, whether you talk before you have consulted anyone, whether anyone outside knows where you are. The Arrests Law gives you concrete rights in those hours, and most of them work only if you ask for them.

Lesson 06.3 taught the difference between a short detention (עיכוב) and an arrest at a protest, and the three-hour limit on detention. This lesson follows an arrest from the street to the station and up to the door of the courtroom. Interrogation is lesson 07.3, the remand hearing lesson 07.4.

## The law

> (א) אין מעצר ועיכוב אלא בחוק או לפי חוק מכוח הסמכה מפורשת בו.
> (ב) מעצרו ועיכובו של אדם יהיו בדרך שתבטיח שמירה מרבית על כבוד האדם ועל זכויותיו.

*Gist:* arrest and detention exist only where a law explicitly allows them, and must be carried out with maximum respect for dignity and rights. ([חוק סדר הדין הפלילי (סמכויות אכיפה – מעצרים), s. 1](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_סדר_הדין_הפלילי_(סמכויות_אכיפה_–_מעצרים)#סעיף_1))

> (ג) לא ייעצר אדם לפי סעיף זה אם ניתן להסתפק בעיכוב.

*Gist:* an officer may arrest without a warrant only for an arrestable offence and on one of the listed grounds, and not at all if a detention is enough. ([s. 23(ג)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_סדר_הדין_הפלילי_(סמכויות_אכיפה_–_מעצרים)#סעיף_23))

> (א) העוצר אדם יזהה את עצמו תחילה בפני החשוד, בציון שמו או כינויו הרשמי והיותו שוטר או עובד ציבור, יודיע לו מיד שהוא עצור ויבהיר לו את סיבת המעצר בהקדם האפשרי …
> (ג) מילוי החובות האמורות בסעיפים קטנים (א) ו־(ב) הוא תנאי לחוקיות המעצר.

*Gist:* the arresting officer must identify himself, tell you at once that you are under arrest and explain why as soon as possible; these duties are a condition of a lawful arrest. ([s. 24](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_סדר_הדין_הפלילי_(סמכויות_אכיפה_–_מעצרים)#סעיף_24))

> (א) מי שנעצר בידי הקצין הממונה לפי סעיף 27 יובא בהקדם האפשרי, ולא יאוחר מ־24 שעות, בפני שופט.

*Gist:* once the officer in charge decides to hold you, you must be brought before a judge as soon as possible and within 24 hours; special rules apply before Shabbat and holidays. ([s. 29](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_סדר_הדין_הפלילי_(סמכויות_אכיפה_–_מעצרים)#סעיף_29))

> (1) את זכותו שתימסר הודעה על מעצרו, לאדם קרוב לו ולעורך־דין ואת זכותו להיפגש עם עורך־דין, … וכן את זכותו להיות מיוצג על ידי סניגור …
> (2) את משך הזמן שניתן להחזיקו במעצר עד שישוחרר או עד שיובא בפני שופט.

*Gist:* the officer in charge must explain the arrest and its reason in a language you understand, your right to have a relative and a lawyer notified, to meet a lawyer and to a defence counsel, and how long you may be held before release or a judge. ([s. 32](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_סדר_הדין_הפלילי_(סמכויות_אכיפה_–_מעצרים)#סעיף_32))

> (ב) על אף הוראות פרק זה, לא ייערך חיפוש כאמור בסעיף קטן (א), אלא על־פי צו של שופט …

*Gist:* searching the contents of a phone or computer counts as a search and needs a judge's warrant that expressly allows it; the police may seize a device as evidence (s. 32) but a seizure is not permission to search it. ([פקודת סדר הדין הפלילי (מעצר וחיפוש), s. 23א](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/פקודת_סדר_הדין_הפלילי_(מעצר_וחיפוש)#סעיף_23א))

## How it works in practice

### Which situation am I in?

| What the officer says or does | What it is | Limit | Your words |
|---|---|---|---|
| Asks for ID, holds you on the spot | Detention (s. 67(א)) | Reasonable time, at most 3 hours, +3 with reasons when many are involved (s. 73) | "Am I detained? What is the suspicion? Your name and number?" Give your identity details. |
| "Come with me to the station" | Request to accompany (s. 67(ב)); still detention, not arrest | Same | "Am I under arrest or detained?" Go; do not resist. |
| "You are under arrest" | Arrest without a warrant (s. 23) | Officer in charge decides at the station; judge within 24 hours | "Why? I want a lawyer. Please notify [name]." Then stay silent except identity details. |
| Shows a judge's warrant | Arrest with a warrant (s. 18) | You get a copy (s. 24(א)) | Same as above; read the warrant. |

Refusing to give identity details or ignoring a lawful detention order can itself lead to arrest (s. 23(ב)). Give name, ID number and address; everything else waits for a lawyer (lesson 07.3).

### From the street to the station

1. **The arrest.** The officer identifies himself (name or official alias, and the police identification under s. 5א of the Police Ordinance), says you are under arrest and why (s. 24). Note the time, the officer's name or number and the words used.
2. **To the station.** Without delay, unless you need urgent medical care (s. 25). Medical care is free; say if you are hurt.
3. **The officer in charge reviews.** If none of the s. 23 conditions existed, you must be released on the spot unless there is a ground for remand (s. 27(ב)). If there is a ground, the officer may hold you or release you on bail and conditions (s. 27(ג)) — conditions are lesson 07.4.
4. **Before the decision, you may speak (s. 28).** The officer must first warn you that you need not say anything that may incriminate you, that what you say may be used as evidence, and that refusing to answer may strengthen the evidence against you. You may say only: "I want to consult a lawyer before I say anything." If your lawyer is at the station, the officer must hear the lawyer if asked.
5. **Rights explained (s. 32), people notified (s. 33).** A relative you name must be told, without delay, that you are under arrest and where; at your request, a lawyer you name or one from the Bar Association list the officer must give you. You sign that the notice was given. Read before you sign.
6. **Public Defender.** The station must tell you, as soon as possible, that you can ask for a public defender if you are eligible (Public Defender Law s. 19). Ask the police to call the Public Defender's arrest department, which answers around the clock (numbers on the [arrest card](../../templates/arrest-card.md); check them on the Public Defender's page). Eligibility is lesson 07.3.

### Counting the hours

| Situation | Latest time before a judge |
|---|---|
| Ordinary day | As soon as possible, and within 24 hours of the arrest (s. 29(א)) |
| The 24 hours end on Shabbat or a holiday | Before Shabbat or the holiday comes in (s. 29(ב)); with special investigative needs approved by a senior officer, within 4 hours after it ends (s. 29(ג)) |
| Arrested less than 4 hours before Shabbat, or during it | 4 hours after Shabbat ends or 24 hours from the arrest, whichever is later (s. 29(ד)) |
| Urgent investigative act that cannot be done after the hearing | Up to 48 hours from the arrest (s. 30) |
| Not brought in time | You must be released (s. 31(ב)) |

These times are maximums, not targets: "בהקדם האפשרי" (as soon as possible) comes first. All the times are also on [Current Numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md).

### Your phone

The police may seize your phone if they reasonably think it is evidence (Arrest and Search Ordinance s. 32). Searching its contents needs a judge's warrant that says so (s. 23א). You may refuse to unlock it and refuse consent to a search; say "I do not consent to a search of my phone" and say nothing else about it. Turn on a strong passcode before any action and turn off face or fingerprint unlock.

### The support person

Choose one person who is not at the action. They hold your arrest card's back page: your full name and ID number, your lawyer or the Public Defender's number, a relative to inform, your medication, and who looks after children or pets. If you do not come back by an agreed time, they call the Public Defender or your lawyer and the station, and come to the court hearing with documents (lesson 07.4).

## Worked example

**Situation.** During the evacuation of an unauthorised outpost in the Binyamin area, Elisha, 19, sits with others in a doorway. Border Police officers carry them out one by one. Elisha does not resist. An officer says, "You are under arrest for obstructing an officer," and leads him to a vehicle at 15:10 on a Friday. Shabbat begins at 17:40.

1. **Was the arrest explained?** The officer gave a reason (s. 275, obstruction) and said "you are under arrest" (s. 24). Elisha notes that he did not give his name; he asks for it and writes the number on his arm with a pen.
2. **What he says.** "I want a lawyer. Please notify my mother, [name, phone]." He gives his name, ID number and address, and nothing else.
3. **At the station, 16:30.** The officer in charge warns him under s. 28 and asks what happened. Elisha says he wants to consult a lawyer first. The officer explains his rights (s. 32) and notifies his mother (s. 33). Elisha asks for the Public Defender; his support person also calls a right-wing legal-aid hotline his group uses, and a private lawyer arrives.
4. **The clock.** He was arrested less than 4 hours before Shabbat, so s. 29(ד) applies: the later of 24 hours from the arrest (15:10 on Saturday) and 4 hours after Shabbat ends at about 18:40 (about 22:40 on Saturday). The latest time is about 22:40 on Saturday night — unless he is released earlier.
5. **Release.** At 17:00 the officer in charge offers release if Elisha agrees to stay away from the area for 15 days (s. 42). His lawyer explains the option of refusing and going before a judge (lesson 07.4). Elisha agrees and signs; he is released before Shabbat.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- The arrest card template: [arrest-card.md](../../templates/arrest-card.md).
- A trusted person who agrees to be your support person.
- The current Public Defender arrest-department number for your district (gov.il Public Defender page, "מידע לעצור").
- 45 minutes.

### Steps

1. Fill the front of the arrest card: the sentences you will say, in Hebrew, word for word.
2. Fill the back: your full name, ID number, emergency contacts, lawyer or hotline, Public Defender district number, medical needs.
3. Practise the four sentences aloud with a friend playing the officer: "Am I detained or arrested? What is the suspicion? Your name and number? I want a lawyer."
4. Work out the latest time to see a judge for three arrests: Tuesday 10:00; Friday 15:00 (Shabbat at 17:30); Saturday 13:00 (Shabbat ends at 18:45).
5. Brief your support person with the support-person sheet in the template; agree a "not back by" time.
6. Set a strong passcode on your phone and turn off biometric unlock for the day of an action.

### Template

[arrest-card.md](../../templates/arrest-card.md) — a pocket card in Hebrew: the sentences to say, your rights with section numbers, and a support-person sheet.

### How you know it worked

- You can say the four sentences without reading.
- Your three calculations name the section you used.
- Your support person can tell you, without looking, whom they will call first and what documents they will bring.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Not asking "detained or arrested?" | You do not know which limits apply | Ask now; write down the answer and the time | Make it the first sentence on your card |
| Refusing to give identity details | A new ground to arrest you (s. 23(ב)) | Give name, ID number and address | Separate identity details from everything else |
| Signing the rights form without reading | You confirm notices that were not given | Write "not read" or ask to read before signing | Read every form; ask for a translation if needed (s. 32) |
| Nobody outside knows you were arrested | No lawyer, no documents for the hearing | Ask the officer in charge to notify a relative (s. 33) | A support person with a "not back by" time |
| Unlocking the phone "to show you have nothing to hide" | A full search without a warrant | Ask for a lawyer before anything more | Strong passcode; "I do not consent to a search" |
| Assuming 24 hours means you will be held 24 hours | Missed chance of release at the station | Ask the officer in charge about release on conditions | Know s. 27: release is the default when no ground exists |

## Review

- Detention is short and limited (3 hours, +3); arrest requires an arrestable offence, a s. 23 ground, and is not allowed if a detention is enough.
- The arresting officer must identify himself and say you are arrested and why; this is a condition of a lawful arrest (s. 24).
- The officer in charge must warn you, explain your rights and notify a relative and, at your request, a lawyer (ss. 28, 32, 33).
- You must see a judge as soon as possible and within 24 hours, with special Shabbat and holiday rules, or be released (ss. 29–31).
- A seized phone may not be searched without a judge's warrant; do not consent.
