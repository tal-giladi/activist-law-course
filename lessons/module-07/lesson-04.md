# 07.4 · Remand Hearings and Release Conditions

By the end of this lesson you will be able to follow a remand hearing in the Magistrates' Court, understand what the police must show to keep you in custody, tell the conditions an officer may set at the station from those a judge may impose, and prepare with your support person a realistic proposal for release on lighter conditions.

> [!WARNING]
> At a remand hearing you should be represented by a lawyer or a public defender (lesson 07.3); if none is present, ask the judge for one before the hearing starts. This lesson explains the rules so that you and your support person can help your lawyer; it is not legal advice.

## Why it matters

For many activists the remand hearing is the only time their case reaches a judge. The police may ask to keep you in custody for days "for investigation", or, more often in protest files, release you on conditions: stay away from a city, from a site or from other activists; report to a police station; a cash deposit. Those conditions can last weeks and stop you from protesting, working or studying. Breaking them is an offence (s. 287) and a ground for re-arrest.

The law requires the least harmful measure that achieves the purpose. Much of what happens at the hearing is about that question, and good preparation by your support person — an address, a supervisor, a deposit, a work letter — often decides the answer.

## The law

> (א) שופט לא יצווה על מעצרו של אדם, אלא אם כן שוכנע כי קיים חשד סביר שהאדם עבר עבירה, שאיננה חטא, ומתקיימת אחת מעילות אלה; …
> (ב) שופט לא יצווה על מעצר לפי סעיף קטן (א), אם ניתן להשיג את מטרת המעצר בדרך של קביעת ערובה ותנאי ערובה, שפגיעתם בחירותו של החשוד פחותה.

*Gist:* a judge may remand a suspect only on reasonable suspicion of an offence that is not a contravention plus a ground (risk of obstructing the investigation, danger, or investigative acts that need custody), and not at all if bail and conditions would achieve the purpose with less harm. ([חוק סדר הדין הפלילי (סמכויות אכיפה – מעצרים), s. 13](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_סדר_הדין_הפלילי_(סמכויות_אכיפה_–_מעצרים)#סעיף_13))

> (א) ציווה שופט על מעצרו של חשוד בנוכחותו, לא תעלה תקופת המעצר על 15 ימים; …
> (ב) לא יוחזק חשוד במעצר ברצף אחד בקשר לאותו אירוע, לרבות המעצר ללא צו, לתקופה העולה על 30 ימים, אלא אם כן הבקשה למעצר נוסף הוגשה באישור היועץ המשפטי לממשלה.

*Gist:* one remand order is at most 15 days; continuous custody for one event beyond 30 days needs the Attorney General's approval; the "investigative acts" ground alone is limited to 5 days, 15 in total (s. 13(א)(3)). ([s. 17](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_סדר_הדין_הפלילי_(סמכויות_אכיפה_–_מעצרים)#סעיף_17))

> … הקצין הממונה רשאי, בהסכמת החשוד, להתנות את השחרור בערובה בתנאים הבאים: …
> (3) איסור כניסה לאזור, לישוב או למקום בארץ, שיקבע, לתקופה שלא תעלה על 15 ימים;
> (4) איסור לקיים קשר או להיפגש עם מי שיקבע, לתקופה שלא תעלה על 30 ימים; …
> (7) איסור יציאה ממקום המגורים במשך כל שעות היממה או חלק ממנה, לתקופה שלא תעלה על 5 ימים.

*Gist:* the officer in charge may set conditions only with your consent and within fixed limits: exclusion from a place up to 15 days, a contact ban up to 30 days, house arrest up to 5 days, a travel ban with passport deposit up to 3 months. ([s. 42(ב)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_סדר_הדין_הפלילי_(סמכויות_אכיפה_–_מעצרים)#סעיף_42))

> (ד) החליט הקצין הממונה לשחרר את החשוד בערובה ולא הסכים החשוד להטלת הערובה, לגובהה או לתנאיה …, ייעצר החשוד ויובא בהקדם האפשרי ולא יאוחר מ־24 שעות, בפני שופט.

*Gist:* if you do not agree to the officer's bail or conditions, you stay under arrest and must be brought before a judge within 24 hours. ([s. 42(ד)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_סדר_הדין_הפלילי_(סמכויות_אכיפה_–_מעצרים)#סעיף_42))

> מי ששוחרר בערובה על ידי הקצין הממונה רשאי לערור בפני בית משפט שלום –
> (1) על גובה הערובה או על סבירות תנאיה, תוך 14 ימים מיום החלטת הקצין המונה; …

*Gist:* conditions you accepted at the station can be challenged in the Magistrates' Court within 14 days of the officer's decision, or later if circumstances change. ([s. 43](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_סדר_הדין_הפלילי_(סמכויות_אכיפה_–_מעצרים)#סעיף_43))

> (ג) ערר כאמור בסעיף קטן (א) … יוגשו בתוך 30 ימים מיום שניתנה החלטת בית המשפט, …

*Gist:* a court's custody or bail decision is appealed to the higher court within 30 days (s. 53); the same court can review it any time new facts arise, circumstances change or considerable time passes (s. 52). ([s. 53](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_סדר_הדין_הפלילי_(סמכויות_אכיפה_–_מעצרים)#סעיף_53))

## How it works in practice

### The hearing, step by step

1. **The request.** A written police request supported by an officer's declaration: the facts giving jurisdiction, a summary of the suspicion, the ground, and previous requests (s. 15(א)). Your lawyer receives it; confidential material goes to the judge only.
2. **Public, in your presence.** The hearing is public unless the judge orders otherwise, and you are present (s. 16). Hearings may be held by video link in some situations; your lawyer can object where the law allows.
3. **Questioning the officer.** Your lawyer (or you, if unrepresented) may question the police representative about the request (s. 15(ד)). Typical questions: what has been done since the arrest, which investigative acts remain, why they need you in custody.
4. **The judge decides** on three questions: is there a reasonable suspicion; is there a ground; can a less harmful alternative achieve the purpose (s. 13(ב))? The result is custody for some days, release on conditions, or release without conditions.
5. **Conditions** (s. 48): the court may add conditions "for a period it sets", such as a ban on entering an area, a contact ban, house arrest, reporting to a station, a supervisor, a deposit or guarantee. It sets them no more than needed (s. 46(א)), considering your means and your ability to keep them (s. 46(ב)). A travel ban needs a real risk you will not appear (s. 48(ב)).

### Officer's conditions vs court conditions

| | Officer in charge (s. 42) | Court (s. 48) |
|---|---|---|
| Needs your consent? | Yes, for the listed conditions | No |
| Exclusion from a place | Up to 15 days | For a period the court sets |
| Contact ban | Up to 30 days | For a period the court sets |
| House arrest | Up to 5 days | For a period the court sets |
| If you refuse | You go before a judge within 24 hours (s. 42(ד)) | — |
| How to challenge | ערר to the Magistrates' Court within 14 days (s. 43) | Review (s. 52) or appeal within 30 days (s. 53) |

Accepting the officer's conditions usually means going home today. Refusing means another night or day in custody and a judge who may impose heavier conditions — or lighter ones. Decide with your lawyer.

### What your support person brings

- An address where you can live if the court wants you away from the protest area.
- A possible supervisor (מפקח): an adult without a criminal record who will attend court and undertake to supervise.
- Proof of work or studies, to ask for exceptions (for example, "except travel to work at…").
- How much money can be deposited (ערבון) and who will sign a guarantee (ערבות).
- Your medication and any medical letter.

### Asking for proportional conditions

A good alternative is specific: "exclusion from Kaplan Street between Begin and Ibn Gvirol for 7 days, except travel to work", not "please release her". Courts are more willing to accept narrow conditions, short periods and exceptions when the support person has the details ready.

### After release

Keep a copy of the decision. Write the end dates of each condition in your calendar. If a condition stops you from something lawful and important (a funeral, an exam, a court date), ask the court for a change in advance (s. 52). Breaking a condition is an offence (Penal Law s. 287) and can lead to re-arrest and forfeiture of the deposit (s. 51).

## Worked example

**Situation.** Rami Haddad, a 28-year-old engineer from Shefa-'Amr, is arrested at a protest in Haifa against home demolitions in Arab towns. The police suspect him of obstructing officers (s. 275) after he stood between an officer and a protester being arrested. At the station, the officer in charge offers release if he agrees to stay out of Haifa for 15 days and not to contact four named organisers for 30 days.

1. **The choice.** Rami works in Haifa. His lawyer explains: accepting means release now, then a ערר within 14 days (s. 43); refusing means a judge within 24 hours (s. 42(ד)). Rami refuses because he cannot work under the condition.
2. **The request.** The next morning the police ask the Magistrates' Court for release on conditions: exclusion from Haifa for 30 days and the contact ban.
3. **Questioning.** The lawyer asks the officer whether any investigative act needs Rami in custody. None. Whether the four organisers are suspects or witnesses. Two are witnesses.
4. **The alternative.** Rami's sister, his support person, has a letter from his employer and offers to be a supervisor and to deposit a modest sum. The lawyer proposes: no entry to the protest site and its surrounding streets for 10 days, no contact with the two witnesses about the event, a deposit.
5. **Decision.** The judge finds a reasonable suspicion but no ground for custody, and adopts most of the proposal: exclusion from the protest area (not the whole city) for 10 days, no contact with the two witnesses about the event, a deposit and a personal guarantee.
6. **After.** Rami writes the end date in his calendar, avoids the area, and tells the other organisers through his lawyer why he cannot speak with two of them.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- The support-person sheet in [arrest-card.md](../../templates/arrest-card.md).
- The fictional remand request below.
- 45 minutes.

Fictional remand request (summary): "The suspect, a resident of Jerusalem, is suspected of breach of a lawful order (s. 287) and improper conduct (s. 216) after remaining on Route 1 following a dispersal order. The police ask for release on conditions: exclusion from Jerusalem for 30 days, house arrest from 20:00 to 06:00 for 30 days, a ban on contact with members of the 'X' WhatsApp group, a deposit of ₪5,000."

### Steps

1. Identify the suspicion, the sections and their class (lesson 07.1). Is there an arrestable offence?
2. Identify the ground the police rely on. Is custody requested at all, or only conditions?
3. For each condition, write: what purpose it serves, whether it is narrower than needed, and a less harmful alternative.
4. Note which conditions an officer could have set with consent, and their limits under s. 42.
5. Fill in the support-person sheet for yourself: address, supervisor, work or study proof, deposit amount.
6. Write the two dates that matter after a decision: 14 days for a ערר on an officer's conditions, 30 days for an appeal on a court's decision.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Signing the officer's conditions without reading them | Weeks of restrictions you could not keep | ערר to the Magistrates' Court within 14 days (s. 43) | Read; ask your lawyer; you may refuse |
| No support person at court | No address, supervisor or deposit to offer; heavier conditions | Ask for a short adjournment to bring them | Brief the support person before any action |
| Asking only "release her" | The court adopts the police conditions | Propose narrow conditions with exceptions | Prepare a concrete alternative |
| Missing the 30-day appeal | The decision stands | Ask for review if facts changed (s. 52) or an extension (s. 53(ג)) | Write the date the day of the decision |
| Breaking a condition "just once" | Re-arrest, forfeited deposit, s. 287 suspicion | Tell your lawyer at once | Ask the court for a change in advance |
| Contacting a co-suspect named in a contact ban | Breach of conditions | Stop; communicate only through lawyers | Write the names on your copy of the decision |

## Review

- A judge may remand only on reasonable suspicion of an arrestable offence plus a ground, and not if a less harmful alternative works (s. 13).
- Remand orders are at most 15 days each and 30 in a row for one event without the Attorney General.
- Officer's conditions need your consent and have fixed limits; refusing brings you before a judge within 24 hours.
- Courts set conditions as needed and proportionate; a prepared support person and a concrete alternative change outcomes.
- Challenge an officer's conditions within 14 days (ערר), a court's decision by review or by appeal within 30 days; never break a condition.
