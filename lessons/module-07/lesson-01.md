# 07.1 · Offences Activists Are Most Often Charged With

By the end of this lesson you will know the handful of Penal Law sections that police and prosecutors reach for after protests and direct actions, how each one is built, which class of offence it is and why that class matters, and you will write a personal charge map for an action you plan.

> [!WARNING]
> In a real criminal case, get a lawyer or the Public Defender. This module teaches your rights so you can protect yourself and recognise when you need help; it does not teach you to defend yourself at trial. Nothing here is legal advice, and nothing here is advice on how to break the law safely.

## Why it matters

Most activists who end up in a criminal file did not plan to commit an offence. They sat on a road, refused to move, shouted at an officer, pulled an arm away during an arrest or sprayed a slogan on a wall. Each of these has a section number, and the section decides almost everything that follows: whether the police may arrest you without a warrant, whether a remand request is realistic, and how long a closed file stays on your police record (lesson 07.5).

Knowing the sections also helps you in the moment. "What is the suspicion?" is a question you are entitled to ask (lesson 07.2), and the answer means much more when you know what "s. 275" is. Lesson 06.3 covered what police may do at a protest and how dispersal works; this lesson is about what you may be accused of afterwards.

## Key terms

| עברית | Transliteration | English |
|---|---|---|
| פשע | pesha | Felony: maximum penalty above three years' imprisonment |
| עוון | avon | Misdemeanour: maximum above three months and up to three years |
| חטא | chet | Contravention: maximum up to three months (or a small fine) |
| עבירה בת מעצר | avera bat ma'atzar | Arrestable offence: any offence that is not a חטא |
| התקהלות אסורה | hitkahalut asura | Unlawful assembly (Penal Law s. 151) |
| הפרעה לשוטר | hafra'a leshoter | Obstructing a police officer (s. 275) |
| הפרת הוראה חוקית | hafarat hora'a chukit | Breach of a lawful order (s. 287) |
| התנהגות פסולה במקום ציבורי | hitnahagut psula bemakom tziburi | Improper conduct in a public place (s. 216) |
| עבירת קנס | avirat knas | Fine offence: dealt with by a fine notice instead of an indictment |

## The law

> "פשע" – עבירה שנקבע לה עונש חמור ממאסר לתקופה של שלוש שנים;
> "עוון" – עבירה שנקבע לה עונש מאסר לתקופה העולה על שלושה חודשים ושאינה עולה על שלוש שנים; …
> "חטא" – עבירה שנקבע לה עונש מאסר לתקופה שאינה עולה על שלושה חודשים, …

*Gist:* the maximum penalty in the section decides the class: above three years is a felony, above three months up to three years a misdemeanour, up to three months a contravention. ([חוק העונשין, s. 24](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_העונשין#סעיף_24))

> שלושה אנשים לפחות שנתקהלו לשם עבירה, או שנתקהלו למטרה משותפת, ואפילו כשרה, ומתנהגים באופן הנותן לאנשים שבסביבה יסוד סביר לחשוש שהמתקהלים יעשו מעשה שיפר את השלום, … הרי זו התקהלות אסורה, והמשתתף בהתקהלות אסורה, דינו – מאסר שנה אחת.

*Gist:* three or more people, even with a lawful aim, who behave so that bystanders reasonably fear a breach of the peace are an unlawful assembly; participation carries up to one year. ([s. 151](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_העונשין#סעיף_151))

> העושה מעשה בכוונה להפריע לשוטר כשהוא ממלא תפקידו כחוק או להכשילו בכך, … דינו – מאסר עד שלוש שנים ולא פחות משבועיים ימים.

*Gist:* an act done with intent to obstruct an officer who is acting lawfully; up to three years, with a statutory minimum of two weeks. ([s. 275](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_העונשין#סעיף_275))

> המפר הוראה שניתנה כשורה מאת בית משפט או מאת פקיד או אדם הפועל בתפקיד רשמי ומוסמך לאותו ענין, דינו – מאסר שנתיים.

*Gist:* breaking an order properly given by a court or an authorised official, which includes breaking release conditions (lesson 07.4); up to two years. ([s. 287(א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_העונשין#סעיף_287))

> (ז) בחוק זה, "עבירה בת מעצר" – כל עבירה למעט חטא.

*Gist:* a police officer may arrest without a warrant only for an offence that is not a contravention (and only if one of the s. 23 grounds also exists). ([חוק סדר הדין הפלילי (סמכויות אכיפה – מעצרים), s. 23](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_סדר_הדין_הפלילי_(סמכויות_אכיפה_–_מעצרים)#סעיף_23))

## How it works in practice

### The sections you will meet most

The table lists the sections that come up after protests and direct actions. Maximum penalties are from the current Wikisource text (checked 13 October 2026). A maximum is a ceiling, not a likely sentence.

| Section | Offence | Key element the prosecution must prove | Max | Class |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 151 | Unlawful assembly (התקהלות אסורה) | 3+ people; conduct giving bystanders reasonable fear of a breach of the peace | 1 year | עוון |
| 152 | Riot (התפרעות) | An unlawful assembly that started a breach of the peace that frightens the public | 2 years | עוון |
| 155 | Continuing after a dispersal order | A dispersal order under s. 153 and a reasonable time to leave | 5 years | פשע |
| 194 | Noise or insult in a public place | Noise or insult likely to cause a breach of the peace, without reasonable cause | 3 months | חטא |
| 196 | Defacing property (graffiti, posters) | Writing or sticking on another's real property unlawfully | 1 year | עוון, but also a fine offence |
| 215 | Public nuisance (מטרד לציבור) | An unlawful act that causes the public harm, danger or inconvenience in using a public right | 1 year | עוון |
| 216 | Improper conduct in a public place | Disorderly conduct, or conduct likely to cause a breach of the peace | 6 months | עוון |
| 273 | Assaulting a police officer | Any assault (even a push) linked to the officer's lawful duty | 3 years, minimum 1 month | עוון |
| 275 | Obstructing an officer | An act with intent to obstruct an officer acting lawfully | 3 years, minimum 2 weeks | עוון |
| 275א | Aggravated obstruction | Throwing an object at an officer or police vehicle to obstruct | 5 years | פשע |
| 287 | Breach of a lawful order | A properly given order by a court or authorised official | 2 years | עוון |
| 288 | Insulting a public servant | Insult by gesture, word or act, linked to the servant's duty | 6 months | עוון |
| 332 | Endangering travellers on a traffic route | Intent to harm or endanger a traveller, plus one of the listed acts | 20 years | פשע |
| 144ד2 | Incitement to violence | A publication calling for or praising violence, with a real possibility it leads to violence | 5 years | פשע |

Speech offences (144ב, 144ד2) are taught in lesson 06.5. Courts have read some of these sections, s. 288 in particular, more narrowly than their words; ask your lawyer about the case law before you assume a section fits.

### Three habits that change the picture

1. **Check the class.** A חטא (for example s. 194) is not an arrestable offence (s. 23(א)(7)): the officer may detain you to identify you (lesson 06.3), but may not arrest you for it alone.
2. **Look for "כחוק" and "כשורה".** Sections 273, 275 and 287 protect officers acting *lawfully* and orders given *properly*. Whether the dispersal order or the instruction was lawful is a defence question for your lawyer; your job on the day is not to resist, and to remember and record what was said (lesson 06.3).
3. **Look for intent.** Section 275 needs intent to obstruct; s. 332 needs intent to harm or endanger a traveller. Sitting on a road to make a political point is not the same act as placing an object to endanger drivers. Without that intent, the same conduct is usually examined under lighter sections such as 215 or 216. Which section applies is a prosecution decision and a defence argument, not something you settle with the officer.

### Physical contact is where files escalate

The offences that carry statutory minimum penalties (ss. 273 and 275) are about the moment of arrest, not the protest itself. Pulling away, linking arms to stop an arrest or holding on to a friend being taken can turn a misdemeanour file into an "assault on an officer" file. Decide in advance that you will not resist physically, even an arrest you think is unlawful (lesson 06.3).

## Worked example

**Situation.** During an anti-government protest in Tel Aviv, about 300 people walk onto the Ayalon highway after the police announce through a loudspeaker that the gathering must clear the road. Noa, a 34-year-old teacher, sits down on the road with others. Officers lift her to the shoulder. She stands, walks back towards the road, and an officer grabs her arm; she pulls it away once. She is arrested.

1. **What she did, in steps:** joined a gathering on a road; stayed after a dispersal announcement; sat down; returned; pulled her arm away.
2. **Possible sections:** s. 151 (unlawful assembly, עוון), s. 155 if a valid s. 153 dispersal order was given and a reasonable time passed (פשע), s. 215 or 216 for blocking traffic (עוון), and s. 275 for pulling her arm away (עוון with a minimum penalty). Section 332 needs intent to endanger travellers; nothing in the facts shows that.
3. **What each depends on.** Was the dispersal order given by an authorised officer and announced as s. 153 requires? Was there a reasonable time to leave? Did pulling her arm show intent to obstruct, or a reflex? These are questions for the lawyer, from the police video and witnesses.
4. **What changes the outcome most.** The arm. Without it, Noa's file is a typical protest file; with it, there is a section with a minimum penalty. The lesson for next time is in her charge map: "if grabbed, go limp or walk, never pull".

## Do it yourself

### You need

- A real action you plan or one you took part in (any cause), or the fictional case below.
- The Penal Law on Wikisource: <https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_העונשין>.
- 40 minutes.

Fictional case if you have none: residents opposed to a new quarry stand in front of trucks at the quarry gate. Police order them to step aside. One resident chains himself to the gate; another sprays "STOP THE QUARRY" on the company's sign; a third shouts at an officer and calls him a thug.

### Steps

1. Write the action as a numbered list of physical acts, as a police report would (who, where, what, after which order).
2. For each act, find the most likely section in the table above. Open the section on Wikisource and copy the Hebrew sentence that defines it.
3. Write the class (פשע / עוון / חטא) from the maximum penalty, and whether it is an arrestable offence.
4. Underline the element the prosecution must prove (intent, a lawful order, a lawful duty, three or more people).
5. Write your charge map: one line per act — the section, the class, and the one thing you will do or avoid so that you are not accused of it.
6. Read one section closely with the statute reading sheet.

### Template

[statute-reading-sheet.md](../../templates/statute-reading-sheet.md) — use it for the one section on your map that worries you most.

### How you know it worked

- Every act on your list has a section number and a class, and you can point to the Hebrew words that make it an offence.
- You found at least one act that is not an offence at all, or only a חטא.
- Your map ends with a short "I will not…" list that a friend could check on the day.

### Self-check

- [ ] I can say why s. 194 alone does not allow an arrest without a warrant.
- [ ] I know which sections need a lawful order or a lawful duty, and which need intent.
- [ ] I know that pulling away during an arrest can add s. 273 or s. 275.
- [ ] My charge map has a section, a class and a line of conduct for each act.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Treating "unlawful assembly" as "a protest without a licence" | You misjudge your risk; licensing is a separate question (lesson 06.2) | Read s. 151: it is about conduct that causes a reasonable fear of a breach of the peace | Learn the elements, not the label |
| Pulling away or holding a friend during an arrest | A s. 273 or s. 275 suspicion with a minimum penalty | Stop at once; say clearly "I am not resisting" | Decide in advance never to resist physically |
| Arguing with the officer about whether the order was lawful | Can add s. 275 or s. 288; the argument is lost anyway | Comply, note the time, the officer and the words | Leave lawfulness to your lawyer and to later complaints (lesson 06.4) |
| Assuming any road block is s. 332 | Panic, or a bad plea decision | Ask your lawyer about the intent element | Know that s. 332 needs intent to harm or endanger |
| Writing on a wall because "it is only a fine" | Section 196 can still be prosecuted; damage to property adds other suspicions | Ask your lawyer whether a fine notice was issued | Use your own property, signs you carry, or permitted spaces |

## Review

- The maximum penalty sets the class: פשע above three years, עוון above three months, חטא up to three months; a חטא alone is not an arrestable offence.
- The common protest sections are 151, 152, 155, 194, 196, 215, 216, 273, 275, 275א, 287, 288 and, rarely, 332.
- Sections 273, 275 and 287 depend on a lawful duty or a properly given order; s. 275 and s. 332 need intent.
- Most escalation happens at the moment of arrest: never resist physically.
- A charge map, written before the action, turns this list into decisions you can keep under pressure.
