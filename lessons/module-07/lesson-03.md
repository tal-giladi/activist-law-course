# 07.3 · Interrogation: Silence, a Lawyer and the Public Defender

By the end of this lesson you will know what an interrogation under warning is, what your right to silence protects and what it costs, when you are entitled to consult a lawyer before you are questioned and when the police may delay it, how to get a public defender as a detainee, and you will practise an interrogation with a partner.

> [!WARNING]
> In a criminal case, get a lawyer or the Public Defender before you answer questions about the facts. Whether to stay silent, answer or give a short version is a decision to make with a lawyer who knows your file; this lesson explains the rules and the trade-offs, not what you should say. This is not legal advice.

## Why it matters

What you say in the interrogation room is often the strongest evidence in a protest file — stronger than the police video. People talk because they want to explain, because they think they did nothing wrong, or because they are told it will get them released sooner. The law gives you two tools against that pressure: the privilege against self-incrimination and the right to consult a lawyer first. Both are strongest when you use them early.

The opposite mistake also exists. Silence is a right, but the warning itself says that silence may strengthen the evidence against you, and a version you first give at trial weighs less than one given early. That is why the first step is always the same: consult, then decide.

## The law

> (2) אדם, הנחקר כך, יהיה חייב להשיב נכונה על כל השאלות, שיציג לו בשעת החקירה אותו פקיד משטרה, …, חוץ משאלות שהתשובות עליהן יהיה בהן כדי להעמידו בסכנת האשמה פלילית.

*Gist:* a person questioned by the police must answer truthfully, except questions whose answers could expose him to a criminal charge. ([פקודת הפרוצידורה הפלילית (עדות), s. 2(2)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/פקודת_הפרוצידורה_הפלילית_(עדות)#סעיף_2))

> (א) אין אדם חייב למסור ראיה אם יש בה הודיה בעובדה שהיא יסוד מיסודותיה של עבירה שהוא מואשם בה או עשוי להיות מואשם בה.

*Gist:* no one must give evidence that admits a fact which is an element of an offence he is or may be charged with. ([פקודת הראיות, s. 47(א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/פקודת_הראיות#סעיף_47))

> … לאחר שהזהירו כי אינו חייב לומר דבר העלול להפלילו, כי כל דבר שיאמר עשוי לשמש ראיה נגדו וכן כי הימנעותו מלהשיב על שאלות עשויה לחזק את הראיות נגדו.

*Gist:* the warning has three parts: you need not incriminate yourself; what you say may be used against you; refusing to answer may strengthen the evidence against you. ([חוק סדר הדין הפלילי (סמכויות אכיפה – מעצרים), s. 28(א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_סדר_הדין_הפלילי_(סמכויות_אכיפה_–_מעצרים)#סעיף_28))

> (א) עצור זכאי להיפגש עם עורך דין ולהיוועץ בו.
> (ב) ביקש עצור להיפגש עם עורך דין …, יאפשר זאת האחראי על החקירה, ללא דיחוי.

*Gist:* an arrested person may meet and consult a lawyer, and the investigator must allow it without delay; the meeting is private (s. 34(ג)). ([s. 34](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_סדר_הדין_הפלילי_(סמכויות_אכיפה_–_מעצרים)#סעיף_34))

> (7) עצור שהוא מחוסר אמצעים, לפי אמות מידה שקבע שר המשפטים …

*Gist:* an arrested person without means is entitled to a public defender; minors and people facing a request for detention until the end of proceedings have their own grounds (s. 18(א)(3), (8)). ([חוק הסניגוריה הציבורית, s. 18(א)(7)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הסניגוריה_הציבורית#סעיף_18))

> חקירת חשוד תתנהל בשפתו או בשפה שהחשוד מבין ודובר אותה, לרבות שפת סימנים.

*Gist:* a suspect is questioned in his own language or one he understands and speaks, including sign language. ([חוק סדר הדין הפלילי (חקירת חשודים), s. 2](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_סדר_הדין_הפלילי_(חקירת_חשודים)#סעיף_2))

## How it works in practice

### Suspect or witness?

Ask at the start: "Am I being questioned as a suspect, under warning?" A witness must answer truthfully, except where an answer could incriminate (Testimony Ordinance s. 2(2)). A suspect is warned under s. 28 and may decline to answer. If a "witness" conversation turns to questions about what you did, ask again; you may say "I want to consult a lawyer before I answer that."

### Consult first

| Situation | Your right | What can delay it |
|---|---|---|
| Arrested, ordinary offence | Meet a lawyer without delay, in private (s. 34(ב)–(ג)) | An act already under way: a written reasoned delay by an officer (רב פקד or above) for a few hours (s. 34(ד)); to protect the arrest of others or evidence: up to 24 hours from the arrest (s. 34(ה)); to save life or prevent a crime: up to 48 hours (s. 34(ו)) |
| Arrested, security offence (defined in s. 35(ב)) | Meet a lawyer as soon as possible | Delays of up to 10 days by the officer, and longer with a court's approval (s. 35); wartime emergency regulations extend these further for war-related security suspects |
| Summoned, not arrested | You may come with a lawyer or consult before you go | Not eligible for the Public Defender as a detainee; find a lawyer before the date |
| Detained briefly (עיכוב), not arrested | You may say you want to consult a lawyer before answering | The Public Defender represents arrested people, not those only detained (ACRI, 2020) |

Even when a meeting is delayed under s. 34(ו), you keep the right to a reasonable chance to meet a lawyer before you are brought to court about your arrest. The Public Defender's guidance says the police should wait for the lawyer for a reasonable time, given the hour and the station's location, before starting the interrogation.

### Getting a public defender as a detainee

1. **Ask.** "I want to consult a public defender." The officer must tell you this option exists (Public Defender Law s. 19) and pass the request on as soon as possible (Public Defender Regulations reg. 1(ב)).
2. **Who is eligible.** An arrested person without means (s. 18(א)(7)): the test applies the order for accused persons — household income up to about two-thirds of the average wage for up to three people, plus a little for each extra person, and no assets that can be realised above a ceiling. The five-year-offence condition that limits accused persons does not apply to detainees. A minor who is arrested is represented regardless of means. The current figures are in the Public Defender's 2026 eligibility table; see [Current Numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md).
3. **If you are unsure you qualify, ask anyway.** ACRI reports that the first consultation at the station is not charged even if you are later found not eligible.
4. **At court.** If no request was passed on, ask the Public Defender's representative at the court (reg. 1(ג)).
5. **Refused?** The district public defender's refusal is in writing; you may appeal (ערר) to the National Public Defender within 7 days (reg. 2(ב)). Some represented people pay a fee under the 2011 regulations; ask.

### In the room

- **Identity details** — give them.
- **The facts** — after you have consulted. If the advice is to stay silent, say a fixed sentence: "על פי ייעוץ משפטי, אני שומר/ת על זכות השתיקה" and repeat it, politely, to every question, including "small talk" about the event.
- **Things you must say** — that you are tired, unwell, hurt, need medicine, or do not understand the language. The Public Defender's guidance lists these as things to tell the investigator; an interpreter is free.
- **Recording** — the investigation is documented in writing; interrogations about offences carrying ten years or more are video recorded (Interrogation of Suspects Law s. 7 and Schedule), with exceptions for security offences (s. 17). Ask whether you are being recorded.
- **The statement (הודעה)** — it is read to you and you are asked to sign (Testimony Ordinance s. 3). Read it yourself. Ask to correct anything wrong. You may refuse to sign; the officer notes the refusal and why. Never sign a statement you did not read.
- **No conversations "off the record"** — with investigators, cellmates or by the station phone. Assume everything is heard.

## Worked example

**Situation.** Yael, an environmental activist, is arrested after a group chains itself to the gate of the Ministry of Energy in Jerusalem to protest a gas-export licence. She is suspected of obstructing officers (s. 275) and of improper conduct (s. 216). At 11:00 the investigator says: "Just tell us your side, then you can go home."

1. **Status.** She asks: "Am I under warning?" Yes. The investigator reads the s. 28 warning.
2. **Consult.** "I want to consult a lawyer before I answer. I have no lawyer; I ask for the Public Defender." The investigator says the Public Defender is busy. Yael repeats the request and asks that it be written in the record (s. 34(ב); Public Defender Law s. 19).
3. **Eligibility.** Yael is a student on a part-time salary living alone. She is likely to be within the means test; the defender on duty will check. A public defender arrives at 12:30 and consults with her in private.
4. **The interrogation.** On advice, Yael answers identity questions, says she was at the gate, and declines to answer the rest. She repeats one sentence to each question.
5. **The statement.** The written statement says "the suspect refused to cooperate". She asks to correct it to "the suspect answered identity questions and, on legal advice, exercised her right to silence on the rest", and signs only after the correction.
6. **What she did not do.** She did not argue about the licence, did not name other activists, and did not discuss the event with the other detainees in the cell.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- A partner to play the investigator (any friend; give them the script below).
- Your arrest card from lesson 07.2: [arrest-card.md](../../templates/arrest-card.md).
- A timer and 40 minutes.

Investigator's script (give it to your partner): start friendly, ask identity questions, then "just tell me what happened", then "your friend already told us everything", then "if you don't talk, you'll stay overnight", then hand over a written statement with one wrong sentence and ask for a signature.

### Steps

1. Five minutes: read the arrest card's front side and the "Consult first" table.
2. Ten minutes: run the interrogation with your partner. Use only the sentences on your card.
3. Note each moment you said more than you planned.
4. Ten minutes: swap roles.
5. Write a three-line personal rule for the room (for example: identity yes; facts after consulting; nothing signed unread).
6. Check your Public Defender eligibility roughly against the 2026 table on the Public Defender's gov.il page, so you know in advance whether to expect a public defender or to arrange a private lawyer.

[arrest-card.md](../../templates/arrest-card.md) — the front side has the consulting and silence sentences.

### How you know it worked

- You asked for a lawyer before any question about the facts, and repeated it under pressure.
- You found and corrected the wrong sentence in the statement before signing — or refused to sign.
- You know whether you are likely to qualify for a public defender as a detainee.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| "Just explaining" before consulting | Your words become the main evidence | Stop; ask for a lawyer now; say nothing more about the facts | Consult first, every time |
| Accepting "the Public Defender won't come" | Interrogation without advice | Repeat the request; ask that it be recorded | Know s. 34 and Public Defender Law s. 19 |
| Talking to cellmates about the event | Statements reported to the police | Stop; say nothing more | Assume every conversation is heard |
| Signing a statement unread | A wrong version under your signature | Tell your lawyer at once; ask to give a correcting statement | Read, correct, or refuse to sign |
| Hiding that you are ill or exhausted | A statement given in a bad state; health risk | Tell the investigator and ask for a doctor | Say it at the start; carry medication |
| Assuming silence is free | Silence may strengthen the evidence; a late version weighs less | Decide with your lawyer when and what to say | Treat silence as a decision, not a reflex |

## Review

- A suspect is questioned under warning: you need not incriminate yourself, what you say can be used, silence may strengthen the evidence.
- An arrested person may consult a lawyer without delay and in private; the police may delay it only for listed reasons and limits (s. 34), longer for security offences (s. 35).
- A detainee without means, and every arrested minor, is entitled to a public defender; ask at the station or at court, and appeal a refusal within 7 days.
- Give identity details, consult before facts, tell the investigator if you are unwell, and never sign a statement you did not read.
