# 19.6 · A Real International Submission

In this lesson you make one real submission to a United Nations human-rights mechanism: either a Special Procedures communication about a specific case with the consent of the people concerned, a response to a mandate-holder's call for input, or a contribution to a shadow report or UPR submission that a coalition of organisations sends. By the end you will have checked a live UN deadline, written to the UN's own criteria, protected the people in your submission, and recorded the result.

## Why it matters

Module 14 taught the UN system on paper and in a dry run. Real submissions are different in three ways. The deadline is real and does not move for you. The people in your submission are real, and a careless detail can expose them to reprisals. And you rarely act alone: most effective UN advocacy from Israel — from any part of the political map — is done by coalitions that combine the data and the voices of several groups.

A single, accurate submission can put your issue into a committee's list of issues, a rapporteur's letter to the government, or a recommendation Israel must answer. Those texts then become arguments at home: in a Knesset paper, a letter to a ministry or a petition (lesson 13.5).

## The law

> (e) The communication should not be exclusively based on reports disseminated by mass media.

*Gist:* a Special Procedures communication must be based on first-hand or reliable direct information, not only on news reports; art. 9 also requires that it is not manifestly unfounded or politically motivated, describes the facts and the rights concerned, uses non-abusive language and comes from a person or group with direct or reliable knowledge. ([HRC resolution 5/2, Code of Conduct, art. 9](https://ap.ohchr.org/documents/E/HRC/resolutions/A_HRC_RES_5_2.doc))

> Written submissions should not exceed 2815 words in the case of individual submissions, to which additional documentation can be annexed for reference. Submissions by coalitions of stakeholders should not exceed 5630 words.

*Gist:* UPR stakeholder submissions have binding word limits — one organisation, or a coalition with double the length. (OHCHR technical guidelines, para. 15; limits also on [Current Numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)) ([guidelines, PDF](https://upr-info.org/sites/default/files/documents/2015-03/ohchr_submissions_technical_guidelines_en.pdf))

Treaty body submissions follow each committee's session note for civil society: the deadline, the platform and whether the submission will be public. There is no individual complaint route against Israel to any treaty body, because Israel has not accepted any (lesson [14.1](../module-14/lesson-01.md)).

## How it works in practice

### Eligibility

- **Who may submit:** anyone — an individual, an informal group, an amuta. No UN accreditation is needed for communications, calls for input, shadow reports or UPR submissions.
- **What you need:** a real issue you know first-hand or through reliable direct sources; written consent from anyone named or identifiable; English (or French or Spanish), or an English summary.
- **What is out of scope for this lesson:** an Article 15 communication to the ICC Prosecutor (Module 16) — it is a criminal-justice step with higher legal and personal risk, and needs a lawyer; and any submission based on collecting testimony in a dangerous place.

### Choosing the route

| Your situation | Route | Template |
|---|---|---|

| You have data or analysis on a theme a mandate-holder is writing about | Response to a call for input (listed on the mandate's page and the OHCHR calls-for-input page) | [special-procedures-communication.md](../../templates/special-procedures-communication.md), adapted |

### Working through a coalition

Many Israeli organisations, of different views, prepare joint shadow reports and UPR submissions. To contribute:

1. **Find the coalition.** Ask the organisation where you did your placement (lesson [19.5](lesson-05.md)), or organisations working on your issue, who is coordinating a submission to the coming review. Look at the previous cycle's submissions on the OHCHR and UPR Info pages to see who wrote what.
2. **Offer a defined piece.** A section on one issue: the treaty article or 2023 UPR recommendation; what Israel reported; facts with sources (FOI answers from lesson [19.2](lesson-02.md) are ideal); one question and one recommendation.
3. **Agree on credit and consent.** Your name may appear as a contributor or not. Agree on who signs, who submits, and that every person in the text consented.
4. **Meet the coalition's internal deadline,** which is usually weeks before the UN's.

If no coalition exists on your issue, you may send your own short submission. Two well-sourced pages are better than twenty pages of opinion.

### Expected timeline

| Stage | Hands-on time | Calendar time |
|---|---|---|
| Choosing the route; checking the live deadline and rules | 1.5 hours | week 1 |
| Evidence and consent | 3 hours | weeks 1–4 |
| Drafting to the template and the word limit | 4 hours | weeks 2–6 |
| Review by a second reader with different views; submission | 1 hour | before the deadline |
| Follow-up and portfolio entry | 30 minutes | 2–6 months after |

A Special Procedures communication can be sent within two to three weeks; the experts usually give the government about 60 days to reply, and the letter is published only after that. A coalition submission takes months and the review itself may come a year later; record the submission and close the entry as "submitted — awaiting review".

### What to record in the portfolio

One [portfolio entry](../../templates/portfolio-entry.md), P-06, with: the route and why; a screenshot or link of the live deadline and rules you checked, with the date; the consent records (kept separately and securely, listed by code in the entry); the submission as sent, or your section of the coalition text; the acknowledgement or reference number; any follow-up (an OHCHR question, a published letter, the committee's list of issues); and your rubric score.

### Staying safe and lawful

> [!WARNING]
> Sending information to the UN can expose you and the people you write about. Get written consent from every person named or identifiable, and record what they agreed to: disclosure to the Government of Israel, publication, or neither. Tell OHCHR in the submission if anyone faces a security risk. Keep files encrypted (Module 17). Report any reprisal for cooperating with the UN to OHCHR. This course covers remote and open-source work only.

- **Facts, not slogans.** Neutral language, dated facts, sources. A text that reads as political will be ignored, and it weakens your cause.
- **Truthfulness at home too.** What you send to the UN may be quoted back in Israel. Make no statement about a person you could not defend under the defamation rules (Module 8).
- **Your own name, or the coalition's.** Do not send a communication about a person without their consent or their family's, and never in their name without authority.

## Worked example

**Situation.** Shira, a religious-Zionist lawyer's assistant in Petah Tikva, finished her placement with a women's rights organisation. She cares about women refused a religious divorce (מסורבות גט) — an issue on which religious, traditional and secular women's groups work together.

1. **Route.** No single recent event fits a communication; the issue is a long-term pattern. CEDAW will review Israel's 7th report in 2027 (to be confirmed). Route: a section of a coalition shadow report.
2. **Live check.** On 13 October 2026 she opens the OHCHR Israel page: the 7th report was submitted in October 2025; the session date is still to be confirmed. She saves a screenshot in P-06 and sets a monthly reminder to check the CEDAW session page.
3. **Coalition.** Her placement supervisor tells her that a coalition of women's organisations, including two Orthodox ones, is drafting a shadow report. She offers a two-page section on CEDAW art. 16 (marriage and family) — Israel maintains a reservation to art. 16 on personal status, which she notes and does not argue against; she frames the section as information on implementation, as the committee will ask anyway.
4. **Evidence.** She uses an FOI answer from the rabbinical courts administration on the number of files with sanctions against get refusers in 2024–2026 (her lesson 19.2 campaign), and two first-hand accounts given with written consent, anonymised as "Woman A" and "Woman B".
5. **Draft.** In the [shadow report outline](../../templates/shadow-report-outline.md) format: the article; what Israel's report says (paragraph quoted); the facts; the gap; one suggested question; one SMART recommendation. 900 words.
6. **Review.** A volunteer from a different background reads it for neutrality; Shira removes one adjective.
7. **Submission.** The coalition submits through the platform named in the session note. Shira is listed as a contributor. P-06 holds her section, the consent log (stored separately), the coalition's confirmation and her score: 12 out of 14 — she loses points on follow-up, which can only happen after the session.

A communication route fits a different case: for example, an Israeli journalist detained abroad, a protester injured in a dispersal in Israel, a Palestinian family facing an imminent demolition, or a hostage's family seeking information — each with the consent of the person or family.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- A real issue, and either consenting people (for a communication) or a review or call for input you can contribute to.
- About 10 hours over one to three months.
- The templates: [special-procedures-communication.md](../../templates/special-procedures-communication.md), [shadow-report-outline.md](../../templates/shadow-report-outline.md), [upr-stakeholder-submission.md](../../templates/upr-stakeholder-submission.md), [interview-consent-form.md](../../templates/interview-consent-form.md), [evidence-chain-of-custody.md](../../templates/evidence-chain-of-custody.md), [portfolio-entry.md](../../templates/portfolio-entry.md).

### Steps

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Act in your own name only. This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing. Check the UN body's current deadline, platform and confidentiality rules on its own page before you send anything, and send nothing without the written consent of the people concerned.

1. Open P-06 with the [portfolio entry](../../templates/portfolio-entry.md) template. Choose the route with the table above and write why.
2. Check the live deadline and rules on the UN body's own page. Save a dated screenshot.
3. If you work through a coalition, contact it and agree on your section, credit, consent and the internal deadline.
4. Collect the evidence: documents, FOI answers, official data, first-hand accounts. Record consent with the [interview consent form](../../templates/interview-consent-form.md) and keep originals with the [chain of custody](../../templates/evidence-chain-of-custody.md) log.
5. Draft offline in the matching template: [communication](../../templates/special-procedures-communication.md), [shadow report](../../templates/shadow-report-outline.md) or [UPR submission](../../templates/upr-stakeholder-submission.md). Keep within the word limit.
6. Check it against the art. 9 criteria (communications) or the committee's guidelines (shadow reports, UPR). Ask a reader with different views to check that it is neutral.
7. Submit — yourself through the official channel, or through the coalition. Save the acknowledgement or reference number.
8. Follow up: answer any OHCHR questions; check the communications database or the list of issues later.
9. Score the submission with the rubric below and close P-06 (or mark it "submitted — awaiting review").

### How you know it worked

Score each row 0, 1 or 2. 11 or more out of 14 means this part of the portfolio is complete; below 8, redo the weakest rows.

| Criterion | 0 — not yet | 1 — partly | 2 — done well |
|---|---|---|---|
| Route | Wrong route (for example a communication about a general pattern) | Right route, reason unstated | Route chosen for a stated reason; ICC and field work excluded |
| Live deadline and rules | Not checked | Checked on a secondary site | Checked on the UN body's page; dated screenshot |
| Evidence | News reports only | Some first-hand or official sources | Dated facts with first-hand or official sources for every claim |
| Consent and safety | No consent record | Consent for some people, disclosure unclear | Written consent for everyone identifiable, covering disclosure and publication; security concerns stated |
| Form and limits | Essay, over the limit | Template used, some elements missing | Template complete; within the word limit; question and recommendation per issue |
| Neutral language | Slogans or abuse | Mostly factual | Factual, attributed, checked by a reader with different views |
| Portfolio entry | Missing | Submission without acknowledgement | P-06 with deadline proof, submission, acknowledgement, follow-up and score |

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| A communication about a long-term pattern with no new event | Not acted on | Turn it into shadow-report or UPR input | Use the route table |
| Missing the coalition's internal deadline | Your section is left out | Offer it for the follow-up stage | Diary the internal date, not only the UN date |
| Naming people who did not consent to disclosure to the government | Risk to them; the case may not be considered | Withdraw or correct before the experts act; tell OHCHR | Consent form first; disclosure choices recorded |
| Relying on news articles | Fails art. 9(e) | Add first-hand or official sources | Collect FOI answers and direct accounts |
| A UPR section over the word limit | Cut by the coalition or rejected | Edit to the limit | Write to the limit from the start |
| Sending an Article 15 communication "for the portfolio" | Legal and personal risk outside the course | Do not send; consult a lawyer if the matter is real | Keep to the routes in this lesson |

## Review

- Choose the route by the facts: a communication for a specific case with consent; a call-for-input response for thematic information; a shadow-report or UPR section for long-term patterns.
- Check the live deadline and rules on the UN body's own page, and record the date you checked.
- Coalitions make Israeli UN advocacy effective from every part of the political map; offer a defined, sourced section.
- Consent, neutral language and first-hand sources are not optional; they decide whether your submission is used and whether anyone is put at risk.
- Record the submission and the follow-up in P-06, even if the review comes after the course.
