# 19.1 · Court Observation: Ten Hearings

In this lesson you go to court as a member of the public and watch ten real hearings of different kinds, at least two court levels, and write a short structured report on each. By the end you will know how to find a hearing, get through security, behave in the courtroom, tell an open hearing from a closed one, and you will have seen with your own eyes the procedure you have so far only read about.

## Why it matters

Every hearing you will ever take part in — a small claim, an administrative petition, a remand hearing for a friend — is easier if you have already sat in that room. Law students and interns learn this by watching; you can too, for free, because Israeli courts hear cases in public. Ten hearings teach you more about how judges think, how long things really take and what self-represented people get wrong than any textbook.

Watching also has a civic value. Public hearings exist so that the public can see justice done. Activists who sit in court — at a protest-related remand hearing, a planning appeal, a petition about their town — are part of that public, as long as they respect the rules of the room.

## The law

> בית משפט ידון בפומבי, זולת אם נקבע אחרת בחוק או אם בית המשפט הורה אחרת לפי חוק.

*Gist:* courts hear cases in public unless a law says otherwise or the court orders otherwise under a law. ([חוק-יסוד: השפיטה, s. 3](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק-יסוד:_השפיטה))

> (א) בית משפט ידון בפומבי.
> (ב) בית משפט רשאי לדון בענין מסויים, כולו או מקצתו, בדלתיים סגורות, אם ראה צורך בכך באחת מאלה:

*Gist:* the general rule is public hearings; the court may close all or part of a hearing for listed reasons — among them state security, foreign relations, morals, protecting a minor or a helpless person, protecting a complainant or defendant in a sex offence, protecting a witness who would otherwise not testify freely, and trade secrets (s. 68(ב)(1)–(13)). Section 68(ה) adds that family matters and certain juvenile hearings are closed unless the court orders otherwise, and s. 68(ג) lets the court hear interim applications behind closed doors. ([חוק בתי המשפט, s. 68](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_בתי_המשפט))

> (א) לא יפרסם אדם דבר על דיון שהתנהל בבית משפט בדלתיים סגורות אלא ברשות בית המשפט.
> (ב) לא יצלם אדם באולם בית משפט ולא יפרסם תצלום כזה אלא ברשות בית המשפט.

*Gist:* you may not publish anything about a closed hearing, and you may not take or publish photographs in a courtroom, without the court's permission; s. 70(ג) adds a ban on publishing about a minor's case without permission. ([s. 70](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_בתי_המשפט))

> לא יפרסם אדם דבר על ענין פלילי התלוי ועומד בבית משפט במטרה להשפיע על מהלך המשפט או על תוצאותיו

*Gist:* do not publish about a pending criminal case with the aim of influencing it (s. 71, the sub judice rule). ([s. 71](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_בתי_המשפט))

Section 72(א) lets the court remove anyone who, in or near the courtroom, behaves violently or threateningly, "מקים רעש כדי להביע הסכמה או אי־הסכמה" (makes noise to express agreement or disagreement), or otherwise disturbs the hearing — and, after an ignored warning, impose up to three months' imprisonment or a fine on the spot. ([s. 72](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_בתי_המשפט))

At the entrance, the Court Guard may search people entering without a judge's warrant, and may:

> לדרוש מאדם למסור לו את שמו ומענו ולהציג לו תעודת זהות או תעודה רשמית אחרת המזהה אותו

*Gist:* a court guard may demand your name and address and an ID card or other official identity document; under s. 106ז(ד) a person who refuses the guard's lawful instructions may be refused entry. ([s. 106ז(א)(2)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_בתי_המשפט); similar general powers in [חוק סמכויות לשם שמירה על ביטחון הציבור, ss. 3–5](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_סמכויות_לשם_שמירה_על_ביטחון_הציבור))

## How it works in practice

### Eligibility

Anyone may attend a public hearing: no registration, no fee, no reason needed. Bring an Israeli ID card, passport or driving licence. Minors may attend, but some hearings are unsuitable for them, and a judge may ask them to leave. If you have a personal connection to a case (a party, a witness, a relative), choose other hearings for this lesson: you are here to observe, not to take part.

### Finding hearings

1. **Net HaMishpat court diary.** On the [Net HaMishpat home page](https://www.court.gov.il/NGCS.Web.Site/HomePage.aspx) the top menu has public options, without login, for the court diary (יומן בית משפט) and for a judge's hearings on a given day. Choose the court, the date and, if you like, the judge; you get case numbers, times and courtrooms. Check the labels yourself — the site changes, and it works best in a desktop browser.
2. **Supreme Court hearing diary.** The [Supreme Court's hearing diary](https://supreme.court.gov.il/Pages/External.aspx) lists hearings of the High Court of Justice and appeals, with the panel and the hearing type.
3. **At the courthouse.** Screens and printed lists outside each courtroom show the day's cases. Ask the information desk or call the courts' information line (*3852) if you are lost.
4. **Live and recorded broadcasts.** Selected Supreme Court hearings of special public interest are broadcast live under a pilot that began in 2020. A broadcast may replace at most two of your ten hearings; watching on a screen does not teach you the room.

Case-number prefixes tell you what you are watching: מ"י (remand request), מ"ת (detention until the end of proceedings), ת"פ (criminal case), ת"ק (small claim), ת"א (civil claim), עת"מ (administrative petition), עמ"נ (administrative appeal), בג"ץ (High Court petition), ע"א / ע"פ (civil / criminal appeal).

### Which hearings count

Aim for variety. Your ten must include:

| Requirement | Why |
|---|---|
| At least two court levels (for example Magistrates' and District, or District and Supreme) | You see how the same procedure changes up the ladder (lesson 01.4) |
| At least one criminal hearing (remand, arraignment, evidence or sentencing) | Module 7 in action |
| At least one civil hearing with a self-represented party, ideally a small claim | Module 11 in action |
| At least one public-law hearing (administrative petition or appeal, or a High Court petition) | Module 5 in action |
| The remaining six of your choice: labour court, planning appeals, a protest-related remand, a long evidence hearing | Depth in your own area |

Short hearings (a five-minute remand extension) count, but at least five of the ten should last 20 minutes or more.

### Hearings you may not attend

Expect the door to be closed, or to be asked to leave, in:

- family matters in the Family Court and family appeals (s. 68(ה));
- juvenile cases and detention hearings of minors (s. 68(ה));
- sex-offence cases, and other cases where the court orders closed doors under s. 68(ב);
- parts of a hearing about security or a protected witness.

If a judge or usher asks the public to leave, leave quietly and pick another hearing. Do not argue; you can ask the court secretary afterwards whether the hearing was closed and under which section — a useful exercise in itself.

### Entering the courthouse

> [!WARNING]
> Court buildings have airport-style security. Do not bring knives, multi-tools, pepper spray or anything that looks like a weapon: the Court Guard may confiscate it and refuse you entry, and carrying some items is an offence. Arrive 20–30 minutes early.

- **Security check.** Bags go through an X-ray machine and you walk through a metal detector. The guard may search you and your bag and ask for ID (s. 106ז). Refusing means you do not get in.
- **ID.** Carry the original document, not a photo of it.
- **Phones.** Phones are allowed into most court buildings but must be silent or off inside the courtroom; some courtrooms and some judges ban them entirely. Never photograph or film inside a courtroom (s. 70(ב)), and do not record audio without the court's permission. Take notes on paper, or in a notes app in flight mode where the usher allows it.
- **Dress and conduct.** There is no legal dress code for the public, but dress as you would for a formal meeting. No food, no talking, no reactions. Do not wear slogans or bring signs into the courtroom: showing agreement or disagreement can be treated as disturbing the hearing (s. 72). Stand when the judge enters and leaves.
- **Seating.** Sit in the public benches, not at the counsel tables. Enter and leave between cases if possible.

### Supreme Court tours

The Supreme Court in Jerusalem runs tours of the building through its Department of Foreign Relations, for individuals and groups, in Hebrew and English, Sunday to Thursday between 8:30 and 14:30. Where possible a tour includes watching a hearing. To book or ask, use the contact details on the [Supreme Court tours page](https://supreme.court.gov.il/Pages/Tours.aspx) (phone and email, checked 13 October 2026). The page does not state a fee; public tours have long been free — confirm when you book, and check whether tours are running during security alerts. A tour is a good first visit, but it does not count as one of your ten hearings unless you also watch a hearing.

### Expected timeline

| Stage | Hands-on time | Calendar time |
|---|---|---|
| Plan the ten hearings and the route | 1 hour | 1 week before the first visit |
| Ten hearings, including travel and security | about 13 hours (four or five court days) | 4–8 weeks |
| Ten reports, written the same day | about 2–3 hours | same days |
| Rubric and portfolio entry | 30 minutes | the week after the last hearing |

Court days start at about 8:30; remand hearings in the Magistrates' Courts start early and move fast. Courts sit less during the recess periods (summer and the holidays), so plan around them.

### What to record in the portfolio

Ten [court observation reports](../../templates/court-observation-report.md), numbered 01–10, and one [portfolio entry](../../templates/portfolio-entry.md) (P-01) that lists the ten hearings in its timeline, notes any hearing you were refused entry to and why, and records your rubric score.

### Staying safe and lawful

- Observe; do not take part. If a party asks you for advice in the corridor, say you are not a lawyer and point to the information desk, Legal Aid or the Public Defender (lesson 18.4).
- Do not publish your reports with names of private parties. If you write about a hearing publicly, check first for a publication ban, a minor, or a closed hearing (ss. 70–71), and stick to what was said in open court (Module 8).
- At protest-related hearings tempers run high. Keep away from confrontations in the corridors, and do not film them inside the building.

## Worked example

**Situation.** Miriam, a retired teacher from Be'er Sheva, wants to see the justice system before she helps her neighbourhood's residents' committee. She plans ten hearings over six weeks.

1. **Planning.** On Net HaMishpat she opens the court diary for the Be'er Sheva Magistrates' Court and the Be'er Sheva District Court for two Mondays and a Wednesday, and the Supreme Court hearing diary for a Thursday. She writes down case numbers by prefix: two מ"י, one ת"פ, three ת"ק, one ת"א, one עת"מ in the District Court sitting as an Administrative Court, and one בג"ץ in Jerusalem.
2. **First day.** Security takes 15 minutes; the guard asks for her ID card and checks her bag. She puts her phone on silent. At the first remand hearing (מ"י) the police ask for five days; the judge gives three and sets conditions. Miriam notes the times and the judge's reasons (lesson 07.4) — without the suspect's name.
3. **Closed door.** A hearing she chose turns out to be in a family matter, and the usher asks her to leave. She records it in her P-01 timeline as "closed, s. 68(ה)" and switches to a small-claims hearing in the next room.
4. **Small claims.** A tenant without a lawyer sues a landlord over a deposit. The judge asks for photos the tenant did not bring and proposes a settlement. In section 6 of her report Miriam writes: "bring every exhibit on paper; the judge reads the claim, not your phone" (lesson 11.5).
5. **Jerusalem.** She books a Supreme Court tour for the same morning as a High Court petition by a regional council against a ministry's budget allocation. She watches 40 minutes of argument about standing and exhaustion of remedies (lesson 04.5).
6. **Reports.** Each report is written the same evening. Her analysis of the administrative petition notes that the court asked about the pre-petition letter first (lesson 05.5).
7. **Rubric.** She scores 13 out of 14; she lost a point for variety, because seven of her ten hearings were in one building.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- An ID card, a notebook and pens, comfortable time on four or five weekdays.
- The [court observation report](../../templates/court-observation-report.md) template, printed or on your laptop.
- The [portfolio entry](../../templates/portfolio-entry.md) template for P-01.
- Optional: the [ruling brief](../../templates/ruling-brief.md) template for a written decision you find later.

### Steps

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Act in your own name only. This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing. In this lesson you file nothing: you attend public hearings as a member of the public. Check each court's current entry rules before you go.

1. Open P-01 using the [portfolio entry](../../templates/portfolio-entry.md) template. Write your aim and your planned dates.
2. Choose two court buildings, ideally at two levels, that you can reach.
3. In the court diaries, list at least 15 candidate hearings that meet the variety table (some will be postponed, closed or settled). Note case numbers, courtrooms and times.
4. On the first day, arrive early, pass security, find the courtroom and sit in the public benches. Follow the conduct rules above.
5. During each hearing, fill in sections 1–3 of the [court observation report](../../templates/court-observation-report.md). Facts only.
6. The same day, write sections 4–6: your analysis, the conduct and access notes, three lessons.
7. Repeat until you have ten reports that meet the variety table.
8. For at least one case, look for the written decision later and brief it with the [ruling brief](../../templates/ruling-brief.md) template.
9. Score yourself on the rubric below and close P-01.

### Template

- [court-observation-report.md](../../templates/court-observation-report.md) — one per hearing.
- [portfolio-entry.md](../../templates/portfolio-entry.md) — entry P-01 for the whole set.
- [ruling-brief.md](../../templates/ruling-brief.md) — for a published decision.

### How you know it worked

Score each row 0, 1 or 2. 11 or more out of 14 means this part of the portfolio is complete; below 8, watch more hearings or rewrite the weakest reports.

| Criterion | 0 — not yet | 1 — partly | 2 — done well |
|---|---|---|---|
| Number and length | Fewer than ten | Ten, but most under ten minutes | Ten, at least five of 20 minutes or more |
| Variety | One court, one kind of case | Two of the four required kinds | Two levels, criminal, civil with a self-represented party, and public law |
| Facts | Opinions mixed into the account | Facts, but no times or decisions | Timed facts and the decision, in every report |
| Procedure | Not linked to the course | Linked in some reports | Every report names at least one rule or lesson it shows |
| Analysis | Missing | General impressions | The real issue, the persuasive argument and a lesson for your own practice |
| Lawful conduct | Photos, names of private parties, or a closed hearing described | One slip (for example a full name) | No photos or recordings, parties anonymised, closed hearings only noted |
| Portfolio entry | Missing | Reports filed, no P-01 | P-01 with timeline, refusals noted and rubric score |

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Taking a photo of the courtroom "for the report" | An offence under s. 70(ב); the usher or guard may remove you | Delete it at once; apologise if asked | Phone silent and in your bag; paper notes |
| Reacting aloud to a decision | Removal, or a penalty under s. 72 | Leave quietly if asked | Treat the bench like a theatre during a play |
| Choosing only short remand hearings | Ten "hearings" that teach little | Add longer evidence or petition hearings | Use the variety table when planning |
| Writing the defendant's or a child's name in a shared report | Privacy breach; possibly a breach of s. 70(ג) | Anonymise and re-share | Use roles and initials from the start |
| Arriving at 9:00 for a 9:00 hearing | Stuck in the security queue; hearing over | Pick a later hearing | Arrive 20–30 minutes early |
| Counting a tour or a broadcast as a hearing | A thin portfolio | Watch the hearing in person | Tours add context; hearings count |

## Review

- Hearings are public by default (Basic Law: The Judiciary s. 3; Courts Law s. 68(א)); family, juvenile and some other hearings are closed.
- The Court Guard may search you and demand ID at the entrance (s. 106ז); bring an original ID and nothing that looks like a weapon.
- No photographs in the courtroom, nothing published about closed hearings or minors, no disturbing the hearing (ss. 70–72).
- Find hearings in the Net HaMishpat court diary and the Supreme Court hearing diary; aim for variety across levels and branches.
- One report per hearing, facts separate from analysis, and one portfolio entry for the set.
