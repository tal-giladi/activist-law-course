# 19.5 · A Clinic or NGO Placement

In this lesson you join a public-interest organisation, or a university legal clinic if you are a law student, as a volunteer for at least three months. You choose the placement, ask for it with a targeted email, agree on tasks and supervision, work within the legal limits on what non-lawyers may do for others, and keep a log of what you did and learned. This placement is the course's closest equivalent to the supervised practice that law students get in clinics and interns get in law offices.

## Why it matters

Everything in this course so far you did for yourself. Organisations do the same things at scale — hundreds of FOI requests, monitoring dozens of committees, documenting cases for years, petitions that change policy for a whole population. Working inside one teaches you how campaigns are really run: how lawyers, researchers and volunteers divide the work, how a file is built for court, and how an organisation decides which battles to fight.

It also gives you something this course cannot: people who know your work. A supervisor who saw you do careful research for three months is worth more than any certificate when you later want to lead your own campaign, join a board or ask an organisation for help.

## The law

> הפעולות המנויות להלן, לא יעשה אותן דרך עיסוק, או בתמורה אף שלא דרך עיסוק, אלא עורך דין; ואלה הפעולות:

*Gist:* only a lawyer may, as an occupation or for any consideration, represent others before courts and listed authorities, draft documents of a legal character for others, or give legal advice and opinions (s. 20(1)–(4)). A volunteer at an organisation works regularly — so "as an occupation" can apply even without pay. ([חוק לשכת עורכי הדין, s. 20](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_לשכת_עורכי_הדין))

> מי שאינו עורך דין ועושה פעולה שנתייחדה לפי חוק זה לעורכי דין, דינו – קנס כאמור בסעיף 61(א)(2) לחוק העונשין.

*Gist:* a non-lawyer who does a reserved act commits an offence punishable by a fine (s. 96; amount on [Current Numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)). ([s. 96](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_לשכת_עורכי_הדין))

The Bar's national ethics committee decided in 2009 that an organisation or clinic giving free legal services must run them like a law office, by lawyers who carry the professional responsibility; students and volunteers act only under their supervision. Lesson [18.4](../module-18/lesson-04.md) explains these rules in full, including the Supreme Court's line between general legal information (allowed) and individual advice (reserved).

If the organisation is mainly funded by foreign state entities, the Foreign State Entity Disclosure Law requires it to say so in its publications and when its representatives address officials or Knesset committees (s. 5א). If you speak for the organisation, ask the staff how they comply. ([s. 5א](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_חובת_גילוי_לגבי_מי_שנתמך_על_ידי_ישות_מדינית_זרה))

## How it works in practice

### Eligibility

| Placement | Who can join | What to check |
|---|---|---|
| University legal clinic | Law students enrolled at that faculty, usually in year 2 or 3, as an academic course with credit | The faculty's clinic list and application dates; non-students cannot join as clinic students |
| NGO legal department | Law students, graduates, interns; sometimes others for research or data work | Minimum commitment, hours, office location, interview |
| NGO research, monitoring, data, outreach or field team | Anyone 18+ (some accept 16+ with a parent's consent) | Volunteer form, interview, confidentiality undertaking, sometimes a background check |

Most organisations want a minimum of three to six months at a few hours a week. This lesson requires **at least three months and about 50 hours** of actual work.

### Approaching them

1. Read the organisation's site: its current campaigns, its reports, its volunteer page or form.
2. Send a targeted request with the [placement request email](../../templates/placement-request-email.md), in Hebrew or English. Attach a one-page CV and one work sample from this course with private details removed.
3. Send to two or three organisations, not twenty. Remind once after ten working days.
4. At the interview, ask: Who will supervise me? Which tasks? How many hours, where? What confidentiality rules? Who is the lawyer responsible for any legal work I touch?
5. Agree in writing — an email is enough — on tasks, hours, duration, supervisor and confidentiality.

### What a volunteer may and may not do

| Fine for a volunteer | Only under a named lawyer's supervision, within the organisation's procedures | Never, as a non-lawyer |
|---|---|---|
| Research law, rulings, protocols and data | Intake: taking down a person's facts on the organisation's form | Telling a person what to claim, whether they will win, what to answer |
| FOI requests in the organisation's name, with its approval | Preparing a first draft that a lawyer reviews and signs | Signing or sending a legal document for a client in your own name |
| Monitoring courts, committees and councils; writing summaries | Answering a hotline from an approved script of general information | Representing anyone before a court, tribunal or listed authority |
| Remote and open-source documentation, archiving, chain of custody (Module 17) | Translating for a lawyer and a client | Calling yourself a "legal adviser" or anything that suggests you are a lawyer |
| Public education: workshops, explainers, general information | Accompanying a person to a hearing as a support person in the public seats | Taking any payment or benefit from the people helped |

If you are asked to do something in the third column, say no politely, explain why, and tell your supervisor. A good organisation will thank you.

### Expected timeline

| Stage | Hands-on time | Calendar time |
|---|---|---|
| Choosing and researching organisations | 2 hours | 1–2 weeks |
| Emails, reminder, interview, written agreement | 2 hours | 2–6 weeks |
| The placement itself, about 4 hours a week | about 44 hours | at least 3 months |
| Log, work samples, supervisor's confirmation, rubric | 2 hours | the last 2 weeks |

The calendar time from the first email to the end of the placement is usually four to five months. Run the placement in parallel with lessons 19.1–19.4 and 19.6; many organisations will let you do one of them as part of your work there.

### What to record in the portfolio

One [portfolio entry](../../templates/portfolio-entry.md), P-05, with: why you chose the organisation; the written agreement (tasks, hours, supervisor); a placement log (date, hours, task, what you learned); two or three work samples, with the organisation's permission and with private details removed; a short confirmation from your supervisor of dates and hours; and your rubric score.

### Staying safe and lawful

- Keep the s. 20 line (table above). The organisation's lawyer, not you, carries responsibility for any legal service.
- Respect confidentiality. Clients' and witnesses' details never leave the organisation's systems; your work samples must be cleared and anonymised.
- Digital security: use the organisation's accounts and devices where possible, follow its security rules, and separate your personal accounts (lesson [17.5](../module-17/lesson-05.md)).
- Field work: this course covers remote and open-source work only. Do not join field monitoring in dangerous areas or conflict zones as part of this lesson; if an organisation offers it, that is its programme and its safety protocol, not the course's.
- Speaking for the organisation: only with its approval, and following its disclosure duties.

## Worked example

**Two placements, two causes.**

**Avital** is a 34-year-old accountant in Tel Aviv who cares about migrant workers. She emails Kav LaOved with the [placement request](../../templates/placement-request-email.md), offering four hours a week for four months, plus her Excel skills. At the interview the coordinator offers a place on the hotline team. Avital asks who supervises and is told that a staff lawyer approves the script and reviews every case. She agrees in writing: hotline shifts using the approved script, data entry of wage claims for the lawyers, and a monthly data summary. On her third shift a caller asks whether to sign a settlement his employer offered. Avital does not answer the question; she records the facts on the intake form and books him an appointment with the lawyer. Her log records 52 hours over four months; her supervisor confirms them by email. Her work sample is the anonymised data summary.

**Eitan** is a 23-year-old geography student in Jerusalem interested in land-use enforcement. He applies to Regavim with the English version of the email, offering mapping skills. He is assigned to a desk team that compares building-enforcement data from local committees with aerial imagery. He files three FOI requests in the organisation's name after a staff member approves the wording, and summarises the published protocols of two district planning committee sessions. When asked whether he could join a field tour in an area under frequent security alerts, he declines for this lesson, as the course scope requires. His log records 49 hours; his supervisor confirms them.

Both score 12 or more out of 14. The mirror test applies: either learner could have done the same work at an organisation with opposite views; the skills and the legal limits are the same.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- About 50 hours over at least three months, plus time to find the placement.
- A one-page CV and one work sample from this course.
- The templates: [placement-request-email.md](../../templates/placement-request-email.md), [helping-others-boundaries.md](../../templates/helping-others-boundaries.md), [portfolio-entry.md](../../templates/portfolio-entry.md); and, depending on your tasks, [foi-request.md](../../templates/foi-request.md), [court-observation-report.md](../../templates/court-observation-report.md), [interview-consent-form.md](../../templates/interview-consent-form.md), [evidence-chain-of-custody.md](../../templates/evidence-chain-of-custody.md).

### Steps

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Act in your own name only. This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing. As a volunteer you act for the organisation and under its supervision; never give individual legal advice, draft legal documents for others, or represent anyone.

1. Open P-05 with the [portfolio entry](../../templates/portfolio-entry.md) template. List three candidate organisations and why.
2. Read each one's site and current work. Remove one if it does not fit.
3. Adapt and send the [placement request email](../../templates/placement-request-email.md). Diary a reminder for ten working days later.
4. At the interview, ask the five questions above. Agree in writing on tasks, hours, duration, supervisor and confidentiality.
5. Print the [helping-others boundaries card](../../templates/helping-others-boundaries.md) and keep it with you.
6. Work at least three months. Log every session: date, hours, task, what you learned.
7. Collect two or three work samples, cleared by the organisation and anonymised.
8. In the last two weeks, ask your supervisor for a short confirmation of dates and hours.
9. Score the placement with the rubric below and close P-05.

### Template

- [placement-request-email.md](../../templates/placement-request-email.md) — the request, in Hebrew and English.
- [helping-others-boundaries.md](../../templates/helping-others-boundaries.md) — the s. 20 do/don't card (from Module 18).
- [foi-request.md](../../templates/foi-request.md), [court-observation-report.md](../../templates/court-observation-report.md), [interview-consent-form.md](../../templates/interview-consent-form.md), [evidence-chain-of-custody.md](../../templates/evidence-chain-of-custody.md) — as your tasks require.
- [portfolio-entry.md](../../templates/portfolio-entry.md) — entry P-05 with the placement log.

### How you know it worked

Score each row 0, 1 or 2. 11 or more out of 14 means this part of the portfolio is complete; below 8, extend the placement or redo the weakest rows.

| Criterion | 0 — not yet | 1 — partly | 2 — done well |
|---|---|---|---|
| Choice | Random or mass emails | Reasoned choice, no research on current work | Cause and fit explained; current work researched |
| Agreement | None | Verbal only | Written: tasks, hours, duration, supervisor, confidentiality |
| Duration and hours | Under two months or 30 hours | Two to three months or 30–45 hours | Three months or more and about 50 hours, logged |
| Legal limits | Gave advice or drafted for a client | Unsure but no incident; no card | Kept the s. 20 line; referred a request and recorded it |
| Confidentiality and security | Client data taken home or shared | Rules followed mostly | Organisation's systems used; samples cleared and anonymised |
| Learning | No reflection | Tasks listed | What you learned about how the organisation builds cases and campaigns |
| Portfolio entry | Missing | Log without confirmation or samples | P-05 with log, samples, supervisor's confirmation and score |

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Emailing twenty organisations a generic text | No answers, or answers you cannot honour | Pick three; rewrite each email | Research first; targeted emails |
| Agreeing on "helping out" with no written scope | Endless admin work, or tasks you should not do | Ask for a written task list now | Agree in writing at the start |
| Answering a client's "what should I do?" | Possible breach of s. 20; harm to the client | Tell the supervisor at once; refer the client | Approved script; referral line from the card |
| Taking client files home on a personal laptop | Privacy breach; loss of trust | Return and delete; report it | Organisation's systems only |
| Quitting after six weeks | No portfolio entry, no reference | Agree a handover; finish your tasks | Choose hours you can keep for three months |
| Joining a risky field tour "for the portfolio" | Physical risk outside the course's scope | Decline; ask for desk work | Remote and open-source work only |

## Review

- Clinics are for enrolled law students; NGOs take volunteers for research, monitoring, data, outreach and supervised legal support.
- Choose by cause and fit, approach two or three organisations with a targeted email, and agree in writing on tasks, hours, supervisor and confidentiality.
- Regular volunteering can be "an occupation" under s. 20: no individual advice, no drafting for clients, no representation — refer instead.
- At least three months and about 50 hours, logged, with cleared work samples and a supervisor's confirmation.
- Remote and open-source work only; field work in dangerous areas is outside this course.
