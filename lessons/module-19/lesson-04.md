# 19.4 · Taking Part in the Knesset or Your Local Council

In this lesson you take part, in person and in writing, in a real session of a Knesset committee or of your local council. You choose the session, request to attend and to speak, submit a position paper or get an elected member to file your question, sit in the room, and then follow the protocol and the result. By the end you will have turned the theory of Module 12 into a real public record with your name in it.

## Why it matters

Committees are where most laws are actually written, and council meetings are where most local budgets and plans are approved. Both are open to the public, and in both a short, well-prepared intervention from a resident can change a clause, put a question on the record or get an official to commit to a date. Most people never try because the first time feels intimidating. After this lesson, it will not.

The lesson also teaches the honest limits. You have no right to speak in a Knesset committee — the chair decides — and residents have no right to speak at council meetings at all. Your influence comes from preparation, a written paper, and allies among the elected members.

## The law

> יושב ראש ועדה רשאי להזמין לישיבה כל מי שיש לו ידע או עניין בנושא שהוועדה דנה בו, לשמוע את דעתו ולבקש ממנו מידע.

*Gist:* a committee chair may invite anyone with knowledge or an interest in the subject to give a view and information — participation is by the chair's leave, not by right. ([תקנון הכנסת, s. 125(א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/תקנון_הכנסת))

> ישיבות הוועדות הן פומביות, אלא אם כן החליטה הוועדה אחרת או אם פרוטוקול הישיבה חסוי כמפורט בפרק זה; יושב ראש ועדה רשאי להגביל את הנוכחות באולם הוועדה, כולה או חלקה

*Gist:* committee sessions are public unless decided otherwise, but the chair may limit who is in the room. ([s. 118(א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/תקנון_הכנסת))

> פרוטוקולים של ישיבות ועדות יהיו פתוחים לעיון הציבור ויפורסמו באתר האינטרנט של הכנסת, בכפוף להוראות סעיף זה.

*Gist:* committee protocols are public and published online (s. 120(א)); a speaker may ask in writing to correct errors in how their words were recorded (s. 119(ג)). ([s. 120](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/תקנון_הכנסת))

For councils, the Municipalities Ordinance's Second Schedule makes council meetings public, allowing closed sessions only for state security or privacy on the legal adviser's opinion (s. 18(א)); a council member may file a question at least 7 days before a regular meeting (s. 36) or a direct question answered within 21 days (s. 38); and s. 248ב requires the approved protocol and a recording or transcript to be online within two working days of approval. ([פקודת העיריות](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/פקודת_העיריות); lesson [12.5](../module-12/lesson-05.md))

## How it works in practice

### Eligibility

- **Knesset:** anyone may watch broadcasts; to enter the building for a committee session you must be on the session's invitation list, which the secretariat prepares from requests. Bring an Israeli ID card or passport. There is no age limit, but the chair decides who speaks.
- **Local council:** anyone may attend a public council meeting. Residents have no right to speak; you act through council members, council committees and written submissions.
- **Not eligible for this lesson:** acting as a paid lobbyist for someone else. If you speak for your own cause, as a volunteer, or for the organisation that employs you, you are not a lobbyist (Knesset Law ch. 12; lesson [12.4](../module-12/lesson-04.md)).

### The calendar in October 2026

The 25th Knesset has been in its election recess since July 2026. Elections are on 27 October 2026, and the new Knesset convenes within fourteen days of election day (Basic Law: The Knesset, s. 12). Committees of the 26th Knesset form only after that, so expect few sessions until then and a busy agenda once they start. In the recess committees meet only with special approval (Rules s. 112; IDI, July 2026).

Plan accordingly:

| If you start… | Do this |
|---|---|
| Before the new committees form | Choose the local-council route, or prepare your paper and watch recordings of past sessions on the Knesset Channel |
| After the committees form | Watch the committee pages weekly; agendas are usually posted about a week ahead |
| Any time | Council meetings run monthly at least (Second Schedule s. 5) |

### Route A — a Knesset committee session

1. **Choose the committee and the topic.** Use the committee pages, Open Knesset and the Knesset Channel to follow your issue (lesson [12.2](../module-12/lesson-02.md)).
2. **Write the paper.** Two to four pages with a one-page summary, ending in specific drafting requests (lesson [12.3](../module-12/lesson-03.md); [position-paper.md](../../templates/position-paper.md)).
3. **Request to take part.** As soon as the agenda is posted, email the secretariat with the [committee participation request](../../templates/committee-participation-request.md): the session, who you are, your interest, a request to speak for two or three minutes, the paper, and your ID number and phone for the entry list. Call a day or two later to confirm.
4. **Entry.** Arrive at least 45 minutes early. You pass a security check and show ID; your name must be on the list. The Knesset has long enforced a visitors' dress code — as reported, no shorts or three-quarter trousers, ripped trousers, cropped or thin-strap tops, short skirts or dresses, flip-flops, or **shirts with political slogans**. Check the current rules on the Knesset website before you go. No signs, banners or props in the committee room.
5. **In the room.** Sit where the usher shows you. If the chair gives you the floor: who you are, one sentence of position, two facts, one concrete request, thanks. Two minutes. Do not heckle; the chair may have you removed.
6. **After.** Read the protocol when it is published. Ask for corrections in writing if your words were recorded wrongly (s. 119(ג)). Send a short thank-you email to the secretariat with your paper attached again, and track the bill or the follow-up the committee asked for.

### Route B — a local council meeting

1. **Find the meeting.** On the authority's website (ישיבות מועצה / פרוטוקולים), or ask the council meetings coordinator. Read the agenda and the previous protocol.
2. **Find allies.** Write to at least two council members, one from the coalition and one from the opposition, with a ready draft question or motion, using Part B of the [council question request](../../templates/council-question-request.md). Mind the 7-day deadline for questions before a regular meeting (s. 36).
3. **Written input.** If the topic goes to a council committee, send it a short position paper ([position-paper.md](../../templates/position-paper.md), adapted for the council).
4. **Attend.** Sit in the public seats. You may not speak, applaud or heckle; take notes.
5. **After.** Check that the protocol and the recording appear online within two working days of approval (s. 248ב). If not, write to the coordinator and then send an [FOI request](../../templates/foi-request.md). Record the mayor's answer to your question.

### Minimum for this lesson

One session attended in person (a broadcast does not count) **and** one written input that becomes part of the record: a paper submitted to the committee, or a question or motion filed by an elected member. Speaking in the session is a bonus, not a requirement — it depends on the chair.

### Expected timeline

| Stage | Hands-on time | Calendar time |
|---|---|---|
| Choosing the session; reading the agenda and background | 1 hour | 1–4 weeks before (longer while the Knesset is in recess) |
| Position paper or draft question | 2 hours | 1–2 weeks before |
| Requests, emails to members, confirmation call | 30 minutes | 7–10 days before |
| The session, including travel and security | 2 hours | one day |
| Protocol, follow-up, portfolio entry and rubric | 30 minutes | 1–3 weeks after |

### What to record in the portfolio

One [portfolio entry](../../templates/portfolio-entry.md), P-04, with: the agenda; your request and the confirmation; your paper or draft question; a note of what happened in the room (time, who spoke, what was decided); the protocol extract that records your input; any correction you requested; the follow-up; and your rubric score.

### Staying safe and lawful

- Speak for yourself or for the group that authorised you; never present yourself as representing people who did not agree.
- Send your ID number only to the secretariat for the entry list, never in a public paper.
- Do not record or photograph inside the committee room unless the rules and the chair allow it; the session is usually broadcast anyway.
- Protest outside, not inside. Disrupting a session can get you removed and barred from the building, and it weakens the paper you came to present. For protests near the Knesset, see Module 6.
- If speaking in public could expose you to harm, ask before the session to appear without the public present (s. 126).

## Worked example

**Situation.** Ruth, a mother of a deaf teenager in Haifa, follows a government bill on accessibility of emergency alerts. Its first reading was in spring 2026, and she expects the new Labour and Welfare Committee to resume work on it after the elections.

1. **Planning.** Because the Knesset is in recess, she prepares in October: she watches the recording of the committee's last session on the Knesset Channel and reads the protocol on Open Knesset.
2. **Local first.** In parallel she takes Route B: the Haifa council meeting in November has emergency preparedness on the agenda. She emails two council members, one from each side, a draft question: how many public warning sirens in the city have visual signals, and what is the plan to complete them. One files it on time.
3. **Council meeting.** Ruth attends, sits in the public seats, and hears the deputy mayor's answer: 40% of the sirens, with a target date. The protocol appears online three working days after approval; she screenshots it.
4. **Knesset.** In December, when the committee posts a session on the bill, she sends the [participation request](../../templates/committee-participation-request.md) with a three-page [position paper](../../templates/position-paper.md) asking to add "visual and vibrating alerts on mobile devices" to clause 3. The secretariat confirms her place.
5. **The session.** She passes security, dressed for the dress code, and waits two hours. The chair gives her ninety seconds. She uses them for one fact (the municipal figure) and the drafting request.
6. **Follow-up.** The protocol records her words with one error; she writes to the protocol department to correct it. The committee asks the ministry for a written answer within 30 days; Ruth diarises it.
7. **Portfolio.** P-04 holds both routes. She scores 14 out of 14.

The same steps work for a farmers' group on a water-pricing bill, a religious-freedom group on a civil-marriage bill, a residents' group of a Bedouin town on regional council budgets, or settlers' councils on road-safety funding.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- An issue, a target session (Knesset or council), and about 6 hours over two to six weeks.
- An ID card; clothes that meet the Knesset dress code if you take Route A.
- The templates: [position-paper.md](../../templates/position-paper.md), [committee-participation-request.md](../../templates/committee-participation-request.md), [council-question-request.md](../../templates/council-question-request.md), [foi-request.md](../../templates/foi-request.md), [portfolio-entry.md](../../templates/portfolio-entry.md).

### Steps

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Act in your own name only. This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing. Check the committee's or the council's current agenda, entry rules and deadlines before you send anything.

1. Open P-04 with the [portfolio entry](../../templates/portfolio-entry.md) template. Choose Route A, Route B or both, with a reason based on the calendar.
2. Find the session and read its agenda and the last protocol on the topic.
3. Write your [position paper](../../templates/position-paper.md) or your draft question with the [council question request](../../templates/council-question-request.md).
4. Send the [participation request](../../templates/committee-participation-request.md) to the committee secretariat, or the question to at least two council members. Confirm by phone.
5. Attend in person. Follow the entry and conduct rules. If given the floor, speak for two minutes and end with one concrete request.
6. Within a week, write down what happened while you remember it.
7. When the protocol appears, find your input in it. Request a correction if needed. If a council protocol is missing, write to the coordinator, then file an [FOI request](../../templates/foi-request.md).
8. Follow the outcome for at least one more step (the next session, the ministry's answer, the mayor's answer).
9. Score yourself with the rubric below and close P-04.

### How you know it worked

Score each row 0, 1 or 2. 11 or more out of 14 means this part of the portfolio is complete; below 8, redo the weakest rows.

| Criterion | 0 — not yet | 1 — partly | 2 — done well |
|---|---|---|---|
| Choice and timing | Session chosen without checking the calendar | Session chosen, recess or deadlines not considered | Route chosen for a reason; recess and deadlines planned |
| Written input | None | Paper or question drafted but not submitted | Paper submitted to the committee, or a question filed by a member |
| Request and allies | No request sent | Request sent late or without confirmation | Request sent when the agenda was posted and confirmed; or two members approached |
| Attendance and conduct | Watched only on screen | Attended, rules partly followed | Attended in person; entry, dress and conduct rules followed |
| Content | General opinions | Position clear, no concrete request | One position, facts with sources, a specific drafting or action request |
| Follow-up | None | Protocol found | Protocol found, correction requested if needed, next step tracked |
| Portfolio entry | Missing | Partial | P-04 complete with exhibits and score |

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Planning a Knesset session during the election recess | No session to attend | Switch to Route B or wait for the new committees | Check the calendar first |
| Arriving without being on the entry list | Turned away at the gate | Call the secretariat from the entrance | Request early; confirm by phone |
| A slogan T-shirt or shorts | Refused entry | Change clothes | Read the dress code the day before |
| A ten-minute speech | Cut off before your request | Lead with the request next time | Two minutes, one request |
| Expecting to speak at a council meeting | Frustration, removal if you insist | Work through a member | Prepare a question for a member |
| Question sent to a member three days before the meeting | Too late for a regular meeting question (s. 36) | Ask for a direct question (s. 38) | Send at least 10 days ahead |

## Review

- Knesset committees and council meetings are public, but speaking is at the chair's discretion, and residents do not speak at council meetings.
- In October 2026 the Knesset is in its election recess; committees of the new Knesset form after it convenes, so plan around it.
- Your influence is the written input: a position paper, or a question or motion filed by a member.
- Get on the entry list, meet the dress code, keep to two minutes, and never disrupt.
- Find your input in the protocol, correct it if needed, and track the next step in P-04.
