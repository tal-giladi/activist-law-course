# 20.2 · The Legal Strategy Map

In this lesson you turn your theory of change into a legal strategy map: which tools from the whole course you will use, in which order, against which deadlines, at what cost and risk. You will work from one decision matrix that compares every tool in the course side by side, and you will finish with a one-page map, a risk register and a budget for your capstone campaign.

## Why it matters

Each module taught a tool in isolation. Real campaigns combine them, and the combination is where most mistakes happen: a petition filed before a pre-petition letter, a complaint that ran past the court deadline, a press conference that made a defamation claim more likely, a UN submission sent before the facts were checked through FOI. Sequencing is a legal skill. Courts check whether you asked first, whether you waited too long, and whether another remedy existed.

The matrix below is a memory aid for the whole course. It does not replace the lessons: every row links back to the lesson that teaches the tool properly, and every number lives on [Current Numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md).

## The law

Three rules explain why order matters.

> (א) נתבקש עובד הציבור, בכתב, להשתמש בסמכות שניתנה לו על פי דין, יחליט בבקשה וישיב למבקש בכתב בהקדם, אך לא יאוחר מארבעים וחמישה ימים מיום קבלת הבקשה.

*Gist:* a written request to use a power starts the authority's duty to decide and answer in writing — the starting point of almost every strategy. ([חוק לתיקון סדרי המינהל (החלטות והנמקות), s. 2](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_לתיקון_סדרי_המינהל_%28החלטות_והנמקות%29#סעיף_2))

> (ג) בית המשפט העליון ישב גם כבית משפט גבוה לצדק; בשבתו כאמור ידון בענינים אשר הוא רואה צורך לתת בהם סעד למען הצדק ואשר אינם בסמכותו של בית משפט או של בית דין אחר.

*Gist:* the High Court of Justice hears only what no other court or tribunal can hear, so an available alternative forum comes first. ([חוק-יסוד: השפיטה, s. 15(ג)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק-יסוד:_השפיטה#סעיף_15))

> דחה בית המשפט את העתירה ללא הזמנת המשיב, רשאי הוא להטיל על העותר הוצאות לטובת המדינה בשיעור שימצא לנכון או להורות לרשם לעשות כן.

*Gist:* a petition dismissed on its face — for example, one filed without asking the authority first — can cost the petitioner costs to the State. ([תקנות סדר הדין בבית המשפט הגבוה לצדק, reg. 6](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/תקנות_סדר_הדין_בבית_המשפט_הגבוה_לצדק#סעיף_6))

The deadline rule from lesson 20.1 applies to the whole map: a petition against an administrative decision must be filed without delay and within the period in reg. 3(ב) ([Wikisource](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/תקנות_בתי_משפט_לענינים_מינהליים_%28סדרי_דין%29#סעיף_3)). Soft tools must fit inside it.

## How it works in practice

### The decision matrix

How to read it:

- **Cost and clock** give the approximate fee and the legal deadline. Check both on [Current Numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md) before you act; they change every year.
- **Typical time** is the usual time to a first result, not a legal limit.
- **Chance** is our rough judgement of how often the tool produces its *typical result* when used correctly, on a three-step scale (high, medium, low). It is not a statistic, and it is not the chance of winning your whole campaign.
- **Risk** is the main thing that can go wrong for *you*.

### Sequencing: five rules

1. **Ask first, in writing.** The formal letter (or the specific request the law requires) opens almost every map. It starts the 45-day duty and later proves you exhausted remedies.
2. **Facts before arguments.** FOI and documentation run early and in parallel. A petition or a UN submission built on unverified facts is the most expensive mistake in the course.
3. **Soft before hard, inside the hard deadlines.** Oversight complaints, council questions and committee sessions are cheap; use them while the court clock runs, never instead of watching it.
4. **One forum per question.** Know which forum decides your question (First Schedule or HCJ, planning committee or court, regulator or court). Use other forums for pressure and records, not to ask the same question twice.
5. **Communicate at milestones, not at every step.** Media and international steps work best when they report a fact on the record — an FOI answer, an oversight finding, a council vote, a court decision (lesson 20.4).

### The deadline spine

Draw one horizontal line for the campaign's months. Mark every hard deadline on it first: a petition clock, an objection period, a treaty-body session, a budget vote. Then place the soft tools before them. If two tools compete for the same weeks, the one with the hard deadline wins.

### Risk register and budget

For each tool you choose, write the main risk from the matrix, how likely it is in your case (low, medium, high), and what you will do to reduce it. Typical entries: "costs order — file only after a lawyer's one-hour review"; "defamation — pre-publication checklist before every post"; "arrest at the protest — arrest card and a legal contact" ([07.2](../module-07/lesson-02.md)).

Write a budget: fees (with the exemption route if you need one), copies, travel, certification, and an hour or two of a lawyer's review if you plan a petition.

### Tools you will not use

The map must say why you are *not* using the tools that do not fit. "No international step: the issue is a municipal service with no treaty angle and Israeli remedies are open" is a good answer. It shows that you chose, rather than collected.

## Worked example

**Situation.** A hotel plan is deposited for a public beach in a coastal town. Avi, a local resident and member of a sea-swimmers' club, uses this as his capstone. His goal (lesson 20.1): "By the district committee's decision on the objections, the plan keeps public access to the beach along its whole length, or the committee gives a reasoned decision we can appeal."

1. **Deadline spine.** The objection period runs from the publication of the deposit (lesson [10.3](../module-10/lesson-03.md)); Avi writes its last day first. If the committee rejects the objection, the appeal route and its short deadline (lesson [10.4](../module-10/lesson-04.md)) come next.
2. **Facts.** Week 1: FOI request to the district planning office for the environmental and coastal surveys and the coastal committee's opinion (05.2). Week 1: he reads the plan on Mavat (10.2) and photographs the beach at the same hours each week (17.1).
3. **Formal and legal.** Week 3: objection in his own name, with ten neighbours filing their own (10.3). The swimmers' club, an informal group with a group agreement (18.1), files one in its members' names.
4. **Political.** Week 4: two council members, one from each side of the council, ask the mayor's position at the next meeting (12.5).
5. **Communication.** After the FOI answer arrives: a press release built on the survey's own figures, checked against the pre-publication checklist (20.4, 08.2).
6. **Hearing.** He speaks at the objections hearing within his written reasons (10.4).
7. **Not used, and why.** No HCJ petition (planning matters go first to the planning appeal route and the Administrative Court); no international step (no treaty issue that adds anything); no protest (the club decides a beach swim-in is enough and needs no licence at its size, after checking lesson [06.2](../module-06/lesson-02.md)).
8. **Risk register.** Defamation risk in naming the developer: name the plan, not the people, and stick to documents. Costs risk on appeal: one hour of a planning clinic's review first. Budget: FOI fee, printing, a possible appeal fee.

**Mirror test.** The same map structure serves farmers in the Jordan Valley objecting to a regional plan that cuts their access road, Bedouin residents objecting to a plan that ignores their village, and a Haredi neighbourhood objecting to the loss of a planned synagogue plot.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- Your theory of change from lesson 20.1.
- [Current Numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md) open in a tab.
- About 2½ hours.

### Steps

1. For each action in your backwards map, find its row in the matrix and copy the forum, cost and clock, typical time, chance and risk.
2. Add any tool the matrix suggests that you missed. Remove any tool that duplicates another forum's question.
3. Draw the deadline spine: hard deadlines first, then soft tools before them.
4. Check the five sequencing rules one by one and fix the order.
5. Write the risk register: one line per tool with likelihood and the step that reduces the risk.
6. Write the budget, with the fee-exemption route if you need it.
7. Write "Tools I will not use, and why" for every family you skip.
8. Check the capstone minimum: at least four tools from at least three families, and at least three real actions.

### Template

Use [Legal strategy map](../../templates/legal-strategy-map.md). It has the tool table, the deadline spine, the risk register, the budget and the "not used" list.

### How you know it worked

- Every hard deadline is on the spine and no soft tool runs past it.
- The first real action is a written request to the decision-maker or an FOI request, unless you explain why not.
- Each tool links to its lesson and each number to Current Numbers.
- A reader can see why you left out each family you did not use.

- [ ] Five sequencing rules checked.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Petition before any written request | Dismissed for not exhausting remedies; costs to the State | Withdraw, send a pre-petition letter, refile if still in time | Rule 1: ask first, in writing |
| Waiting for the ombudsman before petitioning | The 45-day clock runs out | Ask the court to extend the deadline, with reasons (reg. 3(ג)) | Put the court clock on the spine first |
| Asking the same question in two forums at once | Conflicting answers; one forum declines because another is handling it | Choose the forum that decides; use the other for records | Rule 4: one forum per question |
| A press release before the facts are confirmed | Defamation exposure; credibility lost | Correct publicly and fast (lesson 08.2) | Rule 5: communicate at milestones |
| HCJ petition in a First Schedule matter | Struck out for the wrong forum; fee wasted | Refile in the Administrative Court within time | Check the First Schedule (lesson 05.1) |
| No budget | A fee or certification blocks a step at the last moment | Use the fee-exemption route (11.6) | Budget before the first action |
| Collecting every tool "to be thorough" | Burnout and a diluted message | Cut tools that do not create a precondition | Every tool must serve the backwards map |

## Review

- The decision matrix compares every tool by forum, cost and clock, typical time, chance of its typical result and risk; numbers live on Current Numbers.
- Ask first in writing; get facts before arguments; soft before hard, inside the hard deadlines; one forum per question; communicate at milestones.
- The deadline spine places every hard deadline first, then fits the soft tools before them.
- A risk register and a budget come before the first real action.
- A good map says why it leaves tools out.
