# 20.3 · Running the Campaign: the Execution Log

This is where the capstone becomes real. You carry out your strategy map in your own name, and you record every step in an execution log on the day it happens: what you sent, to whom, how you can prove it, which clock it started, what came back and what you decided next. By the end of this lesson your log is set up, your first real action is in it, and you have a weekly routine for reviewing the campaign and changing course when the facts change.

## Why it matters

A campaign without a log loses in three ways. It misses deadlines, because nobody wrote down the day the 30-day FOI clock started. It loses arguments, because it cannot prove when the authority received the letter or that it never answered. And it loses its own lessons, because six months later nobody remembers why the plan changed.

The log is also the raw material of your case study. A seminar paper cites sources; your capstone cites exhibits. Every claim in your final write-up — "the ministry ignored us for 70 days", "the council voted after our question" — should point to a dated line in the log and a numbered document.

## The law

### Silence is an answer — if you can prove the dates

> (ב) לא השיב עובד הציבור או לא הודיע תוך שלושת החדשים כאמור בסעיף 2(א) או (ג), רואים בכך, לענין כל דין, החלטה לסרב לבקשה, ללא מתן נימוקים.

*Gist:* if an official does not answer a written request within the period the law sets, the silence counts, for every legal purpose, as a refusal without reasons. ([חוק לתיקון סדרי המינהל (החלטות והנמקות), s. 6(ב)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_לתיקון_סדרי_המינהל_%28החלטות_והנמקות%29#סעיף_6))

Section 6(א) adds that when no reasoned answer was given, the official must show in court that the decision was lawful. Both rules help you only if your log proves when the request arrived.

### The FOI clock

> (ב) הרשות הציבורית תודיע למבקש המידע ללא שיהוי, ולא יאוחר מ־30 ימים מקבלת הבקשה, על החלטתה בבקשתו; ראש הרשות הציבורית, או מי שהוא הסמיך לכך, רשאי להאריך את התקופה האמורה, במקרה הצורך, ב־30 ימים נוספים, ובלבד שהודיע על כך למבקש בכתב, ונימק את הצורך בהארכת התקופה.

*Gist:* the authority must decide on an FOI request without delay and within the set period from receiving it; an extension must be given in writing, with reasons. ([חוק חופש המידע, s. 7(ב)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_חופש_המידע#סעיף_7))

The log is where you see, on day 30, whether a written, reasoned extension arrived (lesson [05.3](../module-05/lesson-03.md)). The periods are on [Current Numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md).

### Your log holds other people's data

> (9) שימוש בידיעה על עניניו הפרטיים של אדם או מסירתה לאחר, שלא למטרה שלשמה נמסרה;

*Gist:* using or passing on information about a person's private affairs for a purpose other than the one it was given for is an invasion of privacy. ([חוק הגנת הפרטיות, s. 2(9)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הגנת_הפרטיות))

Supporters' contacts, residents' stories and officials' private details in your log were given for the campaign, not for anything else. Record only what you need, and protect the file (lessons [08.4](../module-08/lesson-04.md), [17.5](../module-17/lesson-05.md)).

## How it works in practice

> [!IMPORTANT]
> From this lesson on you take real actions. Act in your own name only, or in the name of a group that authorised you. This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing.

### What goes in the log

One line per event — something you did or something that happened to you.

| Field | Example |
|---|---|
| No. and date | 07 · 2027-01-12 |
| Event | FOI request sent to the municipality's FOI officer |
| Tool and lesson | FOI request, 05.2 |
| In whose name | Own name |
| Proof of sending or receipt | Online form confirmation no. 4471 (exhibit A07) |
| Clock started or stopped | FOI decision period starts on receipt |
| Next deadline | 2027-02-11: check for an answer or a written, reasoned extension |
| Result | — (pending) |
| Assumption tested | "The pool usage data exists" |
| Note | Fee paid online; receipt A08 |

Rules that make the log useful:

- **Same day.** Write the line the day it happens. A log reconstructed later is weaker evidence and usually wrong.
- **Proof for every "sent".** Registered mail receipt, form confirmation, e-mail with the full header, or a hand-delivery stamp. Without proof, an authority can say it never received the letter.
- **Every document numbered.** A01, A02 … in the order received or sent. File names start with the date: `2027-01-12_A07_FOI-request.pdf`. Keep the original of every digital file and never edit it; for photos and videos follow the chain-of-custody rules in lesson [17.4](../module-17/lesson-04.md).
- **Clocks in the calendar.** Every line that starts a clock creates a calendar reminder a few days before it ends.
- **Facts separate from opinions.** "The official did not answer" is a fact. "The official is hiding something" is an opinion; it belongs in your notes, not in a log you may one day attach to a petition.

### The weekly review

Once a week, 20–30 minutes, alone or with your group:

1. **What happened?** New lines since last week.
2. **Which clocks end in the next two weeks?** Act on them now.
3. **Which assumption was tested?** Mark it confirmed or false in your theory of change.
4. **Are the preconditions moving?** For each precondition, is it closer than last week?
5. **Decision:** continue, adjust or escalate. Write the decision in the log as its own line.
6. **Wellbeing check.** How many hours did the campaign take this week? Is that sustainable? (lesson 20.5)

### When to change the plan

| Event | What it usually means | Typical next step |
|---|---|---|
| A positive decision | The goal may be reached | Get it in writing; log how and when it will be carried out; check implementation |
| A reasoned refusal | A decision you can challenge | Analyse the reasons (Module 4); pre-petition letter or appeal inside the deadline |
| Silence past the legal period | A deemed refusal without reasons (s. 6(ב)) | Reminder; FOI Unit or ombudsman complaint; consider a pre-petition letter |
| A partial answer | Progress, and a narrower dispute | Accept what you got; target the rest precisely |
| New facts that undercut your case | An assumption was false | Revise the theory of change; it is a result, not a failure |
| A threat letter or a claim | A legal risk to you | Stop publishing on that point; follow lesson [08.3](../module-08/lesson-03.md) |
| Arrest, detention or a police summons | A criminal-law risk | Lesson [07.2](../module-07/lesson-02.md); lawyer or Public Defender first |
| The decision-maker changes (election, reshuffle) | Your preconditions may reset | Write again to the new holder of the power |

Elections matter now: elections to the 26th Knesset are set for 27 October 2026 (lesson [12.1](../module-12/lesson-01.md)). A new government or minister means new letters; a Knesset step may need a new MK.

### Working with others

- Log who decided what: a group's decision to authorise a step is itself a log line, with the date and how it was taken (lesson [18.1](../module-18/lesson-01.md)).
- Each person who signs a letter signs in their own name and keeps their own copy.
- Do not log other people's personal stories unless they agreed and the campaign needs them (lesson [17.2](../module-17/lesson-02.md)).

### Real or dry run

Each action in the log is marked **real** or **dry run**. The capstone needs at least three real actions in your own name. A step you decided not to take for real — a petition you drafted but did not file — stays in the log as a dry run, with the reason.

## Worked example

**Situation.** A group of religious women in a central Israeli town asks the municipality to add women-only hours at the municipal pool. Michal, a member, runs it as her capstone, in her own name and with the group's written decision. The opposing view, held by other residents, is that public pools should not be divided by sex; Michal records it in her theory of change.

Extract from Michal's log:

| No. | Date | Event | Tool | Proof | Clock / next deadline | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 01 | 2027-01-05 | Group decision: authorise the campaign; Michal signs letters in her own name | 18.1 | Minutes A01 | — | Done |
| 02 | 2027-01-07 | Formal letter to the director of the sports department asking for a decision on additional hours | 02.5 | Registered mail A02, receipt A03 | Duty to decide starts on receipt; reminder set | Pending |
| 03 | 2027-01-07 | FOI request: current timetable, usage figures, the legal opinion behind the current policy | 05.2 | Form confirmation A04, fee A05 | FOI period starts on receipt | Pending |
| 04 | 2027-02-08 | Day 30 check: no answer and no extension notice | 05.3 | — | Reminder sent today (A06) | Assumption "data exists" not yet tested |
| 05 | 2027-02-16 | FOI answer: timetable and usage released; legal opinion withheld as internal deliberation | 05.3 | Answer A07 | Petition clock on the withheld part starts | Partial |
| 06 | 2027-02-17 | Weekly review: usage data shows morning hours almost empty. Decision: adjust — ask for two morning slots rather than evening ones; do not challenge the withheld opinion yet | — | Notes A08 | — | Plan v2 |
| 07 | 2027-02-24 | Council member (opposition) submits a question to the mayor; a coalition member is also briefed | 12.5 | E-mails A09–A10 | Question at least 7 days before the meeting | Pending |
| 08 | 2027-03-04 | Mayor answers at the meeting: "the matter is being examined" | 12.5 | Protocol A11 | — | On record |
| 09 | 2027-04-10 | No decision on the formal letter after 3 months | 20.3 | — | Deemed refusal without reasons (s. 6(ב)); petition clock considered | Escalate |
| 10 | 2027-04-12 | Pre-petition letter to the director, copy to the legal adviser | 05.5 | Registered mail A12 | Short reply date set inside the petition clock | Pending |

**What the log shows.** The usage data (line 05) changed the request (line 06) — a cheaper ask that is easier to grant. The silence (line 09) became a legal fact because line 02 has a dated receipt.

**Mirror test.** The same log structure fits secular residents asking the same municipality to restore mixed hours, a disabled swimmers' group asking for a pool hoist, or Arab residents of a mixed city asking for opening hours that fit their neighbourhood's school day.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- Your strategy map from lesson 20.2.
- A folder (cloud or local, protected — lesson 17.5) and a spreadsheet or document for the log.
- About 75 minutes now, then 20–30 minutes a week for the rest of the campaign.

### Steps

1. Create the folder structure: `log`, `sent`, `received`, `evidence`, `notes`. Set file naming to `date_exhibit_description`.
2. Copy the log template and fill in line 01: the decision to start (and the group's decision, if any).
3. Enter every hard deadline from your deadline spine as a calendar reminder.
4. Take your first real action (usually the formal letter or the FOI request). Log it with the proof of sending.
5. Set a fixed weekly review slot and write the first review line.
6. Add a "Personal data" note to the log: what data you hold, why, who can see it, and when you will delete it.
7. Keep going: one line per event, every week a review line, until the campaign ends.

### Template

Use [Campaign execution log](../../templates/campaign-execution-log.md). It has the log table, the weekly review form, the change-of-plan table and the personal-data note. For each real action you may also fill in a Module 19 portfolio entry.

### How you know it worked

- Every "sent" line has a numbered proof.
- Every clock has a calendar reminder before it ends.
- Every weekly review ends in a decision: continue, adjust or escalate.
- Someone who was not involved could pick up the log and know the next deadline.

### Self-check

- [ ] Folder structure and file naming in place.
- [ ] Line 01 and the first real action logged with proof.
- [ ] Hard deadlines in the calendar.
- [ ] Weekly review slot fixed; first review written.
- [ ] Personal-data note written.
- [ ] Each action marked real or dry run.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sending a letter by ordinary e-mail with no confirmation | The authority says it never received it; no silence to rely on | Resend with proof today; log both | Proof for every "sent" |
| Writing the log from memory at the end | Wrong dates; a weak exhibit | Rebuild from documents and mark the reconstructed lines | Same-day lines |
| Missing day 30 of an FOI request | No reminder; the campaign stalls | Send the reminder and the FOI Unit complaint now | Calendar reminders for every clock |
| Opinions and insults in the log | Harmful if the log is attached to a filing or leaks | Move opinions to private notes | Facts only in the log |
| Never changing the plan | Tools that no longer fit; wasted months | Hold the review; write a "plan v2" line | Weekly review with a decision |
| Supporters' phone numbers in a shared, open spreadsheet | Privacy breach; harassment risk | Restrict access; remove what is not needed | Personal-data note; lesson 08.4 |
| Editing an original photo or document | Its value as evidence is lost | Keep the original; work on copies | Chain of custody (17.4) |

## Review

- The execution log records every event on the day it happens, with proof, an exhibit number, the clock it started and the next deadline.
- Silence after a written request becomes a deemed refusal without reasons (s. 6(ב)) — but only if you can prove the date of receipt.
- A weekly review tests assumptions and ends in a decision: continue, adjust or escalate.
- Refusals, silence, partial wins, threats and new facts each have a typical next step.
- The log holds other people's data: collect only what the campaign needs and protect it (Privacy Law s. 2(9)).
