# 20.4 · Media and Public Communication Within the Law

Public attention is often the precondition that makes a decision-maker act — and the moment a campaign is most exposed legally. In this lesson you learn to report your campaign's milestones in a press release, a post and an interview, sharply and lawfully: true facts from your log, opinions clearly framed as opinions, no one's privacy breached, no call to violence, and nothing that breaks a publication ban or tries to sway a pending criminal case. By the end you will have a checked press release for your own campaign, in Hebrew and, if you need it, in English.

## Why it matters

Journalists cover facts on the record, conflict and people. Your execution log is full of the first: an FOI answer, an official's silence, a council vote, a court decision. Turned into a short, accurate release, each of those can bring the coverage that makes refusal costly for the decision-maker.

The same release can also hand your opponent a lawsuit. The Defamation Law makes the person who brings a statement to the media liable alongside the editor. A quote given in anger, a photo with a child's face, a name under a court's gag order, or a post about a friend's pending criminal case can each turn a campaign into a defence. Modules 6 and 8 taught the rules one by one; this lesson applies them to the way campaigns actually talk.

## The law

### You are liable for what you bring to the media

> (א) פורסמה לשון הרע באמצעי תקשורת, ישאו באחריות פלילית ואזרחית בשל לשון הרע האדם שהביא את דבר לשון הרע לאמצעי התקשורת וגרם בכך לפרסומו, עורך אמצעי התקשורת ומי שהחליט בפועל על הפרסום, ובאחריות אזרחית ישא גם האחראי לאמצעי התקשורת.

*Gist:* when defamation is published in the media, the person who brought it to the media is liable together with the editor and whoever decided to publish it. ([חוק איסור לשון הרע, s. 11(א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_איסור_לשון_הרע))

Section 2 defines publication broadly — spoken, written, drawn or by "any other means" — so a post, a sign, a video and an interview answer are all publications. The defences are in lesson [08.2](../module-08/lesson-02.md).

### Publication bans

> (א) לא יפרסם אדם דבר על דיון שהתנהל בבית משפט בדלתיים סגורות אלא ברשות בית המשפט.

*Gist:* nothing may be published about a hearing held behind closed doors without the court's permission. ([חוק בתי המשפט, s. 70(א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_בתי_המשפט))

> (ד) בית משפט רשאי לאסור כל פרסום בקשר לדיוני בית המשפט, במידה שהוא רואה צורך בכך לשם הגנה על בטחונו של בעל דין, עד או אדם אחר ששמו הוזכר בדיון או לשם מניעת פגיעה חמורה בפרטיות של אחד מהם

*Gist:* a court may ban any publication about its proceedings to protect a party's or witness's safety or to prevent a serious invasion of privacy. (s. 70(ד))

> (ד1)(1) לא יפרסם אדם שמו של חשוד שטרם הוגש נגדו כתב אישום, עד תום 48 שעות מהמועד שבו התייצב החשוד לחקירה או שהיה עליו להתייצב לחקירה, או עד סיומו של הדיון הראשון שהתקיים לפני שופט בעניינו, לפי המוקדם מביניהם

*Gist:* the name of a suspect who has not been charged may not be published for a short period after the interrogation begins, or until the first hearing before a judge if that comes first — even if the suspect is your fellow activist. (s. 70(ד1)(1); the exceptions in s. 70(ד1)(2) include the suspect's own written consent)

Section 70 also bars photographing in a courtroom without permission (s. 70(ב)), publishing about certain minors' cases (s. 70(ג)), and lets a court ban a suspect's name or details of an investigation (s. 70(ה), (ה1)). Breaching s. 70 is an offence (s. 70(ו)).

### Sub judice: do not try to sway a pending criminal case

> (א) לא יפרסם אדם דבר על ענין פלילי התלוי ועומד בבית משפט במטרה להשפיע על מהלך המשפט או על תוצאותיו, וראייה מראש את ההשפעה האמורה כאפשרות קרובה לודאי כמוה כמטרה להשפיע, והכל אם יש בפרסום כדי להשפיע כאמור.

*Gist:* do not publish anything about a pending criminal case with the aim — or the near-certain foresight — of influencing its course or outcome, if it is capable of doing so. ([s. 71(א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_בתי_המשפט))

> (ג) איסור הפרסום אינו חל על פרסום ידיעה בתום לב על דבר שנאמר או שאירע בישיבה פומבית של בית משפט.

*Gist:* a good-faith report of what was said or happened in an open court session is allowed. (s. 71(ג))

The case is "pending" from the moment a request for a remand order or an indictment is filed, until the prosecutor decides not to charge or the proceedings end (s. 71(ב)).

### Privacy and incitement

Publishing a person's private life, health or behaviour in a private place, or using information for a purpose other than the one it was given for, is an invasion of privacy ([חוק הגנת הפרטיות, s. 2](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הגנת_הפרטיות); lesson [08.4](../module-08/lesson-04.md)). Calls for violence and praise of violence or terror that are likely to lead to it are crimes, whoever the target ([חוק העונשין, s. 144ד2](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_העונשין#סעיף_144ד2); lesson [06.5](../module-06/lesson-05.md)).

## How it works in practice

### Communicate at milestones

A release needs news. Good milestones from your log:

- an FOI answer with figures nobody has published;
- the end of a legal period with no answer (a deemed refusal);
- an oversight body's finding;
- a council or committee session where your issue was discussed;
- a filing or a court decision;
- a decision in your favour — and whether it is carried out.

"We are still campaigning" is not news. Hold your fire for the next milestone.

### The press release

One page. Hebrew for Israeli media; English for international outlets and UN-related steps.

1. **Headline:** the fact, not the adjective. "Municipality did not answer residents for 3 months on pool hours" beats "Scandal at the pool".
2. **Lead (first paragraph):** who, what, when, where, why it matters — in two sentences.
3. **The request:** one decision, by one decision-maker, by a date.
4. **A quote:** one or two sentences in your own name, clearly your opinion.
5. **Facts with sources:** each figure points to a document you can share (the FOI answer, the protocol, the court decision).
6. **The other side:** what the authority said, or "the municipality was asked for comment on [date]".
7. **Contact and documents:** your name, phone, and a link to the documents you are willing to publish.

Before it goes out, run the pre-publication check below.

### The pre-publication check (seven questions)

| Question | If yes |
|---|---|
| 1. Does it state a **fact** about an identifiable person or business? | Can you prove it with a document? If not, cut it or frame it as an opinion based on stated facts (08.2) |
| 2. Does it attach a **crime or dishonesty** to a named person? | Remove it unless a court or a public body has found it; refer to the decision, not your conclusion |
| 3. Does it show or name a **private person**, a **minor**, health, or a private place? | Blur, anonymise or get written consent (08.4) |
| 4. Is anyone mentioned a **suspect** in a criminal investigation, or is a **criminal case pending**? | Check s. 70(ד1) timing and s. 71: report only what was said in open court; do not argue the case |
| 5. Is there a **court order** or a **closed hearing** involved? | Ask the court secretariat or the parties' lawyer whether a ban exists; do not publish until you know |
| 6. Could any phrase be read as a **call to violence** or praise of it? | Rewrite (06.5) |
| 7. Did the person or body you criticise get a **chance to reply**? | Ask for comment with a reasonable deadline and quote the answer |

### Talking to journalists

- **Agree the terms before you talk.** "On the record", "not for attribution" and "off the record" are agreements with the journalist, not legal protections. If you do not want to read it, do not say it.
- **Three messages.** Prepare three short sentences: the fact, the request, why it matters to people. Return to them.
- **Do not speculate.** "I don't know; I'll check and come back to you" is a good answer.
- **Send documents, not adjectives.** A journalist who can see the FOI answer writes a stronger story than one who heard your description of it.
- **Speak for yourself.** If your group has a spokesperson (lesson [18.1](../module-18/lesson-01.md)), refer journalists there; do not speak for other people's personal cases.
- **Mistakes in coverage.** Ask the journalist for a correction in writing, politely, with the document. Keep a copy in your log.

### Social media

- You are the publisher of what you post, share and pin; as a group admin you may be responsible for what stays up (lesson [06.5](../module-06/lesson-05.md)).
- Screenshots of other people's private messages and photos of private people need the care in lesson 08.4.
- Do not photograph, film or stream inside a courtroom, or publish such footage, without the court's permission (s. 70(ב)).
- Delete a post that crossed a line quickly, and correct it publicly; that helps a good-faith defence (lesson 08.2).
- If you are attacked online, follow lesson [08.5](../module-08/lesson-05.md), not your instincts.

### English for international audiences

When your campaign has an international step (a shadow report, a Special Procedures communication), an English release reports the step and the facts behind it. The same rules apply: Israeli defamation and privacy law cover what you publish from Israel, and UN bodies publish submissions and communications unless you ask otherwise (lessons [14.2](../module-14/lesson-02.md), [14.4](../module-14/lesson-04.md)). Describe the Israeli government's position fairly and attribute it.

## Worked example

**Situation.** A group of reservists' spouses campaigns for the Ministry of Defence to extend a childcare grant to self-employed spouses. Their spokesperson, Efrat, uses the campaign as her capstone. Two milestones arrive in the same week.

1. **Milestone: an FOI answer.** The ministry's answer (exhibit A14) shows how many applications were refused because the spouse was self-employed. Efrat drafts a release:
   - Headline: "Ministry data: hundreds of reservists' spouses refused childcare grant because they are self-employed."
   - Lead: who, what, the FOI answer's date.
   - Request: "We ask the Director General to decide by 1 March whether to extend the criteria."
   - Quote, in her name, as opinion: "The criteria punish families for how they earn a living."
   - Facts: the figures, with a link to the FOI answer.
   - Other side: "The ministry was asked for comment on [date]."
2. **Check.** Question 2 flags a draft line: "the official who wrote these criteria deliberately discriminates". She cannot prove intent; she rewrites it as an opinion based on the stated facts, about the criteria, not the person.
3. **Milestone: an arrest.** At a small protest outside the ministry, another member, Yoav, is detained and the police ask for a remand order. The group wants to post "Yoav is innocent and the police are lying".
   - Question 4 applies twice. For a short time after the interrogation begins, his name cannot be published without his written consent (s. 70(ד1)); Yoav, through his lawyer, consents in writing.
   - The remand request makes the case "pending" (s. 71(ב)). A post that argues the case to influence the judge risks s. 71. The group instead reports, in good faith, what was said at the open remand hearing (s. 71(ג)), and tells its own campaign story without arguing the criminal case.
4. **Interview.** A radio reporter calls. Efrat agrees "on the record", gives her three messages, refers questions about Yoav's case to his lawyer, and sends the FOI answer by e-mail.

**Mirror test.** The same checks apply to a Bedouin parents' committee publishing FOI data on school transport, to activists against the judicial reform after a protest arrest, and to a settlers' group publishing a court decision on a demolition order.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- Your execution log with at least one milestone (or the next milestone you expect).
- The [pre-publication checklist](../../templates/pre-publication-checklist.md) from Module 8.
- About 90 minutes.

### Steps

1. Pick the milestone from your log that is most newsworthy. Write the one fact and the one request.
2. Draft the press release in Hebrew with the template, with every figure pointing to an exhibit.
3. Run the seven-question check. Mark every sentence you changed and why.
4. Ask the decision-maker for comment in writing, with a reasonable deadline, and log it.
5. Write three interview messages and the answer you will give to the hardest question.
6. Write one social media post (short) from the same release and check it again.
7. If your campaign has an international step, write a short English version.
8. Decide, and log, whether to send it now or wait for a better milestone.

### Template

Use [Press release](../../templates/press-release.md) (Hebrew and English versions) and the [pre-publication checklist](../../templates/pre-publication-checklist.md).

### How you know it worked

- Every factual sentence about a person or body points to a document you can share.
- The release asks one decision-maker for one decision by a date.
- The seven-question check is written next to the release, with the changes it caused.
- The other side was asked for comment and the request is in your log.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| A factual accusation you cannot prove, given to a journalist | You are liable with the editor (s. 11) | Correct it at once, in writing; follow 08.3 if a letter arrives | Question 1 of the check |
| Naming a fellow activist the day they were taken for questioning | A breach of s. 70(ד1) unless an exception applies | Remove it; ask the suspect's lawyer before republishing | Question 4; written consent from the suspect |
| Posting "the judge must release him" campaigns during a remand hearing | Possible sub judice offence (s. 71) and harm to the case | Remove; report only what was said in open court | Leave the criminal case to the lawyer |
| A photo with children's faces in a release | Privacy claim; harm to the children | Replace with a blurred version; apologise to parents | Question 3; consent forms (17.2) |
| A release with no news | Ignored; journalists stop opening your e-mails | Wait for a milestone | Communicate at milestones |
| No request in the release | Coverage without pressure on anyone | Add the decision-maker, the decision and the date | Lead with the request |
| Leaking a document under a court ban | Criminal offence; contempt | Remove; get legal advice | Question 5: check for bans first |
| Angry live reply to an attacker | Escalation; defamation risk both ways | Stop, preserve evidence, follow 08.5 | A rule: no replies in the first hour |

## Review

- Communicate at milestones: report a fact on the record, ask one decision-maker for one decision by a date.
- The person who brings defamation to the media is liable with the editor (Defamation Law s. 11); every fact needs a document.
- Courts Law s. 70 bans publishing closed hearings, courtroom photos, certain minors' cases, court-ordered bans and, for a short period, uncharged suspects' names; s. 71 bans trying to sway a pending criminal case, but allows good-faith reports of open court.
- Privacy and incitement limits apply to every post, sign and interview (08.4, 06.5).
- Agree interview terms first, send documents, give the other side a chance to reply, and correct mistakes fast.
