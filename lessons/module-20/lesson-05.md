# 20.5 · Retrospective and Handing Over

Every campaign ends — with a win, a partial result, a defeat, or simply when you run out of time. In this last lesson you write the retrospective that turns your capstone into a case study, hand the campaign over so that it can continue without you and without a missed deadline, and look after the person who ran it. You finish with a pointer to free university-level learning if you want to go further.

## Why it matters

Campaigns are often lost after they are won: the municipality promises, the activist moves on, and nobody checks that the promise is carried out. Others collapse because the one person who knew the deadlines and the passwords burns out. And most campaigns never record what they learned, so the next group repeats the same mistakes.

A retrospective is also what makes the capstone a piece of learning rather than a story. A seminar paper is judged on its argument; your case study is judged on how honestly you compare what you planned with what happened, and why.

## The law

### Data you hand over stays bound to its purpose

> (9) שימוש בידיעה על עניניו הפרטיים של אדם או מסירתה לאחר, שלא למטרה שלשמה נמסרה;

*Gist:* passing a person's information to someone else for a purpose other than the one it was given for is an invasion of privacy. ([חוק הגנת הפרטיות, s. 2(9)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הגנת_הפרטיות))

> (א) בעל שליטה במאגר מידע ומחזיק במאגר מידע, כל אחד מהם אחראי לאבטחת המידע שבמאגר המידע.

*Gist:* whoever controls a database of personal information, and whoever holds it, are each responsible for its security. (s. 17(א))

A supporters' list handed to a new coordinator for the same campaign is fine; the same list handed to a party, a business or a different campaign is not, without consent. When a campaign closes, delete or anonymise what you no longer need (lesson [08.4](../module-08/lesson-04.md)).

### A filing in your name stays yours

> הפעולות המנויות להלן, לא יעשה אותן דרך עיסוק, או בתמורה אף שלא דרך עיסוק, אלא עורך דין; ואלה הפעולות:
> (1) ייצוג אדם אחר וכל טיעון ופעולה אחרת בשמו לפני בתי משפט, בתי דין, בוררים וגופים ואנשים בעלי סמכות שיפוטית או מעין שיפוטית;

*Gist:* representing another person before a court or tribunal, as an occupation or for payment, is reserved to lawyers. ([חוק לשכת עורכי הדין, s. 20](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_לשכת_עורכי_הדין))

You cannot "hand over" a petition, a complaint or an objection you filed in your own name so that someone else argues it for you. You remain the party, or you withdraw; the new coordinator can file their own steps in their own name, or a group can act in its own name (lesson [18.4](../module-18/lesson-04.md)). Your FOI answers and official replies will keep arriving at your address; plan who reads them.

## How it works in practice

### The retrospective

Write it within two weeks of the end, while you remember. Use your execution log as the source for every claim.

1. **Outcome against goal.** What decision did you get, from whom, when? Full, partial, none, or a reasoned refusal? Has it been carried out?
2. **Timeline.** The ten most important log lines, with exhibit numbers.
3. **Theory of change, tested.** For each precondition: was it created, and did it matter? For each assumption: confirmed or false, and when did you learn it?
4. **Tool by tool.** For each tool: what it produced, what it cost (hours, money), and whether you would use it again, earlier, later or not at all. Compare with the "chance" you expected in lesson 20.2.
5. **The other side.** What did the decision-maker and your opponents do, and why — in their terms, attributed?
6. **Legal lessons.** Deadlines, forums, evidence, communication: what did you get right and wrong?
7. **Unintended effects.** Good and bad — on people involved, on the issue, on your relationships, on your safety.
8. **What you would do differently,** in three sentences.

Write it plainly. "The FOI request took 74 days and produced the figure that made the council act" is better than "the campaign was a great success".

### Holding a group debrief

If you worked with others, hold a debrief meeting soon after the end or after any hard event:

- Start with how people are, and anything urgent.
- Review safety: any threats, any legal exposure still open.
- What went well, what to improve — facts first, no blame.
- Decide the next steps before people leave.

### The hand-over pack

A campaign that continues needs a pack that a new person can use in an hour.

| Part | Contents |
|---|---|
| 1. Status page | Goal, current state, the next three actions, in one page |
| 2. Open clocks | Every running deadline, with its legal source and date (see [Current Numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)) |
| 3. The log and exhibits | The execution log and the numbered documents |
| 4. Filings in your name | What you filed, its status, and what you will still do as the party (or your decision to withdraw) |
| 5. Contacts | Officials, journalists, council members, allies — only those who agreed to be passed on |
| 6. Accounts and assets | Social media pages, e-mail, shared drives, domain, money: who owns each and how ownership moves |
| 7. Personal data | What is handed over, for which purpose, and what is deleted (s. 2(9), s. 17) |
| 8. Risks | Open threats, demand letters, pending cases, safety concerns |
| 9. Lessons | The retrospective, or its summary |

Hand-over rules:

- **Brief in person** (or by video), with the pack open, and record the date as a log line.
- **Move ownership, not just passwords.** Add the new person as an administrator, then remove yourself. Change shared passwords after the hand-over (lesson [17.5](../module-17/lesson-05.md)).
- **The new person acts in their own name.** They send new letters and file new steps as themselves, or the group acts in its own name.
- **Implementation watch.** If you won, the most important job left is checking that the decision is carried out. Put the dates in the pack.

### Closing a campaign

When nobody will continue: send a short final update to supporters, thank the people who helped, publish what you are allowed to publish (lesson 20.4), archive the log and exhibits, and delete or anonymise personal data according to your personal-data note.

### Burnout and safety

> [!WARNING]
> Campaigns expose people to harassment, threats and legal pressure, and to slow exhaustion. If a threat names your home, your family or a time and place, treat it as a physical safety issue first: call the police (100) and tell people you trust (lesson [08.5](../module-08/lesson-05.md)). If you feel overwhelmed, free emotional support is available — ERAN on 1201, and the other lines in [Where to Get Help](../../references/where-to-get-help.md).

Signs of burnout to watch for in yourself and others: the campaign takes over sleep and weekends; every setback feels personal; irritability with allies; cynicism about the cause; physical exhaustion. Stress and fatigue also distort how you judge risk — you may overreact to small threats or miss real ones.

What helps:

- **An hours budget.** Decide the weekly hours at the start (lesson 20.1, filter 6) and record actual hours in the weekly review.
- **Shared roles.** No single person holds all the deadlines, passwords and contacts. The hand-over pack is also a safety net.
- **Breaks after milestones.** Plan rest after a hearing or a big publication.
- **A safety review** at the end: Who has your address? Which accounts need new passwords? Any demand letter still open? Any pending criminal case from a protest?
- **Permission to stop.** A retrospective of a campaign you ended early, honestly explained, is a valid capstone.

## Worked example

**Situation.** Ronen, a wheelchair user in a coastal city, ran his capstone on an accessible path to the municipal beach. Goal: "By 1 June 2027, the municipality decides to build an accessible path to the main beach, or gives a reasoned refusal." He used a formal letter, an FOI request, a query to the disability Commission (lesson [09.4](../module-09/lesson-04.md)), a council question and a press release.

1. **Outcome.** In April the municipality decided to build the path — but at a smaller beach, not the main one. Partial result.
2. **Assumptions tested.** "The main beach falls under the accessibility regulations" — confirmed by the Commission's answer. "The council member's question will push a decision" — partly: the mayor promised a plan, but the decision followed the press coverage of the Commission's answer two weeks later.
3. **Tools judged.** FOI: slow (two reminders) but produced the budget line that made the request concrete. Press release: the most effective step, because it reported an official finding, not an opinion. Council question: useful on the record, weak as pressure alone.
4. **Hand-over.** A parents' group of children with disabilities takes over to push for the main beach. Ronen's pack lists the open clock on his Commission query, the implementation dates for the smaller path, and the contacts who agreed to be passed on. The group sends its own letter in its own name; Ronen keeps receiving the Commission's replies to his query and forwards them to the group.
5. **Personal data.** The supporters' list stays with Ronen and is deleted after he sends a final update with a link to the group's page, so supporters can join it themselves.
6. **Wellbeing.** His weekly review showed 12-hour weeks in March. He writes that he would have shared the media work earlier.

**Mirror test.** The same retrospective and hand-over work for a settlement's road-lighting campaign moving from one resident to the local committee, for an LGBTQ community centre's funding campaign passed to a new board, or for a Bedouin school-transport campaign passed from a parent to the village's education committee.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- Your theory of change, strategy map and execution log.
- About 2 hours, within two weeks of the end of the campaign (or of the capstone period).

### Steps

1. Write the outcome against the goal in three sentences, citing exhibits.
2. Fill in the assumption table and the tool-by-tool table.
3. Write the other side's account, attributed.
4. Write the legal lessons and the unintended effects.
5. Write "what I would do differently" in three sentences.
6. Prepare the hand-over pack, or the closing plan if nobody will continue.
7. Do the safety review and the personal-data clean-up; record both in the log.
8. If you worked with a group, hold the debrief and record its decisions.

### Template

Use [Campaign retrospective](../../templates/campaign-retrospective.md). It has the retrospective sections, the hand-over pack checklist, the closing checklist and the safety review.

### How you know it worked

- Every claim in the retrospective points to a log line or an exhibit.
- Someone new could continue the campaign from the pack without calling you, and no clock is missing.
- Personal data is either handed over for the same purpose or deleted, and the log says which.
- You have written down your hours and what you would change for your own wellbeing.

### Self-check

- [ ] Outcome against goal, with exhibits.
- [ ] Assumptions and tools judged.
- [ ] The other side's account, attributed.
- [ ] Hand-over pack (or closing plan) complete.
- [ ] Filings in your name accounted for.
- [ ] Safety review and personal-data clean-up logged.
- [ ] Debrief held if you worked with others.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Declaring victory at the promise | The decision is never carried out | Write to the decision-maker asking for the timetable; add monitoring to the pack | Implementation watch dates in the pack |
| Retrospective written from memory | Flattering, wrong, useless to others | Rebuild it from the log and exhibits | Cite a log line for every claim |
| Handing over passwords but not ownership | The new person is locked out when you leave; a security hole | Move admin rights and change passwords | Ownership row in the pack |
| Passing the supporters' list to another cause | Privacy breach (s. 2(9)); trust lost | Stop the use; inform the people affected | Personal-data row in the pack |
| The new coordinator "continues" your petition in your name | Acting for another person; the court will not hear them for you | You stay the party or withdraw; they file in their own name | "Filings in your name" row in the pack |
| One person holds every deadline | Burnout; collapse if they stop | Share roles now | Shared roles from the start |
| Ignoring a threat because "it's just online" | Risk to physical safety | Preserve, report, call 100 if specific (08.5) | Safety review at every milestone |

## Review

- A retrospective compares the outcome with the goal, tests each assumption and judges each tool, citing the log for every claim.
- A hand-over pack has the status, open clocks, log, filings, contacts, accounts, personal data, risks and lessons; it moves ownership, not just passwords.
- Filings in your name stay yours; the new coordinator acts in their own name (s. 20).
- Personal data handed over stays bound to its original purpose and must be kept secure (Privacy Law ss. 2(9), 17).
- Plan for burnout and safety from the start: an hours budget, shared roles, rest after milestones, and a safety review at the end.
