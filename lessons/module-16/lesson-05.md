# 16.5 · Sending Information to International Bodies

By the end of this lesson you will be able to choose where information about an alleged international crime should go, prepare a communication to the ICC Prosecutor under Article 15 of the Rome Statute in a dry run, and protect the people and the evidence involved before anything is sent.

> [!NOTE]
> Communications about the Situation in the State of Palestine concern an active investigation. The course takes no position on the proceedings or on Israel's objection to the Court's jurisdiction. The method in this lesson is the same whichever side's conduct the information concerns.

> [!WARNING]
> Information you send can expose witnesses, victims and you. Do not collect testimony in a dangerous place; this course teaches remote and open-source work only. Get informed consent, store files encrypted, and never send originals you cannot replace. Sending false or misleading information is not a harmless mistake: it can hurt real people and your credibility.

## Why it matters

Anyone can send information to the ICC Prosecutor: there is no fee, no lawyer, no standing requirement and no form. Both sides of the conflict have done so. In February 2024 the Hostage and Missing Families Forum filed a submission at the ICC against Hamas leaders over killings, abductions and sexual violence on 7 October; Palestinian human-rights NGOs have long supplied information about Israeli conduct, which the United States cited when it sanctioned three of them in September 2025. A communication only helps if it is specific, sourced and safe, and if it goes to the right body. Many people send it to the wrong place, or send general outrage that the Office cannot use.

## The law

> 1. The Prosecutor may initiate investigations proprio motu on the basis of information on crimes within the jurisdiction of the Court.
> 2. The Prosecutor shall analyse the seriousness of the information received. For this purpose, he or she may seek additional information from States, organs of the United Nations, intergovernmental or non-governmental organizations, or other reliable sources that he or she deems appropriate, and may receive written or oral testimony at the seat of the Court.

*Gist:* the Prosecutor acts on information from any source and must analyse how serious it is; the Office may come back to you for more. ([Rome Statute, art. 15(1)–(2)](https://legal.un.org/icc/statute/english/rome_statute%28e%29.pdf))

> 6. If, after the preliminary examination referred to in paragraphs 1 and 2, the Prosecutor concludes that the information provided does not constitute a reasonable basis for an investigation, he or she shall inform those who provided the information. This shall not preclude the Prosecutor from considering further information submitted to him or her regarding the same situation in the light of new facts or evidence.

*Gist:* if the Office decides there is no basis to investigate, it tells the senders, and new information can reopen the question. (art. 15(6))

> 88. While all senders of communications are provided an acknowledgment of receipt, the Office refrains from disclosing their identity to the public.

*Gist:* every sender gets an acknowledgement, and the Office does not publicise who sent information. ([OTP Policy Paper on Preliminary Examinations, 2013, para. 88](https://www.legal-tools.org/doc/acb906/pdf/))

## How it works in practice

### Where should the information go?

| Your goal | Body | Lesson |
|---|---|---|
| A criminal investigation in Israel of a specific incident | Israel Police or the MAG | 16.3 |
| Prosecution of individuals for core crimes, where the ICC has a situation | ICC Office of the Prosecutor (Article 15) | this lesson |
| A UN letter to the Government about a specific, recent case | UN Special Procedures | 14.4 |
| Input to a UN report on a pattern | Commission of inquiry call for submissions | 14.5 |
| A case against a suspect with a link to another country | That country's prosecutor | 16.4 |

These are not exclusive. Most serious files go to the domestic channel first, then to one or more international bodies, with each one told what the others received.

### Sending to the ICC Prosecutor

- **Online: OTPLink** at <https://otplink.icc-cpi.int/>, launched on 24 May 2023 as the single entry point for Article 15 communications and for information on situations already under investigation. You can describe the incident, mark its location on a map, upload files and choose whether to give your name or remain anonymous. File limits are in [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md). The portal needs an up-to-date browser and may block some VPNs or old systems.
- **By post:** Evidence and Discovery Management Unit (EDMU), Office of the Prosecutor, Post Office Box 19519, 2500 CM The Hague, The Netherlands (address as given by the Coalition for the ICC; confirm on the ICC contact page before posting).
- **Language:** English or French are best; Arabic, Chinese, Russian and Spanish are official languages of the Court. Hebrew documents should be accompanied by an English summary.
- **Format:** the Statute sets no format. The more specific and detailed, the more useful (Coalition for the ICC).
- **No legal advice:** the Office "cannot provide legal advice under any circumstances".

### What happens after you send

1. **Acknowledgement** of receipt (OTP policy, para. 88). It does not mean anything will be done.
2. **Filtering ("Phase 1").** The Office separates information that is manifestly outside the Court's jurisdiction, information about a situation already under preliminary examination, and information about a situation already under investigation (para. 78). Information about Gaza and the West Bank since 13 June 2014 belongs to the investigation opened in 2021 (lesson 16.2).
3. **Use.** It may be analysed with other sources, the Office may ask you for more, or nothing visible may happen. You will usually not be told how it was used.
4. **Confidentiality and disclosure.** The Office protects the confidentiality of information and senders, but if a case goes to trial, material may have to be disclosed to the defence; when it seeks more information the Office "confers" with providers about possible use and disclosure (para. 88).
5. **No reply on the merits** unless the Office concludes there is no reasonable basis in a preliminary examination (art. 15(6)).

### What makes a communication useful

- **Specific incidents:** date, time, exact place (coordinates if possible), what happened, who was harmed, who is alleged to be responsible (unit, rank, organisation), how you know.
- **The crime and its elements** (lesson 16.1): the article of the Statute and the facts for each element, including context or intent.
- **Domestic proceedings** (lesson 16.3): complaints filed, case numbers, outcomes. This speaks directly to complementarity.
- **Evidence handling:** originals preserved; a list of files with their hash values; how and when each was obtained (Module 17).
- **Security:** who might be at risk if identified, and whether they consent to being contacted.

### Positions you should know

- **Israel** rejects the Court's jurisdiction and regards the proceedings as "politically motivated and legally baseless" ([MFA ICC page](https://israelihl.mfa.gov.il/icc)). That does not prevent anyone, including Israelis, from sending information about any party.
- **The United States**, under Executive Order 14203, has designated persons who assist ICC actions against Israel, including three Palestinian NGOs on 4 September 2025, which the State Department said had "directly engaged in efforts by the ICC to investigate" Israeli nationals. US lawsuits challenge the order (Just Security, 2026). If you are a US citizen or resident, the sanctions may affect your dealings with designated persons; get advice from a US sanctions lawyer before acting.
- **The ICC Office of the Prosecutor** invites information from all sources and gives no legal advice.

## Worked example

**Situation.** Two dry-run files are prepared in a university clinic.

**File A — the Ben-Ami family.** Their parents were killed and their brother abducted from a kibbutz on 7 October 2023. They have: the police and army notifications, video published by the attackers (archived with dates and hashes), testimony of a neighbour (with consent), and the brother's account after his release.

1. **Route.** Domestic: the Israeli investigation and the 2026 law (lesson 16.4). International: an Article 15 communication to the OTP, because the situation covers Hamas conduct and the families want the Court's investigation to include their case.
2. **Crimes and elements.** Murder and hostage-taking as war crimes (art. 8(2)(a)(i), (viii)); murder and imprisonment as crimes against humanity, with facts on the coordinated attack on many communities (art. 7(1)(a), (e)).
3. **Domestic steps.** Police file numbers; whether anyone has been indicted under the 2026 law.
4. **Safety and consent.** The brother consents to be contacted; the neighbour does not, so she is described but not named.

**File B — the Abu-Saleh family.** Their house in Khan Yunis was destroyed and two children were killed in a strike in 2024. They have: photos with metadata, a local hospital record, satellite images from a public provider, and a letter their Israeli lawyer sent to the MAG with its reference number and the reply.

1. **Route.** Domestic: the MAG complaint already filed (lesson 16.3). International: an Article 15 communication; also, possibly, a Special Procedures communication (lesson 14.4).
2. **Crimes and elements.** Directing attacks against civilians (art. 8(2)(b)(i)) needs intent; excessive incidental harm (art. 8(2)(b)(iv)) needs facts on the expected military advantage. The file states what is known and what is not, and notes the IDF's published response, if any.
3. **Domestic steps.** The MAG's reply is attached, which is exactly what the OTP needs to assess complementarity.
4. **Safety and consent.** The father consents to his name being used; the file says the family is in Gaza and asks that contact go through the lawyer.

**Mirror test.** Both files use the same template, the same evidence standard and the same neutral language. Neither calls the other side's conduct anything the facts cannot show.

## Do it yourself

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Act in your own name only (or for a person who asked you to). This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing. The default here is a dry run: do not submit to the ICC unless the information is real, first-hand or properly sourced, and every person named has consented.

### You need

- A real incident you know first-hand, or a realistic invented one.
- The [Article 15 communication template](../../templates/article-15-communication.md).
- Your notes from lessons 16.1 (crime elements) and 16.3 (domestic steps).
- A way to compute file hashes (Module 17 shows how; on Windows `Get-FileHash`, on macOS and Linux `shasum -a 256`).
- 100 minutes.

### Steps

1. Decide the route using the table above, and write one sentence on why the ICC is (or is not) the right body.
2. Fill Part A of the template: is it a Rome Statute crime, is it within the situation's territory and dates, what has been done domestically?
3. Write the incident summary (five lines) and the detailed facts in date order, each with its source.
4. Map each fact to the elements of one crime (lesson 16.1).
5. List the evidence files with their hash values and how each was obtained. Do not edit the originals.
6. Fill the consent record for every person named, and the security note.
7. Read your draft as the OTP would: is any sentence an opinion rather than a fact? Rewrite it.
8. Stop. If and only if the case is real and consented, plan the submission (OTPLink or post) and the follow-up log.

### Template

[article-15-communication.md](../../templates/article-15-communication.md) — an English template in the order an OTP analyst reads a file: summary, incidents, crimes and elements, alleged perpetrators, domestic proceedings, evidence list, security and consent, follow-up log.

### How you know it worked

- An analyst who reads only your summary knows what, where, when, who, and which crime.
- Every fact has a date and a source; the evidence list has hashes.
- Domestic steps are listed with reference numbers, or the reason there are none is explained.
- Every named person has a consent record.

### Self-check

- [ ] Route chosen with a reason; domestic channel used or explained.
- [ ] Crime named with its Statute article and elements.
- [ ] Facts separated from allegations and opinions.
- [ ] Evidence list with hashes; originals untouched.
- [ ] Consent and security recorded; no one named without consent.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sending general statements or news links only | Filtered out as not useful | Add specific incidents with first-hand or documented sources | Use the incident table in the template |
| Naming witnesses without consent | Risk to them; loss of trust | Inform the OTP at once and ask that the name be protected | Consent record before drafting |
| Editing or compressing the original files | Authenticity questioned | Send the original copies and their hashes | Hash on collection; work on copies |
| Sending only to the ICC and skipping the domestic channel | Weak on complementarity; no local record | File the domestic complaint and add its number | Start with lesson 16.3 |
| Expecting a reply or a decision about your case | Frustration; repeated duplicate submissions | Send only genuinely new information, referring to your first submission | Know that only an acknowledgement is promised |
| Ignoring sanctions exposure as a US person | Possible legal risk | Get advice from a US sanctions lawyer | Check your status before engaging |

## Review

- Anyone may send information to the ICC Prosecutor under art. 15; there is no format, fee or lawyer requirement.
- OTPLink is the online channel; the postal route goes to the OTP's evidence unit in The Hague.
- Senders get an acknowledgement; the Office filters information, may ask for more, protects senders' identity and may have to disclose material at trial.
- A useful communication names incidents, crimes and elements, domestic steps, evidence with hashes, and consent.
- Choose the body by the goal: Israeli authorities, the ICC, UN mechanisms or a foreign prosecutor, often several in sequence.
