# 16.4 · Universal Jurisdiction

By the end of this lesson you will be able to tell on what basis a foreign or Israeli court claims to try a crime committed elsewhere, describe the proceedings in Europe that concern Israelis and those that concern Hamas accurately and neutrally, and find the Israeli laws that let Israeli courts try international crimes committed abroad.

> [!NOTE]
> This lesson describes pending complaints, investigations and a 2026 Israeli law whose use is still beginning. The course takes no position on any of them. Each step is attributed and dated; check the current status before you write about a case.

## Why it matters

Since 2023, complaints about alleged crimes in Israel and Gaza have been filed with prosecutors in Belgium, France, Germany and elsewhere: some against Israeli soldiers and dual nationals, some against Hamas members over 7 October. Israel, for its part, passed a law in May 2026 to try the 7 October attackers, relying in part on its own 1950 Genocide Law. "Universal jurisdiction" is often used loosely for all of this, but most of these cases rest on other grounds: the nationality of the suspect or of the victims. The ground matters, because it decides which country can act, what the complainant must show, and how the suspect can respond. Activists on every side file such complaints or have to answer them in public; they need to describe them correctly.

## The law

### Israel's own reach over crimes abroad

> (א) דיני העונשין של ישראל יחולו על עבירות־חוץ אשר מדינת ישראל התחייבה, באמנות בינלאומיות רב צדדיות ופתוחות להצטרפות, להעניש עליהן; והוא, אף אם נעברו בידי מי שאינו אזרח ישראלי או תושב ישראל, ויהא מקום עשיית העבירה אשר יהא.

*Gist:* Israeli criminal law applies to foreign offences that Israel undertook in open multilateral treaties to punish, whoever committed them and wherever: a universal-jurisdiction clause tied to treaties. ([חוק העונשין, ס׳ 16(א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_העונשין))

> (ב) לא תהיה העמדה לדין על עבירת־חוץ אלא בידי היועץ המשפטי לממשלה או בהסכמתו בכתב.

*Gist:* no prosecution for a foreign offence without the Attorney General or his written consent. (ס׳ 9(ב))

> מי שעשה מחוץ לישראל מעשה שהוא עבירה לפי חוק זה, אפשר להביאו לדין ולהענישו בישראל, כאילו עשה את המעשה בישראל.

*Gist:* genocide committed outside Israel can be tried in Israel as if committed in Israel. ([חוק השמדת עם, ס׳ 5](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_בדבר_מניעתו_וענישתו_של_הפשע_השמדת_עם))

### The 2026 law on the 7 October perpetrators

> (ב) על אף האמור בתקנות ההגנה, בבואו לדון בכתב אישום כאמור בסעיף קטן (א) יהיה בית המשפט הצבאי מוסמך לדון בעבירה לפי כל דין, ובכלל זה בעבירות לפי חוק בדבר מניעתו וענישתו של הפשע השמדת עם, התש״י–1950, בעבירות של פגיעה בריבונות המדינה או בשלמותה, גרם למלחמה וסיוע לאויב במלחמה לפי סעיפים 97 עד 99 לחוק העונשין, ובעבירות לפי חוק המאבק בטרור, התשע״ו–2016.

*Gist:* a special military court, sitting in Jerusalem (s. 4), tries offences committed in the 7–10 October 2023 attack (and later offences against those abducted), with jurisdiction over Genocide Law, Penal Law ss. 97–99 and counter-terrorism offences. The Knesset passed it on 11 May 2026 and it was published on 13 May 2026. ([חוק העמדה לדין בשל אירועי טבח 7 באוקטובר 2023, ס׳ 3(ב)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/%D7%97%D7%95%D7%A7_%D7%94%D7%A2%D7%9E%D7%93%D7%94_%D7%9C%D7%93%D7%99%D7%9F_%D7%91%D7%A9%D7%9C_%D7%90%D7%99%D7%A8%D7%95%D7%A2%D7%99_%D7%98%D7%91%D7%97_7_%D7%91%D7%90%D7%95%D7%A7%D7%98%D7%95%D7%91%D7%A8_2023_%28%D7%98%D7%91%D7%97_%D7%A9%D7%9E%D7%99%D7%A0%D7%99_%D7%A2%D7%A6%D7%A8%D7%AA%29))

### A safeguard abroad: the UK example

> no warrant shall be issued under this section without the consent of the Director of Public Prosecutions.

*Gist:* since 2011, a private person in England and Wales cannot obtain an arrest warrant for listed offences committed abroad (grave breaches, torture, hostage-taking) without the DPP's consent. The change followed warrant applications against Israeli officials in 2005 and 2009. ([Police Reform and Social Responsibility Act 2011, s. 153](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2011/13/section/153))

## How it works in practice

### Four bases, four different questions

| Basis | The court's link to the crime | Example in this lesson |
|---|---|---|
| Territorial | Crime committed on its territory | Israel trying 7 October offences committed in Israel |
| Active personality | The suspect is its national (including dual nationals) | France: investigations of Franco-Israeli suspects |
| Passive personality | The victim is its national | Germany and France: investigations of 7 October killings and abductions of their nationals; France: killing of two French children in Gaza |
| Universal | No link required, for the gravest crimes | Belgium 2025: complaint against two Israeli soldiers visiting a festival |

In practice, most "universal jurisdiction" systems add conditions: the suspect must be present in the country, a prosecutor (not the complainant) decides, or the case is passed to a court with a closer link, including the ICC. The UN General Assembly's Sixth Committee has discussed "the scope and application of the principle of universal jurisdiction" every year since 2009 without agreeing on a definition.

### Verified European proceedings (as of 13 October 2026)

**Concerning Israelis**

- **United Kingdom, 2005 and 2009.** Magistrates issued arrest warrants on private applications against an Israeli general (2005) and against former Foreign Minister Tzipi Livni (December 2009). The Livni warrant was withdrawn because she was not in the country; Israel's Foreign Ministry called it a "cynical legal move" (CS Monitor). In 2011 the law was changed to require the DPP's consent.
- **Belgium, July 2025.** After complaints by the Hind Rajab Foundation and the Global Legal Action Network, Belgian federal police located and questioned two Israeli soldiers at the Tomorrowland festival and released them. According to the Universal Jurisdiction Annual Review 2026, the Federal Prosecutor's Office referred the complaints to the ICC on 30 July 2025.
- **France, 2024–2025** (UJAR 2026). Investigations on the basis of **nationality**, not universal jurisdiction: into a Franco-Israeli soldier over a video of the mistreatment of Palestinian detainees (prosecutor requested a judicial investigation on 1 October 2025); into two Franco-Israeli activists for alleged complicity in genocide over the blocking of aid trucks (judicial investigation opened May 2025); and, on the basis of the victims' nationality, into the killing of two French children in Gaza in October 2023 (requested 28 November 2025). None of the suspects had been charged at the time of the report.

**Concerning Hamas and 7 October**

- **Germany, 10 October 2023.** The Federal Prosecutor opened an investigation against unidentified Hamas members for membership in a foreign terrorist organisation, hostage-taking and murder, concerning German nationals.
- **France, 12 October 2023.** The national counter-terrorism prosecutor (PNAT) opened a preliminary investigation into murders and abductions of French nationals in connection with a terrorist enterprise. In November 2023 lawyers for French families filed further complaints, including one alleging crimes against humanity by Hamas and its leaders.
- **ICC, February 2024.** The Hostage and Missing Families Forum filed a submission at the ICC against Hamas leaders (lesson 16.5). This is not national jurisdiction but shows the parallel route.

### Who says what on universal jurisdiction

| Who | Position |
|---|---|
| Government of Israel (Sixth Committee, 2017) | Supports combating impunity "given its people's history", but universal jurisdiction must respect subsidiarity and be "used only as a last resort", with safeguards against "political abuse" |
| Government of Israel (Sixth Committee, 11 Oct 2023) | Hamas "committed grave international crimes" and "those responsible must be held accountable" |
| Belgian Federal Prosecutor (2025) | Found it "may possess jurisdiction", questioned the two soldiers, then referred the complaints to the ICC (VRT; UJAR 2026) |
| TRIAL International and partners (UJAR 2026) | Universal jurisdiction is an essential complement to international courts; notes "accusations of double standards" over the differing responses to Ukraine and Gaza, and "very limited action" on Gaza by states usually most active |
| Israeli Knesset (May 2026) | Passed the 7 October tribunal law by 93–0; the Justice Minister called it one of the most important moments of the current Knesset |
| Adalah, with PCATI and HaMoked (on the 2026 law) | "The law denies suspects basic procedural protections essential to a fair trial"; any death sentence would be "an arbitrary deprivation of life" (Adalah, 12.5.2026). The Times of Israel also reports unnamed rights groups' fair-trial concerns (12.5.2026) |

## Worked example

**Situation.** A tour company run by Dalia, a reservist, asks whether its staff "can be arrested abroad because of universal jurisdiction". At the same time, a group of families of 7 October victims, including dual French-Israeli citizens, asks whether they can do anything in France.

1. **Separate the questions.** Dalia's is about the risk of foreign proceedings against Israelis; the families' is about using foreign courts against Hamas members.
2. **Identify the bases.** Against Israelis abroad: universal jurisdiction (Belgium-type), or nationality if staff hold a second nationality (France-type). For the families: passive personality (French victims), already used by the PNAT since October 2023.
3. **Describe, don't advise.** For Dalia: the verified cases (UK 2009, Belgium 2025); the safeguards some countries use (prosecutor's or DPP's consent, presence requirement); and that you cannot assess an individual's risk. Refer to a lawyer in the country concerned (lesson 18.5). For the families: the PNAT investigation exists; a French lawyer can explain joining as a civil party.
4. **Write it neutrally.** "Belgian police questioned two Israeli soldiers in July 2025 after an NGO complaint; the case was referred to the ICC" — not "Belgium hunts soldiers" or "Belgium finally acted".

**Mirror test.** The same analysis applies to a Palestinian NGO asking about filing in France against a dual national, or a Jewish organisation asking about a complaint in Germany against a Hamas member living there.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- The [UJAR 2026 (PDF)](https://trialinternational.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/UJAR_2026_digital.pdf): the introduction and the country sections for Belgium and France.
- Israel's two Sixth Committee statements (sources).
- 75 minutes.

### Steps

1. Pick two proceedings from this lesson or from UJAR 2026: one concerning Israelis and one concerning Hamas or another Palestinian group.
2. For each, fill a card: country, date opened, who filed, jurisdictional basis, suspects (as described publicly), charges, current status, source.
3. Check the status online today; update it with a dated note.
4. Write one paragraph on each, using the same neutral verbs ("filed", "opened", "referred", "questioned", "charged").
5. Read Israel's 2017 statement and write two sentences on its position, quoted.
6. Find Penal Law s. 16 and the Genocide Law s. 5 on Wikisource; write one line on how each gives Israel jurisdiction abroad.
7. Read s. 1 and s. 3 of the 2026 law and write which offences it lets the court try and where the court sits.

### Template

No template. Use one card per case with the fields in step 2.

### How you know it worked

- Each card names the basis correctly (not everything is "universal").
- Your two paragraphs could appear side by side in one newsletter without either looking partisan.
- You can say which Israeli provision would let an Israeli court try a foreign national for genocide abroad.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Calling every foreign case "universal jurisdiction" | Wrong analysis of who can act and why | Name the basis: territory, suspect's or victim's nationality, universal | Fill the basis field first |
| Reporting a complaint as an arrest or a charge | False statement; possible defamation (lesson 08.1) | Use the procedural step that actually happened | Check the prosecutor's own announcement |
| Publishing names of private suspects from NGO complaints | Legal risk for you; harm if the complaint fails | Use only what authorities have made public | Follow the pre-publication checklist (Module 8) |
| Advising a person about their own arrest risk abroad | Unauthorised legal advice (lesson 18.4); the advice may be wrong | Give general information and refer to a lawyer in that country | Describe cases, never predict outcomes |
| Assuming Israel has no jurisdiction over crimes abroad | Missing the Genocide Law s. 5, Penal Law s. 16 and the 2026 law | Cite the provisions and the AG-consent rule (s. 9(b)) | Read the Israeli statutes, not just foreign cases |

## Review

- National courts try crimes abroad on four bases: territory, the suspect's nationality, the victim's nationality, and universal jurisdiction.
- Verified European proceedings include UK warrants (2005, 2009), Belgium's questioning of two soldiers in 2025 (referred to the ICC), French nationality-based investigations, and German and French investigations into 7 October since October 2023.
- Israel supports accountability but says universal jurisdiction must be a last resort with safeguards against political abuse.
- Israel's own law reaches crimes abroad: Penal Law s. 16 (with AG consent, s. 9(b)), Genocide Law s. 5, and the 2026 law creating a military court for the 7 October perpetrators.
- Describe cases with neutral procedural verbs and dated sources.
