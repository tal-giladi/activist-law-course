# 16.2 · The ICC and Israel

By the end of this lesson you will be able to explain how the International Criminal Court claims jurisdiction over the Situation in the State of Palestine although Israel is not a party, follow the case's docket from verified sources, and write a neutral, dated status note that presents the Court's decisions and Israel's position side by side.

> [!NOTE]
> This lesson describes active court proceedings. The course takes no position on them. Every finding is attributed to the body that made it, every position to the party that holds it, and "reasonable grounds to believe" is never presented as guilt. Status is as checked on 13 October 2026; check the sources again before you rely on it.

## Why it matters

The ICC proceedings touch Israelis and Palestinians directly. Warrants for Israel's Prime Minister and former Defence Minister affect where they can travel; the investigation also covers Hamas and other Palestinian armed groups; the United States has sanctioned ICC officials and Palestinian NGOs over the case; and the Prosecutor who sought the warrants was removed from office in 2026 for sexual misconduct (Assembly of States Parties decision); Israel argues that the same allegations taint the warrants he sought. Activists of every view write about the case, petition about it and send information to it. Most public texts get the procedure wrong: they confuse the ICC with the ICJ, call warrants convictions, or call the case "over" because of a procedural ruling. A correct timeline is the basis of any credible advocacy, for or against.

## The law

### How the Court gets jurisdiction

> 2. In the case of article 13, paragraph (a) or (c), the Court may exercise its jurisdiction if one or more of the following States are Parties to this Statute or have accepted the jurisdiction of the Court in accordance with paragraph 3:
> (a) The State on the territory of which the conduct in question occurred or, if the crime was committed on board a vessel or aircraft, the State of registration of that vessel or aircraft;
> (b) The State of which the person accused of the crime is a national.

*Gist:* jurisdiction follows **either** the territory where the conduct happened **or** the nationality of the accused. If the territorial state is a party, nationals of non-parties are covered for conduct there. This is the basis of the Palestine situation. ([Rome Statute, art. 12(2)](https://legal.un.org/icc/statute/english/rome_statute%28e%29.pdf))

> 3. If the acceptance of a State which is not a Party to this Statute is required under paragraph 2, that State may, by declaration lodged with the Registrar, accept the exercise of jurisdiction by the Court with respect to the crime in question.

*Gist:* a non-party can accept jurisdiction ad hoc by declaration; Palestine lodged such a declaration on 1 January 2015, before acceding. (art. 12(3))

### Who can challenge

> 2. Challenges to the admissibility of a case on the grounds referred to in article 17 or challenges to the jurisdiction of the Court may be made by:
> (a) An accused or a person for whom a warrant of arrest or a summons to appear has been issued under article 58;
> (b) A State which has jurisdiction over a case, on the ground that it is investigating or prosecuting the case or has investigated or prosecuted; or
> (c) A State from which acceptance of jurisdiction is required under article 12.

*Gist:* Israel's 2024 challenge relied on these provisions; whether a non-party may use them before a warrant is issued was the question the Appeals Chamber sent back in April 2025. (art. 19(2))

### Warrants, officials, trials

> (a) There are reasonable grounds to believe that the person has committed a crime within the jurisdiction of the Court; and

*Gist:* the standard for a warrant is "reasonable grounds to believe", far below proof beyond reasonable doubt. (art. 58(1)(a))

> 1. The accused shall be present during the trial.

*Gist:* there is no trial in absentia; a case advances only if the person is arrested or appears. (art. 63(1))

> In particular, official capacity as a Head of State or Government, a member of a Government or parliament, an elected representative or a government official shall in no case exempt a person from criminal responsibility under this Statute

*Gist:* official position is no defence before the Court. Whether states must arrest a sitting official of a **non-party** state despite immunities under general international law is disputed among states. (art. 27(1); cooperation duty in art. 86)

## How it works in practice

### Israel and the Rome Statute

- Israel voted against the Statute at the Rome Conference in 1998, signed it on 31 December 2000, and on 28 August 2002 informed the UN Secretary-General that it "does not intend to become a party" and "has no legal obligations arising from its signature" ([UN Treaty Collection](https://treaties.un.org/Pages/ViewDetails.aspx?src=TREATY&mtdsg_no=XVIII-10&chapter=18&clang=_en)).
- Israel's Foreign Ministry states: "Israel is not a party to the Rome Statute and does not recognize the jurisdiction of the International Criminal Court (ICC). Israel views the proceedings against it as politically motivated and legally baseless" ([MFA ICC page](https://israelihl.mfa.gov.il/icc)).

### Who says what on jurisdiction

| Who | Position | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Government of Israel | No sovereign Palestinian state exists, so there is no state that can delegate jurisdiction; the Oslo Accords leave Palestinians without criminal jurisdiction over Area C, Jerusalem and Israeli nationals; Israel has a functioning legal system | AG memorandum (2019); consolidated reply (2025) |
| ICC Pre-Trial Chamber I (2021) | For the Statute, Palestine is a State Party; territorial jurisdiction covers Gaza and the West Bank, including East Jerusalem; no ruling on borders | PTC decision, as summarised by ASIL |
| ICC Appeals Chamber (2025) | Israel's art. 19 challenge must be decided on its substance (April); the investigation after 7 October 2023 is part of the existing situation (December) | Opinio Juris; ASIL |
| Seven States Parties: Australia, Austria, Brazil, the Czech Republic, Germany, Hungary and Uganda | In 2020 they asked to file, and filed, observations with the Chamber arguing, as the Jerusalem Post reported, "that Palestine is not a state" for this purpose. The PTC majority ruled the other way in 2021 | Jerusalem Post, 20.2.2020 |
| B'Tselem (Israeli NGO) | Disputes the AG memorandum and argues that Israel accords itself "almost complete criminal and civil immunity" at home | B'Tselem position paper (2020) |
| United States Government | The ICC "has no jurisdiction over the United States or Israel"; it sanctioned the Prosecutor, both Deputy Prosecutors, judges and, on 4 September 2025, Al-Haq, Al Mezan and the Palestinian Centre for Human Rights | Executive Order 14203; Al Jazeera |

### Sanctions and the Court in 2025–2026

- **Executive Order 14203** (6 February 2025) authorises US sanctions on persons who assist ICC actions against the US or its allies, naming the warrants against Netanyahu and Gallant as a reason. By August 2026, according to Just Security, nine of the Court's 18 judges, both Deputy Prosecutors and the former Prosecutor had been designated, including ICC President Tomoko Akane on 18 August 2026. Lawsuits in US courts challenge the order; some have produced injunctions for individual plaintiffs only.
- **Removal of the Prosecutor.** The Assembly of States Parties removed him for serious misconduct, on findings of sexual misconduct (UN News). Israel argues that the same allegations taint the warrants he sought: its November 2025 request to disqualify him, and to annul those warrants, rests on them. Whether the warrants are affected is for the judges; the Deputy Prosecutors continue the investigation.

### What a warrant does and does not do

- It obliges the 125 States Parties to cooperate in arrest and surrender (art. 86). States have reacted differently: some said they would comply, others raised immunity of officials of non-party states.
- It is not a conviction and creates no criminal record in Israel.
- No trial can take place unless the person is in the Court's custody (art. 63).
- It stays in force until the Court withdraws or varies it; Israel's request to withdraw the Netanyahu and Gallant warrants had not been granted at the time of checking.

### Following the docket yourself

1. The ICC page for the situation is <https://www.icc-cpi.int/palestine>; court records are listed by number (ICC-01/18-NNN). The site often blocks automated tools; open it in a normal browser.
2. Israel publishes its own filings on the [MFA ICC page](https://israelihl.mfa.gov.il/icc).
3. For explanations, use legal blogs that link to the decisions (Opinio Juris, Just Security, EJIL:Talk!) and read the decision itself before quoting.
4. Record every item as: date, document number, chamber, what was decided, what was **not** decided.

## Worked example

**Situation.** Two volunteers prepare a one-page "status of the ICC case" for members' newsletters: Avital, for a pro-Israel campus group, and Rami, for a Palestinian students' association. Each first wrote a sentence; then they agreed to check them together.

- Avital's draft: "In April 2025 the ICC admitted it had no jurisdiction and the warrants were cancelled."
- Rami's draft: "The ICC convicted Netanyahu of war crimes in November 2024."

**Step 1 — Check against the docket.** The Appeals Chamber on 24 April 2025 reversed a procedural ruling and sent Israel's challenge back for a decision on the substance; it did not rule on jurisdiction and did not suspend the warrants (Opinio Juris). The PTC on 21 November 2024 issued warrants on a "reasonable grounds to believe" standard (art. 58), not a conviction.

**Step 2 — Rewrite.**
- "On 24 April 2025 the Appeals Chamber held that the Pre-Trial Chamber had to rule on the substance of Israel's jurisdictional challenge and sent it back. The warrants were not suspended. As of 13 October 2026 no new ruling had been published."
- "On 21 November 2024 the Pre-Trial Chamber issued arrest warrants, finding reasonable grounds to believe that Netanyahu and Gallant bear criminal responsibility for war crimes and crimes against humanity. Israel rejects the Court's jurisdiction and denies the allegations. There has been no trial."

**Step 3 — Add the other side of each story.** Both one-pagers add the Deif warrant and its termination, Israel's position from the MFA page, and the Court's 2021 jurisdiction ruling, each dated and sourced.

**Mirror test.** The same check applies to "the ICC cleared Israel" and "the ICC found genocide" — neither is a statement the Court has made.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- A browser that can open icc-cpi.int (it may not open from some networks or tools; try again later).
- The [MFA ICC page](https://israelihl.mfa.gov.il/icc) and the [Opinio Juris explainer](https://opiniojuris.org/2025/04/25/the-appeals-chamber-decides-israels-appeals-and-refuses-to-suspend-the-arrest-warrants/).
- A spreadsheet or table.
- 70 minutes.

### Steps

1. Open the ICC situation page and its court records. Note the latest five decisions or orders with their numbers and dates.
2. Check whether the Pre-Trial Chamber has ruled on the remanded art. 19 challenge since 13 October 2026. If yes, record what it decided and whether it was appealed.
3. Check the current status of each warrant in the situation (in force, withdrawn, terminated).
4. Check who heads the Office of the Prosecutor and whether a new Prosecutor has been elected.
5. Add Israel's latest filing from the MFA page and one third-party position (a State Party, the Palestinian side, or an NGO), each with its date.
6. Write a 150-word neutral status note with every sentence attributed.
7. Ask someone with different views to mark any sentence that sounds like a position. Fix it.

### Template

No template: use a table with the columns Date | Document no. | Body | Decided | Not decided | Source.

### How you know it worked

- Every row has a document number or a named source and a date.
- Your note never says "convicted", "cleared" or "the case is closed" unless a decision says so.
- A reader from the other side accepts your note as factual.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Calling a warrant a conviction, or a remand an acquittal | Your text is factually wrong and easy to discredit | Quote the decision's own words and standard | Record "decided / not decided" for every ruling |
| Mixing up the ICC (individuals) and the ICJ (states, lesson 13.4) | Wrong forum, wrong parties, wrong consequences | Name the court, the case number and the parties | Check the case number prefix (ICC-01/18 vs ICJ case 192) |
| Writing the status from memory or social media | Outdated or invented facts | Re-check the docket before publishing | Date every status statement |
| Presenting the Court's view as fact and Israel's as "claims" (or the reverse) | Text loses credibility with half its readers | Use the same verbs for both: "the Chamber held", "Israel argues" | Use a "who says what" table |
| Assuming the Prosecutor's removal ended the case | Misinforms readers | The investigation continues under the Deputy Prosecutors | Separate personnel news from case decisions |

## Review

- The ICC has jurisdiction over conduct on the territory of a State Party or by its nationals (art. 12(2)); Palestine became a party in 2015, so conduct by anyone in Gaza and the West Bank is covered in the Court's view.
- Israel is not a party, informed the UN in 2002 that it does not intend to become one, and rejects the Court's jurisdiction.
- Warrants for Netanyahu, Gallant and Deif were issued on 21 November 2024; Deif's case was terminated in February 2025; Israel's jurisdiction challenge was remanded in April 2025 and, as of 13 October 2026, no new ruling had been published.
- A warrant needs only "reasonable grounds to believe"; there is no trial in absentia.
- US sanctions under EO 14203 target ICC officials and Palestinian NGOs; the Prosecutor was removed in July 2026 for sexual misconduct, and Israel argues the same allegations taint the warrants.
