# 03.5 · Judicial Review and the 2023–2026 Constitutional Dispute

Since January 2023 Israel has been in an open dispute over who has the last word: the Knesset and the government, or the Supreme Court. This lesson sets out the law and the facts as of 12 October 2026 — what the 2025 amendment to the Judicial Selection Committee actually says, where the court cases stand, and how each side argues — so that you can explain the dispute accurately to anyone, whatever your own view, and check for yourself what has changed since.

## Why it matters

Every activist in Israel is asked about this dispute, and every petition you file goes to judges chosen under rules that are now contested. Supporters of the changes call them a judicial reform (רפורמה משפטית) that restores the balance between elected officials and the courts; opponents call them a regime overhaul (הפיכה משטרית) that removes checks on the government. This course takes no side. What it does teach is how to tell a fact from a claim, how to attribute every claim, and how to check the current status, so that your own writing is trusted by readers who disagree with you.

## The law

### The Judicial Selection Committee before and after Amendment No. 4

Basic Law: The Judiciary s. 4(b) still shows the old rule: nine members, namely the President of the Supreme Court and two other justices, the Minister of Justice and another minister, two MKs, and two representatives of the Israel Bar Association. Below it, marked "(החל מיום תחילת כהונתה של הכנסת העשרים ושש)" — from the start of the 26th Knesset's term — is the new rule:

> שני חברי הכנסת שייבחרו בכנסת, אחד מסיעות הקואליציה שיבחרו חברי הכנסת מאותן סיעות ואחד מסיעות האופוזיציה שיבחרו חברי הכנסת מאותן סיעות

> שני נציגי ציבור שייבחרו בכנסת, באחד יבחרו חברי הכנסת מסיעות הקואליציה ובאחד – חברי הכנסת מסיעות האופוזיציה

*Gist:* the two MKs will be one from the coalition and one from the opposition, and the two Bar Association seats are replaced by two "public representatives", one chosen by coalition MKs and one by opposition MKs (s. 4(b)(1)(ג)–(ד)). The three justices and two ministers stay.

> בחירת הוועדה בשופט לבית המשפט העליון – תהיה ברוב של חמישה חברים ובהם אחד מהמנויים בסעיף קטן (ד)(2) ואחד מהמנויים בסעיף קטן (ד)(3).

*Gist:* a Supreme Court justice will be chosen by five members, including at least one from the coalition side (a minister, the coalition MK or the coalition's public representative) and one from the opposition side (s. 4(e)). For other courts, the five must include a judge as well (s. 4(d)).

The amendment also adds:
- **s. 4A** — if two Supreme Court seats stay empty for a year and a vote fails, the Minister of Justice may, once per Knesset, trigger a procedure in which the coalition side and the opposition side each propose three candidates and the rest of the committee picks one of them.
- **s. 4B** — no judges may be selected from 90 days before an election (or from other events that lead to elections) until the new members are chosen.
- **s. 4(f)** — special rules if the coalition has more than 80 MKs.

Source: [חוק־יסוד: השפיטה](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק-יסוד:_השפיטה) (Amendment No. 4, ס״ח תשפ״ה 434). The Knesset's English PDF predates the amendment; teach from the Hebrew text.

### The reasonableness amendment

Section 15(ד1), added in July 2023, is shown struck through as annulled by HCJ 5658/23. Lesson [04.3](../module-04/lesson-03.md) covers its text, the 1 January 2024 ruling and the debate on reasonableness; this lesson does not repeat it.

### The legal question: can a court review a Basic Law?

- **What the Court has said.** In *Hasson* (2021) the majority held that the Knesset cannot use a Basic Law to negate Israel's character as a Jewish and democratic state (lesson 03.4). In HCJ 5658/23 (2024), 12 of 15 justices held that the Court may review Basic Laws in extreme cases. The Judicial Selection petitions ask the Court to apply that to Amendment No. 4: is it an "unconstitutional constitutional amendment" that seriously damages judicial independence?
- **What critics of review say.** No Basic Law gives the Court power to review Basic Laws, and there is still no Basic Law on how Basic Laws are made (lesson 03.1). Critics of judicial review, from former President Landau to Professor Gavison, argued long before 2023 that the courts created their own power. In HCJ 5658/23, Justices Sohlberg and Mintz, dissenting, found no legal source for such a power; in 2026 the Knesset argued that, at most, it could be used only for damage so severe that it is not present here.

### The election debate: a range, not two camps

Before the 2026 election, Ynet asked every party for its position. Answers ranged from repealing Amendment No. 4 and setting rules for judicial review in a new Basic Law (Yashar, Beyachad); through a constitution approved by referendum (Yisrael Beytenu) or narrowing reasonableness while removing the Bar's seats (Amcha Israel; the Reservists-Economic Party, which would also allow an override by more than 72 MKs); to an override by 61 votes with added safeguards, and striking laws only by an expanded bench (Religious Zionism); to direct election of justices (Zehut). Likud declined to answer the questionnaire. Read the article itself; positions may change before 27 October.

### The Attorney General dispute in brief

In December 2025 the Court held, 7–0, that the government's new procedure for dismissing the Attorney General was adopted without a proper factual and legal basis and replaced a professional committee with a ministers-only one; ministers Levin and Karhi called on the government not to accept the ruling, and opposition leader Lapid welcomed it (Times of Israel). In July 2026 the Knesset passed a law, in force from 2027, that lets the government depart from the Attorney General's legal opinions, ends her exclusive right to represent the state, and lets the government set the appointment and dismissal procedure. Justice Minister Levin called it a central pillar of the reform; the Israel Democracy Institute warned it could undermine the independence of law enforcement; petitions are pending (Jerusalem Post, 15.7.2026).

## Worked example

**Situation.** Avi runs a neighbourhood WhatsApp group whose members voted for very different parties. After a heated exchange, he asks Rana, a member who took this course, to post a short factual note: "What does the judges law actually do, and is it in force?"

1. **Separate facts from claims.** Facts: passed 27 March 2025 with 67 votes; replaces the two Bar seats with two public representatives chosen by coalition and opposition MKs; makes the two MK seats one coalition, one opposition; Supreme Court picks need five votes, including one coalition-side and one opposition-side member; in force from the 26th Knesset; petitions heard 21 June 2026 by eleven justices; no ruling found as of the date she checks.
2. **Attribute the claims.** "The government and the Knesset say it gives elected officials a fair say and ends the justices' veto. The petitioners and the Attorney General say it makes appointments political and judges dependent on politicians."
3. **Avoid loaded words in her own voice.** She uses "the 2025 amendment", not "the overhaul" or "the reform"; when she quotes a side, she uses its words in quotation marks.
4. **Give the source and the date.** She links the Wikisource text and one news report of the hearing, and writes "checked on [date]".
5. **Test it.** She shows the draft to one friend from each side. Each may dislike the law or the Court, but both agree the note is accurate. She posts it.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- About 60 minutes, a browser, and the Supreme Court's free judgment search at <https://supremedecisions.court.gov.il/> (lesson 02.2).

### Steps

1. **Status check.** Search the news (for example Times of Israel, Jerusalem Post, Ynet, Haaretz, Israel Hayom) and the Supreme Court site for "Judicial Selection Committee ruling" / "הוועדה לבחירת שופטים בג״ץ" and the ACRI case number 70854-03-25. Write down: ruling yes or no; if yes, the date, the vote and the result.
2. **Election check.** Find the election result and whether the 26th Knesset has begun its term. Write whether Amendment No. 4 is now in force.
3. **New legislation.** Check whether any new Basic Law or Basic Law amendment on the judiciary, the Attorney General or judicial review has passed since 12 October 2026.
4. **Write a neutral briefing** of 250–300 words: what the amendment does (from the Basic Law text), its status on the date you checked, and the main argument of each side, attributed.
5. **Steelman.** Write the strongest single argument of the side you disagree with, in a way its supporters would accept.
6. **Fairness test.** Ask one person who supports the amendment and one who opposes it to read your briefing. Note what each wanted changed, and fix anything that is not accurate.

### Template

No separate template. Use this outline:

```text
Checked on: ____ (sources: ____)
1. What the law says (from the Basic Law text):
2. Status: in force? ruling? date and vote:
3. Government / Knesset position (attributed):
4. Petitioners / Attorney General position (attributed):
5. What the Court has said so far (rulings only, not hearing remarks):
6. What to watch next:
```

### How you know it worked

Every factual sentence has a source and a date. Every opinion is attributed to a named person or body. Readers from both sides accept the briefing as fair, even if they disagree with each other about the law.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Saying the Judicial Selection law "is in force" or "was struck down" without checking | You spread wrong information | Correct it with the date and source | Run the status check every time |
| Reporting a justice's question at a hearing as the Court's decision | Misleads readers about the law | Distinguish hearings from rulings | Cite rulings by number and date |
| Using one side's label ("overhaul", "reform") in your own voice | Readers on the other side stop reading | Use neutral names; quote labels in quotation marks | Name the specific law and section |
| Describing the amendment from a news summary | Errors on who sits and what majority is needed | Read s. 4 on Wikisource | Quote the Basic Law text |
| Presenting the dispute as only two camps | Misses the range of real positions | Use the party survey and named critics | Attribute each position |
| Treating the 2026 Attorney General law as a Basic Law | Wrong legal status and wrong review standard | Check the law's title | Read the title on the Knesset or Wikisource page |

## Review

- Amendment No. 4 (27 March 2025) replaces the Bar Association's two seats with two public representatives chosen by coalition and opposition MKs, splits the two MK seats between coalition and opposition, and sets a five-vote majority for Supreme Court picks with one member from each side; it applies from the 26th Knesset.
- Eleven justices heard the petitions on 21 June 2026; as of 12 October 2026 no ruling was found. The election is on 27 October 2026.
- The core question is whether, and when, a court may review a Basic Law; HCJ 5658/23 said yes in extreme cases, and the government and Knesset dispute that power.
- The Attorney General's dismissal was voided 7–0 in December 2025; a 2026 law on her powers takes effect in 2027 and is under challenge.
- Attribute every position, separate hearings from rulings, and check the status yourself before you write.
