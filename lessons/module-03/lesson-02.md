# 03.2 · Basic Law: Human Dignity and Liberty and the Limitation Clause

Basic Law: Human Dignity and Liberty is the shortest path from "this hurts me" to "this may be unconstitutional". In this lesson you read its rights one by one, learn the two-stage test every court uses — is a protected right infringed, and if so, is the infringement justified under the limitation clause — and you finish by running the first stage and the threshold conditions on a real measure that affects your cause.

## Why it matters

Most challenges to a law or a regulation in Israel run through two short Basic Laws from 1992. Lesson 01.3 showed you the limitation clause as a rung on the ladder. Here you learn to use it as a checklist. Activists most often go wrong at the first stage: they argue that a measure is excessive before showing which written right it touches, or they attack a regulation on its merits when the stronger point is that no law expressly authorised it. Getting the order right makes a short letter far stronger than a long one.

## The law

### The rights in Human Dignity and Liberty

> זכויות היסוד של האדם בישראל מושתתות על ההכרה בערך האדם, בקדושת חייו ובהיותו בן־חורין, והן יכובדו ברוח העקרונות שבהכרזה על הקמת מדינת ישראל.

*Gist:* basic rights rest on the value of the human being, the sanctity of life and freedom, and are to be honoured in the spirit of the Declaration of Independence (s. 1). Section 1A adds the purpose: to protect dignity and liberty in order to anchor the values of Israel as a Jewish and democratic state.

> אין פוגעים בחייו, בגופו, או בכבודו של אדם באשר הוא אדם.

*Gist:* no one may harm a person's life, body or dignity as such (s. 2). The other rights: property (s. 3); protection of life, body and dignity (s. 4); personal liberty — no imprisonment, arrest or extradition or any other restriction (s. 5); leaving Israel, and a citizen's right to enter (s. 6); privacy, the home, searches and confidential communications (s. 7).

> כל אדם זכאי לפרטיות ולצנעת חייו.

*Gist:* every person is entitled to privacy and to the privacy of their life (s. 7(a)).

### The two special rules

> אין מגבילים זכויות שלפי חוק־יסוד זה של המשרתים בצבא־הגנה לישראל, במשטרת ישראל, בשירות בתי הסוהר ובארגוני הבטחון האחרים של המדינה, ואין מתנים על זכויות אלה, אלא לפי חוק ובמידה שאינה עולה על הנדרש ממהותו ומאופיו של השירות.

*Gist:* rights of people serving in the army, police, prison service and security agencies may be limited only by law, and only as far as the nature and character of the service require (s. 9).

Section 12 says emergency regulations cannot change, suspend or condition the Basic Law, except that during a declared state of emergency they may limit rights for a proper purpose, for a period and to an extent no greater than necessary.

Full text: [חוק־יסוד: כבוד האדם וחירותו](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק-יסוד:_כבוד_האדם_וחירותו); [English translation (Knesset)](https://main.knesset.gov.il/EN/activity/documents/BasicLawsPDF/BasicLawLiberty.pdf).

### Freedom of Occupation, and its override clause

> כל אזרח או תושב של המדינה זכאי לעסוק בכל עיסוק, מקצוע או משלח יד.

*Gist:* every citizen or resident may engage in any occupation, profession or trade (s. 3). Section 4 is a limitation clause in the same words as s. 8 of Human Dignity and Liberty.

> הוראת חוק הפוגעת בחופש העיסוק תהיה תקפה אף כשאינה בהתאם לסעיף 4, אם נכללה בחוק שנתקבל ברוב של חברי הכנסת ונאמר בו במפורש, שהוא תקף על אף האמור בחוק־יסוד זה; תוקפו של חוק כאמור יפקע בתום ארבע שנים מיום תחילתו, זולת אם נקבע בו מועד מוקדם יותר.

*Gist:* a law that limits freedom of occupation is valid even if it fails s. 4, if it passed by a majority of all MKs and says expressly that it is valid notwithstanding the Basic Law; it lapses after four years unless an earlier date is set (s. 8). Human Dignity and Liberty has no such clause. ([חוק־יסוד: חופש העיסוק](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק-יסוד:_חופש_העיסוק))

## How it works in practice

### Stage one: is a protected right infringed?

1. **Name the right and the section.** Life, body, dignity, property, liberty, movement in and out of Israel, privacy, occupation. Rights that are not written here — equality, expression, religion, protest — come in through "dignity" (lesson 03.4).
2. **Show the infringement with facts.** Who is affected, how, and how much. Courts ask the challenger to lay a factual basis. In *Commitment to Peace and Social Justice* (2005), the Court accepted that dignity includes a minimum of material means for a dignified existence, but dismissed the petitions because the petitioners had not shown with data that the benefit cuts actually left people below that minimum. ([Versa](https://versa.cardozo.yu.edu/opinions/commitment-peace-and-social-justice-society-v-minister-finance))
3. **Check the date.** A law that existed before the Basic Law came into force is protected by s. 10 (lesson 01.3); argue for interpreting it in the spirit of the Basic Law instead.

If stage one fails, stop and use administrative-law grounds (Module 4).

### Stage two: the limitation clause as a checklist

Read s. 8 as four conditions. All four must be met; the burden of justifying the infringement falls in practice on the state.

| Condition | Words in s. 8 | What you check |
|---|---|---|
| 1. By a law, or under express authorisation in a law | "בחוק ... או לפי חוק כאמור מכוח הסמכה מפורשת בו" | Is the limit in a statute? If it is in a regulation, procedure or decision, does a law **expressly** authorise that kind of limit? |
| 2. Befits the values of the State of Israel | "ההולם את ערכיה של מדינת ישראל" | Courts rarely decide a case on this condition alone |
| 3. Proper purpose | "שנועד לתכלית ראויה" | What is the measure for, and is that legitimate? |
| 4. No greater than necessary | "ובמידה שאינה עולה על הנדרש" | The three proportionality tests (lesson 03.3) |

Condition 1 is where activists win cheap and fast. A general power "to regulate" a field does not by itself let a minister or a municipality limit a basic right; the law must say so expressly. Lesson [04.1](../module-04/lesson-01.md) applies the same rule from the administrative side.

### Security forces and emergencies

- **Section 9.** In *Tzemach* (1999) the Court held that a 96-hour military arrest before a detainee saw a judge went further than the nature of military service required. ([Versa](https://versa.cardozo.yu.edu/node/386))
- **Section 12.** Emergency regulations may limit rights only during a declared state of emergency, and are still subject to the proportionality wording in s. 12 itself.

### What a court can do

The court does not always strike a law down on the spot. In *Tzemach* it declared the 96-hour provision void but suspended the declaration for six months so the Knesset could legislate. In *Ressler v. Knesset* (2012) it held the "Tal Law" on deferring military service for yeshiva students unconstitutional, but let it run out at its expiry date and ruled that it could not be extended in its existing form. ([Versa](https://versa.cardozo.yu.edu/opinions/ressler-v-knesset)) In practice, this means a well-argued letter to the Knesset or the ministry often achieves more, sooner, than waiting for a court.

## Worked example

**Situation.** A ministry publishes a regulation requiring anyone who sells home-made food online to take a 120-hour paid course at an approved college before selling. Esti, a single mother in Bnei Brak who sells cakes from home, and Ahmad, a caterer in Tamra, join a group of home cooks who want to object. The regulation cites a section of a public-health law that lets the minister "make regulations for the protection of public health in food businesses".

1. **Stage one.** Right: freedom of occupation (Basic Law: Freedom of Occupation, s. 3). Infringement: the course costs time and money and blocks entry to the trade. Facts: they collect 30 short statements from home cooks on how long the course would keep them out of work.
2. **Condition 1.** The limit is in a regulation, not a law. Does the enabling section **expressly** authorise conditions for entering an occupation, such as mandatory training? A general power "for the protection of public health" may not be enough. They write down the enabling section word for word.
3. **Condition 3.** Purpose: food safety. They accept it is proper — the strongest letters concede what is true.
4. **Condition 4.** They note for lesson 03.3 that a shorter food-hygiene course, or online training with an exam, might achieve the same purpose with less harm.
5. **Next step.** A letter to the ministry's legal adviser asking under which express authorisation the training requirement was set, with a copy to the Knesset committee that approves the ministry's regulations. The arguments are the same for any home cook, religious or secular, Jewish or Arab.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- Your constitutional fact sheet from lesson 03.1.
- One real measure that limits a right connected to your cause: a law, a regulation, a by-law or a published decision. Its text, with a link.
- The [proportionality analysis template](../../templates/proportionality-analysis.md), about 45 minutes.

### Steps

1. Fill section 0 of the template: what the measure is, who made it, under which power, and a link.
2. Stage one (section 1): name the right and the Basic Law section. Copy the Hebrew text. Describe the infringement with at least two concrete facts.
3. Check the date of the measure against the date of the Basic Law (s. 10).
4. Condition 1 (section 2): if the limit is not in a statute, find the enabling section and copy it. Write "express" or "general" and explain in one sentence.
5. Check whether s. 9 (security forces), s. 12 (emergency regulations) or the override clause (Freedom of Occupation s. 8) applies.
6. Write the measure's stated purpose (section 3), quoting the explanatory notes, the minutes or the decision itself. Leave the proportionality tests for lesson 03.3.

### Template

[Proportionality analysis](../../templates/proportionality-analysis.md), sections 0–3.

### How you know it worked

You can point to the exact words of the Basic Law that the measure touches, and to at least two facts that show the infringement. If the measure is in a regulation or decision, you have copied the enabling section and taken a reasoned position on whether it is express. You have not yet argued "disproportionate".

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Arguing "disproportionate" before naming the right | The reader cannot tell which Basic Law applies | Start again at stage one | Always fill section 1 of the template first |
| Describing the harm in general terms | The court finds no factual basis (as in *Commitment*) | Collect concrete facts, numbers and examples | Gather evidence before writing |
| Missing the express-authorisation point | You argue the merits and miss the strongest procedural flaw | Copy the enabling section and test it | Check condition 1 whenever the limit is not in a statute |
| Attacking a pre-1992 law under the Basic Law | The argument fails on s. 10 | Argue for a narrow interpretation instead | Check the law's date |
| Expecting a court to strike a law down immediately | Disappointment; the remedy is often suspended | Ask also for an amendment or a narrow reading | Plan a parallel letter to the Knesset or the ministry |

## Review

- Human Dignity and Liberty protects life, body, dignity, property, liberty, movement and privacy; Freedom of Occupation protects the right to work in any occupation.
- The analysis has two stages: first a protected right and a factual infringement, then the limitation clause.
- The limitation clause has four conditions: a law or express authorisation, the state's values, a proper purpose, and no more than necessary.
- Special rules: s. 9 for the security forces, s. 12 for emergency regulations, and the four-year override clause in Freedom of Occupation s. 8.
- Courts often suspend a declaration of invalidity; a parallel letter to the decision-maker is usually worth sending.
