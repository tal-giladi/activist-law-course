# 08.4 · Privacy Law for Activists

By the end of this lesson you will be able to decide whether a photo, a video, a leaked document or a list of names may be published, run your own group's sign-up form and member list through the rules of Amendment 13, and use the Privacy Protection Authority when someone else misuses data about you or your group — and you will draft a complaint to the Authority.

> [!WARNING]
> Invading someone's privacy is a civil wrong and, for most kinds of invasion, a crime punishable by up to five years' imprisonment (s. 5). Publishing a private person's address, health, sex life or private messages can also put that person in physical danger. Blur or leave out what the public does not need. This lesson is not legal advice.

## Why it matters

Activists collect and publish personal information all the time: photos and videos of protests, names of officials and of opponents, leaked emails, petitions with signatures, WhatsApp groups and volunteer spreadsheets. Each of those can be a privacy problem — for the people in them, and for the activist who publishes or stores them.

The same law also protects activists. It is what you rely on when a counter-group posts your face with your workplace, when an employer reads your private messages, or when your organisation's volunteer list leaks. Amendment 13, in force since 14 August 2025, gave the Privacy Protection Authority (הרשות להגנת הפרטיות) real enforcement powers, including large administrative fines, and widened the definitions: "personal information" now covers almost anything that identifies a person, and political opinions are expressly "information of special sensitivity". Every campaign that keeps a list of supporters now holds sensitive data.

## The law

> לא יפגע אדם בפרטיות של זולתו ללא הסכמתו.

*Gist:* do not invade another person's privacy without consent ([s. 1](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הגנת_הפרטיות#סעיף_1)); consent must be informed, express or implied (s. 3).

> פגיעה בפרטיות היא אחת מאלה:
> (1) בילוש או התחקות אחרי אדם, העלולים להטרידו, או הטרדה אחרת;
> (3) צילום אדם כשהוא ברשות היחיד;
> (4) פרסום תצלומו של אדם ברבים בנסיבות שבהן עלול הפרסום להשפילו או לבזותו;
> (5) העתקת תוכן של מכתב או כתב אחר שלא נועד לפרסום, או שימוש בתכנו, בלי רשות מאת הנמען או הכותב ...
> (10) פרסומו או מסירתו של דבר שהושג בדרך פגיעה בפרטיות לפי פסקאות (1) עד (7) או (9);
> (11) פרסומו של ענין הנוגע לצנעת חייו האישיים של אדם, לרבות עברו המיני, או למצב בריאותו, או להתנהגותו ברשות היחיד.

*Gist:* stalking or harassing, photographing someone in a private place, publishing a photo that may humiliate, copying or using a private letter or electronic message, passing on what was obtained by such an invasion, and publishing intimate matters, health or private-place behaviour are all invasions. ([s. 2](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הגנת_הפרטיות#סעיף_2)) Item (4א) adds photos of an identifiable victim at or just after an incident, and s. 2א covers photos of a body.

> במשפט פלילי, אזרחי או מינהלי בשל פגיעה בפרטיות תהא זו הגנה טובה אם נתקיימה אחת מאלה:
> (2) ... (ה) הפגיעה היתה בדרך של צילום, או בדרך של פרסום תצלום, שנעשה ברשות הרבים ודמות הנפגע מופיעה בו באקראי;
> (3) בפגיעה היה ענין ציבורי המצדיק אותה בנסיבות הענין, ובלבד שאם היתה הפגיעה בדרך של פרסום – הפרסום לא היה כוזב.

*Gist:* a person who appears incidentally in a photo taken in a public place has no claim, and an invasion justified by public interest is protected — if a publication, only if it is not false. ([s. 18](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הגנת_הפרטיות#סעיף_18)) Section 18(1) also imports the permitted publications of the Defamation Law (s. 13), and s. 18(2)(ו) imports its good-faith heads 15(4)–(11).

> ”מאגר מידע“ – אוסף פרטי מידע אישי המעובד באמצעי דיגיטלי, למעט אחד מאלה:
> (1) אוסף לשימוש אישי שאינו למטרות עסק;

*Gist:* after Amendment 13 any digitally processed collection of personal information is a database, except a purely personal, non-business collection and a narrow exception for contact-only lists that reveal nothing more. ([s. 3](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הגנת_הפרטיות#סעיף_3)) The same section lists "information of special sensitivity", including (7): "מידע אישי על אודות דעותיו הפוליטיות או אמונותיו הדתיות של אדם או השקפת עולמו".

> (א) כל אדם זכאי לעיין בעצמו, או על ידי בא־כוחו שהרשהו בכתב או על ידי אפוטרופוסו, במידע האישי שעליו המוחזק במאגר מידע.

*Gist:* you may see what a database holds about you (s. 13), and ask to correct or delete wrong, incomplete or outdated information (s. 14). ([s. 13](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הגנת_הפרטיות#סעיף_13))

> (ב)(1) במשפט בשל עוולה אזרחית לפי סעיף 4, רשאי בית המשפט לחייב את הנתבע לשלם לנפגע פיצוי שלא יעלה על 50,000 שקלים חדשים, בלא הוכחת נזק.

*Gist:* as in defamation, a court may award compensation without proof of damage, doubled with intent to harm, linked to the CPI from May 2007 (s. 29א) — about ₪72,600 today (see [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)). Exemplary damages for specific database breaches, such as refusing access, are in s. 15א.

## How it works in practice

### Photos and video at protests

| Situation | Rule | Practice |
|---|---|---|
| Wide shot of a march in the street | Public place; incidental appearance (s. 18(2)(ה)) | Fine to film and publish |
| Close-up of a speaker on stage, or a police officer acting in role | Public conduct; public interest (s. 18(3)); filming police is lawful (06.3) | Fine; caption accurately |
| Close-up of a counter-protester with a mocking caption ("look at this idiot") | Publishing a photo that may humiliate (s. 2(4)) | Remove the caption or blur the face |
| An injured person on the ground, identifiable | s. 2(4א) | Blur; do not publish without consent |
| Children | No specific exception, but humiliation and safety risks are high | Blur faces of children by default |
| Through a window, in a home, a clinic, a toilet | Photographing in a private place (s. 2(3)) | Do not film |

### Names, addresses, messages and leaks

- **Home addresses, phone numbers, ID numbers, workplaces of private people:** almost never needed for a public point; publishing them can be harassment (s. 2(1)) and may endanger them (see also 06.5 and 08.5).
- **Private messages and emails (s. 2(5)):** a screenshot of someone else's private chat is "copying a letter not meant for publication". Public interest (s. 18(3)) can justify it — for example, an official's messages showing abuse of power — but take advice first.
- **Leaked documents:** if the leak itself was obtained by an invasion of privacy (hacking, reading someone's phone), passing it on is also an invasion (s. 2(10)). Documents about public decisions are a different matter from personal files.
- **Health, sexual orientation, criminal past:** publishing these about a private person is a classic invasion (s. 2(11)) and rarely justified.

### Your own lists after Amendment 13

Most activist groups now hold a "database": a Google Form of volunteers, a mailing list, a WhatsApp community export, a donors' spreadsheet. Because the list shows who supports a cause, it reveals **political opinions** — information of special sensitivity.

What follows for a typical group (not a data broker or a public body):

1. **Purpose.** Use the data only for the purpose you collected it for (s. 8(ב)). A list of petition signatories is not automatically a fundraising list.
2. **Notice when collecting (s. 11).** Tell people whether giving the data is voluntary, the purpose, who controls the list and how to contact them, to whom it will be passed, and their access and correction rights.
3. **Access and correction (ss. 13–14).** Answer a member's request to see or correct their data.
4. **Security (s. 17 and the Data Security Regulations).** Limit who has access, use strong passwords and two-factor login, do not share the full list in open groups.
5. **Registration** is now needed only for data brokers with over 10,000 people and for public bodies (s. 8א(א)); a database with sensitive information on over 100,000 people must be notified to the Authority (s. 8א(ב)). Most groups are below both thresholds, but the other duties apply to everyone.

### When others misuse data about you

| Route | What it can do | What it cannot do |
|---|---|---|
| **Write to the body** (access request s. 13, correction/deletion s. 14, or a demand to stop) | Fast; creates a record you must attach to a complaint | Force compliance |
| **Complaint to the Privacy Protection Authority** | Investigate databases, order a breach to stop, impose administrative fines, criminal referral | Award you compensation; it does not help prepare private claims |
| **Civil claim** (Magistrates' or small claims court) | Compensation, up to the s. 29א amount without proof of damage; orders under s. 29 | Investigate on your behalf; you must prove the invasion |
| **Police complaint** | Criminal investigation of serious invasions (s. 5), threats, stalking | Remove content quickly |

**Filing with the Authority** (checked 13.10.2026): online form, email ppa@justice.gov.il, phone \*3103 (Wednesdays 10:00–14:00), fax or post to the Authority at Kiryat HaMemshala, Jerusalem. You may complain anonymously. Attach your earlier request to the body and its answer; say whether you agree that your details be passed to the body; say if the matter is before a court (the Authority will usually not investigate it then). The Authority decides whether to act according to its enforcement priorities and whether the case shows a systemic failure. The limitation period for a civil privacy claim is seven years.

## Worked example

**Situation.** "Forum for the Majority", a group that supports the government's judicial changes, films a large anti-government protest in Tel Aviv. A volunteer, Avi, proposes to post (a) a video of the crowd, (b) close-ups of five protesters with the caption "the faces of anarchy", and (c) a spreadsheet identifying 40 protesters with their workplaces, "so their employers know". Separately, the forum's own sign-up form collects names, phone numbers, ID numbers and "which party did you vote for".

1. **(a) Crowd video.** Public place, incidental appearances, political event: protected (s. 18(2)(ה), (3)). Publish.
2. **(b) Close-ups with a caption.** A photo published in circumstances that may humiliate (s. 2(4)); the caption also risks defamation if it implies criminality (08.1). Public interest in the protest does not require mocking individuals. Rewrite: no individual close-ups of private participants; quote the protest's official speakers instead.
3. **(c) Spreadsheet with workplaces.** Collecting and publishing identifying information to expose people to pressure from employers: harassment (s. 2(1)), processing personal information about political opinions without any lawful purpose, possibly a threat (06.5). **Do not create it.** If it already exists, delete it.
4. **Own sign-up form.** ID numbers and voting choices are not needed for a mailing list: delete those fields (s. 8(ב): use only for the database's purpose; less data, less risk). Add a short s. 11 notice. Restrict the spreadsheet to two coordinators with two-factor login.
5. **Mirror test.** If an anti-government group posted the faces and workplaces of the forum's members, the forum could complain to the Authority, write to the group demanding deletion, and consider a claim under s. 29א. The rule protects both sides.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- A sign-up form, mailing list or volunteer spreadsheet from a group you know (or design one for an imaginary campaign).
- Section 3 of [pre-publication-checklist.md](../../templates/pre-publication-checklist.md) and [privacy-complaint.md](../../templates/privacy-complaint.md).
- 60 minutes.

### Steps

1. **List audit.** Write every field the form collects. For each: is it personal information? special sensitivity? needed for the purpose? Delete what is not needed.
2. Write a five-line s. 11 notice in Hebrew for the top of the form: voluntary or not, purpose, controller and contact, to whom it is passed, access and correction rights.
3. Write three security rules for the list (who has access, login, sharing).
4. **Publication check.** Take one photo or video from a campaign and run section 3 of the checklist. Decide: publish, blur, crop or drop.
5. **Complaint draft.** Use this scenario, or a real one: *a municipality's contractor that runs a residents' app sent your phone number and your "supports the protest" tag to a political campaign, which then texted you. You wrote to the contractor 30 days ago asking where your data came from and got no answer.* Draft a complaint to the Privacy Protection Authority using the template, with your s. 13 access request attached as the earlier correspondence.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Posting opponents' faces with mocking captions | Privacy claim (s. 2(4)), sometimes defamation | Remove or blur; apologise if appropriate | Show crowds and public speakers, not private individuals |
| Publishing screenshots of a private chat | s. 2(5) invasion; possibly an offence | Remove; take advice before republishing | Use only material meant for publication, or a strong public-interest case |
| Collecting ID numbers and voting choices "just in case" | Excess sensitive data; harm if the list leaks | Delete the fields and the stored data | Collect only what the purpose needs |
| Sharing the full volunteer list in an open group | A data leak; members exposed | Remove; tell members what happened | Two coordinators, two-factor login, no exports |
| Expecting the Authority to award compensation | Disappointment; lost time on a civil claim | Claim separately within 7 years | Use the Authority for enforcement, the court for compensation |
| Complaining to the Authority without first writing to the body | The complaint is weaker and may be returned | Send an access or deletion request now and attach it | Write to the body first, keep the answer |

## Review

- Privacy is invaded by the acts in s. 2: harassment, filming in private places, humiliating photos, private letters and messages, leaks obtained by invasion, intimate matters.
- Incidental appearance in a public-place photo and a justified public interest (if not false) are defences (s. 18).
- After Amendment 13 (14.8.2025) almost any digital list is a database, and supporters' lists reveal political opinions — special sensitivity.
- Collect only what you need, give a s. 11 notice, answer access requests, secure the list.
- The Privacy Protection Authority enforces and fines; compensation (CPI-linked, s. 29א) needs a civil claim within 7 years.
