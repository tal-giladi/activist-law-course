# 08.2 · Defences: Truth, Public Interest and Good Faith

By the end of this lesson you will be able to say, before you publish, which defence would protect each claim you make about a named person, what evidence that defence needs, and what to do if you went too far — and you will build an evidence file and a right-of-reply record for one real claim.

> [!WARNING]
> A defence is something you must prove in court, with evidence, after you have been sued. Being right is not enough if you cannot show it. Build the evidence file before publishing, not after a demand letter arrives. This lesson is not legal advice.

## Why it matters

Section 1 of the Defamation Law is wide: almost any sharp criticism of a named person is "לשון הרע". What protects activists is chapter 3 of the law — the permitted publications and the defences. Journalists, NGOs and campaigners from every camp rely on the same three tools: true facts on a public issue, honest opinion about public conduct, and complaints sent to the right authority.

Each defence has conditions, and most losses come from missing one condition: a claim that was mostly true but had one unprovable detail at its heart, an opinion based on facts the writer got wrong, or a complaint that was posted on Facebook instead of being sent to the regulator. Knowing the conditions in advance changes how you write.

## The law

> במשפט פלילי או אזרחי בשל לשון הרע תהא זאת הגנה טובה שהדבר שפורסם היה אמת והיה בפרסום ענין ציבורי; הגנה זו לא תישלל בשל כך בלבד שלא הוכחה אמיתותו של פרט לוואי שאין בו פגיעה של ממש.

*Gist:* truth is a full defence only together with public interest; an unproven side detail that causes no real harm does not destroy it. ([s. 14](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_איסור_לשון_הרע#סעיף_14))

> במשפט פלילי או אזרחי בשל לשון הרע תהא זאת הגנה טובה אם הנאשם או הנתבע עשה את הפרסום בתום לב באחת הנסיבות האלו:
> (2) היחסים שבינו לבין האדם שאליו הופנה הפרסום הטילו עליו חובה חוקית, מוסרית או חברתית לעשות אותו פרסום;
> (3) הפרסום נעשה לשם הגנה על ענין אישי כשר של הנאשם או הנתבע, של האדם שאליו הופנה הפרסום או של מי שאותו אדם מעונין בו ענין אישי כשר;
> (4) הפרסום היה הבעת דעה על התנהגות הנפגע בתפקיד שיפוטי, רשמי או ציבורי, בשירות ציבורי או בקשר לענין ציבורי, או על אופיו, עברו, מעשיו או דעותיו של הנפגע במידה שהם נתגלו באותה התנהגות;
> (8) הפרסום היה בהגשת תלונה על הנפגע בענין שבו האדם שאליו הוגשה התלונה ממונה על נפגע, מכוח דין או חוזה, או תלונה שהוגשה לרשות המוסמכת לקבל תלונות על הנפגע או לחקור בענין המשמש נושא התלונה ואולם אין בהוראה זו כדי להקנות הגנה על פרסום אחר של התלונה, של דבר הגשתה או של תוכנה;

*Gist:* good faith protects you in twelve listed situations; for activists the key ones are a duty to inform (2), a legitimate interest (3), opinion on public conduct (4), and a complaint to the competent authority (8) — but not a public post about that complaint. ([s. 15](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_איסור_לשון_הרע#סעיף_15)) The full list also covers criticism of a public work or act (6), a fair report of a public meeting (9), denying earlier defamation (10), and passing information to an editor (11).

> (א) הוכיח הנאשם או הנתבע שעשה את הפרסום באחת הנסיבות האמורות בסעיף 15 ושהפרסום לא חרג מתחום הסביר באותן נסיבות, חזקה עליו שעשה את הפרסום בתום לב.
> (ב) חזקה על הנאשם או הנתבע שעשה את הפרסום שלא בתום לב אם נתקיים בפרסום אחת מאלה:
> (1) הדבר שפורסם לא היה אמת והוא לא האמין באמיתותו;
> (2) הדבר שפורסם לא היה אמת והוא לא נקט לפני הפרסום אמצעים סבירים להיווכח אם אמת הוא אם לא;
> (3) הוא נתכוון על ידי הפרסום לפגוע במידה גדולה משהייתה סבירה להגנת הערכים המוגנים על־ידי סעיף 15.

*Gist:* stay within what is reasonable and you are presumed to be in good faith; publish something false that you did not believe, or did not check reasonably, or that harms more than needed, and you are presumed not to be. ([s. 16](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_איסור_לשון_הרע#סעיף_16))

Section 13 lists **permitted publications** that can never ground a claim: what is said in the Knesset, by the government or the State Comptroller in their role, by judges, parties and witnesses in court proceedings (13(5)), a fair and accurate report of a public court hearing (13(7)), and a fair copy of a public register (13(10)). Section 19 lets the court reduce damages where the publisher apologised, corrected or stopped distribution in the same place and manner, without reservations.

## How it works in practice

### Which defence fits?

| Your claim | Best defence | What you need |
|---|---|---|
| "The council minutes show the CEO approved his brother's tender." | s. 14 truth + public interest; or s. 13(10) if it copies a public register | The minutes; a public-interest reason (public money) |
| "Approving this tender was corrupt in spirit and he should resign." | s. 15(4) opinion on public conduct | True, stated facts that the opinion relies on; no hidden factual accusation |
| A complaint to the State Comptroller, the police, the Ombudsman or a professional regulator about an official | s. 15(8) | Sent to the body competent to deal with it; facts you honestly believe |
| A Facebook post announcing that complaint and repeating its accusations | No s. 15(8) protection for the post | You need s. 14 or another s. 15 head for the post itself |
| Quoting what a witness said in open court | s. 13(7) fair and accurate report | Accurate, balanced, not a gag-ordered hearing |
| Warning your neighbourhood WhatsApp group about a contractor who did not finish your building's work | s. 15(2) or (3) | A real shared interest; measured language; facts you checked |

### Truth: what you must actually prove

- **The sting, not every word.** You must prove the core of what a reasonable reader understood. "Took bribes" needs proof of bribes, not of "acted unethically".
- **Public interest is a separate condition.** True gossip about an official's private life usually fails it; true facts about how public money, power or safety were used usually pass.
- **Evidence you can bring to court:** documents (FOI answers, 05.2; protocols; registers; court rulings), photos and videos with their original files, witnesses willing to testify. "Everybody knows" is not evidence.

### Good faith: how a court looks at your conduct

- Did you **check**? Sources, documents, a second source for a serious accusation.
- Did you give the subject **a chance to respond**, and report the response fairly?
- Is the **tone** proportionate — no more harm than needed to make the point (s. 16(ב)(3))?
- In *Anonymous v. Orbach* (CFH 2121/12, 2014) the Supreme Court held that publication on a matter of significant public interest can be protected under the duty-to-inform head, s. 15(2), when it meets strict standards of responsible journalism; careful verification was central. Activists who publish investigations are judged by a similar yardstick: the more serious the accusation, the more checking is expected.

### When you went too far

Remove or correct quickly, in the same place and with the same prominence, and without "but". An unconditional correction does not erase liability, but s. 19 lets the court take it into account, and it often ends the dispute (08.3).

## Worked example

**Situation.** "Taxpayers' Watch Petah Tikva", a volunteer group focused on municipal spending, learns from an FOI answer (05.2) that the municipality paid ₪1.2 million to a consulting firm without a tender. The firm's registered owner, according to the Companies Registrar, is the brother of the municipal CEO, Dan Levi. The group's draft post: "Dan Levi stole a million shekels for his brother. Corrupt to the bone."

1. **Scan (08.1).** Identifiable person (Levi), lowers him, a post is a publication. Two claims: "stole" (fact — a crime) and "corrupt to the bone" (opinion mixed with that fact). High risk.
2. **Evidence.** The FOI answer (payment, no tender); the Registrar extract (ownership); the municipality's own tenders page (no exemption published). No evidence that Levi decided the payment or gained personally.
3. **Defences.** Truth (s. 14) covers "paid ₪1.2 million without a tender to a firm owned by the CEO's brother": proven, and public money is clearly a public interest. It does **not** cover "stole". Opinion (s. 15(4)) covers a value judgement *on those stated facts*.
4. **Right of reply.** The group emails the municipality's spokesperson and Levi with the facts and a 5-day deadline. The answer: "The firm was hired under the tenders exemption for unique expertise; the CEO recused himself."
5. **Rewrite.** "According to an FOI answer, the municipality paid ₪1.2 million without a tender to a firm owned by the CEO's brother. The municipality says it used the unique-expertise exemption and that the CEO recused himself. We think this needs an independent check, and we have asked the State Comptroller to examine it." Plus a complaint to the Comptroller (05.4) with the full material — protected by s. 15(8).
6. **Result.** Every factual sentence is provable, the opinion is clearly opinion, the response is reported, and the serious question went to the competent body.

The same method applies to a left-leaning group exposing a minister's appointment of a party activist, or to a residents' group in Bnei Brak questioning a yeshiva's budget.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- One medium- or high-risk text from your scan in 08.1.
- Sections 2 and 4 of [pre-publication-checklist.md](../../templates/pre-publication-checklist.md).
- Access to at least one free source of evidence: an FOI answer, a public register (Companies Registrar, amutot register, land registry extract), council minutes or a court decision.
- 60 minutes (plus waiting time for a reply).

### Steps

1. Write the factual core of your text in one sentence — the "sting" a reasonable reader would take away.
2. List every piece of evidence you hold for that core, with its date and source. Mark what is missing.
3. Write one sentence on why the public needs to know.
4. Choose the defence (s. 13, s. 14 or the s. 15 paragraph) and test its conditions with the questions in section 2 of the checklist.
5. Draft a short right-of-reply email to the person or body: the facts you intend to publish, a question, and a reasonable deadline (usually 3–7 days). Send it only if you really intend to publish.
6. Rewrite the text so that every factual sentence is covered by evidence and every opinion is clearly framed as opinion on stated facts; add the response.
7. Decide whether any part should go to an authority instead of (or before) the public (s. 15(8)).

### Template

Sections 2 ("Evidence and defences") and 4 ("Right of reply") of [pre-publication-checklist.md](../../templates/pre-publication-checklist.md).

### How you know it worked

- Your one-sentence sting matches the evidence; nothing in the text claims more than you can prove.
- You can name the defence and the paragraph for each sentence.
- The right-of-reply email is neutral, specific and gives a real deadline.

### Self-check

- [ ] Truth is paired with a public-interest reason.
- [ ] My opinions rest on facts stated in the text or well known, and those facts are true.
- [ ] A complaint went to the competent authority, not only to Facebook.
- [ ] The subject had a fair chance to respond, and I reported the response.
- [ ] I know how to correct in the same place and manner if I got something wrong.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Proving something milder than what you wrote ("unethical" instead of "stole") | The truth defence fails on the sting | Correct the wording publicly | Write only the sting you can prove |
| True facts about private life ("his divorce", "her illness") | No public interest; also a privacy claim (08.4) | Remove | Ask: does this concern public conduct? |
| Opinion based on a wrong fact | s. 15(4) fails because the opinion rests on a falsehood | Correct the fact; restate the opinion | Check every fact your opinion relies on |
| Posting a complaint publicly "for transparency" | s. 15(8) protects only the complaint itself, not the post | Remove the post; keep the complaint | Send to the authority; post only provable facts |
| No right of reply | Weak evidence of good faith; s. 16(ב)(2) risk if a fact turns out false | Offer a reply and publish it now | Make the reply email part of every investigation |
| Correction with "but" or in a smaller place | s. 19 mitigation may not apply | Publish an unconditional correction with equal prominence | Correct once, plainly |

## Review

- Permitted publications (s. 13) can never be sued on; truth (s. 14) needs truth **and** public interest; good faith (s. 15) needs one of twelve listed situations.
- Opinion on public conduct (s. 15(4)) is protected only if the facts behind it are true and stated or known.
- A complaint to the competent authority is protected (s. 15(8)); a public post about it is not.
- Section 16 presumptions turn on checking, belief in truth and proportionate harm; give a right of reply.
- An unconditional correction in the same place reduces damages (s. 19) and often ends the dispute.
