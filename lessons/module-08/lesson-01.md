# 08.1 · Defamation Basics

By the end of this lesson you will be able to look at any post, leaflet, sign or speech that names a person or a company and decide whether it is "לשון הרע" (defamation) in the legal sense, who could sue over it, and how much that could cost — and you will scan five of your own campaign texts and rewrite the risky ones before they go out.

> [!WARNING]
> Publishing claims about identifiable people can cost you money. A court can award tens of thousands of shekels per publication without the plaintiff proving any damage, plus legal costs. This lesson teaches how the law works; it is not legal advice. If you have already received a letter or a claim, go to 08.3 and consider getting a lawyer.

## Why it matters

Activists name names: the official who signed the permit, the factory that pollutes, the rabbi who said something offensive, the NGO funded from abroad, the MK who voted for a bill. Naming is often the point of a campaign — and the Defamation Law is the tool most often used to hit back. A single Facebook post, a share, a WhatsApp forward or a sentence in a speech can lead to a demand letter (08.3) and a claim.

The good news is that the law is built around a few clear questions. Most harsh criticism survives them, especially opinions about public conduct and true facts on public issues (08.2). The trouble almost always comes from the same mistakes: stating as fact something you cannot prove, attaching a crime to a named person, or adding a personal insult that serves no public purpose. Those mistakes are the same whether the campaign is against a settlement outpost, a Pride parade, a mosque, a yeshiva or a high-tech company.

## Key terms

| עברית | Transliteration | English |

## The law

> לשון הרע היא דבר שפרסומו עלול –
> (1) להשפיל אדם בעיני הבריות או לעשותו מטרה לשנאה, לבוז או ללעג מצדם;
> (2) לבזות אדם בשל מעשים, התנהגות או תכונות המיוחסים לו;
> (3) לפגוע באדם במשרתו, אם משרה ציבורית ואם משרה אחרת, בעסקו, במשלח ידו או במקצועו;
> (4) לבזות אדם בשל גזעו, מוצאו, דתו, מקום מגוריו, גילו, מינו, נטייתו המינית או מוגבלותו;

*Gist:* defamation is anything whose publication **may** humiliate, degrade or harm a person — no proof that it actually did is needed — and "person" includes a corporation. ([s. 1](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_איסור_לשון_הרע#סעיף_1))

> (א) פרסום, לענין לשון הרע – בין בעל פה ובין בכתב או בדפוס, לרבות ציור, דמות, תנועה, צליל וכל אמצעי אחר.
> (ב) רואים כפרסום לשון הרע, בלי למעט מדרכי פרסום אחרות –
> (1) אם הייתה מיועדת לאדם זולת הנפגע והגיעה לאותו אדם או לאדם אחר זולת הנפגע;
> (2) אם הייתה בכתב והכתב עשוי היה, לפי הנסיבות, להגיע לאדם זולת הנפגע.

*Gist:* any medium counts; spoken words must actually reach someone other than the target, while writing only has to be likely to reach someone. ([s. 2](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_איסור_לשון_הרע#סעיף_2)) Section 3 adds that the meaning and the target can be implied from the context, so "a certain senior inspector in the Southern District" can still be identifiable.

> לשון הרע על חבר בני אדם או על ציבור כלשהו שאינם תאגיד, דינה כדין לשון הרע על תאגיד, אלא שאין בה עילה לתובענה אזרחית או לקובלנה

*Gist:* defamation of a group that is not a corporation ("the settlers", "the Haredim", "the leftists") gives no one a civil claim or a private complaint; only the Attorney General can prosecute it. ([s. 4](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_איסור_לשון_הרע#סעיף_4)) Incitement offences about groups are a separate matter (06.5).

> המפרסם לשון הרע, בכוונה לפגוע, לשני בני־אדם או יותר זולת הנפגע, דינו – מאסר שנה אחת.

*Gist:* the criminal offence needs intent to harm and publication to at least two people. ([s. 6](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_איסור_לשון_הרע#סעיף_6)) The civil wrong needs only one person besides the target and no intent (s. 7), and the injured person can prosecute the offence privately by a קובלנה (s. 8).

> (ב) במשפט בשל עוולה אזרחית לפי חוק זה, רשאי בית המשפט לחייב את הנתבע לשלם לנפגע פיצוי שלא יעלה על 50,000 שקלים חדשים, ללא הוכחת נזק.
> (ג) במשפט בשל עוולה אזרחית לפי חוק זה, שבו הוכח כי לשון הרע פורסמה בכוונה לפגוע, רשאי בית המשפט לחייב את הנתבע לשלם לנפגע פיצוי שלא יעלה על כפל הסכום כאמור בסעיף קטן (ב), ללא הוכחת נזק.

*Gist:* a court may award compensation without any proof of damage up to a ceiling, doubled where intent to harm is proved; the amounts are linked to the consumer price index from September 1998 (s. 7א(ה)), so the real ceiling today is much higher than ₪50,000. ([s. 7א](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_איסור_לשון_הרע#סעיף_7א))

Section 9 lets the court also order that copies not be distributed and that a correction or the judgment be published.

## How it works in practice

### The four questions

Ask them in this order for every text that names or points to someone.

| # | Question | If "no" |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Is there an **identifiable** person or corporation — by name, photo, job title, or context? | No claim (a group that is not a corporation: s. 4) |
| 2 | Would a reasonable reader think **less** of them — humiliation, contempt, harm to their job or business? | Not defamation |
| 3 | Was it **published** — reached (speech) or could reach (writing) someone other than them? | Not a publication |
| 4 | Is it a statement of **fact** (checkable) or an **opinion** on known facts? | Opinion is easier to defend (08.2) |

If the answer to questions 1–3 is "yes", the text is defamation. That does not mean you will lose: the defences in 08.2 decide that. It means you need evidence or a clear opinion frame before you publish.

### Fact or opinion?

- **Fact:** "The CEO paid the inspector to approve the permit." It can be true or false, and you must be able to prove it.
- **Opinion:** "Approving this permit was a disgrace, and the CEO should resign." A value judgement on known conduct.
- **Disguised fact:** "In my opinion the CEO bribed the inspector." Adding "in my opinion" does not turn a factual accusation into an opinion.

Insults and curses are not automatically defamation; courts look at the context and how a reasonable reader would understand them (Kol Zchut). But a name-calling post adds risk and rarely adds an argument.

### Online

- **Writing your own post, story, video, tweet or comment** is publication. So is a message in a WhatsApp or Telegram group.
- **Sharing** someone else's defamatory post is a new publication by you. A **like** is not (LCA 1239/19, 8.1.2020). Sharing with your own text that clearly rejects the post is different from sharing it as it is.
- **Comments in a long thread** count too. A 2026 private bill would cap compensation for comments at ₪1,000; it has not passed (08.3).

### Who can sue, where, and for how much

- The person or company named or identifiable, within **7 years** of publication (Prescription Law, as summarised by Kol Zchut). A dead person's family can sue only in the limited cases in s. 25; defamation of the dead is otherwise a crime only (s. 5).
- The court depends on the amount claimed: small claims up to the small-claims ceiling, the Magistrates' Court above it — both in [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md). A claim over an online post can be filed in any district.
- Compensation without proof of damage: about ₪84,000 per publication, about ₪169,000 with intent to harm (CPI-linked; see [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)). Courts usually award far less than the ceiling, but the ceiling is what demand letters quote. Proven damage can be claimed on top.
- The **criminal** route (a police complaint under s. 6, or a private קובלנה under s. 8) is rarer; it needs intent to harm and carries up to a year's imprisonment.

## Worked example

**Situation.** "Clean Arava", a residents' group of farmers in the southern Arava, believes that a dairy plant is discharging waste into a stream. Its coordinator, Noa Ben-Ami, drafts five texts for a campaign. You are asked to scan them.

| Text | Q1 identifiable? | Q2 lowers? | Q3 published? | Q4 fact or opinion | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. "The dairy plant is poisoning our stream." (post) | Yes, the company (a corporation) | Yes | Yes | Fact: checkable | Defamation. Publish only with water-test results; better: "Tests on 3 June found X mg/l downstream of the plant's outlet." |
| B. "Plant manager Eli Dror bribed the council inspector to close his eyes." (WhatsApp group of 200) | Yes, Dror; and the inspector, identifiable by role | Yes, a crime | Yes | Fact, very serious | **Do not publish.** No evidence of a bribe. If you suspect one, report it to the police or the State Comptroller (05.4); a complaint to a competent body has its own protection (08.2). |
| C. "The council's decision not to inspect is a scandal. Residents deserve answers." (sign) | The council (a public body) | Mildly | Yes | Opinion on a known decision | Low risk. Keep. |
| D. Sharing a stranger's post: "The plant owners are crooks who don't care if kids get sick." | Yes, the owners | Yes | Yes, a share is publication | Mixed: insult plus factual claim | Do not share as it is. Write your own post with the facts you can prove. |
| E. "Farmers in the Arava are fed up with polluters." (speech) | No specific person | — | — | — | Not defamation of anyone in particular. Keep. |

**Mirror test.** The same scan applies to a campaign by a religious group against a nightclub, or by a settlers' group against an NGO it says "funds terror": text A needs proof, text B needs evidence that you have and the right channel, text C is protected criticism.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- Five texts from a campaign you care about, any side: drafts of your own, or real posts you have seen. At least one should name a person and one should name a company or organisation.
- Section 1 ("Defamation scan") of [pre-publication-checklist.md](../../templates/pre-publication-checklist.md).
- 45 minutes.

### Steps

1. Copy each text exactly, with where it would appear (post, group, sign, speech) and roughly how many people would see it.
2. Run the four questions on each text and fill one row of the scan table.
3. Underline every factual claim. Next to each, write the evidence you have (document, photo, official data, witness) or "none".
4. For each text with a factual claim and "none", write a rewrite: state only what you can prove, or turn it into an opinion on known facts, or send the allegation to the competent authority instead.
5. Remove any insult that adds no argument.
6. Mark each text "publish", "publish after rewrite" or "do not publish".

### Template

Section 1 of [pre-publication-checklist.md](../../templates/pre-publication-checklist.md) — the four-question scan table, the evidence column and the verdict.

### How you know it worked

- Every factual claim about a named person has evidence next to it, or has been removed.
- Your rewrites keep the campaign's message; only the unprovable accusation or the gratuitous insult is gone.
- You can explain in one sentence why each "publish" text is safe.

### Self-check

- [ ] I checked whether each target is identifiable even without a name.
- [ ] I did not treat "in my opinion" as a shield for a factual accusation.
- [ ] I counted shares and group messages as publications.
- [ ] No text accuses a named person of a crime without evidence.
- [ ] I know the compensation ceiling is in current numbers, and that it applies per publication.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Writing "in my opinion he took bribes" | Treated as a factual accusation of a crime; no evidence, no defence | Delete; consider a correction (08.3) | Accuse only with evidence; send suspicions to the police or the Comptroller |
| Sharing a defamatory post "to show what people are saying" | A share is your own publication | Delete the share; post your own framed report | Share only with a clear framing, or not at all |
| Thinking a WhatsApp group is private | Writing that could reach others is a publication; screenshots travel | Delete; apologise in the same group if appropriate | Treat every group as public |
| Naming a low-level employee instead of the decision-maker or the body | A private person sues; little public interest | Rewrite to target the decision and the authority | Criticise institutions and decisions, not junior staff |
| Assuming "they are a public figure, so anything goes" | Public figures can sue too; the defences still need truth or good faith | Check the defences (08.2) | Run the four questions on every text |
| Panicking and deleting everything after a complaint | You lose your own record of what was published | Keep a dated copy before any removal | Archive every campaign text when you publish it |

## Review

- Defamation is anything whose publication may humiliate, degrade or harm an identifiable person or corporation (s. 1); writing only needs to be likely to reach one other person (s. 2).
- Groups that are not corporations cannot sue (s. 4); individuals and companies can, within 7 years.
- A statement of fact needs proof; an honest opinion on known conduct is much safer; "in my opinion" does not convert a fact.
- Shares and group messages are publications; likes are not.
- Compensation without proof of damage is CPI-linked and per publication, doubled with intent to harm; the criminal route needs intent and two recipients.
