# 08.5 · Platforms, Takedowns and Online Harassment

By the end of this lesson you will be able to respond to an online attack in the right order — preserve the evidence, report it on the platform, use the legal channels, and go to the police or the Magistrates' Court when needed — and to respond when your own content is taken down. You will build an evidence log and a response plan for a realistic harassment case.

> [!WARNING]
> If an online threat includes your home, your family or a time and place, treat it as a physical safety issue first: call the police (100) and tell people you trust. Do not confront or "expose" the harasser yourself — that can escalate the danger and expose you to claims. This lesson is not legal advice. Incitement limits on your own posts are covered in [06.5](../module-06/lesson-05.md) and are not repeated here.

## Why it matters

Activists of every camp are targets of online attacks: false accusations, doctored photos, their home address posted with a call to "visit", floods of threats, fake profiles in their name. Women, LGBTQ people and minority activists are hit especially hard, and the attacks come from every political direction. Activists also lose content: posts removed after mass reporting, accounts suspended, videos blocked in Israel after a government request.

There is no single "takedown law" in Israel. Removal happens through a patchwork: the platforms' own rules, their legal-removal channels, police complaints, restraining orders under the Prevention of Stalking Law, and orders a court can give in a defamation or privacy case. Knowing which tool fits which attack — and preserving evidence before anything disappears — is what makes the difference.

## The law

> (א) הטרדה מאיימת היא הטרדתו של אדם בידי אחר בכל דרך שהיא או נקיטת איומים כלפיו, בנסיבות הנותנות בסיס סביר להניח כי המטריד או המאיים עלול לשוב ולפגוע בשלוות חייו, בפרטיותו או בחירותו של האדם או כי הוא עלול לפגוע בגופו.
> (ב) בלי לגרוע מהוראות סעיף קטן (א), הטרדה מאיימת כלפי אדם יכול שתהא, בין השאר, באחד מאלה:
> (3) ביצירת קשר עמו בעל פה, בכתב או בכל אמצעי אחר;
> (4) בפגיעה ברכושו, בשמו הטוב, או בחופש התנועה שלו;

*Gist:* harassment or threats "in any way", where there is reason to expect the harasser to strike again, are threatening harassment — including messages by any means and attacks on a person's good name. ([Prevention of Stalking Law, s. 2](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_למניעת_הטרדה_מאיימת#סעיף_2))

> (ג) הוראות סעיפים קטנים (א) או (ב) לא יחולו אם נוכח בית המשפט כי המעשה שבשלו מתבקש צו מניעת הטרדה מאיימת נעשה בנסיבות שמהוות הגנה טובה במשפט פלילי או אזרחי בשל פגיעה בפרטיות, בהתאם להוראות סעיף 18(2)(ב) עד (ד) או (3) לחוק הגנת הפרטיות

*Gist:* no order will be given against conduct that has a privacy-law defence, such as a justified public interest — so legitimate criticism and documentation of public conduct cannot be silenced by a "harassment" order. ([s. 4(ג)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_למניעת_הטרדה_מאיימת#סעיף_4))

> (א) צו מניעת הטרדה מאיימת יאסור ... על הפוגע לעשות את אלה, כולם או מקצתם ...:
> (1) להטריד את הנפגע, בכל דרך ובכל מקום;
> (2) לאיים על הנפגע;
> (4) ליצור עם הנפגע כל קשר בעל פה, בכתב, או בכל אמצעי אחר;

*Gist:* the order can forbid harassing, threatening or contacting you by any means, for up to 6 months, extendable to a year and in special cases two (ss. 5–6); it can be given ex parte with a hearing within 7 days (s. 7); breach allows arrest (s. 8). ([s. 5](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_למניעת_הטרדה_מאיימת#סעיף_5))

> (א) נוסף לכל עונש וסעד אחר רשאי בית המשפט, במשפט פלילי או אזרחי בשל לשון הרע, לצוות –
> (1) על איסור הפצה של עותקי הפרסום המכיל את לשון הרע או על החרמתם ...
> (2) על פרסום תיקון או הכחשה של דבר המהווה לשון הרע או על פרסום פסק הדין ...

*Gist:* in a defamation case the court can order that the publication stop being distributed and that a correction or the judgment be published. ([Defamation Law, s. 9](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_איסור_לשון_הרע#סעיף_9)) The Privacy Law has a parallel power (s. 29). Publishing a sexual image that may humiliate, without consent, is sexual harassment and carries the same penalty as a deliberate privacy invasion ([Prevention of Sexual Harassment Law, s. 3(א)(5א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_למניעת_הטרדה_מינית)).

## How it works in practice

### Step 1: preserve before anything else

Platforms delete, users edit, accounts vanish. For each item record:

- a full-screen screenshot showing the content, the account name and handle, the date and time;
- the direct URL of the post or profile (copy the link, not just the screenshot);
- for videos, a screen recording;
- a PDF or web-archive copy where possible;
- your own log line: date found, where, what it says, who saw it.

Do not reply, quote-tweet or forward it while you collect: you spread it further and may say something you regret.

### Step 2: choose the route

| Attack | First route | Legal route if that fails |
|---|---|---|
| Threats ("we know where you live", "you will pay") | Platform report (violence or harassment rules) | Police complaint for threats (Penal Law s. 192, 06.5); stalking order |
| Your address, phone or family posted ("doxxing") | Platform report (private information rules) | Stalking order; privacy claim (08.4); police if combined with threats |
| A flood of messages from a known person | Block and report | Stalking order against that person |
| False factual accusations | Platform report rarely helps; legal-removal form | Demand letter, then a defamation claim with s. 9 orders (08.1–08.3) |
| Sexual or intimate images | Platform report (non-consensual intimate imagery) | Police complaint (s. 3(א)(5א)); urgent court order |
| Fake profile in your name | Platform impersonation report | Privacy claim (using a name or photo, s. 2(6) if for profit), police if fraud |

**The platform route.** Every major platform has an in-app report under its community rules (harassment, threats, private information, impersonation), and a separate **legal-removal channel** for content illegal under local law: Google has a "report content for legal reasons" form; Meta restricts content in a single country when it is reported as illegal there, including by members of the public. Use the legal form when the content breaks Israeli law but not the platform's rules (typical for defamation). Quote the Israeli law and attach evidence.

### Step 3: the restraining order (צו מניעת הטרדה מאיימת)

- **Where:** the Magistrates' Court near your home (a family court if the harasser is a relative). You can file without a prior police complaint.
- **Form:** the court's "בקשה לצו מניעת הטרדה מאיימת"; you need the harasser's name, ID number if known, address and phone, plus your evidence log.
- **Cost:** no court fee (Kol Zchut, checked 13.10.2026).
- **Speed:** the judge can give a temporary order the same day without the other side; a hearing with both sides follows within 7 days.
- **Content:** no contact "by any means" — including social-media messages, tags and comments — no threats, no harassment, distance from your home or work.
- **Limits:** you must know who the person is. An order does not delete existing posts; ask the court for the relevant terms and use the platform route. Rejected requests can be appealed to the District Court within 30 days.

### When the attacker is anonymous

In *Mor v. Barak* (LCA 4447/07, 2010) the Supreme Court's majority refused to create a procedure for ordering a website to reveal an anonymous user's identity without legislation. A private bill to create such a procedure (P/301/25) reached the committee stage before a first reading but did not pass before the Knesset dissolved on 17.7.2026. In practice: report to the platform, file a police complaint when there are threats (the police have powers that private people do not), and document everything in case the person is identified later.

### When your own content is removed

- Use the platform's **appeal** at once; quote its own rules and explain the context (news, criticism, documentation).
- Content may have been reported by the State Attorney's **Cyber Unit**, which sends voluntary requests to platforms about content it considers an offence. In *Adalah v. State Attorney's Office* (HCJ 7846/19, 12.4.2021) the High Court dismissed a petition against this practice; petitioners such as Adalah and ACRI argue it is censorship without due process, while the State argues the platforms decide independently. You can ask the platform whether a government request was involved, and file an FOI request about the unit's activity (05.2).
- Archive your own important content regularly, outside the platform.

### Takedown bills: status on 13.10.2026

There is no general takedown law. The "Facebook law" idea — a court-ordered removal procedure for content that is a criminal offence — was a government bill in an earlier Knesset that was never completed. In the 25th Knesset, private bills P/707/25 and P/4173/25 on "removal of content" were only tabled. With the Knesset dissolved and elections on 27.10.2026, none will become law unless tabled again.

## Worked example

**Situation.** Rana Halabi, a Druze activist campaigning for women's representation on local councils, gives a TV interview. Within hours, anonymous accounts post her photo with her home village and street, and comments such as "someone should teach her a lesson at home". One known man from a neighbouring village sends her eight private messages a day, including "I'll see you at the council meeting". A page posts a doctored screenshot showing her "praising" an attack.

1. **Safety first.** Rana tells her family and a friend, and reports the "teach her a lesson at home" comments to the police (100, then a written complaint at the station) as threats.
2. **Preserve.** She screenshots each post and message with handle, URL and time, screen-records the profile pages, and keeps a log.
3. **Platform route.** Reports the address posts as private information and the comments as threats; reports the doctored screenshot as false and harassing; uses the legal-removal channel for the doctored screenshot as defamation under Israeli law.
4. **Restraining order.** Against the known man: she files in the Magistrates' Court with her log. The judge gives a temporary order that day — no contact by any means, no presence near her home or the council building during meetings — and sets a hearing within a week.
5. **Defamation.** The doctored screenshot accuses her of supporting terror. If the page owner is identified, a demand letter and a claim with a s. 9 order are possible; meanwhile she posts a short factual denial on her own page — protected by s. 15(10) of the Defamation Law.
6. **Mirror test.** The same steps fit an ultra-Orthodox woman activist targeted by secular accounts, or a left-wing activist whose address is posted by right-wing accounts. The law does not ask which side the victim is on.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- A real case you know of, or the Rana scenario above.
- Section 5 of [pre-publication-checklist.md](../../templates/pre-publication-checklist.md).
- 50 minutes.

### Steps

1. Build an evidence log (a table: date found, platform, URL, account, exact text or description, screenshot file name, who saw it). Fill at least six rows from the scenario.
2. Classify each row: threat, private information, false accusation, contact from a known person, intimate image, impersonation.
3. For each class, write the first route and the legal route from the table above.
4. Write a short platform report (5 lines) for the worst item, quoting the platform's rule.
5. List what you would bring to the Magistrates' Court for a restraining order, and the three terms you would ask for.
6. Write a safety note: who you tell, where you will not go alone, what you do if a threat names a time and place.
7. Write a one-paragraph public response that states facts and does not attack the harassers personally.

### Template

Section 5 ("Online safety and harassment risk") of [pre-publication-checklist.md](../../templates/pre-publication-checklist.md), plus your evidence log.

### How you know it worked

- Every item in the log has a URL, a date and a screenshot reference.
- Each item has a route, and the routes match the type of attack.
- The restraining-order list names a specific person and specific conduct.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reporting first, so the post is removed before you documented it | No evidence for the police or court | Ask others for screenshots; check archives | Preserve, then report |
| "Exposing" the harasser's own address in return | Privacy and harassment claims against you; escalation | Remove at once | Use the platform, the police and the court |
| Using the in-app report for defamation | "Does not violate our rules" | Use the legal-removal form, citing Israeli law | Pick the channel by the kind of harm |
| Asking for a restraining order against an unknown account | The court cannot address an order to nobody | Police complaint; collect identifying details | Use the order for known people |
| Seeking a "harassment" order against someone who only criticises your public role | Refused under s. 4(ג); costs possible | Withdraw; answer the criticism publicly | Separate criticism from harassment |
| Keeping your campaign archive only on the platform | Account suspended, years of work lost | Request a data download; appeal | Back up regularly |

## Review

- Preserve evidence (screenshot, URL, date, log) before reporting, replying or deleting.
- Use the platform's rules for threats, doxxing and impersonation, and its legal-removal channel for content illegal under Israeli law, such as defamation.
- A restraining order against threatening harassment is free, fast and can cover online contact — but needs a known person and cannot be used to silence legitimate criticism (s. 4(ג)).
- Courts can order distribution to stop in defamation and privacy cases (s. 9; s. 29); there is no unmasking procedure and no general takedown law as of October 2026.
- When your content is removed, appeal, ask whether a government request was involved, and keep your own backups.
