# 06.2 · Permits: When You Need One

By the end of this lesson you will be able to tell, for any protest, whether you need a police licence, a safety notice, a special permit or nothing at all; to file the right form on time; and to answer the conditions the police attach. Most protests in Israel need no licence — but organisers who get this wrong either give up a right they had or march without a licence they needed.

## Why it matters

The licensing rules look simple — 50 people, 5 days — but the details decide almost everything. A rally of 3,000 against government corruption needs no police licence; a march of 60 people does. A vigil outside the Knesset needs the Speaker's permit from 10 people. A protest of 500 or more needs a written notice to the Interior Ministry even when the police licence is not required. And when a licence is required and you march without it, or break its conditions, the march becomes an unlawful assembly under the law, which is the legal basis for dispersal and arrest (06.3).

The police procedure is written so that organisers and police talk before the event. Organisers who apply early, give real details and propose their own conditions usually get a workable licence. Organisers who say nothing usually get conditions they did not choose.

## The law

> ”אסיפה“ – חמישים איש או יותר שהתקהלו כדי לשמוע נאום או הרצאה על נושא בעל ענין מדיני או כדי לדון בנושא כזה;
> ”תהלוכה“ – חמישים איש או יותר המהלכים יחד, או המתקהלים כדי להלך יחד, ממקום למקום, בין שהם בתנועה ממש ובין אם לאו, בין שהם ערוכים בצורה כלשהי ובין אם לאו;

*Gist:* an assembly is 50+ people gathered to hear or discuss a political speech; a procession is 50+ people moving (or gathering to move) together. ([פקודת המשטרה, s. 83](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/פקודת_המשטרה#סעיף_83))

> סבור מפקד משטרת המחוז כי קיום הבטחון הציבורי או הסדר הציבורי מחייבים זאת, רשאי הוא לדרוש, בהודעה לציבור, כללית או מיוחדת, שכל הרוצה להועיד, לארגן או לקיים במחוזו אסיפה או תהלוכה יבקש בכתב מאת הממונה רשיון לכך, לא פחות מחמישה ימים או זמן אחר שיפורש בהודעה לפני היום שנועד לקיום האסיפה או התהלוכה.

*Gist:* where the district commander has published a notice (all districts have), organisers must apply in writing at least five days ahead. ([s. 84(א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/פקודת_המשטרה#סעיף_84))

> (1) ליתן את הרשיון;
> (2) ליתן את הרשיון בערובה או בתנאים או בסייגים אחרים שיראה לנכון לחייב בהם, והתנאים והסייגים יירשמו על גבי הרשיון;
> (3) לסרב ליתן את הרשיון.

*Gist:* the commander may grant, grant with conditions or a deposit, or refuse; conditions must be written on the licence. ([s. 85](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/פקודת_המשטרה#סעיף_85)) No fee is charged for a licence (s. 86).

> רק אסיפות בעניינים שהם בבירור מדיניים, כפשוטו של מושג זה במובן הצר של המילה, דהיינו בקשר לסוגיות מתחום יחסי החוץ והביטחון של המדינה טעונות רישיון. כל אסיפה אחרת אינה דורשת רשיון. יודגש כי בנוגע לתהלוכות, לא חל כל שינוי

*Gist:* after HCJ 6536/17, only assemblies about foreign relations and security need a licence; processions of 50+ still always need one. ([AG guideline 3.1200, para. 8](https://free-justice.openapi.gov.il/free/moj/portal/rest/searchpredefinedapi/v1/SearchPredefinedApi/Documents/LegalAdvisor/e9~GhUz~~oS7ArbJp93YY+iCnG5cZ2jRGdXIgwg+9PE=))

> לא יאוחר מ־5 ימים לפני עריכת עצרת עם ימסור מארגן עצרת העם למי ששר הפנים ימנה אותו לכך, הודעה בכתב על מקומה, מועדה וכן על תכניתה אם יש בה חשיבות לענין בטיחות המשתתפים בעצרת והנמצאים בשכנותה

*Gist:* a rally of 500+ needs a written safety notice five days ahead to the Interior Ministry's appointee, even when no police licence is needed; without the notice it needs a permit (s. 7). ([חוק הבטיחות במקומות ציבוריים, s. 5](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הבטיחות_במקומות_ציבוריים#סעיף_5))

## How it works in practice

### Step 1 — Do you need a licence?

| Your event | Police licence? | Other requirement |
|---|---|---|
| Fewer than 50 people, any form | No | The police may still set conditions under Police Ordinance ss. 3–5 |
| 50+ standing outdoors with signs and chants, no political speech | No | Coordination form recommended |
| 50+ outdoors hearing speeches on rule of law, corruption, cost of living, religion and state, the environment | No (HCJ 6536/17; AG guideline para. 8) | Coordination form recommended |
| 50+ outdoors hearing speeches on a peace deal, borders, a military operation | Yes | — |
| 50+ indoors (hall, auditorium) | No | Owner's consent |
| 50+ walking from place to place, or a vehicle convoy | Yes, whatever the subject | — |
| 500+ (any of the above) | Depends on the rows above | Safety notice to the Interior Ministry District Commissioner, 5 days ahead |
| 10+ at the Knesset plaza | — | Knesset Speaker's permit (Knesset Building and Precincts Law s. 7) |
| Speech or signs at a Jewish holy site | — | Coordination under the Holy Places Regulations |

### Step 2 — File the form

The police procedure (221.110.19, released under FOI) sets the route:

- **Form A** (בקשה לקבלת רישיון) for a licensable assembly or procession. **Form B** (יידוע ותיאום) for any other protest of 50+, voluntary but useful.
- File with the **operations officer (קצין אג"ם) of the police station** where the event takes place: by hand, by fax to the station's published number, or by email where the station accepts it. Keep proof of receipt.
- File **at least 5 days ahead**. For a sudden event (a decision announced this morning), file at the first opportunity and explain the urgency; the commander may still decide, but the rule is discretionary.
- The form asks for organisers' names and ID numbers, the subject, expected numbers, times, place, route, stage and sound, stewards, vehicles, and whether you notified the Interior Ministry and the municipality. You may be asked to come to the station within 24 hours.
- The district commander decides. Under the procedure, if you filed 5 days ahead, the decision and conditions reach you **up to 48 hours before** the event and are published on the police site.

A Hebrew template for both forms, the safety notice and a reply to conditions is in [protest-notice.md](../../templates/protest-notice.md).

### Step 3 — Read the conditions

The procedure has a "bank" of standard conditions: stewards in yellow vests, hours, boundaries, no blocking roads beyond what was approved, sound limits, a working PA, a ban on "incitement signs". For each condition ask:

1. Is it about **time, place or manner**, or about the **message**?
2. Is it backed by a concrete risk?
3. Is there a less restrictive option you can propose?

A **security deposit** (ערובה) is allowed only for licensable events and, under the procedure, only in exceptional cases (a concrete fear the organiser will not comply, or a history of breaking conditions), set by the organiser's means. A condition that hides the protest from its audience (a route through empty streets at 2 a.m.) is not a fair balance (AG guideline, section ז).

### Public figures' homes

Under the AG guideline (section ה) and *Dayan v. Wilk*:

- **Elected officials and senior office-holders.** A protest at their private home is allowed only where there is no effective alternative. Even then, the police set distance, hours and noise conditions.
- **Junior civil servants.** The police do not allow protests at or near the home of a civil servant who does not set policy. If such a protest starts, the police move it away.
- **Official residences.** The Prime Minister's and the President's residences are not private homes, so the ordinary rules apply there.

## Worked example

**Situation.** "Mothers for Accessibility", a disability-rights group, plans a march of about 400 people in Haifa: from Paris Square to the municipality, with a short rally outside it. The demand is that the city make its bus stops accessible. The march is on a Sunday at 17:00; today is Monday. The organiser, Rina Haddad, asks what she must do.

1. **Licence?** A march of 50+ is a procession: a licence is required whatever the subject. The rally at the end, about accessibility, is not a "political matter" in the narrow sense, but the procession already needs a licence.
2. **Safety notice?** About 400 people, so a public rally of 500+ is not expected. Still, if numbers might pass 500, Rina sends the safety notice as a precaution.
3. **Timing.** Sunday is six days away: she files Form A today with the Haifa station operations officer by email, phones to confirm, and saves the confirmation.
4. **Details.** The form gives the route street by street, assembly at 16:30, dispersal by 19:00, 15 stewards, a small PA, two vehicles for wheelchair users, and alternative dates.
5. **Conditions received Friday.** The licence moves the route off the main road onto a parallel street, limits the PA to 70 dB, and requires stewards. Rina accepts the PA limit and the stewards. She answers the route change in writing: the parallel street has no step-free crossing, and moving there would endanger the marchers. She proposes the original route in the right lane only, for 30 minutes.
6. **Result.** The station agrees to the right-lane option. Rina keeps the licence and conditions on her phone and briefs the stewards (06.3).

## Do it yourself

### You need

- The protest you described in 06.1.
- The [protest-notice.md](../../templates/protest-notice.md) template.
- The website or phone number of the police station where the protest will take place (police.gov.il, station pages).
- 50 minutes.

### Steps

1. Use the Step 1 table to decide: police licence, coordination form, safety notice, Knesset or holy-site permit, or nothing.
2. Count back 5 days from your protest date and write the last filing date. If 500+ may come, write the same date for the safety notice.
3. Fill in Form A or Form B from the template completely. Invent nothing; leave unknown fields marked "to be updated".
4. Write three conditions you would accept in advance (for example stewards, hours, a sound limit) and one you would resist, with your reason and a less restrictive alternative.
5. If your protest is near a public figure's home, plan the distance, hours and noise level, and name an effective alternative site, or explain why none exists.
6. Draft a two-paragraph reply to an imagined condition that moves you away from your audience.
7. File for real only if you are actually organising the protest.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> If you file for real: act in your own name only, as an organiser of a protest you actually plan. This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing; the police procedure and forms may have been updated since 2021.

### Template

[protest-notice.md](../../templates/protest-notice.md) — Form A (licence request), Form B (coordination notice), the safety notice for a 500+ rally, and a reply to conditions.

### How you know it worked

- You can state in one sentence why your protest does or does not need a licence, citing s. 83 or AG guideline para. 8.
- Your filing date is at least 5 days before the event, and you know how you will prove receipt.
- Every condition you would resist has a less restrictive alternative written next to it.

### Self-check

- [ ] Assembly, procession or neither — decided with the narrow "political matter" test.
- [ ] 500+? Safety notice planned.
- [ ] Knesset plaza or holy site? Special permit planned.
- [ ] Filed at the station of the event's area, 5+ days ahead, with proof of receipt.
- [ ] Conditions checked: time, place, manner — not message.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Marching 50+ people without a licence because "the subject isn't political" | The procession is an unlawful assembly (s. 89); dispersal and arrests are lawful | Stop moving and turn it into a stationary protest; apply for the next date | Remember: processions always need a licence |
| Applying for a licence for a rally that needs none | You invite conditions and delay you did not need | Withdraw, or file Form B (coordination) instead | Check the narrow "political matter" test first |
| Filing 2 days ahead without explaining urgency | The commander may refuse for lack of time | File at once with a written reason for the urgency | Count back 5 days when you pick the date |
| No safety notice for a 3,000-person rally | The rally needs a permit (Safety Law s. 7); the police may stop it | File the notice now and ask for a permit | Add the 500+ check to your plan |
| Arguing with the station by phone only | No record; conditions stay | Put every request and answer in writing | Write, keep copies, ask for reasons |
| Protesting at a junior official's home | The police will move you; you may face harassment complaints | Move to the office or a public site | Target the decision-maker at their workplace |

## Review

- Only assemblies on foreign relations and security, and every procession of 50+, need a police licence; most rallies do not.
- 500+ people need a safety notice to the Interior Ministry 5 days ahead; the Knesset plaza needs the Speaker's permit from 10 people.
- File with the station operations officer at least 5 days ahead; the licence is free; keep proof of receipt.
- Conditions must be about time, place and manner, backed by concrete risk; answer the ones you dispute in writing with an alternative.
- A licensable event without a licence, or in breach of its conditions, is an unlawful assembly — the gateway to dispersal and arrest.
