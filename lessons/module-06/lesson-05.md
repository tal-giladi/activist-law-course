# 06.5 · Social Media, Signs and the Limits of Incitement

By the end of this lesson you will be able to check a sign, a chant or a post against the criminal limits on speech, rewrite it so that the message stays sharp and the risk goes away, and respond calmly when an officer demands that a sign or flag come down.

> [!WARNING]
> Incitement and terror-support offences carry prison sentences of up to five years, and posts are permanent evidence. Group admins and people who share or "hold for distribution" a forbidden publication can be liable too. When in doubt, do not publish. This lesson is not legal advice; for a specific text that worries you, ask a lawyer before posting.

## Why it matters

Israeli law protects harsh, offensive and shocking political speech. It does not protect calls to violence, incitement to racism, or praise of terror that is likely to lead to it. The line is drawn by a few tests that are easy to learn and easy to forget in a heated moment — and a single chant at a march or a post in a WhatsApp group can turn a protest movement into a criminal file, or hand its opponents the story.

The limits apply to every camp: marchers chanting about another national group, people posting after an attack, critics calling for harm to judges, politicians or protest leaders. The tests below do not depend on who speaks.

## The law

> (א) המפרסם קריאה לעשיית מעשה אלימות, או דברי שבח, אהדה או עידוד למעשה אלימות, תמיכה בו או הזדהות עמו (בסעיף זה – פרסום מסית), ועל פי תוכנו של הפרסום המסית והנסיבות שבהן פורסם, יש אפשרות ממשית שיביא לעשיית מעשה אלימות, דינו – מאסר חמש שנים.

*Gist:* calling for, praising or supporting violence is an offence when its content and circumstances create a real possibility that violence will follow. ([חוק העונשין, s. 144ד2(א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_העונשין#סעיף_144ד2))

> (א) המפרסם דבר מתוך מטרה להסית לגזענות, דינו – מאסר חמש שנים.
> (ב) לענין סעיף זה, אין נפקא מינה אם הפרסום הביא לגזענות או לא ואם היה בו אמת או לא.

*Gist:* publishing with the purpose of inciting racism is an offence whether or not it worked and whether or not it is true. ([s. 144ב](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_העונשין#סעיף_144ב)) A prosecution needs the Attorney General's written consent (s. 144ה); quoting religious texts is not an offence unless done to incite racism (s. 144ג(ב)).

> (ב) העושה אחד מאלה, דינו – מאסר חמש שנים:
> (1) מפרסם קריאה ישירה לביצוע מעשה טרור;
> (2) מפרסם דברי שבח, אהדה או עידוד למעשה טרור, תמיכה בו או הזדהות עמו, ועל פי תוכנו של הפרסום והנסיבות שבהן פורסם יש אפשרות ממשית שיביא לעשיית מעשה טרור.

*Gist:* a direct call to terror is always an offence; praise of terror is an offence when there is a real possibility it will lead to terror. Publicly identifying with a terror organisation — praise, its flag, symbol, slogan or anthem — carries three years (s. 24(א)). ([חוק המאבק בטרור, s. 24](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_המאבק_בטרור#סעיף_24))

> הצורך באופן שיטתי ומתמשך פרסומים של ארגון טרור המנוי בתוספת השנייה ... בנסיבות שמעידות על הזדהותו עם ארגון הטרור, דינו – מאסר שנה

*Gist:* a temporary provision, in force from 12.11.2023 and renewed for two years from 27.11.2025, makes systematic consumption of certain terror organisations' publications an offence in circumstances that show identification with them. (s. 24(ג1))

> (א) המפקח הכללי רשאי לאסור הנפתו, הצגתו או פרישתו של דגל או סמל שיש בו כדי לעורר להפרעת השלום.

*Gist:* the Inspector General may ban a flag or symbol likely to provoke a breach of the peace; police may remove it (s. 82(ג)). ([פקודת המשטרה, s. 82](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/פקודת_המשטרה#סעיף_82)) In HCJ 1386/22 *Diab* (7.11.2022), as summarised by the IDI, the Court held that police may prevent the waving of a Palestinian flag only where there is a high probability of a serious breach of the peace, judged case by case.

## How it works in practice

### The quick test for any sign, chant or post

| Question | If yes | Offence to check |
|---|---|---|
| Does it call for, praise or support violence against people? | Is there a real possibility it leads to violence (audience, timing, tension, reach)? | s. 144ד2 |
| Does it target a group for its colour, race or national-ethnic origin, with hostility or humiliation? | Is the purpose to incite racism? | s. 144ב (AG consent needed) |
| Does it praise a terror act, or display a terror organisation's flag, slogan or symbol? | In public, to identify with it, or with a real possibility of leading to terror? | Counter-Terrorism Law s. 24 |
| Does it threaten a specific person with harm to body, liberty, property, reputation or livelihood, to frighten them? | — | s. 192 (threats) |
| Is it a gross insult to religious belief or feelings? | — | s. 173 (rarely prosecuted, but used) |
| Is it a false factual claim about a named person? | — | Defamation (Module 8, civil and criminal) |

Harsh criticism of the government, insulting comparisons, satire, anger and "the government must fall" are protected. The AG guideline says the police may limit a protest for its content only where an offence such as incitement or sedition is reasonably suspected (para. 13(ג)), and a police demand at the scene should target the specific sign (para. 22(א)).

### At the protest: when an officer wants a sign down

1. Ask, calmly: which sign, and which offence?
2. If the sign is clearly lawful, say so once, and ask the officer to note the demand and his name; if he insists, take it down and complain afterwards (06.4). Resisting turns a speech question into an obstruction case.
3. Film the sign and the demand (06.3). Keep the sign.
4. Organisers: put your own sign rules in the briefing, and remove any sign that crosses the line before the police do.

### Online

- **The same offences apply** to posts, stories, videos, comments and group messages. A "share", a "like" with a comment, or a group admin who keeps a post up can be part of publication or of holding for distribution (s. 144ד3; s. 24(ג)).
- **Context counts.** The "real possibility" test looks at the audience and timing: the same words posted to 30 friends on a quiet day differ from a post to 50,000 followers an hour after an attack.
- **Fair reporting.** Quoting a forbidden statement to criticise or report it is protected when it is a fair and accurate report (s. 144ג(א); s. 144ד2(ג)). Make the frame obvious.
- **Platforms** remove content under their own rules, often faster than the law requires; appeals and takedown requests are covered in Module 8.

## Worked example

**Situation.** A conservative religious group, "Jerusalem Family Values", plans a quiet protest along the route of the Jerusalem Pride March, and a social-media campaign before it. The organiser, Shira Avraham, collects the proposed texts and asks for a legal check. After the 2015 stabbing at that march, she knows the police will be strict.

1. **Sign A: a verse from Leviticus.** Quoting a religious text is not racism incitement (s. 144ג(ב)); sexual orientation is not one of the racism grounds in s. 144א anyway. The real question is violence: on its own, held silently, there is no real possibility of violence. **Keep**, though the police may set distance conditions on the protest to separate it from the march.
2. **Sign B: "They will burn in hell".** A religious belief, not a call for anyone to act. Offensive, but no call to violence. **Keep** — or rewrite to state the group's position positively.
3. **Post C: "Whoever stops this march is doing God's work — be there."** Along this route, where an attacker stabbed marchers in 2015, "stopping" the march can be read as a call to physical action. There is a real possibility of violence. **Rewrite** to "Join our silent protest on the pavement, by the rules agreed with the police."
4. **Post D: a photo of a march organiser with his home address.** It is not incitement on its face. In this context, though, it could be a threat or harassment, and it invades privacy (Module 8). **Delete.**
5. **Briefing.** No chants and no physical contact; stewards remove any sign not on the approved list; the group's liaison carries the list.
6. **Mirror test.** For a Pride group's counter-campaign, or for an anti-occupation group's posts after an attack, ask the same questions. Is there a call to violence, praise of terror, a threat, or a target's home address? Signs that are harsh but non-violent survive; calls and threats do not.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- Five signs, chants or posts for your protest — drafts, or ones you have seen in a campaign you know (any side).
- Section 4 ("Messages") of [protest-legal-plan.md](../../templates/protest-legal-plan.md).
- 45 minutes.

### Steps

1. Write each text exactly, with where and how it will appear (sign, chant, post, audience size).
2. Run the quick-test table on each: which offence could apply, and why or why not.
3. For any "maybe", write a rewrite that keeps the political point and removes the risk element (the call to act, the target's personal details, the praise of violence).
4. Write the group's sign and chant rules in five lines for the briefing.
5. Write a two-sentence script for the moment an officer asks a sign to come down.
6. Run the mirror test on your strongest text: would you accept the same text from your opponents?

### How you know it worked

- Every text has a named offence considered and a one-line reason.
- Your rewrites keep the political message; only the risk element is gone.
- Your sign rules can be read aloud in under a minute.

### Self-check

- [ ] No call to violence, praise of terror, threat or target's home address.
- [ ] Hostile statements about a national or ethnic group checked against s. 144ב.
- [ ] Reports of others' statements are clearly framed as reports.
- [ ] Group admins know they must remove forbidden posts.
- [ ] I will take a sign down if ordered, and complain later.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Posting in anger right after an attack or a violent event | The "real possibility" test is most easily met then | Delete at once; do not repost; consult a lawyer if contacted by police | Wait a day; have someone else read it |
| Publishing an opponent's home address with a call to "visit" | Threats or incitement investigation; privacy claim | Delete; apologise if appropriate | Target institutions and public offices, not homes |
| Leaving a member's violent post in the group | The admin may be part of the distribution | Remove it and post the group rules | Write and enforce group rules |
| Refusing physically to lower a sign | Obstruction charge; the sign is lost anyway | Lower it, film, complain | Brief the group: comply first, complain later |
| Assuming "it's just a joke" | Context, not intent alone, decides the violence test | Remove and clarify | Ask: could a reader act on it? |
| Self-censoring lawful harsh criticism | The protest loses its voice | Use the quick test to show it is lawful | Know that offence and anger are protected |

## Review

- Harsh, offensive and satirical political speech is protected; calls to violence, incitement to racism, praise of terror and threats are not.
- Incitement to violence and praise of terror use the "real possibility" test (content plus circumstances); racism incitement needs a purpose and the AG's consent to prosecute.
- Displaying a terror organisation's flag or symbol in identification with it is an offence; other flags can be restricted only on a high probability of serious disorder.
- Posts, shares and group admins are all part of publication; timing and audience size matter.
- At a protest, ask which sign and which offence, comply if ordered, document, and complain afterwards.
