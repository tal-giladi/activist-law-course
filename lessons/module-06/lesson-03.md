# 06.3 · At the Protest: Police Powers, Dispersal and Filming Police

By the end of this lesson you will know what the police may do at a protest and in what order, the limits on detention and arrest, your right to film officers, and how to prepare yourself and your group so that a confrontation does not become an injury, a criminal file or lost evidence.

> [!WARNING]
> Protests can turn physical. Police may use reasonable force to disperse an unlawful assembly, and you can be detained or arrested even when you believe you did nothing wrong. Never resist physically, even an arrest you think is unlawful — that is a separate offence and the most common way a protest becomes a criminal case. Argue later, in writing, with evidence. If you or someone near you is hurt, get medical help first and document second. This lesson is not legal advice; for detention and interrogation rights in depth, see Module 7.

## Why it matters

Most protest cases are decided by what happened in a few minutes: whether the officer gave a dispersal order, whether the protester stepped back or pushed, whether anyone filmed it. Protesters who know the sequence the law requires can comply where they must, refuse where they may, and record the moment the police skip a step. Protesters who do not know it either back down from rights they had or end up charged with obstructing an officer.

## The law

> שלושה אנשים לפחות שנתקהלו לשם עבירה, או שנתקהלו למטרה משותפת, ואפילו כשרה, ומתנהגים באופן הנותן לאנשים שבסביבה יסוד סביר לחשוש שהמתקהלים יעשו מעשה שיפר את השלום, או שבעצם התקהלותם יעוררו אנשים אחרים, ללא צורך וללא עילה מספקת, להפר את השלום, הרי זו התקהלות אסורה, והמשתתף בהתקהלות אסורה, דינו – מאסר שנה אחת.

*Gist:* even a lawful-purpose gathering of three or more becomes an unlawful assembly if the participants' own conduct gives reasonable grounds to fear a breach of the peace. ([חוק העונשין, s. 151](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_העונשין#סעיף_151)) The fear must come from the protesters' conduct, not from others' opposition to the protest (AG guideline 3.1200, para. 20(ד)).

> ממונה על מחוז או קצין מחוז או שופט בית משפט שלום, ובהעדרם – שוטר בדרגת מפקח משנה ומעלה, הרואה שלושה אנשים לפחות מתפרעים, או חושש שהם עומדים להתפרע, רשאי, לאחר שהודיע על נוכחותו בתקיעת חצוצרה, בשריקת משרוקית או בכל אמצעי כיוצא באלה או ביריית אור וירי מאקדח – להורות להם שיתפזרו בשקט.

*Gist:* a formal dispersal order comes from a senior officer, after announcing presence, and tells the crowd to disperse quietly; staying on after a reasonable time is a serious offence (s. 155). ([s. 153](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_העונשין#סעיף_153))

> (ב) בכל מקרה, לא יעוכב אדם או כלי רכב לפרק זמן העולה על שלוש שעות; ואולם בעיכוב הקשור למספר רב של מעורבים, רשאי הקצין הממונה להאריך את משך העיכוב לפרק זמן נוסף שלא יעלה על שלוש שעות נוספות, מנימוקים שיירשמו.

*Gist:* detention is limited to three hours, extendable by three more, with written reasons, when many people are involved. ([חוק המעצרים, s. 73(ב)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_סדר_הדין_הפלילי_(סמכויות_אכיפה_–_מעצרים)#סעיף_73))

> (א) שוטר שהוא לבוש מדים יענוד תג זיהוי גלוי לעין הנושא את שמו ושם משפחתו.
> ...
> (ד) שוטר ימסור את פרטי זהותו ואת מספרו האישי, לבקשת כל אדם בכל ענין הקשור להיותו שוטר, בין אם הוא לבוש מדים ובין אם לאו.

*Gist:* a uniformed officer wears a visible name tag, and any officer must give their name and personal number on request — unless doing so would defeat the action or endanger someone (s. 5א(ה)). ([פקודת המשטרה, s. 5א](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/פקודת_המשטרה#סעיף_5א))

> שימוש במכונית להתזת מים (בסעיף זה – מכת"זית) לשם פיזור הפגנה ייעשה באמצעות מים נקיים בלבד ללא תוספת חומר, ובכלל זה ללא תוספת של צבע או ריח.

*Gist:* a 2026 amendment (תשפ״ו) limits water cannons to clean water; from 15.2.2027 their use must be filmed, the footage kept a year, and a person hurt may request it. ([s. 89א](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/פקודת_המשטרה#סעיף_89א))

> (ב) על אף הוראות פרק זה, לא ייערך חיפוש כאמור בסעיף קטן (א), אלא על־פי צו של שופט

*Gist:* searching the contents of a computer — including a phone — needs a judge's warrant that expressly allows it. ([פקודת סדר הדין הפלילי (מעצר וחיפוש), s. 23א(ב)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/פקודת_סדר_הדין_הפלילי_(מעצר_וחיפוש)#סעיף_23א))

## How it works in practice

### The order the AG guideline expects

AG guideline 3.1200, para. 22, describes the steps the police take when they believe an offence is being committed:

1. **Demand** that the protesters stop the specific conduct: get off the road, take down an inciting sign, go back to the licensed route. The guideline says this should be preferred to dispersal.
2. **Disperse**: order protesters who are committing an offence (for example, blocking a road without a licence) to leave, and use reasonable force against those who resist.
3. **Detain or arrest**: ask for name and address, ask the person to come to the station, or arrest under the Arrests Law.
4. **Charge**: an indictment for unlawful assembly must set out the facts that created the reasonable fear.

### Detention or arrest?

| | Detention (עיכוב) | Arrest (מעצר) |
|---|---|---|
| Ground | Reasonable suspicion of an offence, to identify, question or deliver documents (s. 67) | Reasonable grounds to believe an offence was committed, plus a statutory ground such as danger or failure to identify (s. 23) |
| Limit | 3 hours; +3 with written reasons when many are involved (s. 73) | Brought before a judge within 24 hours (Module 7) |
| What you ask | "Am I detained or arrested? What for? What is your name and number?" | The same, then: "I want a lawyer" — then stay silent except for identity details (Module 7) |
| Paperwork | A detention report if you were taken to the station (s. 74) | Arrest documents; a copy of any warrant |

You must give your name and identity details when lawfully asked; refusing can itself justify detention or arrest. Everything else can wait for a lawyer.

### Filming police

- **It is lawful.** No statute forbids filming police in a public place, and in most cases an officer may not order you to stop or touch your camera (Kol Zchut). There is no published police procedure on citizens filming police; the rule comes from the general freedom to do what no law forbids, and privacy law generally does not forbid filming officers performing public duties in a public place (Module 8 covers privacy).
- **The limit is obstruction.** Filming from a reasonable distance is not obstruction. Physically blocking an arrest, pushing a phone into an officer's face or refusing to move back from an operation can be (Penal Law s. 275).
- **Your phone.** The police may seize a phone as evidence in some cases, but searching its contents needs a judge's warrant (s. 23א). Do not unlock it on request; say you do not consent to a search. Back up footage to the cloud as you film.
- **Make it evidence.** Keep the time and date on. Film the officer's tag or the unit, the street sign, and the moments before and after the incident. Do not edit the original; make copies for sharing. Write a short note within the day: who, what, when, where.
- **Police filming you.** The police film protests routinely and use body cameras; ask that body cameras be switched on when force is used, and mention the camera in any complaint (06.4).

### Before you go: the safety brief

- **Personal**: ID card, charged phone with cloud backup, a lawyer's or hotline number written on your arm, water, any medication. Leave weapons, knives and anything that looks like one at home.
- **Group**: who the stewards are, who is the police liaison, who films, who does not engage, a meeting point if separated, and a list of detainees with their contacts.
- **Liaison**: the organisers' liaison carries the licence or coordination record and talks to the senior officer on the spot, in writing where possible.

## Worked example

**Situation.** About 200 Haredi yeshiva students block Route 4 near Bnei Brak in protest against draft arrests. There is no licence and no coordination. Officers announce through a loudspeaker that the road must be cleared; most protesters move to the pavement, about 30 stay seated. Police drag the seated protesters off the road; one, Moshe Klein, is detained. His friend Ari films the detention from the pavement. An officer without a visible name tag orders Ari to stop filming and grabs for his phone.

1. **Was the gathering unlawful?** Blocking a main road gives reasonable grounds to fear a breach of the peace (AG guideline para. 20(ה)(1)); the police first demanded that the road be cleared, as para. 22 expects. Removing those who stayed was within their powers, with reasonable force.
2. **Moshe's detention.** Lawful if there was reasonable suspicion of an offence; it must end within 3 hours (6 with written reasons, many being involved). Moshe gives his name and ID number and asks why he is held and the officer's name and number.
3. **Ari's filming.** From the pavement, at a distance, Ari is not obstructing. The order to stop has no basis; grabbing the phone is unlawful unless it is seized as evidence. Ari keeps filming the officer's face and the missing tag, says calmly "I am filming from the pavement and not interfering; I do not consent to a search of my phone", and does not pull back physically if the phone is taken.
4. **After.** Ari uploads the backup, writes a note with the time and place, and asks for the officer's details from the commander on site. The missing name tag and the phone grab are disciplinary matters (06.4); if Ari was hurt, it is a matter for the Police Investigations Department.
5. **Mirror test.** Replace the students with anti-government protesters blocking the Ayalon highway, or farmers blocking a junction with tractors: every legal step is the same.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- The protest you have been planning since 06.1.
- Section 3 ("At the protest") of [protest-legal-plan.md](../../templates/protest-legal-plan.md).
- A phone with a camera, for a five-minute filming drill in a public place (no police needed).
- 45 minutes.

### Steps

1. List what in your protest could give the police grounds to demand a change: road blocking, noise, entering private property, a sign that might be called incitement (06.5).
2. For each, write what you will do when asked: comply at once, comply and object in writing later, or (only for clearly unlawful orders) decline politely without physical resistance.
3. Write the personal and group safety brief from the lists above: roles, phone numbers, meeting point, a detainee list.
4. Write a one-paragraph script for the police liaison: who you are, what was agreed, a request for the senior officer's name, and a request to put any new order in writing.
5. Filming drill: in a public place, film a 60-second street scene with the time and date visible, a street sign, and a wide-then-close sequence. Check that the backup uploaded and that the file's metadata shows the time.
6. Write a five-line detention card: "Am I detained or arrested? Why? Your name and number? I give my identity details. I want a lawyer. I do not consent to a search of my phone."

### Template

Section 3 of [protest-legal-plan.md](../../templates/protest-legal-plan.md) — roles, scripts and the detention card.

### How you know it worked

- Your brief names a person for each role and a meeting point.
- Your drill footage shows when and where it was taken without your explanation.
- You can say, without notes, the 3-hour detention limit and the officer's duty to give name and number.

### Self-check

- [ ] I know the order: demand, disperse, detain or arrest.
- [ ] I will never resist physically, even an order I think is unlawful.
- [ ] I can film from a distance without obstructing, with backup on.
- [ ] I know that searching my phone needs a judge's warrant.
- [ ] Every person in my group knows the meeting point and the detainee contact.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pulling your arm away during an arrest | A charge of obstructing an officer or assault (s. 275) on top of the protest offence | Stop resisting at once; say clearly you are not resisting | Decide in advance: never physical resistance |
| Staying on the road after a clear demand to clear it | Lawful removal with force; possible unlawful-assembly charge | Move to the pavement and continue the protest there | Plan a pavement position in advance |
| Unlocking your phone for an officer "to show the video" | Consent replaces the judge's warrant; the whole phone may be searched | Say you withdraw consent; note the time and officer | Never unlock; offer to send the specific clip later through a lawyer |
| Editing or trimming the original footage | The defence or complaint loses credibility | Keep and hand over the original file | Edit only copies |
| No record of the officer's identity | The complaint stalls | Ask the commander on site; file anyway with time, place, photo | Film the name tag or unit first |
| Arguing the cause with officers on the line | Escalation, no benefit | Let the liaison talk to the commander | Assign one liaison and one filmer |

## Review

- Even a lawful protest becomes an unlawful assembly if its participants' conduct gives reasonable grounds to fear a breach of the peace — but not because of others' opposition.
- The expected sequence is demand, then dispersal, then detention or arrest; comply first and challenge later.
- Detention is limited to 3 hours (6 in multi-person events, with written reasons); officers must wear a name tag and give name and number on request.
- Filming police from a reasonable distance is lawful; searching your phone needs a judge's warrant; back up and keep originals.
- Never resist physically; that turns a protest into a criminal case.
