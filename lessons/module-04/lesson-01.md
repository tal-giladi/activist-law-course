# 04.1 · Authority and the Principle of Legality

Every lawful act of a public authority starts with a power that some law gave it. In this lesson you learn to ask the first question of every administrative challenge — "under which section?" — and to find, read and test the enabling provision behind a real decision, so that you can write the first paragraph of a decision audit.

## Why it matters

A private person may do anything the law does not forbid. A public authority may do only what the law allows. This is the principle of legality (עקרון חוקיות המינהל), and it is the activist's most powerful starting point. If a ministry, council or committee acted without a power, or used a power that belongs to someone else, the decision falls without any need to argue about its wisdom. Courts are far more comfortable cancelling a decision for lack of authority than for being unwise.

The question is also practical. Officials often answer "this is our policy" or "this is the procedure". Your reply is always the same: please identify the section of law that gives you this power. Lesson 01.3 taught you to trace a rule up the hierarchy; this lesson turns that habit into a test.

## The law

> אין פוגעים בזכויות שלפי חוק־יסוד זה אלא בחוק ההולם את ערכיה של מדינת ישראל, שנועד לתכלית ראויה, ובמידה שאינה עולה על הנדרש או לפי חוק כאמור מכוח הסמכה מפורשת בו.

*Gist:* a basic right may be limited only by a statute, or by an authority acting under express authorisation in a statute — and only for a proper purpose and no more than necessary. ([חוק־יסוד: כבוד האדם וחירותו, s. 8](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק-יסוד:_כבוד_האדם_וחירותו#סעיף_8))

> כל רשות מרשויות השלטון חייבת לכבד את הזכויות שלפי חוק־יסוד זה.

*Gist:* every public authority, not only the Knesset, must respect the rights in the Basic Law. ([s. 11](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק-יסוד:_כבוד_האדם_וחירותו#סעיף_11))

> הממשלה מוסמכת לעשות בשם המדינה, בכפוף לכל דין, כל פעולה שעשייתה אינה מוטלת בדין על רשות אחרת.

*Gist:* the government as a whole has a residual power to act for the state where no law gives the matter to another body — but always "subject to any law". ([חוק־יסוד: הממשלה, s. 32](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק-יסוד:_הממשלה#סעיף_32))

> מסים, מילוות חובה ותשלומי חובה אחרים לא יוטלו, ושיעוריהם לא ישונו, אלא בחוק או על פיו; הוא הדין לגבי אגרות.

*Gist:* no tax, compulsory payment or fee may be imposed or changed except by a statute or under one. ([חוק־יסוד: משק המדינה, s. 1(א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק-יסוד:_משק_המדינה#סעיף_1))

> הסמכה לעשות דבר או לכפות עשייתו – משמעה גם מתן סמכויות עזר הדרושות לכך במידה המתקבלת על הדעת.

*Gist:* a power to do something includes the ancillary powers reasonably needed to do it — but not new, independent powers. ([חוק הפרשנות, s. 17(ב)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הפרשנות#סעיף_17))

## How it works in practice

### The four authority questions

Ask them in this order. A "no" to any one is a legality problem.

1. **Is there a power at all?** Find the section that lets *this kind of body* do *this kind of thing*. A procedure, a policy paper or a website FAQ is not a power; it must itself rest on one (lesson 01.3).
2. **Is it this body's power?** The law names the holder: "השר", "הוועדה המקומית", "המנהל". A different official may act only under a lawful delegation (אצילה). Basic Law: The Government s. 33 lets a minister delegate most statutory powers to a public servant, but not the power to make regulations. Ask for the delegation document.
3. **Does the power cover this act?** Read the section closely (lesson 01.5). A power to *license* is not a power to *charge a fee*; a power to *regulate* is not a power to *ban*. Ancillary powers (Interpretation Law s. 17) fill small gaps, not big ones.
4. **Is an express power needed?** Two kinds of act need clear, specific authority:
   - limiting a basic right (Basic Law: Human Dignity and Liberty s. 8: "הסמכה מפורשת");
   - imposing a fee, tax or other compulsory payment (Basic Law: The State Economy s. 1).

   Courts also hold that "primary arrangements" — the basic policy choices on a contested matter — must be made by the Knesset, not left to an official. The leading example is the yeshiva-student deferral line of cases that began with *Ressler* (lesson 04.5).

### Where the residual power stops

The government's residual power (s. 32) is real: it lets the cabinet set up a committee, run a programme or sign a contract without a specific law. But it belongs to the *government*, not to a single ministry official, it is "subject to any law", and it cannot be used to limit a basic right, which needs express authority under s. 8. Local authorities have no residual power at all: they act only under the Municipalities Ordinance, the Local Councils Ordinance, other laws and their by-laws (חוקי עזר).

### How to ask the question in writing

In your formal letter (lesson 02.5), add one paragraph:

> אבקש לקבל את הוראת החוק המסמיכה את הרשות לקבל את ההחלטה, וכן את מסמך האצילה, ככל שההחלטה התקבלה בידי מי שהסמכות הואצלה לו.

The answer — or the silence — tells you a lot. A Freedom of Information request (lesson 05.2) can also ask for the delegation document.

### What the court does

When an act is outside the power, the court can declare it void or cancel it. In practice it often sends the matter back to the right body to decide again, lawfully. Courts sometimes limit the remedy (for example, they do not order refunds of fees already paid) — but the decision itself still falls.

## Worked example

**Situation.** A municipality sends letters to residents of one street who hung banners from their balconies during a period of protests. The letters demand a "signage fee" of a few hundred shekels per banner and threaten fines. Some banners support the government and some oppose it. Noa, one resident, wants to check the demand before paying.

1. **Find the power the letter relies on.** The letter cites "נוהל שילוט עירוני". Noa asks the municipality in writing for the section of law and the by-law behind the procedure.
2. **Read the answer.** The municipality points to its signage by-law (חוק עזר לשילוט). Noa reads the version published on the municipality's website and checks that it is the current one. The by-law requires a licence and a fee for *commercial* signs "המוצב לשם פרסומת"; it does not mention private political banners on a home.
3. **Test the four questions.**
   - Power at all: yes, for advertising signs.
   - This body: yes, the municipality.
   - Covers this act: probably not; a political banner on one's balcony is not advertising under the by-law's definition.
   - Express power needed: yes, twice — it is a fee (Basic Law: The State Economy s. 1) and it limits expression (Basic Law: Human Dignity and Liberty s. 8). A procedure cannot supply either.
4. **Conclusion for the audit.** "The demand appears to lack authority: the by-law covers advertising signs only, and neither a fee nor a limit on expression can rest on an internal procedure."
5. **Next step.** Noa writes to the municipal legal adviser, quoting the by-law definition and asking that the demand be withdrawn within 30 days. Her neighbours, whose banners carry the opposite message, sign the same letter in their own names.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- One real decision of a public authority that affects you or your cause: a letter, a published decision, a condition in a permit, a fee demand. The issue from your Module 1 issue map is ideal.
- The [decision audit template](../../templates/decision-audit.md), section A ("Authority").
- Wikisource and the Knesset legislation database (lesson 02.1).
- 45 minutes.

### Steps

1. Write down the decision in one sentence: who decided what, when, and how you know.
2. Find the enabling provision. Start with the decision itself (it often cites a section), then the main law of the field from your issue map. Quote the section exactly.
3. Name the holder of the power in the section, and compare it with the official who actually signed.
4. If they differ, look for a delegation (Basic Law: The Government s. 33, or the delegation section of the specific law). Note whether you found the delegation document.
5. Read the section against the act. Does the section's verb ("רשאי לתת היתר", "יקבע", "יטיל") cover what was done?
6. Mark whether the act limits a basic right or imposes a payment. If yes, write where the express authority is.
7. Fill section A of the template: power found / holder / delegation / scope / express authority / conclusion.

### Template

[Decision audit](../../templates/decision-audit.md), section A. You will fill the other sections in lessons 04.2–04.5.

### How you know it worked

You can point to one quoted section of a statute or regulation that gives this body the power to make this decision, or you can explain precisely why there is none. Someone reading your section A would know which of the four authority questions is the weak point.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Arguing the decision is unwise before checking the power | You fight on the hardest ground and miss the easy one | Add an authority paragraph to your letter | Always ask "under which section?" first |
| Accepting "this is the procedure" as an answer | The real gap (no power behind the procedure) is never exposed | Ask in writing for the enabling section and the delegation | Treat procedures as needing a source (lesson 01.3) |
| Assuming a local authority has a residual power | Your analysis is wrong in the other direction too | Look for the specific ordinance or by-law | Remember s. 32 is the government's, not a council's |
| Ignoring delegation | You call a decision unauthorised when a valid delegation exists | Request the delegation document before claiming lack of power | Check s. 33 and the specific law's delegation clause |
| Reading a power too broadly ("license" = "charge") | The authority wins easily on the text | Quote the exact verb and definition | Read the section word by word (lesson 01.5) |

## Review

- An authority may do only what a law empowers it to do; a private person may do anything not forbidden.
- Ask four questions: is there a power, is it this body's, does it cover this act, is express authority needed?
- Limits on basic rights and compulsory payments need express authority in a statute.
- The government's residual power (s. 32) is subject to any law and does not belong to councils or single officials.
- Ask for the enabling section and the delegation in writing; the answer often decides the case.
