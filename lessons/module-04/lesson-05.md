# 04.5 · Standing, Justiciability and Exhausting Remedies

Before a court asks whether a decision was lawful, it asks whether it should hear the case at all. Most petitions by self-represented people that fail, fail here: no prior request to the authority, an appeal route skipped, a late filing, or a petitioner the court thinks is the wrong person to bring the case. In this lesson you learn the threshold questions (שאלות סף) and check your own case against them.

## Why it matters

Threshold dismissals are quick, final for practical purposes, and often come with an order to pay costs. They are also the easiest problems to prevent, because almost all of them are in your control: write to the authority first, use the appeal route the law gives you, file on time, and make sure the person or group most affected is on board.

Standing is also part of the wider public debate. Israel's broad public-petitioner standing, developed from *Ressler* (1988) onward, is defended by groups such as the Association for Civil Rights in Israel as essential to the rule of law, and criticised by others, such as the Kohelet Policy Forum, which has proposed requiring a personal, real and direct interest. Groups across the spectrum — the Movement for Quality Government, ACRI, Regavim and many others — use public petitions today.

## The law

> בית המשפט העליון ישב גם כבית משפט גבוה לצדק; בשבתו כאמור ידון בענינים אשר הוא רואה צורך לתת בהם סעד למען הצדק ואשר אינם בסמכותו של בית משפט או של בית דין אחר.

*Gist:* the High Court of Justice hears matters where it sees a need to give relief for justice — but only if no other court or tribunal has jurisdiction. That is the root of the alternative-remedy rule. ([חוק־יסוד: השפיטה, s. 15(ג)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק-יסוד:_השפיטה#סעיף_15))

> בית משפט לענינים מינהליים ידון בעתירה מינהלית ובערעור מינהלי בהתאם לעילות, לסמכויות ולסעדים שלפיהם דן בית המשפט העליון בשבתו כבית משפט גבוה לצדק

*Gist:* the Administrative Affairs Court applies the same grounds and the same threshold doctrines as the High Court, in the matters listed in the First Schedule (s. 5). ([חוק בתי משפט לענינים מינהליים, s. 8](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_בתי_משפט_לענינים_מינהליים#סעיף_8))

> נעשתה פניה מוקדמת בכתב לרשות – יצורפו העתקים של ההתכתבות לענין זה; לא נעשתה פניה מוקדמת – הנימוקים להגשת העתירה בלא פניה מוקדמת;

*Gist:* an administrative petition must attach your prior written correspondence with the authority — or explain why there was none. The same regulation requires the petition to state the petitioner's interest in the matter (reg. 5(ב)(4)). ([תקנות בתי משפט לענינים מינהליים (סדרי דין), reg. 5(ב)(6)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/תקנות_בתי_משפט_לענינים_מינהליים_%28סדרי_דין%29#סעיף_5))

> לא נקבע מועד כאמור, תוגש העתירה בלא שיהוי, לפי נסיבות הענין, ולא יאוחר מארבעים וחמישה ימים מיום שההחלטה פורסמה כדין, או מיום שהעותר קיבל הודעה עליה או מיום שנודע לעותר עליה, לפי המוקדם.

*Gist:* unless a law sets another deadline, an administrative petition must be filed without delay and no later than the deadline in reg. 3(ב), counted from the earliest of publication, notice or knowledge (see [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)); the court may extend it (reg. 3(ג)) and may dismiss even a timely petition for delay (reg. 4). ([reg. 3](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/תקנות_בתי_משפט_לענינים_מינהליים_%28סדרי_דין%29#סעיף_3))

The doctrines of standing and justiciability come from case law. In *Ressler*, the Court recognised the "public petitioner" where the issue is of public importance, reveals a serious flaw in the administration or concerns the rule of law, and held that "Access to the courts is the cornerstone of the rule of law" (Versa headnote). It also distinguished normative from institutional justiciability, holding that the legality of a minister's deferral policy is a legal question for the court even if the policy itself is for political bodies. ([Ressler, Versa](https://versa.cardozo.yu.edu/opinions/ressler-v-minister-defence))

## How it works in practice

### 1. Standing

- **Personal interest.** If the decision harms you (your permit, your street, your benefit), you have standing. Say so in the petition with facts.
- **Public petitioner.** A person or group with no personal stake may petition where the issue is of public importance or concerns the rule of law — typically good-governance, environmental and enforcement cases.
- **The main limit.** If a directly affected person exists and has chosen not to petition, courts generally will not let a public petitioner bring the case instead (Hebrew Wikipedia, "זכות עמידה", citing rulings from 2019 and 2021). The practical fix: act together with the affected person, in their own name as well.
- **Groups.** An amuta or an informal group can petition; the people who sign must be able to show the group's decision to act (Module 18).

### 2. Justiciability

- **Normative:** is there a legal standard to apply? After *Ressler*, almost always yes, because every act of government is governed by some legal norm.
- **Institutional:** is a court the right body? Courts still decline some matters at the heart of foreign policy, security policy or political negotiations. Frame your case around a legal duty (authority, procedure, discrimination), not around which policy is wiser.
- **Mootness.** If the decision no longer has effect (the event passed, the person resigned), courts may decline to rule. In the May 2025 Shin Bet ruling the dissenting justice held the case was moot because the head had resigned; the majority ruled anyway because of its public importance (lesson 04.4). Plan your timing so that the court can still give a practical remedy.

### 3. Exhausting remedies

Two separate requirements:

1. **Prior request (פנייה מוקדמת).** Before any petition, ask the authority in writing for what you want, give it reasonable time, and keep proof (lesson 02.5). The pre-petition letter is lesson 05.5. A petition without one is commonly dismissed in limine.
2. **Alternative remedy (סעד חלופי).** If a law gives a specific route — an objection to a plan, an appeal (ערר) to an appeals committee, a statutory appeal to a court, an appeal under s. 4 of the Decisions and Reasons Law — use it first. Planning, tax, social-security and many licensing decisions have such routes.

### 4. Delay

Count from the earliest of publication, notice or knowledge. Courts look at whether you delayed, whether the authority or third parties relied on the decision in the meantime (built, paid, hired), and how serious the alleged illegality is. Waiting for an answer to your prior request is a reasonable explanation; waiting months for no reason is not.

### 5. Forum

| If the decision is… | Usually goes to |
|---|---|
| In a matter listed in the First Schedule to the Administrative Courts Law (for example many local-authority, planning, FOI and licensing matters) | The Administrative Affairs Court (District Court) — an administrative petition |
| By the government, a minister or a national body, in a matter not listed | The High Court of Justice (בג״ץ) |
| Subject to a specific statutory appeal | That appeal route, not a petition |

Check the First Schedule item for your matter before choosing (lesson 01.4; Module 5 walks through filing).

## Worked example

**Situation.** An event hall is built without a permit on agricultural land outside a moshav in the north. The local committee issued a stop-work order eighteen months ago but has done nothing since; weddings are held every week. Dalia, chair of a residents' association that campaigns for open spaces, wants to act. (The same analysis would apply to illegal building in any community, Jewish or Arab, in Israel or in the West Bank, where enforcement is the issue.)

1. **Standing.** Dalia does not live next to the hall. The association is a public petitioner: the issue is enforcement of planning law, which concerns the rule of law. A neighbour, Avi, who suffers the noise, agrees to join in his own name, which strengthens standing.
2. **Justiciability.** The question is whether the committee is failing its legal duty to enforce — a legal standard. Not a policy question.
3. **Exhaustion.** The association has never written to the committee. Dalia writes to the committee's chair and engineer, with a copy to the legal adviser and the national planning-enforcement unit, quoting the stop-work order and asking for enforcement within 30 days.
4. **Alternative remedy.** She checks whether planning law gives a specific route for complaints about non-enforcement; she also notes the oversight bodies (Module 5).
5. **Delay.** The failure to enforce is continuing, so delay is less of a risk than with a one-off decision, but the hall owner's investment grows every month. She acts now and records the dates.
6. **Forum.** A petition against a local planning committee is likely an administrative petition; she will confirm the First Schedule item before filing (Module 5).

## Do it yourself

### You need

- Your decision audit (sections A–D of the [decision audit template](../../templates/decision-audit.md)).
- The First Schedule to the Administrative Courts Law on Wikisource.
- 50 minutes.

### Steps

1. Write your standing in one sentence: personal interest (what harm?) or public petitioner (why public importance?).
2. Name the person or group most directly affected. Are they acting? If not, can they join you?
3. Write the legal standard the court would apply, in one line, to show normative justiciability. Note any institutional-justiciability risk.
4. List your prior written requests to the authority, with dates and proof. If there are none, mark this as the next step.
5. Search the enabling law for an objection, appeal or ערר route. Write it down with its deadline (see [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md) or the law).
6. Write the date you first learned of the decision and the date the petition deadline would expire.
7. Choose the likely forum and the First Schedule item, if any.
8. Fill section E, then the overall assessment in section F of the template.

### Template

[Decision audit](../../templates/decision-audit.md), sections E and F.

### How you know it worked

Section E answers every threshold question with a fact and a date. If a court read only section E, it would see no reason to dismiss your case before looking at the merits — or you have listed exactly what you must do first.

### Self-check

- [ ] My standing is stated with facts, and the most affected person is involved or accounted for.
- [ ] I have a prior written request with proof, or it is my next step.
- [ ] I checked the law for an appeal or objection route.
- [ ] I know the date the clock started and the date it runs out.
- [ ] I identified the likely forum and why.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Filing without a prior written request | Dismissal in limine, often with costs | Withdraw, write to the authority, refile if needed | Always send a prior request and keep proof |
| Skipping a statutory appeal or objection | "Alternative remedy" dismissal | Use the statutory route, if still in time | Search the enabling law for ערר / השגה / ערעור |
| Petitioning for someone who chose not to | Standing denied | Bring the affected person in, in their own name | Act together with those most affected |
| Waiting months after learning of the decision | Dismissal for delay, even inside the deadline | Explain the delay; ask for an extension | Count from the earliest date you knew |
| Framing the case as a policy disagreement | Institutional non-justiciability | Reframe around a legal duty or right | Lead with authority, procedure or discrimination |
| Filing in the wrong court | Transfer or dismissal; lost time | Ask for transfer to the competent court | Check the First Schedule before filing |

## Review

- Standing: a personal interest, or a public petitioner on a matter of public importance — but not instead of an affected person who chose not to act.
- Justiciability: almost everything has a legal standard; courts still avoid pure policy questions and moot cases.
- Exhaustion means a prior written request and using any appeal or objection route first.
- File without delay; the clock runs from the earliest of publication, notice or knowledge (see current numbers).
- Choose the forum by the First Schedule: Administrative Affairs Court for listed matters, High Court of Justice otherwise.
