# 04.4 · Fair Procedure: Hearing, Reasons, Conflict of Interest

How a decision is made matters as much as what it decides. In this lesson you learn the three procedural duties activists use most — to hear the person affected before deciding, to give reasons, and to keep personal interests out of public decisions — and you write a short Hebrew request that puts an authority on notice before it decides.

## Why it matters

Procedural grounds are often the most practical ones. They do not ask a court to second-guess the authority's judgment; they ask whether it followed the rules of fair decision-making. They are also the ground on which you can act *before* the decision: a written request for a hearing, sent in time, can change the outcome or at least create the record you will need later.

Recent high-profile cases show how much these duties matter. In May 2025 the High Court ruled 2–1 that the government's dismissal of the head of the Shin Bet was unlawful, citing, among other things, that he was not given a proper hearing, that the Senior Appointments Advisory Committee was not consulted, and that the Prime Minister had a conflict of interest. The Prime Minister called the ruling "disgraceful" and argued that the law gives the government the power to dismiss the head of the service; the dissenting justice held that the case was moot (Times of Israel, 21.5.2025). In December 2025 a seven-justice panel annulled the government's dismissal of the Attorney General and the new procedure adopted for it, and ministers Levin and Karhi called on the government not to accept the ruling (Times of Israel, 14.12.2025). Whatever one thinks of these rulings, they rest heavily on procedure.

## The law

> עובד הציבור שנתבקש כאמור בסעיף 2(א) וסירב לבקשה, יודיע למבקש בכתב את נימוקי סירובו.

*Gist:* a public servant who refuses a written request must give the reasons for the refusal in writing. ([חוק לתיקון סדרי המינהל (החלטות והנמקות), s. 2א](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_לתיקון_סדרי_המינהל_%28החלטות_והנמקות%29#סעיף_2א))

> החלטה או פעולה של עובד הציבור לא יהיו פסולות בשל כך בלבד שהוא לא מילא אחרי הוראות הסעיפים 2א, 2ב, 4 או 5; ואולם בכל הליך לפני ועדת ערר או ועדת ערעור או לפני בית המשפט, על עובד הציבור הראיה כי החלטה או פעולה שלא ניתנה לגביהן תשובה או הודעה כאמור, נעשו כדין.

*Gist:* missing reasons do not void a decision by themselves, but in any appeal or court case the authority then has to prove the decision was lawful. ([s. 6(א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_לתיקון_סדרי_המינהל_%28החלטות_והנמקות%29#סעיף_6))

Section 3 lists the exceptions: for example where the law allows a decision without reasons, where state security or foreign relations require secrecy, or where the request was to be appointed to a post. Section 4 then requires the authority to say which exception it relies on, and gives a right of appeal (ערר) to the minister (see [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md) for the deadline).

> חבר במוסד תכנון שיש לו, במישרין או בעקיפין, בעצמו או על ידי קרובו, סוכנו או שותפו או על ידי קרוביהם, כל חלק או טובת הנאה בכל ענין העומד לדיון במוסד או בועדה מועדותיו –

*Gist:* a member of a planning body with any direct or indirect interest in a matter, personally or through a relative, agent or partner, must declare it and must not attend the discussion or vote (s. 47(א)(1)–(2)); breaking the rule is an offence (s. 47(ג)). ([חוק התכנון והבניה, s. 47](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_התכנון_והבניה#סעיף_47))

> חבר מועצה, קרובו, סוכנו או שותפו, או תאגיד שיש לאחד מהאמורים חלק העולה על עשרה אחוזים בהונו או ברווחיו או שאחד מהם מנהל או עובד אחראי בו, לא יהיה צד לחוזה או לעיסקה עם העיריה;

*Gist:* a council member, close relatives, agents and partners, and companies they hold over 10% of or manage, may not contract with the municipality, except in listed cases. ([פקודת העיריות, s. 122א](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/פקודת_העיריות#סעיף_122א))

The **right to be heard** has no general statute; it comes from the rules of natural justice that the courts apply to every administrative body ([כללי הצדק הטבעי, Hebrew Wikipedia](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/כללי_הצדק_הטבעי)). The general **conflict-of-interest** rule also comes from case law, in Barak J.'s words: "אסור לו לעובד הציבור להימצא במצב בו קיימת אפשרות ממשית של ניגוד עניינים" ([ניגוד עניינים, Hebrew Wikipedia](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/ניגוד_עניינים)). Ministries apply it through the Attorney General's guidelines (search the [AG guidelines database](https://www.gov.il/he/Departments/DynamicCollectors/legal-advisor-guidelines) for "ניגוד עניינים").

## How it works in practice

### The hearing: five elements

A proper hearing usually has all of these:

1. **Notice** that a decision is being considered, and what it is.
2. **The material** the authority relies on: complaints, reports, expert opinions (sometimes in summary, if there is a lawful reason to withhold details).
3. **Reasonable time** to prepare.
4. **A real chance to respond**, in writing or in person, depending on the stakes.
5. **An open mind**: the decision-maker considers the response before deciding, and writes how.

Hearings are required before decisions about a specific person: revoking a licence, dismissing an employee, cancelling a benefit, imposing a sanction. General decisions (a new regulation, a city-wide policy) usually do not require an individual hearing, although some laws require public consultation. In real emergencies an authority may act first and hear afterwards — but it must then actually hear and be willing to change its decision.

**Ask for the hearing yourself.** If you learn that a decision affecting you or your group is coming, write at once:

> אבקש לקבל את כל החומר שעליו נשענת ההחלטה המתוכננת, ולהציג את טענותיי בכתב ובעל פה בטרם תתקבל החלטה סופית.

### Reasons

- Ask in writing for the reasons of any refusal (s. 2א). Keep the request and the answer.
- If no reasons come, the decision stands for now, but the burden shifts: in any appeal or court case the authority must prove it acted lawfully (s. 6(א)).
- If the authority relies on a s. 3 exception, it must say which one (s. 4), and you can appeal to the minister.
- Read the reasons against your audit: they often reveal an extraneous consideration (lesson 04.2) or show that a relevant one was ignored (lesson 04.3).

### Conflict of interest

Ask three questions about each person who took part in the decision:

1. **Personal interest?** Their own, or through a relative, partner, employer or business (Planning Law s. 47 lists these for planning bodies; Municipalities Ordinance s. 122א for municipal contracts).
2. **Institutional conflict?** Two public roles that pull in opposite directions — for example, an official who both promotes a project and supervises it.
3. **Was it handled?** A declaration, a recusal recorded in the minutes, a conflict-of-interest arrangement approved by the legal adviser.

The test is a *real possibility* of conflict, not proof that the person was actually influenced. Evidence comes from the minutes (who was present and voted), company registers, declarations of interests where published, and local reporting. Allege conflict carefully and factually: a false accusation can expose you to a defamation claim (Module 8).

## Worked example

**Situation.** Moshe owns a small kiosk in a Haredi neighbourhood. For six years he has had a municipal permit to place four tables on the pavement. One morning he receives a letter cancelling the permit "following complaints and an inspection", effective in seven days. He was never told of any complaint or inspection. A neighbour mentions that the deputy mayor who chairs the municipal business committee co-owns the restaurant across the street.

1. **Hearing.** No notice, no material, no chance to respond: the cancellation of an existing permit is a decision about a specific person, so a hearing was required. There is no urgency (the letter gives seven days).
2. **Reasons.** "Following complaints and an inspection" is a label, not reasons. Moshe asks for the complaints, the inspection report and the reasons under s. 2א.
3. **Conflict of interest.** Moshe checks the companies register for the restaurant's owners and asks the municipality, in neutral language, whether any member of the committee that decided had a personal interest and whether a conflict-of-interest arrangement exists. He does not accuse anyone publicly.
4. **Letter.** He writes to the director of the business licensing department, with a copy to the municipal legal adviser, asking that the cancellation be suspended, that he receive the material, and that he be heard before any new decision. He sends it by email and registered mail (lesson 02.5).
5. **Outcome.** The legal adviser suspends the cancellation and schedules a hearing. At the hearing Moshe shows that the inspection measured the wrong pavement width; the committee, without the deputy mayor, renews the permit with a condition about passage width.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- Your decision audit so far (sections A–C of the [decision audit template](../../templates/decision-audit.md)).
- The decision letter and any minutes you have.
- The [formal letter template](../../templates/formal-letter.md).
- 50 minutes.

### Steps

1. Check the five hearing elements for your decision. Mark each: given, partly given, not given, not required (and why).
2. Check reasons: were they given? Are they real reasons or labels? Did the authority rely on a s. 3 exception?
3. List the people who took part in the decision (from the minutes or the letter). For each, note any known personal or institutional interest and whether it was declared.
4. Fill section D of the decision audit template.
5. Draft a one-page Hebrew letter in your own name that asks for the reasons and the material and, if the decision is not yet final, for a hearing. Use the paragraph bank in section G of the template.
6. Calculate the reply dates under the Decisions and Reasons Law (lesson 02.5; see [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)).

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Waiting until after the decision to ask for a hearing | The authority says you had your chance; the record is thin | Ask for a hearing on reconsideration now | Write as soon as you learn a decision is coming |
| Accepting a label ("following complaints") as reasons | You cannot test the decision | Ask for reasons and the material under s. 2א | Always ask for reasons in writing |
| Assuming missing reasons void the decision | The decision stays in force while you wait | Use s. 6(א): the authority must prove legality in any proceeding | Ask for reasons, then decide on the next step |
| Publicly accusing an official of conflict without evidence | Defamation risk; the real issue is lost | Ask neutral questions in writing first | Collect minutes and registry data before alleging |
| Ignoring your own urgency | The authority acts before your letter arrives | Ask for a suspension until a hearing is held | Note the effective date and send by a channel with fast proof |

## Review

- Before a decision about a specific person, the authority must give notice, the material, time, a real chance to respond and an open mind.
- A refused written request must be answered with written reasons (s. 2א); without them, the authority bears the burden of proving legality (s. 6(א)).
- A real possibility of conflict of interest is enough; planning bodies and municipalities have specific statutory rules (Planning Law s. 47, Municipalities Ordinance s. 122א).
- Ask for the hearing, the reasons and the material in writing, early, and in your own name.
- Recent rulings on senior dismissals turned largely on procedure; the government disputed them, and that dispute is part of the current picture.
