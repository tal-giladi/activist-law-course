# 02.5 · Writing a Formal Letter to an Authority

By the end of this lesson you will be able to write and send a formal Hebrew letter to a ministry, a local authority or any public body, in a form that obliges it to answer, leaves you proof of every step, and prepares the ground for a complaint or petition if it does not.

## Why it matters

The formal letter is the activist's most-used legal tool. Most problems with authorities are solved by a clear written request to the right official. When they are not, the letter becomes **Exhibit 1** in every later step: the ombudsman complaint, the pre-petition letter, the petition. Courts dismiss petitions where the petitioner never asked the authority first. A letter sent by phone call or to the wrong address does not count.

A written request also starts a legal clock. Under the Decisions and Reasons Law, a public servant asked in writing to use a power must decide and answer within 45 days (see [Current Numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)).

## The law

> (א) נתבקש עובד הציבור, בכתב, להשתמש בסמכות שניתנה לו על פי דין, יחליט בבקשה וישיב למבקש בכתב בהקדם, אך לא יאוחר מארבעים וחמישה ימים מיום קבלת הבקשה.

*Gist:* a written request to use a legal power must be decided and answered in writing within 45 days of receipt. ([חוק לתיקון סדרי המינהל (החלטות והנמקות), s. 2](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_לתיקון_סדרי_המינהל_%28החלטות_והנמקות%29#סעיף_2))

> (1) יעביר את הבקשה לעובד הציבור שלדעתו מוקנית לו אותה סמכות, ויודיע על כך בכתב למבקש, בציון פרטים על זהותו ומענו של עובד הציבור;

*Gist:* an official without the power must pass your request to the right one and tell you who; the 45 days then run from when that official receives it (s. 2ב(ב)). ([s. 2ב](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_לתיקון_סדרי_המינהל_%28החלטות_והנמקות%29#סעיף_2ב))

> (ב) לא השיב עובד הציבור או לא הודיע תוך שלושת החדשים כאמור בסעיף 2(א) או (ג), רואים בכך, לענין כל דין, החלטה לסרב לבקשה, ללא מתן נימוקים.

*Gist:* if the authority does not answer within three months, the law treats its silence as a refusal without reasons, which you can then challenge. ([s. 6(ב)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_לתיקון_סדרי_המינהל_%28החלטות_והנמקות%29#סעיף_6))

> (א) מקום שנקבעה תקופה קצובה במספר ימים או שבועות מיום פלוני, אותו יום לא יבוא במנין.

*Gist:* when counting a period in days, do not count the starting day; s. 10(ג) adds that rest days count, unless the period ends on one. ([חוק הפרשנות, s. 10](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הפרשנות#סעיף_10))

> הפעולות המנויות להלן, לא יעשה אותן דרך עיסוק, או בתמורה אף שלא דרך עיסוק, אלא עורך דין;

*Gist:* writing letters for other people as a job or for payment is reserved to lawyers; write in your own name, or help a group write in its own name (lesson 01.1). ([חוק לשכת עורכי הדין, s. 20](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_לשכת_עורכי_הדין#סעיף_20))

## How it works in practice

### 1. Choose the addressee

Write to the **official who holds the power** — the law or regulation usually names the post (for example "המהנדס", "הממונה", "המנהל"). Find the person's name on the authority's website. Send a copy (העתק) to the authority's legal adviser (היועץ המשפטי / הלשכה המשפטית). For a local authority, a copy to the mayor's office is common. Do not send to a general "info@" address if you can avoid it.

### 2. Choose a channel that leaves proof

| Channel | Proof you keep | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| The authority's online form on gov.il or its own site | Screenshot and reference number | Often requires National Digital Identity login |
| Email to the official | Sent email plus a "read" or reply; ask for confirmation | Save as PDF with headers |
| Registered mail with proof of delivery (דואר רשום עם אישור מסירה) | The signed delivery card | Slow, but strongest proof of receipt |
| Hand delivery | A stamped copy ("נתקבל") with date | Ask the clerk to stamp your copy |

The 45 days run from **receipt**, so keep proof of the receipt date.

### 3. Structure (Hebrew conventions)

1. Your name, address, phone, email — top right.
2. Date (Gregorian; Hebrew date optional).
3. Addressee: name, post, authority, address.
4. **"בלי לפגוע בזכויות"** (optional; useful if a dispute may follow).
5. **"הנדון:"** — one line, underlined or bold, naming the subject.
6. **"סימוכין:"** — earlier letters and reference numbers, if any.
7. Numbered paragraphs: facts → legal basis (quoted) → request.
8. A specific request and a date to answer (do not set a date shorter than the law gives without a reason, such as urgency).
9. Closing: "בכבוד רב," name and signature.
10. List of attachments (נספחים) and copies (העתק).

### 4. Tone

Polite, short, specific. Two pages at most. Officials are more likely to help a writer who makes it easy to say yes. Save stronger language for the follow-up (a demand letter) and keep threats out of the first letter.

### 5. Request or demand?

A **first letter** asks. A **demand letter** (מכתב דרישה) follows when the first is ignored or refused: it repeats the request, cites the deadline that passed, sets a final date and names the next step (an ombudsman complaint, an FOI request, a pre-petition letter). Before a court petition you send a formal pre-petition letter — that is lesson 05.5. For information held by the authority, use a Freedom of Information request (lesson 05.2), not a general letter.

### 6. Follow up

Put dates in your calendar: day 45 (answer due under s. 2) and three months (silence becomes a refusal under s. 6(ב)). At day 45 send a short reminder. Keep everything in one folder, numbered as future exhibits.

## Worked example

**Situation.** Residents of a Bedouin village in the Negev ask for a school-bus stop on the main road, because children walk along the road shoulder. They have phoned the regional council twice. Salim, a parent, will write in his own name and attach a petition signed by 40 parents.

1. **Addressee.** The regional council's head of transport, with copies to the council's legal adviser and the Ministry of Education's district transport officer.
2. **Facts (paragraphs 1–3).** Number of children, distance, photos of the road shoulder (נספח 1), the two phone calls with dates.
3. **Legal basis (paragraph 4).** He quotes s. 2(א) of the Decisions and Reasons Law and asks for a written decision.
4. **Request (paragraph 5).** "לקבוע תחנת הסעה בצומת … ולהשיב לפנייה זו בכתב בתוך 45 ימים מיום קבלתה, כנדרש בסעיף 2(א) לחוק."
5. **Delivery.** He sends it by email and by registered mail with proof of delivery. The card comes back signed on 3 March.
6. **Dates.** Counting from 3 March without counting that day, the 45th day is 17 April. Three months end on 3 June.
7. **Outcome.** On 10 April the council replies that the request was passed to the Ministry of Education (s. 2ב). Salim notes that the 45 days now run from the ministry's receipt, and writes to the ministry with the council's letter attached.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- Your one-page IRAC argument from lesson 02.4.
- The template: [formal-letter.md](../../templates/formal-letter.md).
- 60 minutes.

### Steps

1. Identify the official who holds the power and find their name, post and address.
2. Copy the template into a document. Fill in the header, date, addressee and subject line.
3. Turn your IRAC page into numbered paragraphs: facts, then the quoted rule, then the request.
4. Write a request that is specific (what act) and timed (by when).
5. List your attachments and number them נספח 1, 2, 3.
6. Choose a delivery channel that gives you proof of receipt.
7. Calculate two dates: the 45-day answer date and the three-month date. Write them in your calendar.
8. Read the letter aloud. Cut anything that does not help the reader say yes.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Sending is optional in this module; a dry run is enough. If you do send it: act in your own name only. This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing.

### How you know it worked

- A stranger could tell from your subject line alone what you want.
- You have two dates in your calendar and a delivery method that will prove receipt.
- Every paragraph is numbered, and every attachment is listed and referred to in the text.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Phoning instead of writing | No deadline starts; nothing to show a court | Send the request in writing now | Always put requests in writing |
| Writing to a general inbox | The letter is lost; receipt cannot be proved | Resend to the official with the power | Find the post and name before writing |
| No proof of receipt | The authority says it never received it | Resend by registered mail or ask for a stamped copy | Choose a channel that leaves proof |
| Vague request ("please deal with this") | A vague answer, or none; hard to show refusal | Follow up with a specific, timed request | Ask for one specific act by a date |
| Threats or insults in the first letter | The official becomes defensive; the letter may be used against you | Send a calm follow-up | Keep the first letter polite and factual |
| Writing letters for others for payment | Possible offence under the Bar Association Law | Stop; help them write in their own name | Write only for yourself or your own group |

## Review

- A written request to the official with the power starts a 45-day clock (Decisions and Reasons Law s. 2).
- An official without the power must pass it on and tell you; the clock restarts from the right official's receipt.
- Three months of silence counts as a refusal without reasons (s. 6(ב)).
- Structure: header, הנדון, numbered facts, quoted law, a specific request with a date, attachments, copies.
- Keep proof of receipt and every letter: they are the exhibits of any later complaint or petition.
