# 02.3 · Secondary Sources

By the end of this lesson you will be able to use free secondary sources — Kol Zchut, the Knesset Research and Information Center, think-tank papers and law journals — to get oriented on an issue quickly, follow them to the primary law and rulings, and know which of their statements you must check before you rely on them.

## Why it matters

When you start on a new issue, you do not know which law applies, which authority is responsible or what the courts have said. Searching the law books blind is slow. A good secondary source gives you the map in fifteen minutes: the laws, the key rulings, the authority, the deadlines.

But a secondary source is **not law**. An authority or a court will not accept "Kol Zchut says" as an argument. Secondary sources can also be out of date, simplified or written from one side of a debate. The professional habit is: **orient with secondary sources, cite primary sources.**

## Key terms

| עברית | Transliteration | English |
|---|---|---|
| מקור ראשוני | makor rishoni | Primary source: a law, regulation or ruling |
| מקור משני | makor mishni | Secondary source: commentary, guides, research about the law |
| ספרות משפטית | sifrut mishpatit | Legal literature (books and articles) |
| כתב עת משפטי | ktav et mishpati | Law journal or law review |
| מסמך ממ״מ | mismach mamam | A Knesset Research and Information Center paper |
| נייר עמדה | neyar emda | Position paper (advocacy, written for one side) |
| מקורות משפטיים ורשמיים | mekorot mishpatiyim verishmiyim | Kol Zchut's list of legal and official sources at the bottom of each page |
| הנחיות היועץ המשפטי לממשלה | hanchayot hayoetz hamishpati lamemshala | Attorney General guidelines (binding inside government) |

## The law

There is no law that lists secondary sources as a source of law. The Foundations of Law Act tells a court where to look when the law is silent:

> ראה בית המשפט שאלה משפטית הטעונה הכרעה, ולא מצא לה תשובה בדבר חקיקה, בהלכה פסוקה או בדרך של היקש, יכריע בה לאור עקרונות החירות, הצדק, היושר והשלום של המשפט העברי ומורשת ישראל.

*Gist:* a court decides from legislation, then binding case law, then analogy, then these principles; commentary and guides are not on the list, which is why you always follow them back to a law or a ruling. ([חוק יסודות המשפט, s. 1](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_יסודות_המשפט))

> הלכה שנפסקה בבית המשפט העליון מחייבת כל בית משפט, זולת בית המשפט העליון.

*Gist:* binding force belongs to Supreme Court rulings, not to articles about them, however respected the author. ([חוק-יסוד: השפיטה, s. 20(ב)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק-יסוד:_השפיטה#סעיף_20))

## How it works in practice

### The four free secondary sources you will use most

| Source | What it is good for | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| **[Kol Zchut](https://www.kolzchut.org.il/he/)** (כל זכות) | Plain-Hebrew guides to rights and procedures, with forms. Each page ends with **"מקורות משפטיים ורשמיים"**, linking the laws and regulations behind it. | Check the "עודכן לאחרונה" date at the bottom. Numbers may lag a year. Arabic, Russian and other versions exist for many pages. |
| **[Knesset Research and Information Center](https://main.knesset.gov.il/Activity/Info/research/Pages/default.aspx)** (ממ״מ) | Neutral background papers written for MKs: data, legal background, comparisons with other countries, cost estimates for bills. Use its **"חיפוש מסמכים"** box. | Each paper is dated; the law may have changed since. It describes, it does not advocate. |
| **Think tanks and NGOs** (for example the [Israel Democracy Institute](https://www.idi.org.il/), and NGOs from all parts of the spectrum) | Deep analysis, model documents, collections of rulings on one issue. | Many write from a position. Read two organisations with different views on contested issues. |
| **Law journals** (for example [משפטים](https://lawjournal.huji.ac.il/), Hebrew University) | Thorough analysis of a doctrine, with every ruling footnoted. | Long and academic. Read the introduction and conclusion, then mine the footnotes for rulings. |

### The method: orient, extract, verify

1. **Orient.** Read one or two secondary sources on your issue. Write a five-line summary in your own words.
2. **Extract.** List every primary source they mention: laws with section numbers, regulations, case numbers, Attorney General guidelines.
3. **Verify.** Open each primary source with the methods of lessons 02.1 and 02.2. Check that the section says what the guide says, and that it is current.
4. **Cite.** In your letter, cite the law and the ruling. You may mention a Knesset research paper for data ("לפי נתוני מרכז המחקר והמידע של הכנסת"), but never as the legal basis.

### Which statements must you verify?

- **Always verify:** numbers (fees, ceilings), deadlines, the name of the responsible authority, any "you are entitled to…".
- **Verify if you rely on it:** statements about what courts have held.
- **Fine to take as background:** history, statistics from official bodies, comparative information.

### How long it takes

A source map for a new issue: about an hour. It saves many hours later.

## Worked example

**Situation.** A coastal residents' group wants to stop sewage overflows reaching their beach. They do not know which law applies or who is responsible.

1. **Orient on Kol Zchut.** They search "זיהום ים" and "ביוב". A page explains the general duties and, at the bottom under "מקורות משפטיים ורשמיים", names the relevant laws.
2. **Orient at the Knesset.** In the Research and Information Center search box they type "זיהום חופים". A paper prepared for a Knesset committee gives data on overflow incidents, lists the responsible bodies (the local water corporation, the Ministry of Environmental Protection, the local authority) and names the laws.
3. **Extract.** Their list: two laws with section numbers, one set of regulations, one Attorney General guideline mentioned in a footnote, and one HCJ case number from a think-tank paper.
4. **Verify.** They open each law on Wikisource and in the Knesset database (lesson 02.1). One section number in the think-tank paper was renumbered by a later amendment; they correct it. They look up the HCJ case (lesson 02.2) and find a later ruling citing it.
5. **Map.** Their source map has two columns: primary (cite) and secondary (background), each with a "checked on" date.

They now know which body to write to and on what legal basis — the subject of lessons 02.4 and 02.5.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- Your issue, your law card (02.1) and ruling cards (02.2).
- About 60 minutes.

### Steps

1. Find one Kol Zchut page on your issue. Copy its "מקורות משפטיים ורשמיים" list and its last-updated date.
2. Search the Knesset Research and Information Center for your issue. Save one paper and note its date.
3. Find one more secondary source: a think-tank paper, an NGO guide or a journal article. If your issue is contested, find a second one from a different viewpoint.
4. From all three, list every primary source mentioned.
5. Verify each primary source: current text (02.1) or still good law (02.2). Mark each ✔ verified or ✘ wrong / outdated, with what was wrong.
6. Write your source map: a table with columns *Source | Primary or secondary | What it says about my issue | Checked on | Verified?*.

### How you know it worked

- Every legal statement you plan to use points to a primary source you opened yourself.
- You found at least one thing a secondary source got wrong or simplified — or you checked carefully enough to be sure there was none.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Citing Kol Zchut or an article as the legal basis | The authority dismisses the point as "not law" | Replace with the law or ruling it relies on | Cite primary, use secondary to orient |
| Copying a fee or deadline from an old guide | Late or underpaid filing | Check [Current Numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md) and the regulation | Always verify numbers |
| Relying on one advocacy paper on a contested issue | One-sided picture; you are surprised by the counter-argument | Read a source from the other side | Two viewpoints for every contested issue |
| Trusting a section number in a footnote | You quote a renumbered or repealed section | Re-check on Wikisource and the Knesset database | Verify every section number |

## Review

- Secondary sources orient you; primary sources are what you cite.
- Kol Zchut's "מקורות משפטיים ורשמיים" list and Knesset Research papers are the fastest routes to the right laws.
- Always verify numbers, deadlines and the responsible authority against the primary source.
- On contested issues, read sources from more than one viewpoint.
- Date everything in your source map.
