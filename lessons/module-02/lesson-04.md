# 02.4 · Building a Legal Argument

By the end of this lesson you will be able to turn your research into a short, structured argument — issue, rule, application, conclusion — that an official, an ombudsman or a judge can follow and check line by line.

## Why it matters

Officials and judges read many letters a day. A letter that tells a story and ends with "this is unjust" is easy to answer with a standard refusal. A letter that names the legal question, quotes the rule, shows that each part of the rule is met by a provable fact, and states exactly what you want, is hard to refuse without reasons — and a refusal without reasons is itself a legal problem for the authority.

The same four-part structure is used in every document in this course: letters, complaints, objections, petitions and the research memo at the end of this module.

| עברית | Transliteration | English |

## The law

Most rules are lists of conditions. Read them as checklists.

> נתבקש עובד הציבור, בכתב, להשתמש בסמכות שניתנה לו על פי דין, יחליט בבקשה וישיב למבקש בכתב בהקדם, אך לא יאוחר מארבעים וחמישה ימים מיום קבלת הבקשה.

*Gist:* when a public servant is asked in writing to use a power the law gives them, they must decide and answer in writing within 45 days. Elements: (1) a public servant, (2) a written request, (3) to use a legal power, (4) 45 days from receipt. ([חוק לתיקון סדרי המינהל (החלטות והנמקות), s. 2(א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_לתיקון_סדרי_המינהל_%28החלטות_והנמקות%29#סעיף_2))

> עובד הציבור שנתבקש כאמור בסעיף 2(א) וסירב לבקשה, יודיע למבקש בכתב את נימוקי סירובו.

*Gist:* a refusal must come with written reasons. ([s. 2א](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_לתיקון_סדרי_המינהל_%28החלטות_והנמקות%29#סעיף_2א))

> […] ואולם בכל הליך לפני ועדת ערר או ועדת ערעור או לפני בית המשפט, על עובד הציבור הראיה כי החלטה או פעולה שלא ניתנה לגביהן תשובה או הודעה כאמור, נעשו כדין.

*Gist:* if the authority did not give the required answer or reasons, it carries the burden of proving its decision was lawful in any later appeal or court case — a strong point to put in an argument. ([s. 6(א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_לתיקון_סדרי_המינהל_%28החלטות_והנמקות%29#סעיף_6))

> אין פוגעים בזכויות שלפי חוק־יסוד זה אלא בחוק ההולם את ערכיה של מדינת ישראל, שנועד לתכלית ראויה, ובמידה שאינה עולה על הנדרש או לפי חוק כאמור מכוח הסמכה מפורשת בו.

*Gist:* the limitation clause is a four-element test — authorised by law, fitting the state's values, a proper purpose, no more than necessary; Module 3 teaches it in depth, but notice that it, too, is a checklist. ([חוק-יסוד: כבוד האדם וחירותו, s. 8](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק-יסוד:_כבוד_האדם_וחירותו#סעיף_8))

## How it works in practice

### IRAC in four paragraphs

| Part | Hebrew heading you can use | What it does | Typical length |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I**ssue | השאלה | One sentence: the question the reader must decide | 1–2 lines |
| **R**ule | הדין | The section quoted exactly, plus the ruling that interprets it, each with its source | 3–8 lines |
| **A**pplication | יישום על העובדות | Each element of the rule, matched to a fact and the proof of that fact | the longest part |
| **C**onclusion | מסקנה ובקשה | What follows, and exactly what you ask for, by when | 2–4 lines |

### The element table

Before you write a sentence, make a table: **element | my fact | my proof**. Every element needs a fact; every fact needs proof (a document, a photo, a date-stamped email, an affidavit — lesson 02.6). An empty cell is the weak point the authority will find.

### Answer the counter-argument

Ask: what will the official say back? Common answers: "the law sets a different deadline", "this needs further checking", "it is not our authority", "we have no budget". Look in the law for the exception they will use (in the Decisions and Reasons Law, s. 2(ב) lists exceptions) and deal with it in one paragraph: "גם אם…" (even if…).

### Rules of style

- One point per paragraph; number the paragraphs.
- Facts first, then law: officials need to know what happened before they care what the law says.
- Quote the law, do not paraphrase it. Quote briefly.
- Cite rulings by case number and paragraph.
- No adjectives doing the work of arguments ("outrageous", "scandalous"). Let the elements carry it.
- End with a request the reader can say yes to: a specific act, by a specific date.

## Worked example

**Situation.** Parents in a Haredi neighbourhood asked their municipality in writing, on 1 June, to install a pedestrian crossing outside a girls' school. On 20 August they received one line: "הבקשה נדחתה". They want to write back.

**Element table**

| Element (s. 2 + 2א) | Fact | Proof |
|---|---|---|
| Public servant | The municipal engineer, to whom the request was addressed | Municipal website listing the post |
| Written request | Letter of 1 June | Copy with email delivery receipt |
| To use a power under law | Placing traffic signs and crossings is a municipal power | The relevant law and regulation, quoted |
| Answer within 45 days | Answer came on 20 August, 80 days later | Date on the reply |
| Refusal with written reasons | One-line refusal, no reasons | The reply itself |

**Their argument (summary of the Hebrew text they wrote):**

1. **Issue.** May the municipality refuse the request without reasons, after 80 days?
2. **Rule.** They quote s. 2(א), s. 2א and s. 6(א), each with its source.
3. **Application.** They go element by element through the table. Then the counter-argument: "Even if the matter needed further examination under s. 2(ב)(3), s. 2(ג) required the municipality to tell us so in writing within 45 days, and it did not."
4. **Conclusion.** They ask for written reasons within 14 days, and for the request to be reconsidered, attaching a traffic count the parents made.

The letter itself is written in lesson 02.5.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- Your law card, ruling cards and source map (02.1–02.3).
- 50 minutes.

### Steps

1. Write your **issue** as one question that can be answered yes or no.
2. Choose the **one rule** that best answers it. Quote it exactly.
3. Break the rule into elements. Make the element table: element | my fact | my proof.
4. Fill every cell. Where you have no proof, write how you will get it (a photo, an FOI request, an affidavit).
5. Add one ruling that interprets the rule (if you found one), cited by case number and paragraph.
6. Write the **counter-argument** the authority is most likely to use, and your one-paragraph answer.
7. Write the **conclusion**: exactly what you ask for and by when.
8. Put it together: four numbered sections, no more than one page.

### How you know it worked

- A friend who does not know the case can read your page and tell you what you want and why the law gives it to you.
- Every element in your table has both a fact and a proof.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Telling the story and skipping the rule | A polite standard refusal | Add the rule section with the exact quote | Write the element table first |
| Missing one element of the rule | The authority answers on that element only | Add the fact and proof, or switch to a rule you can meet | Check every element against your table |
| Facts with no proof | "We have no record of this" | Gather the document, photo or affidavit | Proof column in the table |
| Ignoring the obvious exception | The reply quotes the exception and closes the file | Write back answering it | Read the whole section, including the exceptions |
| A vague request ("act on this") | Nothing happens, and it is hard to show a refusal later | Ask for a specific act by a date | End every argument with a measurable request |

## Review

- Issue, rule, application, conclusion: one question, the quoted rule, element-by-element facts with proof, a specific request.
- Read every rule as a checklist of elements.
- An element without a provable fact is your weak point; fix it before you send.
- Answer the authority's best counter-argument in your own text.
- No reasons from the authority shifts the burden to it (Decisions and Reasons Law s. 6(א)).
