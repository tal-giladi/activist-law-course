# 02.2 · Finding Rulings for Free

By the end of this lesson you will be able to find Supreme Court and High Court of Justice rulings for free, read a case number at a glance, check whether a ruling has been overturned or narrowed, and quote it without breaking a publication ban.

## Why it matters

A law tells you the rule; rulings tell you how courts apply it. When you write to an authority, quoting one ruling that decided a case like yours is often more persuasive than three paragraphs of argument. When you petition, the court expects you to know its rulings on your issue.

Many people believe rulings are only available in paid databases. The rulings that matter most to activists — Supreme Court and High Court of Justice (בג״ץ) judgments — are published free by the judiciary. The paid databases add search tools and lower-court coverage, not access to the law itself.

## Key terms

| עברית | Transliteration | English |
|---|---|---|
| פסק דין | psak din | Judgment: the final decision in a case |
| החלטה | hachlata | Decision: a ruling on an interim matter during the case |
| הלכה | halacha | Binding legal rule set by a court (precedent) |
| אמרת אגב | imrat agav | Obiter dictum: a remark that is not part of the binding rule |
| מספר הליך | mispar halich | Case number, for example בג״ץ 5658/23 |
| דיון נוסף | diyun nosaf | Further hearing: a larger Supreme Court panel re-examines a ruling |
| ערכאה קודמת | ercaa kodemet | The lower court whose decision is under appeal |
| איסור פרסום | isur pirsum | Publication ban (gag order) |
| פ״ד | piskei din | The official law reports of the Supreme Court |

## The law

> (א) הלכה שנפסקה בבית משפט תנחה בית משפט של דרגה נמוכה ממנו.
> (ב) הלכה שנפסקה בבית המשפט העליון מחייבת כל בית משפט, זולת בית המשפט העליון.

*Gist:* a Supreme Court rule binds every lower court; a lower court's ruling only guides courts below it, so a Supreme Court ruling is the strongest authority you can quote. ([חוק-יסוד: השפיטה, s. 20](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק-יסוד:_השפיטה#סעיף_20))

> לא החליט בית המשפט העליון כאמור בסעיף קטן (א), רשאי כל בעל דין לבקש דיון נוסף כאמור; […] אם ההלכה שנפסקה בבית המשפט העליון עומדת בסתירה להלכה קודמת של בית המשפט העליון, או שמפאת חשיבותה, קשיותה או חידושה של הלכה שנפסקה בענין, יש, לדעתם, מקום לדיון נוסף.

*Gist:* a party can ask for a further hearing before a larger panel, so an important ruling may have been changed later; always check. ([חוק בתי המשפט, s. 30(ב)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_בתי_המשפט#סעיף_30))

> (א) לא יפרסם אדם דבר על דיון שהתנהל בבית משפט בדלתיים סגורות אלא ברשות בית המשפט.

*Gist:* closed-door proceedings may not be reported without the court's permission, and s. 70 adds further bans (minors, gag orders); check before you quote names. ([חוק בתי המשפט, s. 70](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_בתי_המשפט#סעיף_70))

## How it works in practice

### Reading a case number

A case number has three parts: **type of proceeding**, **serial number**, **year** (two digits). *בג״ץ 5658/23* is High Court of Justice petition number 5658 filed in 2023. Common types:

| Abbreviation | Proceeding | Court |
|---|---|---|
| בג״ץ | Petition to the High Court of Justice | Supreme Court |
| עע״מ | Appeal in an administrative matter | Supreme Court, from an administrative court |
| ע״א / רע״א | Civil appeal / application for leave to appeal | Supreme Court |
| דנג״ץ / דנ״א | Further hearing of a HCJ ruling / a civil appeal | Supreme Court, larger panel |
| עת״מ | Administrative petition | District court sitting as administrative court |

Lower courts, in the Net HaMishpat system, use a different format with a month, for example *עת״מ 35192-09-24*: serial, month, year.

### Tool 1: the Supreme Court judgments search

[supremedecisions.court.gov.il](https://supremedecisions.court.gov.il/) is the judiciary's free search. It holds Supreme Court judgments and decisions from **1997 onward**, plus judgments printed in the official reports from 2006 (volume סא onward). No signup.

- **"חיפוש חופשי ולפי מס' הליך"** — type a case number such as `5658/23`, or free text.
- **"שופטים, צדדים ובאי כח"** — search by a party's name, for example the ministry or NGO you are dealing with.
- **"מדור/הליך"** — limit to בג״ץ, administrative appeals and so on.
- **"תאריך המסמך"** and sorting by date help you find the newest rulings first.
- Filter for **פסק דין** rather than every decision, unless you are following a live case.

### Tool 2: Versa (English translations)

[Versa](https://versa.cardozo.yu.edu/opinions), run by Cardozo School of Law, publishes free English translations of landmark Supreme Court opinions, browsable by topic (administrative law, constitutional law, freedom of expression and more). Use it to understand a ruling, then quote the Hebrew original from the judiciary site.

### Lower courts

District and Magistrates' Court rulings are harder. Some are on the judiciary's website and Net HaMishpat; many are only in paid databases. For activists this rarely matters: the binding rules come from the Supreme Court.

### Is the ruling still good law?

1. Search the case number in the judgments search. A later **דנג״ץ** or **דנ״א** with the same number means a further hearing was held; read its result.
2. Search the case number as **free text**. Later rulings that cite it appear; read the newest two or three to see whether they follow it, narrow it or distinguish it.
3. Check whether the law the ruling interpreted has been amended since (lesson 02.1).

### Never pay for what is free

Nevo's case-law pages need a paid subscription. You do not need them: everything in this course can be found through the two tools above.

## Worked example

**Situation.** Noa uses a wheelchair and campaigns for accessible public transport in her city. She wants to show the municipality how the High Court has treated accessibility duties.

1. **Search.** On supremedecisions she enters the free text `נגישות תחבורה ציבורית` and limits the type to בג״ץ and עע״מ. She sorts by date, newest first, and filters to judgments.
2. **Skim.** For each result she reads the first page (parties, judges) and the last page (the outcome and orders). Five of twenty results are actually about accessibility duties; she saves them as PDFs.
3. **Pick.** She chooses the newest judgment that ordered an authority to act and notes its case number.
4. **Check.** She searches that number as free text. Two later rulings cite it, both following it. There is no further hearing.
5. **Understand.** She looks for an English translation on Versa under the relevant topic to make sure she understood the holding. If there is none, she rereads the Hebrew holding paragraph twice.
6. **Record.** Her ruling card: case number, parties, date, court, the one-paragraph holding in Hebrew, a one-line English gist, "still good law — checked 12.10.2026, cited in two later judgments".

The same method found her the case-number anatomy practice above: *בג״ץ 5658/23* (the 2024 ruling on the "reasonableness" amendment to a Basic Law) is a High Court petition from 2023. That ruling is contested; lesson 03.5 presents the positions of all sides.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- Your issue and your law card from lesson 02.1.
- 50 minutes, a browser and a folder for PDFs.

### Steps

1. Write 3–4 Hebrew search phrases for your issue: the name of the law, a key term from the section, the authority's name.
2. On [supremedecisions.court.gov.il](https://supremedecisions.court.gov.il/), run each phrase. Limit to judgments. Sort newest first.
3. Skim the first and last pages of the top ten results. Keep the two most relevant.
4. For each, find the holding paragraph — usually near the end, often starting "סוף דבר" or "אשר על כן" — and copy it exactly.
5. Check each ruling for a further hearing and for later rulings that cite it.
6. Browse [Versa](https://versa.cardozo.yu.edu/opinions) by the topic closest to your issue. Note one translated ruling that helps you understand the field.
7. Check that nothing you quote is under a publication ban (minors, closed-door hearings, gag orders); if in doubt, quote the legal rule and leave out names.
8. Write a ruling card for each: case number, parties, date, holding quote, gist, "still good law" check with date.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Quoting a ruling later reversed in a further hearing | The authority or court points out it is not the law | Quote the further-hearing ruling instead | Search the case number for דנג״ץ / דנ״א |
| Quoting a remark in passing as if it were the holding | Your argument rests on obiter, which binds no one | Find the paragraph that decides the case | Read the outcome first, then trace back the reasoning |
| Relying only on a district-court ruling | It does not bind other district courts | Look for a Supreme Court ruling on the point | Search the Supreme Court first |
| Publishing names under a ban | Possible criminal liability | Remove the post, consult Legal Aid or a clinic | Check s. 70 bans; quote the rule, not the names |
| Paying for a database to find a HCJ ruling | Wasted money | Use the free judgments search | Start with supremedecisions and Versa |

## Review

- Supreme Court rulings bind every lower court (Basic Law: The Judiciary s. 20); they are free on the judiciary's search from 1997.
- A case number tells you the proceeding, the serial number and the year.
- Versa gives free English translations to help you understand; quote the Hebrew.
- Before relying on a ruling, check for a further hearing and for later rulings that cite it.
- Check publication bans before quoting names.
